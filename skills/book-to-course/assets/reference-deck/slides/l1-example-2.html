Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

The second room was a lot harder. After high school I had no idea what I wanted to do with my life. I eventually took a job on Wall Street. I didn't initially understand that what was happening at the firm was illegal. Once I realized it, I quit and found another job. But about two years later the FBI showed up at my door. They wanted me to cooperate in their investigation of the people who owned the firm. I refused. And at twenty years old I found myself in federal prison. Another guy and I were the two youngest inmates on the entire unit. Suddenly reading people wasn't about getting a laugh in a classroom. I was surrounded by people from completely different cultures and backgrounds. Different races. Different ages. Different life experiences. There were rules. Some were written. Many were unwritten. And I had to figure them out quickly. Who could I trust? Who should I stay away from? When should I talk? When should I keep my mouth shut? How would someone interpret what I was about to say? If I did one thing, how would another person respond? What was really happening underneath the obvious interaction? Again I didn't think about any of this formally. I simply navigated it. And somehow I navigated it well enough to make some very good friends. I built genuine relationships with people whose lives were completely different from mine. Look at that list of questions again. Not one of them is about choosing the right words. Every one is about reading the game. Then I came home and faced a different problem.
