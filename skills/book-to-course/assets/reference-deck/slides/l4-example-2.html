Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Let's walk through it one step at a time. Model. Your first read was that their resistance was about status. Act. You offered them public credit. Observe. They're still resistant. Now comes the step most people skip. Update. Instead of deciding they're irrational, you ask what you misunderstood about the game. Maybe the real cost to them isn't status at all. Maybe it's workload. Your project might land on their team's plate. Or maybe it's risk. If your project fails, maybe they'll be the one who pays for it. Either one would explain why credit didn't help. Credit doesn't reduce anyone's workload. It doesn't reduce their risk. In fact public credit might even raise their risk. Their name is now attached to something they're worried about. Then adapt. You stop offering credit. You start asking about capacity. What would this project cost you? What would make it workable for your team? Now you're having a completely different conversation. And notice that you still might be wrong. Maybe it isn't workload either. That's fine. You go around the loop again. Each pass gives you a better picture of the game. The surprise didn't mean you failed. It improved your model. Here's why this loop matters so much. You'll never have perfect information about another person. Nobody does. The people who get good at this aren't the ones who predict perfectly. They're the ones who learn fastest from what happens. So here's a question for you. Is there someone in your life right now whose response keeps surprising you? What's one update you could make to your model of their game?
