Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Let me flag the four mistakes I see most when people start this course. The first is treating "game" as a cynical word. In this course a game includes cooperation. It includes friendship and family life. Seeing the structure is about understanding. It's not about winning at someone else's expense. If you catch yourself using these ideas to get one over on people, stop and go back to the four ethical tests. The second mistake is expecting scripts. This course won't give you seven phrases for every situation. I know that can be frustrating at first. Scripts feel safe. But a script only works in the situation it was written for. Better questions transfer to situations no script anticipates. The third mistake is overanalyzing everything. Once people learn these models, some of them start running a full audit on every conversation. Don't. Most interactions don't need a strategic audit. Use as much analysis as the stakes and the uncertainty justify. A quick chat with a coworker about lunch needs none. A salary negotiation needs a lot. The fourth mistake is mistaking your model for the truth. Every read of another person is a hypothesis. Test it. Remember the resistant colleague. The first read was status. The real issue was something else. If you'd treated your first read as fact, you'd never have found out. So here's a question. Which of these four are you most likely to fall into? Most people have a favorite. Knowing yours ahead of time makes it much easier to catch. Now here's your assignment for this week.
