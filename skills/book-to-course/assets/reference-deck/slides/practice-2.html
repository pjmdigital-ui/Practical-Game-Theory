Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Here are your three questions. First, who are the players? And I want you to include anyone who isn't in Dana's conversations. Those are often the most important ones. Second, why might better wording fail here? Dana has already explained and rephrased and gotten firm. Why isn't any of that working? Third, what question should Dana be asking instead of "How do I say this more clearly?" Here's a hint if you get stuck. Don't start by asking what's wrong with Marcus. Start by asking what makes his current behavior sensible from where he stands. That's the move we talked about in lesson four. You're not excusing him. You're trying to see the game from his side. Look at his bonus. Look at the client. Ask what happens to Marcus if he finishes early. Think about it from his chair for a minute. Then think about it from the client's chair. Here's the common mistake people make with this exercise. They jump straight to a script for Dana. They write a better email. That's exactly the trap Dana's colleague fell into. The point of this exercise is to see the structure before you reach for words. And there's no single right answer here. If your list of players is longer than mine, that's great. If you see an incentive I miss, even better. What matters is that you're asking structural questions instead of wording questions. So go ahead and pause the video now.
