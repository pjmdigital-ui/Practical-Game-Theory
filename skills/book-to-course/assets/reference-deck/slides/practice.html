Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Let's practice. Here's a scenario I want you to work through. Dana manages a small marketing team. For three weeks she's asked a senior designer named Marcus to submit his drafts two days before client deadlines. She wants that time so she can review them. She has explained why. She has rephrased the request. Finally she sent a firm email. Marcus keeps submitting the night before. Now one of Dana's colleagues gives her some advice. Work on your delivery. That's the classic communication answer. Say it better and he'll comply. But Dana suspects the problem isn't her words at all. And look at what's underneath. Marcus is paid a bonus based on client satisfaction scores. He isn't paid on internal deadlines. And the client contact loves Marcus. That client often requests last-minute changes directly from him. So take a moment with this. Dana has been treating this as a conversation problem. She's been trying to find the right words. Three times now. Each version clearer and firmer than the last. And nothing has changed. Does that sound familiar? It's exactly the pattern from lesson one. The same approach keeps failing. And the natural response is to try a better version of the same approach. Before you go on, I want you to look closely at the four cards on the screen. Who is actually involved here? What does each of them want? What does each of them gain from things staying the way they are? On the next slide you'll get three questions to answer. Pause the video when you get there and write your answers in your workbook.
