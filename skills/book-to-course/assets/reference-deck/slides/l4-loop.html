Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

And here's the loop. Every read you make of another person is a hypothesis. It's not a fact. So you model. Based on what you know, you form your best understanding of the game. Then you act. Eventually you have to make a move. Then you observe. What actually happens? Then you update. Where did reality differ from your prediction? And then you adapt. You change your strategy or you change your model. Then you go around again. That loop may matter more than any single model in this course. Because strategic thinking isn't perfect prediction. It isn't always being right. It's learning faster from what happens. And it means surprise is a gift. When someone doesn't respond the way you predicted, they've just shown you that your model of the game was incomplete. Here's an example from the book. Say you predict that if you refuse a request, the other person will get angry. You refuse. They say "No problem. I completely understand." Good. You were wrong. And that's valuable. Maybe you overestimated how much they depended on you. Maybe you misunderstood what they cared about. Don't ignore the gap because you want your model to be right. Update. Here's the common mistake. When people surprise us, the easiest thing to say is "They're irrational." Sometimes people are. But that label stops all learning. A better question is "What did I misunderstand about the game?" Maybe their payoff was different. Maybe they had information you didn't. So think about the last time someone surprised you. Did you update your model? Or did you decide something was wrong with them? Let's walk through the loop with a real example.
