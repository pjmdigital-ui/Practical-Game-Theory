Part II deals with uncertainty. It's about what people know and believe. It's also about which words deserve to be trusted.

Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Part Two is about seeing what isn't obvious. In one word it's about uncertainty. Part One assumed we could see the game fairly clearly. Real life is rarely that clean. People know different things. They believe different things. And they say things that may or may not be true. Model six is Map Information and Beliefs. Its question is what do I actually know, and what am I assuming? This one is humbling. Most of us treat our assumptions as facts without noticing. Model seven is Separate Words From Credibility. Its question is what makes this message believable? Anyone can say anything. So the real question is what makes a statement worth trusting. Model eight is Commitment Changes the Game. Its question is what actually binds a promise, a threat or a boundary? A promise can be persuasive in one situation and meaningless in another. The difference is usually whether something actually binds the person to follow through. Look at the progression here. Information. Credibility. Commitment. First we ask what people know. Then we ask which messages deserve belief. Then we ask which promises and threats are real. Here's the common misunderstanding with this part of the course. People hear "credibility" and think it means being suspicious of everyone. It doesn't. Thinking strategically isn't the same as thinking suspiciously. Suspicion assumes hostile intent. Strategic thinking just tests possibilities. The framework should make you less naive. It shouldn't make you cynical. So here's a question. Think of the last promise someone made you. What actually made you believe it? Now let's look at Part Three.
