Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Throughout this course I'll use the word "player." That's the correct term in game theory. But I know it can sound cold when we're talking about human relationships. It shouldn't. A player is simply a decision-maker within the interaction. That's all it means. Your spouse is a player. Your business partner is a player. Your customer is a player. So is your employee. So is your boss. And so are you. Recognizing someone as a player doesn't mean reducing them to a selfish calculating machine. Real human beings have complicated motivations. They care about money. They also care about fairness. They care about winning. They also care about relationships. They care about status and identity. They care about loyalty and security. They care about principles and reputation. And they care about countless other things. That's exactly why we need to learn what matters to each person. We can't just assume everyone wants the same thing. So here's the warning I want you to hear clearly. If you leave this course treating people as incentives and leverage points, you've learned the wrong lesson. The person across from you has fears. They have responsibilities. They have dignity. The word "player" helps us think clearly about choices. It never replaces seeing the whole person. People are always more than players. Ask yourself this. When you hear the word "player," what comes to mind first? If it's someone working an angle, I want you to replace that picture. Picture someone making a decision that affects you.
