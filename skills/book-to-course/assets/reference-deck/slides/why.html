Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Most communication training starts with words. What to say. How to phrase it. Which questions to ask. How to handle the objection. Those things matter. I've spent a big part of my career teaching them. But here's what I eventually realized. By the time you're choosing words you've already made a series of quieter decisions. You see four of them on the screen. What is happening here? What does this person want? What do they know? How are they likely to respond? Most of us make those calls in a split second and never look at them again. And if those judgments are wrong, excellent communication doesn't save you. It just helps you carry out the wrong strategy more effectively. Think about the last time you said exactly what you meant to say and it still went badly. The words were probably fine. The read of the situation was off. That's the layer this course teaches. We call it Internomics. It's the study of what happens between people. It takes the ideas of game theory and applies them to everyday communication. You won't do any math. Instead you'll learn eleven mental models that change the questions you ask before you speak. And I want to be clear about one thing right away. The goal is not to make you a better manipulator. The goal is to help you see the game behind the conversation. Once you see it you have far more choices about how you play. So here's a question to hold onto. When a conversation goes wrong for you, where do you look first? At your words or at your read of the situation? Let's look at what you'll be able to do by the end of this module.
