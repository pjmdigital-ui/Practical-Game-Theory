Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Now let's bring this home. Dana's situation was someone else's game. This exercise is about yours. Step one. Think of one recurring interaction that frustrates you. It could be at work. It could be at home. It could be with a customer. The key word is recurring. Pick something that keeps happening. Step two. Write down the last thing you said in it. The actual words as best you remember them. Step three. This is the important one. List everything you assumed before you said those words. What did you assume they wanted? What did you assume they knew? How did you assume they'd respond? Be thorough here. Most people are surprised by how many assumptions are packed into one sentence. Step four. Go back over your list and circle any assumption you have never actually tested. You've never asked. You've never checked. You just believed it. Here's why this matters. Those circled assumptions are your model of the game. And if Dana taught us anything, it's that the model can be wrong while the words are perfectly fine. Every circle is a place where your read might be off. The common mistake is to treat this as a confession of everything you did wrong. It isn't. You're not grading yourself. You're just making the invisible visible. So here's my challenge for you. Pick one circled assumption and find a gentle way to test it this week. Ask a question you've never asked. See what you learn. Now let's cover the mistakes I see most often when people start this course.
