# The Internomics Cheat Sheet

**Bonus 1 · The eleven mental models on one card**

Keep this next to you before the conversations that matter. Find what you're noticing and start with the question it points to.

## The 11 Mental Models

| # | Model | The question to ask | Watch for (signal it matters) | Quick move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | See the Game | What's really going on here? | You're rehearsing the perfect sentence before you understand the situation. | Map who can affect the outcome. Include anyone not in the room. |
| 2 | See the Payoffs | What payoff would make their behavior make sense? | You're about to call someone irrational or difficult. | List more than one currency. Status and fairness count. |
| 3 | Think in Responses | If I do this, what are they likely to do next? And if they do that, what will I do? | "I was just being honest." You're judging the move by your intention. | Ask what your move makes them want to do. Then what? |
| 4 | Manage Predictability | What does my predictability enable the other player to do? | People know exactly how to pressure you. | Be consistent where trust matters. Protect your bottom line. |
| 5 | See the Equilibrium | What equilibrium have we created? | The same argument keeps coming back. Nobody likes it. Nobody changes. | Change something structural. Not just the next sentence. |
| 6 | Map Information & Beliefs | What do they know—or believe—that I don't? | "He knows why I'm upset." Certainty without evidence. | Separate facts from assumptions. Label how confident you are. |
| 7 | Separate Words From Credibility | What makes this message credible? | Would they say this even if it were false? | Look for what's costly to fake. Make your own truth easy to evaluate. |
| 8 | Commitment Changes the Game | What makes this commitment real? | Threats. Deadlines. "Final" offers. "My hands are tied." | Use a consequence you'll actually enforce. Ask what binds them. |
| 9 | The Shadow of the Future | What changes because there is a next time? | You'll deal with this person again. | Cooperate conditionally. Leave a path to repair. |
| 10 | Diagnose the Coordination Problem | Are we actually in conflict—or are we failing to coordinate? | Everyone wants the outcome. Nobody acts. | Make clear who acts and when. Say it in front of everyone. |
| 11 | Change the Game | Do I need a better move—or a better game? | Better words and more pressure keep failing. | Ask what the system actually rewards. Make the smallest testable change. |

## Quick Model Selector

| If you notice… | Start with |
|---|---|
| You're rehearsing what to say before a big conversation | Model 1 · See the Game |
| Their behavior seems stubborn or irrational | Model 2 · See the Payoffs |
| The same argument keeps repeating | Model 5 · See the Equilibrium |
| You're sure "they know" or sure what they think | Model 6 · Map Information & Beliefs |
| They said yes but nothing changed | Model 7 · Credibility, then Model 8 · Commitment |
| You're tempted to win this round with someone you'll see again | Model 9 · The Shadow of the Future |
| Everyone agrees on the goal and nothing happens | Model 10 · Diagnose the Coordination Problem |
| The same bad outcome returns no matter who's involved | Model 11 · Change the Game |

## The Ethical Standard

::: {.box}
**Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice.**

- **Truth.** Say only what you believe is true. Don't create a false impression.
- **Autonomy.** The other person stays free to choose. That includes saying no.
- **Dignity.** Would you be comfortable if they saw your full reasoning?
- **Informed choice.** They have the information they'd need to decide well.

Will this work? Should I do it? Never collapse them into one.
:::

## Golden Rules

1. **Don't start with the words. Start with the situation.**
2. **Your read is a hypothesis.** Surprise is information. Ask what you misunderstood about the game.
3. **Model → act → observe → update → adapt.** Your model of the game is provisional.
4. **Use as much analysis as the stakes and uncertainty justify.** Most interactions don't need a strategic audit.
5. **Here's what I know. Here's what I think it means. Here's how confident I am.** Certainty feels good. Accuracy is better.

## Before Any Important Conversation

- [ ] Who can affect the outcome? Include anyone not in the room.
- [ ] What does each of us actually value? Look past the money.
- [ ] What is my move likely to make them do? And what will I do then?
- [ ] What do I actually know? What am I only assuming?
- [ ] Does my plan pass truth, autonomy, dignity and informed choice?

## The Question to Carry

::: {.box}
Before your next move, ask:

**What game am I playing?**

Then look again. The first game you see may not be the real one.

After it's over, ask:

**What did I learn from what just happened?**

Don't just learn what to say. Learn to see the game behind the conversation.
:::
