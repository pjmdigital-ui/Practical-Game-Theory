# The AI Practice Partner Pack

**Bonus 2 · 18 ready-to-paste prompts that turn ChatGPT or Claude into a sparring partner with hidden motives**

Reading about the game is not the same as seeing it live.

You can understand payoffs perfectly on the page. Then a real person pushes back. Your pulse jumps. You answer the words instead of the game.

That gap closes with reps. Not more reading. Reps.

Real reps are expensive. A bad negotiation costs money. A bad conversation with your partner costs a weekend. So most of us only practice when it counts. That's the worst time to learn.

This pack gives you a free practice room.

You paste a prompt into an AI chat assistant. ChatGPT works. Claude works. So does any capable chat assistant. The AI becomes the other person. It has a hidden payoff. Or hidden information. Or a pattern you haven't spotted yet.

You talk to it like a real person. When you're done you type DEBRIEF. The AI steps out of character and tells you what game you were in. It shows you what you spotted and what you missed. Then it gives you one better move.

Ten minutes. No stakes. Real learning.

## How to Use This Pack

1. **Pick a prompt.** Start with a model you're studying this week. Starter prompts are a good first round.
2. **Open a fresh chat.** Use a new conversation for every scenario. Old context leaks into new games.
3. **Paste the whole prompt and send it.** The AI will open the scene in character.
4. **Play the conversation.** Type what you'd actually say. Ask questions. Test your read. Most sessions run 8 to 15 exchanges.
5. **Type DEBRIEF.** Read the breakdown slowly. Then answer the five questions in "After Each Session" below.

::: {.box}
**Four rules for good practice**

**Play it straight.** Say what you'd really say. Don't try to outsmart the AI or hunt for the answer. The point is to see your habits.

**Say your reasoning out loud after.** Before you read the debrief note what game you think you were in. Then compare.

**Run it twice.** The second run is where the learning lands. Use the better move and watch what changes.

**Keep it ethical.** Practice seeing the game and making honest moves. Don't use these prompts to rehearse pressure or deception. If a move only works because the other person can't see it, it's not a move to practice.
:::

## The Master Prompt

Every scenario in this pack is built on one template. Here it is. Fill in the brackets and you can practice any conversation you like.

```text
Let's run a live practice conversation. You will play a character. I will play myself.

YOUR ROLE: You are [WHO THE AI PLAYS: name, job and relationship to me].

THE SETTING: [WHERE WE ARE AND WHAT THE CONVERSATION IS ABOUT ON THE SURFACE].

YOUR HIDDEN PAYOFF: [WHAT YOU REALLY CARE ABOUT. THE VALUE OR FEAR THAT DRIVES YOU BUT THAT YOU WON'T SAY OUT LOUD].

WHAT YOU KNOW THAT I DON'T: [FACTS, DEADLINES, ALTERNATIVES OR CONSTRAINTS ONLY YOU KNOW].

YOUR PATTERN: [HOW YOU RESPOND TO DIFFERENT MOVES. WHAT MAKES YOU OPEN UP AND WHAT MAKES YOU DIG IN].

THIS SCENARIO PRACTICES: [THE MODEL OR SKILL I WANT TO BUILD].

RULES:
- Never reveal your hidden payoff, hidden information or pattern until I type DEBRIEF. Let me discover them through good questions and careful listening.
- Stay realistic and in character. Act like a real person would. Don't make it too easy or too hard.
- Keep each reply short: 1 to 4 sentences, like a real conversation.
- Reward honest, curious moves. Resist pressure the way a real person would.

When I type DEBRIEF, step out of character and give me:
1. The game we were in and the models in play.
2. Your hidden payoff and hidden information.
3. What I noticed.
4. What I missed.
5. One better move I could have made, and it must be honest.
6. A score from 1 to 5 on how well I saw the game.

Begin now with your opening line in character.
```

### What each bracket does

**Your role.** Give the character a name and a relationship to you. A boss behaves differently from a brother. The relationship sets the stakes.

**The setting.** This is the surface topic. The thing the conversation is supposedly about. Keep it to one or two sentences.

**Your hidden payoff.** This is the engine of the exercise. Status. Fear of blame. A family obligation. Something that makes their behavior make sense once you see it.

**What you know that I don't.** Hidden information. A deadline. A second offer. A boss leaning on them. This is what you'll practice drawing out.

**Your pattern.** How the character reacts to your moves. "Digs in when threatened. Opens up when asked about her constraints." This is what makes the practice feel real.

**This scenario practices.** Name the model. It focuses the debrief on the skill you're building.

## After Each Session

The AI's debrief is useful. Your own debrief is where the skill sticks.

Answer these five questions in writing. Do it before you start a second run.

1. **What game did I think I was in at the start?** Write your first read. Be honest about it.
2. **When did my read change?** Find the exact line. What did they say or do that shifted it?
3. **What did I respond to?** The words or the game? Name one moment you answered the surface.
4. **What question would have found the hidden part sooner?** Write it out word for word.
5. **What will I do differently in the real version?** One move. Make it specific.

### Log it in your Interaction Journal

You started an Interaction Journal in Module 0. Practice rounds belong there too.

Use one page per session. Fill in the usual fields. Date and setting. Who was involved. What you wanted. What you think they wanted. What you said. How they responded.

Then add three lines for practice:

- **Prompt and model:** Which scenario and which model it trained.
- **Score:** The AI's score out of 5 for each run.
- **Replay question:** The one question you'll ask yourself before the real conversation.

Watch the scores over a few weeks. Not to chase a number. To see which models you read quickly and which ones still hide from you.

## Build Your Own

The best prompt is the one built from your own life. Got a hard conversation coming up? Practice it first.

1. **Name the conversation.** Who is it with? What's the surface topic? Write one sentence.
2. **Guess their hidden side.** What payoff would make their behavior make sense? What might they know that you don't? What pattern have you seen from them before? Write your best guess for each.
3. **Fill in the Master Prompt.** Put your guesses into the brackets. Name the model you most need to practice.
4. **Run it three ways.** Once with your best guess. Once with a different hidden payoff. Once with a harder version of the character. If your plan works across all three, you're ready. If it only works in one, you've learned something.

::: {.box}
**Protect real people's privacy.** Change names. Remove company names and private details. Don't paste in personal information about anyone. The AI only needs the shape of the game. It doesn't need the person.
:::

Remember this. The character you build is a guess. The real person may surprise you. That's fine. Practice isn't about predicting them perfectly. It's about walking in already asking the right question.
