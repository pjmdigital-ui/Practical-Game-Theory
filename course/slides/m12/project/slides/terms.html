Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here are the key terms for this final module. They're all in your workbook. Strategic judgment means choosing which lenses matter in this situation. Then acting on your best model. Then learning from what happens. It's three things, not one. Recognition is noticing the structure of an interaction almost automatically. It's the opposite of reciting steps. That's the driver who doesn't think "check mirror" anymore. A provisional model is your current best interpretation of the game. You hold it as a hypothesis to test. Never as a conclusion to defend. The word provisional is doing a lot of work there. Prediction error is the gap between what you expected and what happened. Some people call it surprise. It's the most useful signal that your model has a hole in it. And you only get it if you made a prediction first. Multiple hypotheses means holding several plausible explanations at once. Then gathering evidence that tells them apart. Remember the resistant customer and the five possible reasons. Transfer means recognizing a familiar structure in a situation you've never seen before. A commitment problem in your own habits. A coordination problem in a project. A mechanism-design problem hiding behind "people just need to try harder." And calibration means making intuition trustworthy by checking it against outcomes. Your confidence should rise only as your accuracy does. Not just because time has passed.
