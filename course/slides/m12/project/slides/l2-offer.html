Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Let me show you what choosing a lens looks like. Suppose someone says to you "I need your answer by tomorrow. This is absolutely the final offer." The old question is "What should I say?" But now you have other lenses. And every one of them produces a useful question. See the Game asks who actually has authority here. Is this person even making the decision? Are there other players? See the Payoffs asks what happens to them if I say no. Do they genuinely need an answer tomorrow? Think in Responses asks what they'll likely do if I decline. Manage Predictability asks something uncomfortable. Have I taught them that artificial pressure makes me move? Map Information and Beliefs asks what I actually know about this deadline. What am I merely assuming? Credibility asks what makes "final offer" believable. Commitment asks whether they're actually constrained or just saying they are. The Shadow of the Future asks whether I'll negotiate with this person again. What precedent does my response create? Coordination asks whether we even disagree about the underlying outcome. And Change the Game asks whether I need to accept their yes-or-no structure at all. Perhaps I can introduce another option. That's a lot of good questions. But here's the point. You probably don't need all eleven. If you tried to run every one of them before replying, you'd freeze. Judgment means recognizing which two or three matter most right now. Maybe here it's credibility and the shadow of the future. Maybe it's authority. So which two would you pick? And how would you know? Part of the answer is knowing when to use the framework at all.
