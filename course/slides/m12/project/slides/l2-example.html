Example · The one-word text

Sometimes "Sure" means "Sure."

The situation

You ask a friend something. They text back one word: "Sure."

What happened

You start wondering: "What strategic signal does the absence of an exclamation point communicate about their latent payoff structure?"

The lesson

Not every interaction needs analysis. Don't turn ordinary life into chess.

Part of judgment is knowing when to put the lenses down.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's my favorite example of the tool used badly. It's from the book and it always gets a laugh. Probably because most of us have done some version of it. You ask a friend something. Maybe whether they can make dinner on Friday. They text back one word. "Sure." And suddenly you're thinking "What strategic signal does the absence of an exclamation point communicate about their latent payoff structure?" Don't do that. I mean it. Not every interaction requires strategic analysis. Sometimes "Sure" means "Sure." Run it against the seven criteria from a minute ago. Do the stakes matter? Not really. Is a pattern repeating? No. Is trust uncertain? It's your friend. Nothing here meets the bar. So the right amount of analysis is zero. If you catch yourself turning a one-word text into a signaling puzzle, that's not strategic judgment. That's the opposite of it. And it has a real cost. You get anxious. You send a follow-up that makes things weird. You start treating a friend like a counterparty. Now let me be fair to the other side. Say the same friend has gone cold for months. And "Sure" is one more sign in that pattern. That's different. Then a pattern is repeating. Then the lens might help. The point isn't that short texts never mean anything. The point is that the framework has an on switch and an off switch. Part of judgment is knowing when the lenses help. The other part is knowing when to put them down and just take people at their word. That brings us to the takeaway for this lesson.
