Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here are a few more disguises from the book. I picked them because they come from home and from work. Your child isn't going to say "I have detected that your repeated concessions have created an exploitable strategy." They're just going to ask again. And again. That's Manage Predictability. If asking a fourth time has worked before, asking a fourth time is the rational move. Your employees won't announce that the compensation mechanism creates a social dilemma. They'll just stop sharing leads. Nobody holds a meeting about it. The leads quietly stop moving between people. That's a game whose rules reward keeping things to yourself. And your partner won't say "We have entered a stable negative equilibrium reinforced by reciprocal expectations." You'll just have the same argument again. Same words. Same kitchen. Same ending. That's See the Equilibrium. Each side is responding sensibly to what they expect from the other. So the pattern holds. Notice what all three have in common. The symptom is small and ordinary. The structure is big and invisible. And if you only respond to the symptom, you'll keep getting the same symptom. Reprimanding the child doesn't change what the child has learned works. Telling employees to share more doesn't change what they're paid for. Saying the perfect sentence in the argument doesn't change what each of you expects. So here's my question. Which symptom in your own life keeps showing up without a label? Write it down. You might use it for the Capstone. Which brings us to the takeaway for this lesson.
