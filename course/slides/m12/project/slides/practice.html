Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Let's practice with a scenario from the workbook. Dana is the operations director at a regional logistics company. For the third quarter in a row, the sales team has promised clients delivery windows her warehouse can't meet. Her team absorbs the overtime. Her team takes the angry calls. On Monday the VP of Sales emails her. His name is Marcus. He copies the CEO. The email says "We just landed the Harlan account. They need three-day turnaround starting next month. This is non-negotiable. The CEO is aware." Dana's first impulse is to reply-all. She wants to send a detailed list of every missed promise from the last nine months. You can probably feel that impulse yourself. Now here are a few more facts. Marcus's team is paid commission on signed contracts. They're not paid on retained accounts or profitable ones. Dana doesn't know whether the CEO actually approved three-day turnaround. Maybe the CEO was just told about the deal. She'll work with Marcus for years. The Harlan account would be good for the company if it could be served reliably. And two of her shift leads have privately told her they're close to quitting. So here's what I want you to do. Pause the video. Work through the four questions on the screen. Which two or three lenses matter most here? Which ones can Dana set aside for now? Separate the facts from her likely story. What competing hypotheses should she hold about Marcus and the CEO? What's wrong with the reply-all on both questions? Then design a move. State a prediction. And say what she should do if she's wrong. Take your time. When you're ready, I'll walk you through one strong answer.
