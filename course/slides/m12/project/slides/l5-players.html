Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

This matters enough to say explicitly. Game theory uses words like players. Strategies. Payoffs. Moves. Those words can make human interactions sound mechanical. They're not. Look at the screen. People love. People sacrifice. People act from principle. People forgive. People make mistakes. People change their minds. People care about fairness. People choose relationships over money. And people sometimes do the right thing when doing so clearly costs them. A model that ignores those realities isn't sophisticated. It's incomplete. That's important. Some people think being strategic means being cold. They think the tough-minded view is that everyone is just maximizing self-interest. That's not the tough-minded view. It's the lazy view. It leaves out half of what actually drives human behavior. So why use the word "player" at all? The point is not to reduce someone to a piece you move around. It's to remind you of something. They are a decision-maker too. They have agency. They see the world from a position different from yours. And your outcome depends partly on what they decide. That should make us more respectful of other people's agency. Not less. Think about what that means for the ethics we just talked about. If someone is a decision-maker, then hiding information from them takes away something that belongs to them. Pressuring their insecurity bypasses their choice. That's why autonomy and informed choice sit right next to strategy. They're not separate. They come from taking the word "player" seriously. So here's a question for you.
