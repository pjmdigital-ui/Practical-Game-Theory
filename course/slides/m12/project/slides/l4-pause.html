Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

If I could choose one practical effect for this course to have on your life, it might be this. A tiny space between stimulus and response. Something happens. Your first interpretation appears. It always does. It's instant. And before acting, you ask one question. What game am I in? That small pause can completely change what happens next. Look at the table. Someone pressures you. Instead of automatically resisting, you examine their incentives. Why do they need this? What happens to them if it doesn't happen? Someone rejects your proposal. Instead of immediately arguing harder, you ask what payoff you're missing. Remember the last example. Sometimes the problem isn't that they didn't understand you. Someone makes a threat. Instead of reacting to the intensity of the language, you ask whether carrying it out is credible. Loud isn't the same as committed. A team fails. Instead of blaming the people, you inspect the system. What does the game reward? Notice that the pause doesn't have to be long. Often it's one breath. It's the difference between replying to an email right now and replying after lunch. That pause is strategic judgment beginning to operate. And it ties straight back to facts and stories. The pause is where you notice your story. It's where you ask how confident you really are. Here's the common misunderstanding. People think the pause means being passive or slow. It doesn't. Sometimes after the pause you act fast. The point is that you choose the move. Your first reaction doesn't choose it for you. So which of these four rows is hardest for you?
