Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

There's a danger in a course like this. Once you learn to see games everywhere, you can start overanalyzing everything. So I want to be clear about this. The purpose of this framework isn't to turn human life into chess. It's to help in specific moments. The book lists seven of them. They're on the screen. When the stakes matter. When something doesn't make sense. When a pattern keeps repeating. When incentives are unclear. When trust is uncertain. When multiple people affect the outcome. And when your normal way of thinking just isn't working. Those are the moments to slow down and pick up the lenses. The rest of the time, put them down. Think of it like a flashlight. You don't carry it around at noon. You reach for it when the room gets dark. Here's the test I use. Is my analysis making this clearer? Or is it making it murkier? Strategic thinking should reduce confusion. If your analysis is making ordinary life needlessly suspicious and complicated, you're using the tool badly. That's not a small point. Some people finish a course like this and become exhausting to be around. Every dinner is a negotiation. Every text is a signal. That's not judgment. That's a new kind of blind spot. So here's a question for you. Look at your last week. How many of your interactions actually met one of these seven criteria? My guess is fewer than you'd think. And there's a related trap I want to name next. It's the slide from strategic into suspicious.
