Example · The clear explanation

They understood perfectly. They still said no.

The situation

Your model: "If I explain my reasoning clearly, they'll agree." So you explain.

What happened

They understand perfectly. They can restate your case. They still refuse.

The lesson

Maybe this was never an information problem. Maybe your interests are genuinely opposed.

Explaining it again more slowly won't fix a payoff problem.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's the second example from the book. This one is less comfortable. You think "If I explain my reasoning clearly, they'll agree." That's a very common model. Most of us carry it around without noticing. It assumes disagreement comes from missing information. So you explain. You do a good job. You lay out the reasons. And they understand perfectly. They can even restate your case back to you accurately. And they still refuse. That tells you something. It's a surprise. And surprise is information. So run the Update step. Maybe this was never an information problem. Maybe your interests are genuinely opposed. That's a completely different game. In an information problem, a better explanation helps. In a conflict of interests, a better explanation does almost nothing. You could explain it ten more times. You'd just get ten more refusals and a more irritated person on the other side. This is where people get stuck. They assume the other person must not really get it. So they explain again more slowly. Then they explain it louder. Then they decide the other person is stubborn or irrational. But the loop says something else. Update the model. If interests are opposed, you need different tools. You think about payoffs. You think about what you could offer. You think about whether there's a better game to play. Or whether this is a disagreement you simply have to accept. So here's a question for you. Is there someone you keep explaining things to? What if they already understand? What would you do differently if the problem were payoffs instead of information?
