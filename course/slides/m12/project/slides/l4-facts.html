Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's a skill that sounds simple and isn't. Separate the fact from your story about the fact. Suppose someone doesn't return your call. The fact is that they didn't return your call. That's it. That's all you actually know. The interpretation is that they're disrespecting you. Now that's possible. I'm not saying it's wrong. But it's not the same thing. And the moment you treat it as the same thing, you've stopped seeing the game. Look at the row at the bottom. Maybe they forgot. Maybe they're avoiding the conversation. Maybe they're overwhelmed. Maybe they never received the message. Maybe they're strategically delaying. Five different explanations. Each one calls for a different response. If they forgot, a friendly nudge works. If they're overwhelmed, pressure makes it worse. If they're avoiding the conversation, you need to think about why. If they're delaying on purpose, that's a real strategic move worth reading carefully. And if you respond to all five as if it were disrespect, you'll get four of them wrong. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People think separating fact from story means having no story. That's not it. You'll always have a story. Your brain makes one instantly. The skill is noticing that it's a story. Then holding it at arm's length long enough to check it. So here's a question for you. Think of the last time someone didn't respond to you. What was the story you told yourself? How sure were you? Was it right? And this connects to one of the most practical habits in the whole course. It's the pause before you respond.
