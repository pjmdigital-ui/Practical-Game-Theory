Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's the real test of whether a lens has become yours. I don't want you to learn eleven ideas that work only when the example looks exactly like the chapter. That would be memorizing. The real test is whether you can recognize the same structure in a situation you've never seen before. That's called transfer. Look at the table. Can you recognize a commitment problem in parenting? In business? In politics? In a negotiation? In your own habits? It's the same structure each time. Someone needs to make a promise or a threat believable. Can you recognize a coordination problem in a marriage? In project management? In a community group? Again it's the same shape. People want compatible things but don't know what the others will do. Can you recognize a repeated-game problem in customer relationships? In leadership? In friendship? Each one changes because there's a next time. And here's my favorite. Can you recognize a mechanism-design problem when everyone around you thinks "We just need people to try harder"? That sentence is often a sign that the rules are producing the behavior. Nobody wants to hear that. But that's often where the real fix lives. That's transfer. And that's the difference between memorizing information and developing a mental model. So here's my question for you. Think of one structure from this course. Now name a place in your life where it showed up in disguise. That's what the next example is about. The world doesn't label the game for you.
