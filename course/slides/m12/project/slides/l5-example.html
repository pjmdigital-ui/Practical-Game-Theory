Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's the last example in the book, and I chose it on purpose. Someone you care about says, "You never listen to me." The tactical reflex is to ask, "What's the best communication technique here?" Active listening, maybe. Mirroring. A question. Those can help. But before reaching for a technique, see the game. Is this an isolated comment or a repeated pattern? What payoff are they seeking: understanding, agreement, respect, attention? Have your past responses taught them that raising a concern leads to defensiveness? Are you in an equilibrium where they criticize because they expect you not to listen, and you defend because you expect them to criticize? Is this really disagreement, or a failure to coordinate on what "being heard" means? Could you change the game by changing the pattern? Now you're thinking at a different level. You may still use the technique. But now it's serving an understanding of the situation. That's exactly how it should be.
