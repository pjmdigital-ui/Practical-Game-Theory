Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's the last example in the book. I chose to end on it for a reason. It pulls the whole course into one ordinary moment. Imagine someone you care about says "You never listen to me." The tactical response might be "What's the best communication technique to use here?" Maybe active listening. Maybe mirroring. Maybe a question. Those can help. I'm not against techniques. But before reaching for a technique, see the game. Look at the middle card. What happened before this? Is this an isolated comment or a repeated pattern? That's See the Equilibrium. What do they mean by "listen"? What payoff are they seeking? Understanding? Agreement? Respect? Attention? Those call for very different responses. Do they believe you don't care? Why? What information are they using? That's Map Information and Beliefs. Have your past responses taught them that raising a concern leads to defensiveness? That's Manage Predictability. Are you each anticipating the other's reaction before the conversation even starts? Have you entered an equilibrium where they criticize because they expect you not to listen? And you defend because you expect them to criticize? Is the issue actually disagreement? Or are you failing to coordinate around what "being heard" means? Could you change the game by changing the pattern? That might work better than finding the perfect sentence. Now you're thinking at a different level. You may still use a communication technique. But now the technique is serving an understanding of the situation. That's exactly how it should be. So here's a question for you. Is there a sentence like this one that you keep hearing at home or at work? Which lens would you pick up first? That brings us to the final takeaway of the course.
