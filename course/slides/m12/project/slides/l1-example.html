Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's why recognition matters so much. The world will not label the game for you. Nobody is going to walk into your office and say "Hello Paul. I am now presenting you with an incomplete-information bargaining game containing asymmetric outside options and a credibility problem." I'd love it if they did. It would make my job a lot easier. But that's not what happens. They're going to say "We need an answer today." That's it. Four words of pressure. And underneath those four words is a whole game. Who actually needs the answer today? What happens to them if you say no? What other options does each side have? And what makes "today" believable? None of that gets announced. It's sitting there quietly underneath an ordinary sentence. So the lesson is simple. Games come disguised as ordinary life. You get the symptom. Your job is to see the structure underneath it. This is where people often go wrong. They wait for a situation that looks like a game theory problem. They wait for a negotiation table or a formal deal. But most of the strategic moments in your life won't look strategic at all. They'll look like a deadline. A repeated request. A silence. A complaint you've heard before. Here's a question to try this week. When someone hands you a deadline, pause for one breath. Ask yourself what game is sitting under it. Let's look at a few more disguises. They come from home and work alike.
