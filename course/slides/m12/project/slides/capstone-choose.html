Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Choosing the right interaction matters more than people think. It needs to be real. It needs to be current. And it needs to be consequential. You should actually make a move within the two-to-three-week window. And it must meet at least three of the book's seven criteria. High stakes. Something doesn't make sense. A recurring pattern. Unclear incentives. Uncertain trust. Multiple people affecting the outcome. Or your normal approach not working. Good candidates include a compensation or contract negotiation. A recurring conflict with a partner or a family member or a colleague. A team that keeps failing the same way. A boundary you need to set. A decision about whether to stay in a situation or leave it. A proposal you need others to adopt. One firm rule. Please avoid anything involving physical danger or ongoing abuse. In those situations safety and appropriate professional support come before any analysis. Now the right card shows what you'll hand in. First, a situation brief of 250 to 400 words. Facts kept strictly separate from your story. Plus a confidence rating and what winning would actually mean to you. Second, the completed Strategic Judgment Worksheet. Third, a hypothesis table with at least three explanations and a plan to tell them apart. Fourth, a move design of 300 to 500 words. Include the alternatives you rejected, your prediction and the full ethics check. Fifth, at least three Practice Log entries. And sixth, a debrief of 600 to 900 words. End it with one paragraph on which lens you'll now notice faster elsewhere. That's transfer. So which situation in your life comes to mind first? Let's look at how it's scored.
