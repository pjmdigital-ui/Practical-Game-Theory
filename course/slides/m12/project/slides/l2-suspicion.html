Become less naive. Don't become cynical.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

This deserves emphasis because the line can get blurry. Thinking strategically is not the same as thinking suspiciously. Let me walk down the table. Suspicion asks "What are they hiding?" Strategic thinking asks "What possibilities should I consider?" Those sound close. They aren't. The first question already has an answer built in. It assumes there's something hidden. The second question keeps the door open. Suspicion assumes hostile intent. Strategic thinking tests hypotheses. Hostility might be one of the hypotheses. But it has to earn its place with evidence like any other. Suspicion reads ambiguity negatively. A late reply means disrespect. A short answer means anger. Strategic thinking simply recognizes that it's ambiguous. And it's comfortable saying "I don't know yet." Suspicion makes trust difficult. Strategic thinking understands why trust can be enormously valuable. Think back to the Shadow of the Future. Trust is what lets two people cooperate across many rounds. A suspicious person often destroys the very thing that would have protected them. So here's the warning sign to watch for. If you notice that this course has made you more guarded or more cynical, something has gone wrong. If you're quicker to assume the worst, that's a sign the tool is being misused. The framework should make you less naive. It should not make you cynical. Ask yourself honestly. Over the last few months, have you moved toward the right column of this table? Or toward the left?
