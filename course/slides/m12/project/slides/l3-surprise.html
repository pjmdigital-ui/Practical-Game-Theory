Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

The moments when another person surprises you are some of the most strategically useful moments you'll ever get. You thought they'd cooperate. They didn't. Ask: what did I misunderstand? You thought they'd walk away. They stayed. What payoff did I underestimate? You thought the threat was empty. They followed through. What made the commitment stronger than I realized? You thought the new rule would improve behavior. It made things worse. What response did the design create that I failed to anticipate? And one of the easiest ways to stop learning is to say, "They're irrational." Sometimes people are. So are you. So am I. But first ask: what would have to be true for this behavior to make sense? Maybe they have information you don't. A different payoff. A longer game. A different reading of your behavior. History you're discounting. And yes, maybe they just made a mistake. Just don't use the word "irrational" as a substitute for thinking.
