Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Your tool for this module is the Strategic Judgment Worksheet. It's the last one you'll add to your workbook. Use it when the stakes matter. Use it when something doesn't make sense or a pattern keeps repeating. Use it when trust is uncertain. Don't use it for "Sure." Part one is the situation. First write what happened using facts only. No adjectives. No motives. Then write your story. Then put a number on how confident you are in that story. Zero to a hundred. Then write your immediate impulse. That's the thing you'd say or do with no pause at all. Writing the impulse down is surprisingly useful. It's much easier to question once it's on paper. Part two is the eleven questions. Scan all of them quickly. Then mark the two or three that matter most. And write down which ones you're setting aside and why. That second part is where judgment shows up. Part three is competing hypotheses. Write at least three. Note the evidence for each. Then write what would tell them apart. And ask what would have to be true for their behavior to make sense. Part four is the move. Write your planned move and your prediction. Ask will this work. Then ask should I do it. Run the four checks. Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice. Ask what you're cooperating toward. And ask whether the right move is vulnerability or walking away. Then there's the Practice Log. It's one entry per meaningful interaction. Model. Act. Prediction. Observe. Update. Adapt. Over weeks that log is how your intuition gets calibrated. Let's try it on a real scenario.
