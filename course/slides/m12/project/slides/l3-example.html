Example · The refused request

You predicted anger. You got understanding.

The situation

Your model: "If I refuse this request, they'll become angry." You refuse anyway.

What happened

They say: "No problem. I completely understand."

The lesson

Good. You were wrong. That's valuable. Don't ignore the gap because you want your model to be right.

Update: maybe you overestimated their dependency. Maybe you misread their payoff. Maybe the relationship was sturdier than you believed.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's the example from the book. I like it because the surprise is a happy one. And happy surprises are the easiest ones to ignore. You have a model. "If I refuse this request, they'll become angry." Maybe it's a colleague who always asks you to cover. Maybe it's a family member. Either way you've been saying yes for a while because you expected a blowup if you didn't. So you refuse. And they say "No problem. I completely understand." Now here's where most people go wrong. They feel relief. They move on. They never ask what just happened. But this is exactly the moment the loop is built for. You were wrong. Good. That's valuable. Run the Update step. Where did reality differ from your prediction? Perhaps you overestimated their dependency on you. Perhaps you misunderstood their payoff. Maybe they didn't care about this request as much as you assumed. Perhaps you believed the relationship was more fragile than it was. Each of those is a different update. And each one changes how you'll act next time. If the relationship is sturdier than you thought, you can be more honest in it. If their dependency was lower than you thought, you've been carrying a weight that wasn't yours. Don't ignore the discrepancy because you want your model to be right. Update.
