Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Two moves people forget belong in a strategist's toolkit. First, sometimes the correct move is vulnerability. That may sound strange in a game theory course. But look at the structure. You're in a repeated relationship. Both sides are protecting themselves. Neither takes the first cooperative step because each fears exploitation. You could stay protected forever. Or one person takes a measured risk: a disclosure, a concession, an apology. That can change the other person's beliefs and reopen cooperation. The risk is exactly why it carries information. And sometimes the opposite is true. You've communicated clearly, tested your assumptions, enforced boundaries, allowed for repair, and the game is still destructive. Strategic judgment may tell you: stop playing. That's not defeat. An outside option is part of the game. There's no virtue in becoming brilliant at surviving a game that keeps damaging you when a better alternative exists. Know which games you refuse to play.
