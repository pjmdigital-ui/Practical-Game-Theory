Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's a move people forget belongs in a strategist's toolkit. Sometimes the correct move is vulnerability. I know that may sound strange in a course about game theory. But look at the structure. You're in a repeated relationship. Both sides are protecting themselves. Neither person reveals information. Neither takes the first cooperative step because each fears exploitation. And so nothing changes. Month after month. Think of two longtime co-founders. Each one holds back their real concerns about where the company is going. Each one fears the other will use it against them. So they both stay guarded and the company drifts. You could remain protected indefinitely. That's the safe move in the short run. Or one person can take a measured risk. They disclose something. They offer trust. They make a concession. They apologize. That action may change the other person's beliefs. It may shift the equilibrium. It may reopen cooperation. And here's the part that makes it strategic and not just nice. There is risk. That's precisely why the action carries information. If it cost nothing, it wouldn't prove anything. Remember Separate Words From Credibility. Costly signals are believable because they're costly. A real apology is a costly signal. So strategic thinking isn't always about protecting yourself. Sometimes it's understanding when taking a risk can transform the game. Notice the word measured. This isn't about handing over everything to someone who has exploited you before. It's a calibrated first step. And then you watch what happens. That's the loop again. So is there a relationship in your life where both of you are waiting for the other to go first? And sometimes the opposite move is right. Let's talk about walking away.
