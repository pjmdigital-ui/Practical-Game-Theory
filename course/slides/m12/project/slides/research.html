Research spotlight · Learning in games

Real players learn from feedback

What it suggests

Researchers such as Fudenberg and Levine argue that learning models improve game theory's descriptive power. Experiments show behavior can change a lot with experience. Players don't start at equilibrium.

What it doesn't prove

That any one person will learn quickly or learn the right lesson. Experience without feedback can entrench a wrong story. Learning models describe many players over many rounds. They don't predict your next conversation.

That's why the loop became a rule over the whole book instead of a twelfth model.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Let's look at the evidence behind this module. I want to be upfront first. The book's Conclusion introduces no new experiments. Its evidence comes from the research audit behind the whole framework. Two findings from that audit explain why this module exists. Here's the first. The audit compared the eleven models with the topics covered in university game-theory curricula. Those curricula include learning in games. That means evolutionary and non-equilibrium learning. It's not only equilibrium analysis. Researchers such as Fudenberg and Levine have argued that learning models improve game theory's descriptive power. In other words, they help it describe what real people actually do. And experimental evidence shows behavior can change substantially through experience. People don't start out already playing the equilibrium. What does that suggest for you? Real players don't arrive knowing the solution. They adjust from feedback. And so should you. That's exactly why the audit made the learning loop a rule over the whole book. It isn't a twelfth model. It sits above all eleven. Now here's what it doesn't prove. It doesn't show that any particular person will learn quickly. It doesn't show they'll learn the right lesson either. Experience without feedback can entrench a wrong story. That's the twenty-years problem we talked about with calibration. And learning models describe patterns across many players and many rounds. They don't predict what one person will do in your next conversation. So don't read this as "experience makes you wise." Read it as "experience plus honest feedback can make you better."
