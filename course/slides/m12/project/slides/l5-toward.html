Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Now the opposite mistake is possible too. After a course like this, it's easy to romanticize cooperation. We've spent whole modules on how to build it. The Shadow of the Future. Coordination. Trust. So it can start to feel like cooperation is always the goal. It isn't. Cooperation is just a strategic structure. It's a shape. And the same shape can hold very different things. Look at the table. Two people can cooperate to build a hospital. Or to commit a fraud. A team can coordinate around serving customers. Or around hiding wrongdoing. A repeated game can sustain trust. It can also sustain corruption. Every tool we've used to explain healthy cooperation also explains unhealthy cooperation. A group that covers for each other is cooperating beautifully. Reciprocity is working. The shadow of the future is long. Everyone trusts everyone. And what they're doing together may be terrible. So the ethical question is not "Are the players cooperating?" It's "What are they cooperating toward?" Game theory can help us understand the structure. It cannot decide our moral ends for us. That part is your job. Here's why this matters for you personally. At some point you'll be invited into a cooperative arrangement that feels warm and loyal. And the loyalty will be real. But you still have to ask what it's in service of. So here's a question to think about. Is there a group you belong to where the cooperation is strong? What is it actually pointed toward? Next I want to say something about the word "player" itself.
