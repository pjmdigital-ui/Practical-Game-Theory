Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Everything we've done in this course depends on your interpretation of the game. And your interpretation can be wrong. Often it will be. Look at these six cards. You think the person cares about money. They care about status. So your generous offer lands flat because it never addressed what they wanted. You think they're angry. They're scared. So you respond to anger with firmness. That makes the fear worse. You think they're bluffing. They're committed. So you call the bluff and they follow through. You think the problem is free riding. It's actually confusion about ownership. Nobody is shirking. Nobody knows whose job it is. You think everyone knows the rule. Half the group has a different understanding of it. And you think an incentive will increase quality. People learn to game the metric instead. None of that means you were careless. It means you're human. You're working with incomplete information about other people's minds. Everyone is. So the framework needs one rule sitting above all eleven models. Your model of the game is provisional. Don't fall in love with your diagnosis. Test it. Here's the trap I see most often. Once people form a read, they start collecting evidence that confirms it. And they quietly ignore evidence that doesn't. The diagnosis becomes part of their identity. Being wrong starts to feel like losing. So here's my question. Which of these six have you gotten wrong in the last year? Pick one. What did you do when you found out? Next is the loop that keeps you honest about all of this.
