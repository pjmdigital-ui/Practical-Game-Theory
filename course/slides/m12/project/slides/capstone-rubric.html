Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's how the Capstone is scored. There are six criteria. Each one is rated Developing, Proficient or Strong. The full rubric is in your workbook. On screen you're seeing what Strong looks like. First is seeing the game. Strong work maps the full structure. That includes players not in the room. It includes outside options and timing. And it keeps facts separate from story with explicit confidence levels. Second is lens selection. Strong work gives a sharp rationale for the lenses chosen. It explains why the others matter less. And it shows the restraint of "more analysis isn't always better." Third is hypotheses and prediction. Strong work has several well-differentiated hypotheses. It has a deliberate way to tell them apart. And it makes a checkable prediction before acting. Fourth is move design. The move follows from the analysis. It weighs alternatives like vulnerability or walking away or changing the game. It accounts for future rounds and precedent. Fifth is ethics. The move passes all four checks with specific reasoning. It considers what any cooperation is directed toward. And it treats the other person as a decision-maker whose agency you respect even when it costs you. Sixth is the back half of the loop. Observe. Update. Adapt. Strong work treats surprise as information. Nobody gets labeled irrational. The model gets revised specifically. And there's a lesson that transfers. Now one rule matters most. A passing Capstone is Proficient or better on all six. Ethics must be at least Proficient no matter what. A strategy that works but fails that check is incomplete. And notice what isn't on the list. Getting the outcome you wanted. People surprise us. No framework gives you control over them. This tests whether you saw clearly, acted with integrity and learned. Let's close with the question to carry.
