Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's how the Capstone is scored. Six criteria, each rated Developing, Proficient, or Strong, and the full rubric is in your workbook. Seeing the game: strong work maps the full structure, including players not in the room, outside options and timing, and keeps facts separate from story with explicit confidence. Lens selection: a sharp rationale, and the restraint of "more analysis isn't always better." Hypotheses and prediction: several well-differentiated hypotheses, a deliberate way to tell them apart, and a checkable prediction made before you act. Move design: the move follows from the analysis and weighs vulnerability, walking away or changing the game. Ethics: passes all four checks with specific reasoning, and treats the other person as a decision-maker whose agency you respect even when it costs you. And Observe, Update, Adapt: surprise treated as information, no one labeled irrational, and a lesson that transfers. One rule matters most. A passing Capstone is Proficient or better on all six, and ethics must be at least Proficient no matter what. A strategy that works but fails that check is incomplete.
