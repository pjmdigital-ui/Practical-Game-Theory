Example · The resistant customer

Hold several hypotheses at once

H1: It's a price objection.

H2: They don't trust you yet.

H3: They need someone else's approval.

H4: They're improving their negotiating position.

H5: They've chosen a competitor and are being polite.

You don't need to choose one yet. Ask what would tell them apart.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Imagine a customer who suddenly becomes resistant. One interpretation: it's a price objection. Another: they don't trust you. Another: they need someone else's approval. Another: they're interested, but trying to improve their negotiating position. Another: they've already chosen a competitor and they're being polite. You don't need to choose one immediately. The best strategists hold several models at once, and then ask questions or watch for behavior that helps tell them apart. A question about who else is involved separates one hypothesis from the others. A question about timing separates another. That's much more powerful than "I know exactly what they're doing." Certainty feels good. Accuracy is better.
