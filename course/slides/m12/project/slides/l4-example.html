Example · The resistant customer

Hold several hypotheses at once

The situation

A customer who seemed interested suddenly becomes resistant.

What happens next

The weak move is to decide at once: "I know exactly what they're doing." The strong move is to keep several explanations open.

The lesson

You don't need to choose one yet. Ask questions and watch behavior that tells them apart.

Certainty feels good. Accuracy is better.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's the book's example for multiple hypotheses. A customer suddenly becomes resistant. Now they're slow to reply and raising new concerns. Your mind hands you an explanation in about half a second. "I know exactly what they're doing." That's the weak move. It feels great and it's often wrong. The best strategists hold several models at once. Then they ask questions that help tell them apart. Once you're sure it's a price objection, you'll start discounting. This isn't indecision. You still act. But your action is designed to learn something. Next are the five hypotheses from the book.
