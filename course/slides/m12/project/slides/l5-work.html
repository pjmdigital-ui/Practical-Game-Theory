Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Throughout this course we've returned to four principles. Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice. I want to be clear about their role. They're not decorative. They're not a nice paragraph at the end of each module. They're constraints on how this framework should be used. Here's why that matters. Game theory can help explain deception. It can explain coercion and manipulation. It can explain intimidation and exploitation. Understanding how those things work is useful. It helps you see them coming. But explaining something doesn't make it good strategy in the larger sense. A strategy can "work" and still be wrong. Look at the three cards. You may be able to exploit someone's ignorance. That doesn't mean you should. You may discover exactly which insecurity would pressure someone into agreement. That doesn't mean using it respects their dignity. You may be able to hide relevant information so someone makes the choice you prefer. That doesn't mean they made an informed choice. They made your choice with their signature on it. So we need two different questions. Will this work? And should I do it? Never collapse them into one. Here's where people slip. They ask the first question very carefully. They model the payoffs. They predict the responses. Then they quietly assume that if it works, it must be fine. That's the collapse. Think about a sales rep who invents a story that two of a prospect's rivals just signed. It might close the deal. It still violates truth and informed choice. So here's a question for you. Is there a move you've made that worked and that you'd be uncomfortable explaining to the other person? Next let's look at the opposite mistake. It's assuming cooperation is always good.
