Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Throughout this course we've returned to four principles: truth, autonomy, dignity, and informed choice. They're not decorative. They're constraints on how this framework should be used. Game theory can help explain deception, coercion, manipulation, and exploitation. That doesn't make them good strategy in the larger sense. A strategy can "work" and still be wrong. You may be able to exploit someone's ignorance. That doesn't mean you should. You may discover exactly which insecurity would pressure someone into agreement. That doesn't mean using it respects their dignity. You may be able to hide information so they make the choice you prefer. That doesn't mean they made an informed choice. And the opposite mistake is possible too. Cooperation isn't automatically good. People can cooperate to build a hospital or to commit a fraud. So the ethical question isn't "Are they cooperating?" It's "What are they cooperating toward?"
