Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here are Part Two and Part Three. Part Two was about seeing what isn't obvious. Six is Map Information and Beliefs. What do I know? What am I assuming? What might they know that I don't? Seven is Separate Words From Credibility. What makes this believable? Talk is cheap. Costly signals and track records are not. Eight is Commitment Changes the Game. What actually binds them? Or me? Part Three was about thinking beyond the moment. Nine is the Shadow of the Future. What changes because there is a next time? Ten is Diagnose the Coordination Problem. Are we actually in conflict? Or are we failing to coordinate? Remember the extra test from that module. If everyone else cooperated, would I still have an incentive not to? Eleven is Change the Game. Do I need a better move? Or do I need a better game? And then there's the line at the bottom. The book adds it after the eleven. And above them all: what did I learn from what just happened? That question is what turns these eleven into a skill. Without it, you have a list. With it, you have a loop that gets better every time you use it. That's strategic judgment. Here's a challenge before we move on. Read down this table one more time. Which question would have changed something you did last month? Just notice it. Then let's look at how the questions themselves changed across the book.
