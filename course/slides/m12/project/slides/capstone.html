Capstone project · Two to three weeks

One real interaction, the full cycle

1. Pick and frame · Days 1–2. Write the situation brief.

2. Scan wide · Day 3. All eleven questions, unedited.

3. Choose the lens · Day 3. Circle two to four.

4. Go deep · Days 4–5. Fully analyze the chosen lenses.

5. Hold hypotheses · Day 5. At least three, and how to tell them apart.

6. Design the move · Day 6. Alternatives, ethics, a checkable prediction.

7. Act · Days 7–14. Log facts first, interpretation second.

8. Observe, update, adapt · Days 7–21. Follow up and log it.

9. Debrief · Final days. Be honest about what you got wrong.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

And now, the Capstone. Over two to three weeks, you'll take one real, high-stakes interaction from your own life through the full strategic-judgment cycle. Choose something real, current and consequential, where you'll actually make a move in the project window, and that meets at least three of the criteria: high stakes, something doesn't make sense, a recurring pattern, unclear incentives, uncertain trust, multiple people, or your normal approach not working. A negotiation, a recurring conflict, a team that keeps failing the same way, a boundary, a decision about whether to stay or leave. Please avoid anything involving physical danger or ongoing abuse. There, safety and professional support come first. Then follow the nine steps. Scan all eleven questions once, the only time you deliberately run the whole set, then circle two to four. Over-analysis counts against you, not for you. Your deliverables: a situation brief, the worksheet, a hypothesis table, a move design, at least three Practice Log entries, and a debrief that ends with the lens you'll now notice faster elsewhere. And understand what this tests. Not whether you got the outcome you wanted. People surprise us, and no framework gives you control over them. It tests whether you saw the game clearly, acted with integrity, and learned faster from what happened.
