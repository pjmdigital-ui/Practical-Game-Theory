Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

And now the Capstone. This is the course-completion project. Over two to three weeks you'll take one real high-stakes interaction from your own life through the full strategic-judgment cycle. You'll see the game. You'll choose the lenses that matter. You'll design an ethical move. You'll act. And you'll learn from what happens. Here are the nine steps. Days one and two: pick your interaction and write the situation brief. Name what a good outcome would be. That can include outcomes other than winning. Preserving the relationship. Telling the truth at a cost. Walking away. Day three: scan wide. Answer all eleven questions briefly without editing. This is the only time you deliberately run the whole set. Then choose the lens. Circle the two to four that explain the most. Write one line on each of the others saying why it matters less here. And understand this. Over-analysis counts against you. It doesn't count for you. Days four and five: go deep on the lenses you chose. Map the players, including people not in the room. Then build your hypothesis table. Where you can, design a low-risk question that would tell the hypotheses apart before your main move. Day six: design the move and two alternatives. Ask both questions separately. Consider vulnerability. Consider walking away. Consider changing the game. Write a specific prediction you could be wrong about. Days seven through fourteen: act and log it. Facts first. Interpretation second. Then observe, update and adapt. Make at least one follow-up move if it's appropriate. Finally write the debrief. Be honest about what you got wrong. A clean prediction that came true teaches less than a surprise you learned from. Let's talk about how to choose your interaction.
