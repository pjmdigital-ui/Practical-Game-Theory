Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Remember this loop from Module Zero? We've come full circle. This is the rule that sits above all eleven models. It's the loop that holds everything together. Let me walk it slowly. Model. Based on the information you have, form your best understanding of the game. Who are the players? What do they want? What do you expect them to do? Act. Make a move. Observe. What actually happens? Not what you hoped would happen. What actually happened. Update. Where did reality differ from your prediction? Adapt. Change your strategy. Or change your model. Sometimes both. Then repeat. That is strategic thinking. Not perfect prediction. Not omniscience. Not always being right. Learning faster from what happens. I want you to notice something about the Update step. It's a very specific question. It's not "Did it go well?" Plenty of things go well for reasons that have nothing to do with your read. And plenty of things go badly even when your read was right. The question is "Where did reality differ from what I predicted?" And you can only answer that if you made a prediction in the first place. That's why the Practice Log asks you to write the prediction down before you act. Without it, memory rewrites the story. You'll remember that you saw it coming. Most people skip the prediction because it feels like extra work. It's actually the part that makes the loop work. So here's a question for you. When was the last time you wrote down a prediction about a person before a conversation? Next let's talk about what to do when the prediction misses.
