Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

I want to make one distinction before we finish. The eleven models in this course are not a procedure. You're not supposed to walk into every conversation and think "Okay. Step one: identify the game. Step two: identify the payoffs. Step three." That's not how good judgment works. Think about an experienced driver. They aren't consciously saying "Check mirror. Calculate distance. Estimate speed. Adjust steering angle." They've developed perception. They notice things. A car drifting into their lane registers before they could put it into words. The same thing should happen here. At first you'll ask the questions consciously. That's fine. That's how anybody learns anything. You'll sit down with the worksheet. You'll go through the lenses one at a time. It will feel slow and a little clunky. Then over time something shifts. You start noticing the structure almost automatically. Someone makes a demand and the payoff question just shows up. A pattern repeats and you see it as a pattern. That's what I mean by recognition. Here's the common misunderstanding. People finish a course like this and think the goal is to run the framework every time. It isn't. The framework is scaffolding. You use it hard at the start so that later you don't need to. So ask yourself something. Which of the eleven questions already show up for you without effort? And which ones do you still have to go looking for?
