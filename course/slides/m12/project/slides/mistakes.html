Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Let me name the most common mistakes. You'll recognize most of them from this module. That's on purpose. The first is running all eleven models every time. The models are lenses. They're not a checklist. Full analysis of everything is slow and noisy. Pick the two or three that matter most right now. The second is analyzing interactions that don't need it. Turning "Sure" into a signaling puzzle makes ordinary life needlessly suspicious and complicated. Save the framework for high stakes and recurring patterns. Save it for unclear incentives and uncertain trust. The third is falling in love with your diagnosis. Your first model will often be wrong. Treat it as provisional. Actively look for evidence that would disprove it. The fourth is labeling people irrational. That word often replaces thinking. Ask what would have to be true for the behavior to make sense. And allow that sometimes people simply make mistakes. The fifth is confusing strategic with suspicious. Suspicion assumes hostile intent and reads ambiguity negatively. Strategic thinking recognizes ambiguity and tests hypotheses. The sixth is collapsing "Will this work?" into "Should I do it?" A strategy can work and still violate truth or autonomy. It can work and still violate dignity or informed choice. Ask both questions every time. And the seventh is at the bottom of the slide. It's trusting uncalibrated intuition. Experience without feedback can produce confident error. Use the Practice Log so your intuition earns its trust. Here's my question. Which one of these seven is yours? Be honest. Most of us have a favorite. Now let's turn that into your assignment for this week.
