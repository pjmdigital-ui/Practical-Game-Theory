Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

You now have eleven lenses. And here's the honest truth. At this point the risk isn't that you know too little. The risk is that you try to use everything everywhere all at once. The book is blunt about this. The eleven models are not eleven steps. You're not supposed to walk into a conversation and think "Step one: identify the game. Step two: identify the payoffs." That's not how good judgment works. Strategic judgment is the skill that sits above the models. It has four parts. You can see them on the screen. First, you choose the two or three lenses that matter right now. Second, you know when a situation doesn't need analysis at all. Sometimes "Sure" just means "Sure." Third, you keep two questions apart. Will this work? Should I do it? Fourth, you treat your read of any situation as provisional. Because your first model will often be wrong. The person you thought cared about money cares about status. The person you thought was angry is scared. The threat you thought was a bluff was a commitment. So this module adds one rule over all eleven models. Your model of the game is provisional. Update it from play. The loop that holds everything together has five steps. Model. Act. Observe. Update. Adapt. You won't predict perfectly. You won't be right every time. What you can do is learn faster from what happens. Here's a question to hold while we go. Since you started this course, have you ever caught yourself over-thinking a moment that didn't need it? Keep that in mind. Let's look at what you'll be able to do by the end.
