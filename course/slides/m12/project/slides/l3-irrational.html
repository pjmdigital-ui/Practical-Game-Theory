Don't use the word "irrational" as a substitute for thinking.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

One of the easiest ways to stop learning is to say "They're irrational." It feels like an explanation. It isn't. It's a door closing. Now let me be honest. Sometimes people are irrational. So are you. So am I. But before reaching for that word, ask one question. What would have to be true for this behavior to make sense? The book gives six possibilities. They're on the screen. Maybe they have information you don't. They know something about the deadline or the budget or the boss that you haven't heard. Maybe the payoff you think they're maximizing isn't the payoff they actually care about. You assumed money. They're protecting their standing with their team. Maybe they're playing a longer game than you are. What looks like a bad move this round might be a smart move over ten rounds. Maybe their interpretation of your behavior is completely different from yours. You think you were being direct. They think you were being dismissive. Maybe they're responding to history you're discounting. Something happened two years ago that you've forgotten and they haven't. And yes, maybe they're simply making a mistake. That's on the list too. The point is not to explain every behavior as secretly rational. That would be its own kind of blind spot. The point is to avoid using the word irrational as a substitute for thinking. So here's a challenge. Think of someone whose behavior you've written off as irrational. Go down these six cards.
