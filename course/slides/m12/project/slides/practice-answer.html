Fix the game, not just the email

The lenses

Payoffs and Change the Game: Sales is paid on signed deals. Credibility: "aware" isn't "approved." Shadow of the Future: this sets a precedent.

The hypotheses

Marcus is using the CEO's name as pressure. Marcus doesn't grasp capacity. The CEO approved without data. The CEO plans to add resources.

The reply-all

Won't work: it turns a design problem into a public fight. Shouldn't: public shaming fails dignity. It doesn't inform the CEO's choice.

The move

A brief and blameless reply. What three-day turnaround would take. What she can guarantee today. Twenty minutes this week to decide.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's one strong answer. Three lenses stand out. First, See the Payoffs and Change the Game. Sales is paid on signed contracts. They're not paid on deliverable ones. So over-promising is the rational response to that incentive. This is a pattern the system produces. It's not one bad act by Marcus. A better email won't fix it. The durable fix sits at the level of the game. For example, a sign-off rule for delivery terms. Second, Information and Credibility. "The CEO is aware" is not the same as "The CEO approved three-day turnaround knowing warehouse capacity." The CEO may not know about the overtime or the shift leads. Third, the Shadow of the Future. If Dana quietly absorbs this, she teaches the organization that operations will always bend. Her story is "Marcus is throwing my team under the bus." That's one hypothesis among four. A question to the CEO helps tell them apart. Now the reply-all. Will it work? Probably not. It invites Marcus to defend himself. It makes Dana look like an obstacle in front of the CEO. Should she do it? Her facts may be true. But publicly shaming a colleague is a dignity problem. So instead she sends a brief reply without blame. She says what three-day turnaround would take. She says what she can guarantee today. She asks for twenty minutes this week to decide. Her prediction is that the CEO picks a staged commitment. And if the CEO says "Just make it work"? She updates instead of calling anyone irrational. Maybe the CEO knows something she doesn't. And if the game stays destructive over several rounds, her own outside options deserve a serious look.
