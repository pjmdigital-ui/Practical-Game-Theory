Practice · Model answer

Fix the game, not just the email

The lenses

Payoffs and Change the Game (paid on signed deals). Credibility ("aware" isn't "approved"). Shadow of the Future.

The hypotheses

Marcus is applying pressure, or doesn't grasp capacity. The CEO approved without data, or plans to add resources.

The reply-all

Won't work: it turns a design problem into a public fight. Shouldn't: shaming fails dignity and informs no one.

The move

A blameless reply: what three-day would take, what she can guarantee now, 20 minutes to decide. If wrong, update.

Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

Here's one strong answer. Three lenses stand out. See the Payoffs and Change the Game: Sales is paid on signed contracts, not deliverable ones, so over-promising is the rational response. This is a pattern the system produces, and the durable fix is at the level of the game, like a sign-off rule for delivery terms. Information and Credibility: "The CEO is aware" is not "The CEO approved three-day turnaround knowing warehouse capacity." And the Shadow of the Future: if she quietly absorbs this, she teaches the organization that operations will always bend. Her story, "Marcus is throwing my team under the bus," is one hypothesis among four, and a question to the CEO helps tell them apart. The reply-all probably won't work, and publicly shaming a colleague is a dignity problem. Instead, a brief, blameless reply: what three-day turnaround would take, what she can guarantee today, and twenty minutes this week to decide. If the CEO says "Just make it work," she updates rather than calling anyone irrational. Maybe the CEO knows something she doesn't. And if the game stays destructive over several rounds, her own outside options deserve a serious look.
