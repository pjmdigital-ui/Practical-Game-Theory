Internomics · Module 12 — Strategic Judgment

People often talk about intuition as if it's almost mystical. I don't think it is. Sometimes what we call intuition is simply patterns learned so deeply that we no longer experience the reasoning consciously. You walk into a room and something feels off. Why? Maybe you've unconsciously noticed who's speaking and who's silent. Who looks at whom before answering. Who's being deferred to. Where the tension shows up. Look at the three cards. An experienced negotiator hears a statement and senses "That isn't really the issue." An experienced leader notices "These people don't disagree. They're waiting for permission." An experienced salesperson notices "This objection isn't about price." I know that last one well. Sales is where I learned about resistance and incentives long before I had names for them. Expert judgment often begins as explicit thinking. Then it becomes more and more automatic through repeated exposure and feedback. That's what we're trying to build in this course. But here's the catch. Experience alone doesn't guarantee good intuition. You can repeat the wrong interpretation for twenty years. If nobody gives you feedback, your confidence can grow while your accuracy stays flat. That's how you get the person who's been doing something forever and is confidently wrong about it. So intuition needs calibration. That's why the loop matters so much. Your intuition should become more trustworthy because it has survived contact with reality. Not because you've repeated the same story often enough to believe it. So here's a question for you. Where in your life do you feel most confident about reading people? When did you last check that confidence against what actually happened? That's the takeaway for this lesson.
