Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's how this plays out inside a company. Department A believes, "Department B will never cooperate with us." So A stops sharing information. Department B sees this and thinks, "They don't trust us." So B stops sharing information too. And A says, "See? We were right." Now the belief has become self-reinforcing. Neither side necessarily started with malicious intent. The belief itself altered behavior, and the behavior reinforced the belief. So what if you could change the belief? You could just say, "We're willing to cooperate." But that statement is cheap, and they may not believe it. You might need to change their information instead: provide evidence, change your behavior, offer a verifiable concession, involve a trusted third party, take an action that would be costly if you weren't sincere. Notice where that's heading. Information leads to credibility, and credibility is our next module.
