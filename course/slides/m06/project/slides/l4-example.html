Example · Two departments

A misunderstanding can become self-fulfilling

The situation

Department A believes, "Department B will never cooperate with us." So A stops sharing information.

What happened

B sees this and thinks, "They don't trust us." So B stops sharing too. A says, "See? We were right."

The lesson

Neither side started with malice. The belief changed behavior. The behavior reinforced the belief.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's how all of this plays out inside a company. Imagine two departments. Department A believes Department B will never cooperate with them. That's the situation. So what does A do? A stops sharing information. Why bother sharing with people who won't cooperate anyway? Now look at the middle card. Department B sees this. And B draws its own conclusion. They don't trust us. So B stops sharing information too. And then Department A looks at B's behavior and says, "See? We were right." Do you see what just happened? Now the belief has become self-reinforcing. A's belief produced A's behavior. A's behavior produced B's belief. B's belief produced B's behavior. And B's behavior became the evidence that confirmed A's original belief. It's a closed loop. Here's the lesson on the right. Neither side necessarily started with malicious intent. Nobody set out to build a wall between the departments. The belief itself altered behavior. And the behavior reinforced the belief. That's a recurring pattern in strategic interaction. Beliefs can influence actions. And actions can reinforce beliefs. If you've ever worked in an organization with a tense relationship between two teams, you've probably seen this. Each side has a long list of evidence about the other. And each side honestly can't see its own part in creating that evidence. Here's the question I want you to ask. When someone's behavior confirms your belief about them, ask whether your own behavior might have helped produce it. That's a hard question. It's also one of the most useful ones in this whole course. So what could break the loop? Let's look at that.
