Research spotlight · Tadelis & Zettelmeyer

Same cars. Different information.

What it shows

A field experiment in wholesale car auctions randomly disclosed quality information. Good news and bad news could both raise competition and revenue. Buyers got better matched to cars.

What it suggests

Changing the information structure can change outcomes in unexpected ways. Disclosing an unflattering fact isn't automatically a loss.

What it doesn't prove

That more information always improves outcomes. The effect depends on the game. This is one market setting.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's our first research spotlight. It comes from Tadelis and Zettelmeyer and was published in the American Economic Review. They ran a large-scale field experiment in wholesale car auctions. For some vehicles they randomly disclosed quality information. For others they didn't. Here's what they found. It surprises most people. Both good news and bad news could increase competition and revenue. You'd expect bad news to hurt. So why didn't it always? Because buyers differed in which cars suited them. Better information improved the matching between buyers and vehicles. In other words the information helped the right cars reach the right buyers. Look at what didn't change. The cars didn't change. The buyers didn't change. The information changed. And so the game changed. That's the point of the middle card. Changing the information structure can change behavior and outcomes. Sometimes it does so in ways people don't expect. For conversations this means disclosing an unflattering fact isn't automatically a loss. It can help the right counterpart find you. Think about a job candidate who's upfront about a gap in experience. Or a seller who names the weakness in their product. They may lose the wrong buyers and win the right ones. Now the card on the right matters just as much. This study doesn't show that more information always improves outcomes. The effect depends on the game. It's evidence from a specific market setting. It isn't a universal rule that disclosure always pays. So hold it as a possibility worth testing. Don't treat it as a law. Here's a question for you.
