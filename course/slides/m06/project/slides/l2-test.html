Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here are the questions that turn a bare answer into something you can actually weigh. The seller just told you the car has never had any major problems. Before you decide how much that's worth, run through these. First, what does the seller gain if you believe them? In this case the answer is obvious. They make the sale at a better price. Second, what do they lose if you discover they're lying? Maybe a lot. Maybe nothing. A dealer with a lot full of cars and a local reputation has more to lose than a stranger selling one car online. Third, how easy would the lie be to verify? If a mechanic could spot the problem quickly, a lie becomes riskier for them. Fourth, what reputation do they have? We'll come back to reputation later with two salespeople. And fifth, are there other ways to verify the claim? A service history. An inspection. A second opinion. None of these questions accuses anyone. They simply put the statement back inside the game it came from. That's the key move of this whole lesson. A statement never arrives alone. It arrives with a speaker and a set of incentives. Now look at the dark card. Notice where we're heading. We're moving from information to credibility. Credibility gets its own module next.
