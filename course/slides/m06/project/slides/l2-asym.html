Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Information asymmetry simply means one player knows something relevant that another player doesn't. And once you start looking, you see it everywhere. A doctor knows more about medicine than the patient. A mechanic knows more about cars than the customer. A lawyer knows more about the legal system than the client. A salesperson may know more about the product than the buyer. An employee knows more about their own effort than their manager does. And a child knows a lot more about what they did five minutes ago than their parent does. None of these relationships is a battle. But in every one of them, somebody is making decisions with less information than the other person has. And that changes the game.
