Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Obviously beliefs can be wrong. Look at the first three cards. You can believe I'm bluffing when I'm not. I can believe you're going to cooperate when you're actually going to defect. And we can both misunderstand each other at the same time. Here's the part that matters most. Sometimes those mistaken beliefs create stable patterns. Remember the equilibrium from Module Five? That was a pattern where nobody has a reason to change their move given what everyone else is doing. Now imagine that the equilibrium is being sustained partly because each person has incorrect beliefs. Each person is wrong about what the other wants or intends. Picture two people who each believe the other doesn't want to talk about a sensitive topic. So neither raises it. And each one's silence looks like confirmation to the other. That pattern can last for years. It feels stable. It feels like just how things are between them. But it's resting on a belief that may simply be false. That's where information becomes extremely powerful. Look at the dark card. If you change the information, you may change the equilibrium. You don't always need to change anyone's incentives. You don't need to force anything. Sometimes the whole pattern is propped up by a misunderstanding. Remove the misunderstanding and the pattern loses its support. Here's a question to take with you. Think of one stuck pattern in your life. A standoff at work. An argument that keeps repeating at home. What belief is each person holding that might not be true? And what piece of information could test it? There's one more layer we need. It's about whether everyone knows that everyone knows.
