Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Pause the video here and work through these five questions. Use the Information Map. Write your answers down. Don't just think them. First, sort what Dana knows from what she believes and what she's assuming. Be strict about it. A fact is something she actually observed. Second, what information gaps run in each direction? What does Jordan know that Dana doesn't? And what does Dana know that Jordan doesn't? Third, find at least one higher-order belief. That's a belief about what someone else believes. Then find one place where Dana treated shared information as common knowledge. Fourth, how could the beliefs on both sides become self-fulfilling? Think back to the two departments. Fifth, what information would change either person's decision? And how should Dana open Monday's conversation? Write the actual opening sentence she should use. Here's a tip. The hardest part for most people is question one. We tend to fold our interpretations into our facts without noticing. So when you list Dana's facts, check each one. Could a camera have recorded it? If not, it probably belongs in the belief column. Take as long as you need. When you're ready, unpause and I'll walk you through how I'd answer it.
