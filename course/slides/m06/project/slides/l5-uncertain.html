Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

This distinction is important. When you don't know what someone is thinking, it's tempting to assume they're hiding something. Sometimes they are deliberately withholding information. But sometimes they don't know themselves. Sometimes the information simply isn't available yet. Sometimes you just haven't asked. And sometimes you've interpreted ambiguous evidence incorrectly. Strategic thinking doesn't automatically assume deception. It asks a much healthier question: what information do I actually have, and how confident should I be in the conclusions I'm drawing from it? That one question will save you from a lot of conflicts you'd otherwise start yourself.
