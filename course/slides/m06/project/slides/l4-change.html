Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

This is where communication enters the game. Suppose you're in Department B. You know the other side incorrectly believes you're unwilling to cooperate. What do you do? The obvious move is to tell them. You walk over and say, "We're willing to cooperate." But they may not believe you. Why not? Because the statement is cheap. It costs you nothing to say. And a team that really wasn't willing to cooperate could say exactly the same words. So you may need to do something that changes their information. Look at the six options on the screen. You might make a commitment. You might provide evidence. You might involve a trusted third party they already believe. You might simply change your behavior and let them see a different pattern. You might offer a verifiable concession. Or you might take an action that would be costly if you weren't sincere. That's the dark card. Notice what all six have in common. None of them asks the other side to take your word for it. Each one gives them something they can see or check or rely on. Each one uses the signaling logic from lesson three. Back to the two departments. Imagine B shares a piece of information first. It's something useful that A didn't ask for. That's a small cost. It's also visible and checkable. It gives A a reason to update. And notice where we're heading. Information leads to credibility. Credibility is exactly what our next module is about. Here's a question for you. Is there someone who doesn't believe you're on their side right now? Which of these six could you try this week? Let's land this lesson.
