Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's the classic example: the bank run. Imagine you have money in a bank. You believe the bank may fail. You don't know whether that's true. But you also know something else: if enough other depositors withdraw their money, the bank could actually fail. So you go and withdraw yours. Other people see you doing it. They get nervous. They withdraw theirs. More people see the withdrawals and get even more nervous. Suddenly a belief about what might happen helps create the very outcome people feared. The outcome isn't determined only by the bank's underlying financial condition. It's also shaped by what depositors believe other depositors are going to do. Now go back to that company that's doing well while employees believe layoffs are coming. They start looking for other jobs. They stop taking risks. They save more. They become less committed. And that behavior may then affect the company itself.
