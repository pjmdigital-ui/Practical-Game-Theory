type (noun)

Something important about me that you don't know.

Skilled or inexperienced. Patient or desperate. Trustworthy or not. Willing to walk away or not. You can't observe it directly. You have to infer it. And I know you're trying to.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's one piece of formal vocabulary worth keeping. In game theory players can have private information about their own type. A type is a set of characteristics relevant to the game that other players don't observe. MIT's treatment of what are called Bayesian games describes incomplete information as uncertainty about other players' payoffs or types. You don't need to worry about any of the mathematics. Just think of type as the line in orange. Something important about me that you don't know. What could that be? Maybe I'm highly skilled. Maybe I'm inexperienced. Maybe I'm wealthy. Maybe I'm desperate. Maybe I'm patient or impatient. Maybe I'm trustworthy. Maybe I'm risk-averse. Maybe I'm willing to walk away. You can't see any of these things directly. You have to infer them from what I say and what I do. And here's the part that makes it a game. I know you're trying to infer them. So I have a choice about what I let you see. And you know I have that choice. Think about the last time you met someone who could matter to your career. You were reading their type. They were reading yours. Each of you was also managing what the other could read. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. Type isn't a label you stick on someone. It's your best current guess about something hidden. It should stay a guess until the evidence firms it up. We'll come back to that idea in lesson five. For now let's watch types at work in the most familiar setting there is. The job interview.
