Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Now the common-knowledge failure. Dana said the director was watching this one. But she said it in a hallway Jordan wasn't part of. So the information existed on the team. But it wasn't shared knowledge that everyone had it. Dana assumed it was in Jordan's information set without any evidence. That's the room where only half the people heard the announcement. Next, how does this become self-fulfilling? If Dana walks in treating her hypothesis as fact, Jordan will likely get defensive and guarded. Dana may read that as confirmation that he doesn't care. Meanwhile Jordan expects blame. So he may stop sending early warnings. What's the point? And that confirms Dana's belief that he doesn't flag risks. That's the two-departments pattern in miniature. So what information would change things? When finance actually delivered. What Jordan's mid-week message was meant to signal. And whether Jordan knew about the director's attention. Here's how I'd have Dana open Monday. She names the facts and asks instead of concluding. "The report went out two days late and the director raised it with me. I'm not sure I have the full picture. Walk me through what happened with finance's numbers. And tell me if you'd heard the director was watching this one." Then she shares what she knows. Jordan can't respond to information he never had. Notice what's missing from that opening. There's no accusation. There's no pressure. Neither of them needs to deceive or pressure anyone. They both need a more accurate map.
