Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

This deserves careful wording. Look at the cards on the screen. A rumor can change behavior. A misunderstanding can change behavior. A false belief can change behavior. A prediction can change behavior. A credible warning can change behavior. Notice that only some of those are true. The rumor might be false. The misunderstanding is by definition a mistake. The prediction hasn't happened yet. And yet every one of them can move people. So where does the strategic effect actually come from? It comes from what people believe and how those beliefs affect their choices. That's the dark card on the right. Think about what this means in your own life. You can be completely innocent of something and still face the consequences of someone believing you did it. A team can be perfectly stable and still lose people to a rumor. A partner can misread one comment and pull away for a week. The facts didn't change. The beliefs did. And the beliefs drove the behavior. This is why I keep saying you need a map of beliefs and not only a map of facts. If you only track what's true you'll be constantly surprised by how people act. If you also track what they believe you'll see their moves coming. Here's the common misunderstanding to watch for. Some people hear this and conclude that truth doesn't matter. It does.
