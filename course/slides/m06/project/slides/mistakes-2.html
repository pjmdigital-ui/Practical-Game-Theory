Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here are four more. The fifth mistake is taking statements at face value. Or swinging the other way and dismissing them entirely. Words aren't automatically facts. And cheap talk isn't automatically worthless. Evaluate the speaker and the incentives. Look at the history and the context. Then ask why you should believe it. The sixth is assuming uncertainty means deception. Not knowing what someone thinks doesn't mean they're hiding something. They may not know themselves. The information may not exist yet. Or you may simply not have asked. Remember the five cards from lesson five. Only one of them involved anyone hiding anything. The seventh is saying "perception is reality." I know it's a popular phrase. But it's sloppy. Beliefs drive behavior. That part is true. But truth still decides whether beliefs are accurate. And false beliefs can collapse when reality pushes back. If you forget that, you'll start managing impressions and stop paying attention to what's real. And the eighth is a big one. It's gathering more information instead of the right information. When we feel uncertain, our instinct is to collect everything. Ask more questions. Read more emails. Check one more source. But most of that information won't change anything. So ask what would actually change your decision. Then go after that. That's what Part Five of the Information Map is for. Think about a decision you're stuck on right now. What's the one fact that would actually unstick it? That's the information worth chasing. Now here's your assignment for the week.
