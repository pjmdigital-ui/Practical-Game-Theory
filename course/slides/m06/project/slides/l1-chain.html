Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Let's slow the bank run down so you can see the machinery. It starts with a belief. My bank may fail. That belief leads to an action. You withdraw. Your action becomes visible information for other people. They see it and get nervous. And their nervousness leads to their own action. They follow. Every arrow on this slide is a place where a belief turns into behavior. And every box after the first one is a place where behavior turns into new information for someone else. That's the loop. Belief drives action. Action becomes information. Information shapes the next belief. Here's the principle I want you to take from it. What people believe other people will do can affect what they themselves do. That's different from the earlier chapters. We're no longer only predicting what someone will do. We're predicting what they'll do based on what they believe about everyone else. Now take it back inside the company. Employees believe layoffs are coming. So they start looking for other jobs. They stop taking risks. They save more. They become less committed. Coworkers see that and wonder what those people know. The mood spreads. And that behavior may then affect the company itself. Nobody had to lie. Nobody had to plan it. The spiral ran on beliefs. Here's a question for you. Where in your team or your family is one person's worry quietly becoming everyone's worry?
