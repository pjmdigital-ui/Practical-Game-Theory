Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

So here's the habit. Don't treat your initial interpretation as a fact. Treat it as a hypothesis. Look at the line under the title. You think he's angry with me. Fine. That's a hypothesis. It might be right. But it's not a fact yet. Now ask three questions. First, what evidence would support it? Maybe he's been short with you in two meetings. Second, what evidence would contradict it? Maybe he asked for your input yesterday and thanked you for it. Third, what else could explain the behavior? Maybe he's under pressure from his own boss. Maybe he's tired. Maybe he's worried about something that has nothing to do with you. Then as new information appears, you update. This sounds obvious. Everyone agrees with it in principle. But look at the bottom line on the slide. Human beings are remarkably good at forming an interpretation and then looking for evidence that confirms it. Once you've decided he's angry, every short reply looks like proof. Every friendly moment looks like an exception. Strategic thinking asks for something different. It's the same loop we've used since Module Zero. Model. Act. Observe. Update. Adapt. Your read of another person is just the model step. It's supposed to change when the evidence does. And this matters especially here, because incomplete information is unavoidable. You will never know everything about what someone else knows or believes. So the goal isn't certainty. The goal is a read that keeps getting better. Here's a question for you. What's one read you have of someone right now that you've never actually tested?
