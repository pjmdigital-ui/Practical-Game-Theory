Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Let's start with a simple idea. Information has value. Think back to the negotiation from Module Two. You have an outside option. But the other person doesn't know what it is. That's valuable information. Look at the two cards. If they believe this is Paul's only option, they may negotiate one way. They might push harder. They might hold firm on price because they think I can't leave. If they believe Paul can walk away and has three other offers, they may behave very differently. Same Paul. Same deal on the table. The only thing that changed is what they believe about my alternatives. So information itself has strategic value. And once you see that, a lot of ordinary behavior starts to make sense. Look at the bottom of the slide. That's why people ask questions. That's why they investigate and gather intelligence. That's why they sometimes stay silent. It's why they reveal information selectively. It's why they make statements and observe behavior and test one another. They're all trying to do the same thing. They're trying to improve their model of the game. Here's a question for you. The next time someone asks you a casual question in a negotiation, notice what they might be trying to learn. And the next time you go quiet, notice what you're protecting. Neither is sinister. It's just what people do when information has value. Now let's look at how common these gaps really are.
