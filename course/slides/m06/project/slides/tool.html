Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Your tool for this module is the Information Map. Use it before any important interaction. Or use it after one that confused you. It starts with seven questions. Let me walk you through them. First, what do I know? Separate facts from assumptions. This is harder than it sounds because assumptions feel like facts from the inside. Second, what don't I know? Name the uncertainty out loud. Third, what do they know that I don't? That's where you look for information asymmetry. Fourth, what do I know that they don't? Recognize your own informational advantage. Sometimes the most useful move is simply to share it. Fifth, what do I think they believe? Now you're modeling their beliefs and not just their actions. Sixth, what do they think I believe? If the stakes are high, this matters too. That's the higher-order layer from lesson four. And seventh, what evidence could change my mind? I love this last one. It protects you from becoming trapped inside your own assumptions. If you can't name anything that would change your mind, you're not holding a hypothesis anymore. You're holding a conviction. Here's how I'd suggest using it. Don't try to answer all seven perfectly. Just write a line for each. You'll be surprised how often question three or question four changes your whole plan. Picture the used car one more time. Run these seven questions before you walk onto the lot. You'd walk in with a very different plan. The workbook takes the map further than these seven questions.
