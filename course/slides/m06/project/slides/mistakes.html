Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Four mistakes to watch for. First, promoting a hypothesis to a fact. Forming an interpretation is natural. The error is treating it as settled before you have evidence. Label it, name what would contradict it, and update as information arrives. Second, assuming "they know." "He knows why I'm upset" is very often an assumption, and a person cannot respond to information they never had. Third, treating a signal as proof, or swinging the other way and dismissing everything. Words aren't automatically facts, and cheap talk isn't automatically worthless. Ask why you should believe it. And fourth, confusing shared information with common knowledge. If coordination matters, make the information public and mutual. Your workbook lists a few more, including the big one: don't just gather more information. Go after the information that would actually change your decision.
