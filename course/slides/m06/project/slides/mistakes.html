Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here are four mistakes to watch for. There are four more on the next slide. The first is promoting a hypothesis to a fact. Forming an interpretation is natural. You can't stop your mind from doing it. The error is treating that interpretation as settled before you have evidence. So label it a hypothesis. Name what would contradict it. And update as information arrives. That's exactly what Dana didn't do. The second is assuming "they know." Think of the sentence "He knows why I'm upset." That's very often an assumption. And a person cannot respond to information they never had. So ask yourself what evidence you have that it's actually in their information set. Did you tell them? Did they hear it? Or did you just assume? The third is treating a signal as proof. A degree is evidence. A confident tone is evidence. A single gesture is evidence. None of them is proof. The weight of a signal depends on how informative it is and what it cost to send. A confident tone costs nothing. So don't let it carry more weight than it can bear. The fourth is confusing shared information with common knowledge. Telling half the room isn't the same as everyone knowing that everyone knows. If coordination matters, make the information public and mutual. Send it to everyone at once. Say it where everyone can see that everyone heard it. Here's a question for you.
