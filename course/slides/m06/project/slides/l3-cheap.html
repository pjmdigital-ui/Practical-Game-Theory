Cheap talk

Cheap talk isn't useless. It can carry real information.

It doesn't change the material payoffs, but it can still inform, especially when interests are aligned. The key question: why should I believe you?

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's a concept that becomes very important when we get to communication. In formal game theory, cheap talk is communication that doesn't directly change the material payoffs of the game. Saying something doesn't cost you anything, and it doesn't bind you to anything. Now, despite the name, cheap talk isn't necessarily useless. Words can be extremely valuable. Messages can carry meaningful information even when sending them is costless, especially when the sender's interests are sufficiently aligned with the receiver's. If we both want the same thing, I have no reason to mislead you, and you have good reason to listen. But the strategic value of words depends partly on what incentives the speaker has to say them. So the key question is: why should I believe you?
