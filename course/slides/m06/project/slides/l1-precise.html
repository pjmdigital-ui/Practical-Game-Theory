Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's where I want to be careful with my words. You've probably heard the phrase "perception is reality." I don't use it. It's too sloppy. And it lets you off the hook for finding out what's actually true. Here's what's right about it. Beliefs drive behavior. We just saw that a rumor or a misunderstanding can move people as much as a fact can. But here's what's wrong with it. Truth still matters. Truth determines whether a belief is accurate. And inaccurate beliefs can eventually collapse when reality produces contradictory evidence. Think back to the company that's doing well while employees fear layoffs. That fear can do real damage for a while. But if the quarters keep coming in strong and nobody gets laid off, the belief starts to crack. Reality pushes back. So the more precise claim is the one on the right. Perception is part of the causal structure of strategic interaction. In plain language, beliefs are one of the things that make outcomes happen. They sit right alongside the facts as causes. They don't replace the facts. Why does this precision matter for you? Because it gives you two jobs instead of one. You need to know what's true. And you need to know what people believe is true. If you only do the first job you'll be blindsided by how people act. If you only do the second job you'll drift into managing impressions and stop caring about reality. Strategic thinkers do both. Now let's see what happens when beliefs about other people start feeding on each other.
