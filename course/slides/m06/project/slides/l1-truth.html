Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

This distinction is extremely important. There is what is actually true. And there is what each player believes is true. Those aren't necessarily the same thing. Most of the time we blur them together. We assume everyone is reacting to the same reality we see. But people don't react to reality directly. They react to their picture of it. Let me make that concrete. Imagine a company that's actually doing very well. Management knows it. The numbers are strong. But the employees believe layoffs are coming. What do they do? They may start looking for other jobs. They may stop taking risks. They may save more money. They may become less committed. None of that behavior is responding to the objective reality management can see. It's responding to the employees' belief about the future. And here's the part people miss. That behavior may then affect the company itself. Good people leave. Nobody tries anything bold. The belief starts to have real consequences even though it started out wrong. So when someone's behavior doesn't make sense to you, don't start by asking whether they're right. Start by asking what they believe. Once you know the belief, the behavior usually makes a lot more sense. Here's a question for you. Can you think of a time someone acted on a belief about you that simply wasn't true?
