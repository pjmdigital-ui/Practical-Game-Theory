Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Now we get into something that can become surprisingly deep. You don't just have beliefs about the other person. You have beliefs about what they believe about you. "I believe you think I'm bluffing." And perhaps you believe I think you believe I'm bluffing. It can go on indefinitely. Game theorists call these higher-order beliefs, or belief hierarchies. Poker makes it intuitive. You don't just ask, "What cards does my opponent have?" You ask, "What do they think I have?" And, "What do they think I think they have?" That's why bluffing is possible. A bluff doesn't work because the statement is true. It works because the other player believes it, or believes it enough to act differently. And of course, beliefs can be wrong. Sometimes those mistaken beliefs sustain a stable pattern, like the equilibria from Module Five. If an equilibrium rests on incorrect beliefs, changing the information may change the equilibrium.
