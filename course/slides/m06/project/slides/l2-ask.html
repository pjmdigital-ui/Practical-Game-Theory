The problem with "just ask"

The other person gets to decide what to tell you.

And they may have an incentive to tell you what benefits them. Information has strategic value.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Information has value. Think back to the negotiation from Module Two. If the other side believes "This is Paul's only option," they negotiate one way. If they believe "Paul can walk away and has three other offers," they behave differently. That's why people ask questions, investigate, stay silent, reveal things selectively, observe, and test one another. They're trying to improve their model of the game. So the obvious advice is: if you don't know, just ask. But here's the problem. The other person gets to decide what to tell you, and they may have an incentive to give you information that benefits them. You ask the used car seller, "Has this car ever had any major problems?" He says, "No." You've received information. Should you automatically believe it? What does he gain if you believe him? What does he lose if you find out he lied? Now we're starting to move from information to credibility.
