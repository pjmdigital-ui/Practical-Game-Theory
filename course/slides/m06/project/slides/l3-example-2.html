Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's one more example. I love it because it's so simple. Imagine two salespeople. Both of them tell you, "This is our best price." That's the situation. Exactly the same sentence. Now here's what you know about them. Salesperson A has a reputation for being completely transparent. Salesperson B has a reputation for making exaggerated claims. Same words. Different information. Why? Because you aren't evaluating the words in isolation. When A says it, you think there's a good chance it really is the best price. When B says it, you think it's probably the opening line. The sentence is identical. What you learn from it is completely different. That's the lesson on the right. You never weigh words alone. You weigh the whole game around them. Look at the row along the bottom. You're evaluating the speaker. You're evaluating the incentives. You're evaluating the history and the context. You're evaluating the alternatives and the consequences. Every one of those changes how much a sentence is worth. That's why communication can never be separated from the game surrounding it. Now turn this around on yourself. You have a reputation too. When you say "this is my final offer" or "I'll have it done by Friday," people are weighing your history before they weigh your words. If your history says you mean it, your cheap talk becomes valuable. If your history says you exaggerate, even your true statements get discounted. Here's a question for you. Which kind of salesperson are you in the eyes of the people you work with? Let's land this lesson.
