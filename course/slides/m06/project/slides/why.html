Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Imagine you're negotiating to buy a used car. The seller knows the car has a serious mechanical problem. You don't. You think you're negotiating over "What is this car worth?" The seller knows you're actually negotiating over "How much can I get you to pay before you discover the problem?" Same car. Same conversation. Different information. And therefore a very different game. Now reverse it. Suppose you've already been approved for financing elsewhere, and the seller doesn't know that. Your alternatives look different to you than they do to the seller. The information structure changes the game. And here's why that matters for communication: communication is, in large part, an attempt to change what another person knows or believes. So before you can communicate well, you need an honest map of who knows what, who believes what, and what you're merely assuming.
