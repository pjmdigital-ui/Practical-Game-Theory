Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Let me start with a car. Imagine you're negotiating to buy a used car. The seller knows the car has a serious mechanical problem. You don't. You think you're negotiating over one question. What is this car worth? The seller knows you're actually negotiating over a different question. How much can I get you to pay before you discover the problem? Same car. Same conversation. Different information. And therefore a very different game. Now reverse it. Suppose you've already been approved for financing elsewhere. The seller doesn't know that. Your alternatives look different to you than they look to the seller. Again the information structure changes the game. Here's why this matters so much for us. Two people can stand in exactly the same situation and still play very different games. You may know something they don't. They may know something you don't. You may both know something while one of you doesn't realize the other knows it. And sometimes what matters most isn't what's actually true. It's what each person believes is true. Communication is in large part an attempt to change what another person knows or believes. So before you can communicate well you need an honest map. Who knows what? Who believes what? And what are you merely assuming? Think about a conversation this week where you walked in assuming the other person had the same information you had. Did they really?
