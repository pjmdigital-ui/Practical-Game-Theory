The everyday question: What does this person have to lose by making this claim?

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Suppose I tell you, "I'm extremely confident." That costs me almost nothing to say. You don't have much reason to update your beliefs. Now suppose I put ten thousand dollars of my own money behind my prediction. That's different. My action has become costly. The reason costly signals can be more informative is that different types of players face different costs or benefits from sending them. Someone who's genuinely confident and someone who's bluffing can say the same words for free. They can't both comfortably risk the same money. That's one of the central ideas behind signaling theory, and it gives you a very practical everyday question: what does this person have to lose by making this claim? If the answer is nothing, be more cautious about treating it as strong evidence.
