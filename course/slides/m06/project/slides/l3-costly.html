Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's the idea that makes signaling powerful. Suppose I tell you I'm extremely confident. That costs me almost nothing to say. So you don't have much reason to update your beliefs. Anyone can say it. People who are right say it. People who are wrong say it too. Now suppose I put ten thousand dollars of my own money behind my prediction. That's different. My action has become costly. Look at the arrow on the screen. We moved from the left card to the right card. The words stayed the same in spirit. What changed is that I now have something to lose. Why does that make the signal more informative? Look at the line at the bottom. Different types of players face different costs or benefits from sending it. Someone who's genuinely confident and someone who's bluffing can both say the same words for free. They can't both comfortably risk the same money. The confident person expects to get the money back. The bluffer expects to lose it. So the costly action separates them in a way the cheap words never could. That's one of the central ideas behind signaling theory. And you see it everywhere once you know to look. A friend who says they'll help you move is offering words. A friend who shows up at seven in the morning with a truck has paid a cost. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Costly doesn't only mean money. It can mean time or effort or reputation. Anything that would hurt to give up if the claim turned out false. That leads to a question you can use every single day.
