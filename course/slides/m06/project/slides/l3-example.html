Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Let's bring this home with something personal. Here's the situation. Suppose someone tells you, "I really value our relationship." That's a statement. And it's a nice one to hear. But what does it cost them to say it? Almost nothing. They could say it whether they mean it or not. Now look at the middle card. Imagine they repeatedly demonstrate that value. They make sacrifices. They keep commitments. They show up when it's difficult. They behave consistently over time. Now you have something much more informative. Why? Because each of those actions costs something. Time. Convenience. Other options they gave up. And someone who didn't really value the relationship would find those costs much harder to keep paying. That's the signaling logic from a few slides ago showing up in ordinary life. Here's the lesson. The pattern of costly behavior tells you far more than the sentence. But be careful here. This doesn't mean words are worthless. Words can be extremely valuable. A partner who says "I value you" and also shows up is giving you two pieces of evidence that point the same way. The words just can't carry the whole load by themselves. Their strategic value depends partly on what incentives the speaker has to say them. There's a flip side for you too. If you want someone to believe you value them, your words will only take you so far. What are you doing that would be costly if you didn't mean it? That's worth sitting with for a moment.
