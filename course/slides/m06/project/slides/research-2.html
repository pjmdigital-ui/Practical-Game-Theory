Research spotlight · Costa-Gomes & Weizsäcker

People don't always act on their own beliefs

What it shows

In 14 one-shot games people also stated what they expected opponents to do. On average they failed to best respond to their own stated beliefs in almost half the games.

What it suggests

Beliefs matter. But people don't always turn beliefs into consistent action. That includes you.

What it doesn't prove

That beliefs are irrelevant or people are random. Nor that knowing a belief lets you reliably predict behavior.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

The second spotlight comes from Costa-Gomes and Weizsäcker in the Review of Economic Studies. Participants played fourteen one-shot games. They also reported what they expected opponents to do. On average they failed to best respond to their own stated beliefs in almost half the games. So beliefs matter. But people don't always turn beliefs into consistent action. This doesn't show beliefs are irrelevant or people are random. Related work by Rutström and Wilcox found that asking for beliefs with incentives could alter early play under some conditions.
