Definition

A signal helps you infer what you can't observe.

What's hidden

A candidate's underlying ability. The employer can't see it directly.

The signal

A degree. It can provide information about the candidate.

Why it can work

In Michael Spence's job-market model the cost of education differs across types.

But a signal isn't proof. It's evidence. Its value depends on how informative it actually is.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's a key idea. A signal is an action or piece of information that can help another player infer something they don't directly observe. Education is the classic example. What's hidden is the candidate's ability. The signal is a degree. Michael Spence's job-market signaling model showed why it can work. The cost of getting the education differs across types. If it cost everyone the same, it would tell the employer much less. Here's the mistake to avoid. A signal isn't proof. It's evidence. Next let's look at why cost matters so much.
