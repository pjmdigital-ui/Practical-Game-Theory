Two people, two different games

Facts: the report was late; the director raised it; Jordan mentioned finance mid-week. "He doesn't take it seriously" is only a suspicion.

Gaps: Jordan knows finance was late and he worked Thursday. Dana knows the director was watching.

Beliefs: Jordan thinks Dana already knows and wants blame. Dana assumed a hallway remark reached Jordan.

Spiral: her certainty makes him guarded; he stops early warnings; each sees "proof."

Opening: "I'm not sure I have the full picture. Walk me through finance's numbers." Then share what she knows.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's how I'd work it. Dana's facts: the report was two days late, the director raised it publicly, and Jordan sent a mid-week message about finance's numbers. Her belief that Jordan doesn't take the director's priorities seriously sits at "I suspect," but she's acting as if it's "I know." The gaps run both ways. Jordan knows finance delivered late, he worked late Thursday, and his message was meant as a warning. Dana knows about the director's attention and Friday's public embarrassment. Each is reacting to a different game. The higher-order belief: Jordan believes Dana already knows finance was late and is choosing to blame him anyway. And the common-knowledge failure: the director comment existed on the team, but Dana assumed it was in Jordan's information set without any evidence. If she walks in treating her hypothesis as fact, Jordan gets defensive, she reads that as confirmation, and he stops sending early warnings. That's the two-departments pattern in miniature. So she opens with facts and a question: "The report went out two days late and the director raised it with me. I'm not sure I have the full picture. Walk me through what happened with finance's numbers, and tell me if you'd heard the director was watching this one." Nobody needs to pressure anybody. They both need a more accurate map.
