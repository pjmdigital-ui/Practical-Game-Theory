Two people. Two different games.

Facts: The report was two days late. The director raised it publicly. Jordan mentioned finance's numbers mid-week.

Beliefs: "He doesn't take it seriously" sits at "I suspect." Dana is acting as if it's "I know."

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's how I'd work it. Let's start with Dana's facts. The report was two days late. The director raised it publicly. And Jordan sent a mid-week message mentioning finance's numbers. That's it. Those are the things a camera could have recorded. Now her beliefs. She believes Jordan didn't flag the risk. She believes he doesn't take the director's priorities seriously. Her confidence in that second belief honestly sits at "I suspect." But she's acting as if it's "I know." That gap between her confidence label and her behavior is the heart of the problem. Then her assumptions. She's assuming Jordan knew the director was watching closely. And she's assuming his message was routine. Now the information gaps. They run both ways. Jordan knows what Dana doesn't. Finance delivered late. He worked late Thursday. And his mid-week message was meant as a warning. Dana knows what Jordan doesn't. She knows about the director's heightened attention. She knows about Friday's public embarrassment. So each of them is reacting to a different game. That's why the title says two people and two different games. Now the higher-order belief. Jordan believes Dana already knows finance was late. And he believes she's choosing to blame him anyway. He's not responding to what Dana knows. He's responding to what he thinks she knows. And he's wrong about that. Did you catch all of these? If you missed the higher-order belief, don't worry.
