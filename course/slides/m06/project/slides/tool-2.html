Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's the rest of the Information Map in your workbook. These four parts help you keep facts and beliefs and assumptions in separate columns. Part two is sort it. You take everything from the seven questions and put it in one of four rows. Facts are things you actually know happened. They're observable and verifiable. Unknowns are what you're missing. Beliefs are what you currently think is true. And assumptions about them are what you're assuming they know or believe. Then you label your confidence in each belief. I know. I have good evidence. I suspect. I'm guessing. That labeling step is where the magic happens. You'll often find a belief you've been treating as "I know" really sits at "I suspect." Part three is shared or common. Is the key information actually in their information set? What evidence do you have that they know it? And do you both know that you both know it? Or are you assuming it's shared? Part four is about messages you're relying on. What did they tell you that matters? What do they gain if you believe it? What would it cost them if it were false? How could you verify it? And then the key question from lesson three. Why should I believe it? Part five is the dark card. What single piece of information would change my decision or theirs? And how can I get it? You can ask or observe. You can verify or share. Here's the common mistake. People gather more information when they need the right information. Part five keeps you focused on what actually matters.
