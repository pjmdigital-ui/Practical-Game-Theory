Example · The job interview

Information or self-interested persuasion?

The situation

The candidate knows how capable they are and how hard they really work. The employer doesn't.

What happened

The candidate shares experience and examples. They offer references and work samples. The employer knows they want to look good.

The lesson

Claims aren't automatically facts. The employer has to separate information from self-interested persuasion.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Let's put this together in a job interview. Here's the situation. The employer doesn't know exactly how capable you are. You know more about yourself. You know how good you really are. You know how hard you actually work. So what happens? You provide information. You tell them about your experience. You give examples. You answer questions. You provide references. You show them your work. That all sounds straightforward. But now something interesting happens. The employer knows that you have an incentive to present yourself favorably. Every candidate does. So your claims aren't automatically treated as facts. The employer has to separate information from self-interested persuasion. And here's the layer that makes it truly strategic. You know the employer is doing that. So you're not just presenting yourself. You're presenting yourself to someone who is discounting your presentation. That's the lesson on the right. Notice that nobody here has to be lying. You can be completely honest and still face a real problem. Your honest claims can sound exactly like what a less capable candidate would say. A weaker candidate can also say "I work hard" and "I'm a fast learner." So the words alone don't separate you from them. If you've ever sat on the hiring side of the table you know this feeling well. Everyone sounds great. You're trying to figure out which parts of the story you can actually lean on. Here's a question to sit with. When you present yourself, which of your claims could anyone make? And which ones could only someone like you back up? That question leads straight into signals. First let's land this lesson.
