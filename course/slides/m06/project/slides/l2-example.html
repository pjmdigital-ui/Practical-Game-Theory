Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Consider a job interview. The candidate knows how capable they really are and how hard they actually work. The employer doesn't. So the candidate provides information: experience, examples, answers, references, work samples. But now something interesting happens. The employer knows the candidate has an incentive to present themselves favorably. So those claims aren't automatically treated as facts. The employer has to separate information from self-interested persuasion. And the candidate knows the employer is doing that. That's what makes this a strategic communication problem, not just a conversation. Notice that nobody here has to be lying. The candidate can be completely honest and still face the problem that their honest claims sound exactly like what a less capable candidate would say.
