Example · The job interview

Information or self-interested persuasion?

The situation

The candidate knows how capable they are and how hard they really work. The employer doesn't.

What happened

The candidate shares experience and examples. They offer references and work samples. The employer knows they want to look good.

The lesson

Claims aren't automatically facts. The employer has to separate information from self-interested persuasion.

Internomics · Module 6 — Map Information & Beliefs

Here's a job interview. The employer doesn't know exactly how capable you are. You know how good you really are and how hard you work. So you share experience and examples and references. But the employer knows you have an incentive to present yourself favorably. So the employer has to separate information from self-interested persuasion. Nobody here has to be lying. Your honest claims can sound exactly like what a less capable candidate would say. Which of your claims could anyone make? And which could only someone like you back up? First let's land this lesson.
