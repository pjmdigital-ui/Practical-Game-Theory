Where the ethical line falls

Two kinds of unpredictability

Strategic: legitimate

Your competitor knows when you launch every year. They know your pricing pattern and your promotional schedule.

Changing those patterns so they can't optimize against you is competing.

Manipulative: out of bounds

Unpredictability used to deceive another person.

Unpredictability used to destabilize another person.

You can surprise a competitor without deceiving a customer.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

I'll be honest with you. I initially hesitated to include unpredictability in this framework at all. I worried it would sound like a license to play games with people. I don't hesitate anymore. That's because there's an important distinction between two things. One is manipulative unpredictability. The other is strategic unpredictability. Let me show you the strategic kind first. Suppose you're competing against another business. Your competitor knows you always launch new products at the same time every year. They can prepare for it. They know your pricing pattern. They know your promotional schedule. They know exactly how you respond to their moves. There may be real strategic value in changing those patterns. And that isn't inherently unethical. Competition is not inherently unethical. You can compete aggressively and still be honest. You can protect information without lying. You can surprise a competitor without deceiving a customer. You can vary your strategy so another player can't simply optimize against a fixed pattern. That's legitimate strategic behavior. Now the manipulative kind. That's unpredictability used to deceive someone. Or it's used to destabilize them. Keeping a partner off balance so they're easier to control falls here. So does inventing chaos so someone can't think clearly. That's not what we're doing in this course. Here's a quick test you can use. Ask who the uncertainty is aimed at and why. Is it stopping someone from exploiting a fixed pattern? Or is it meant to confuse a person who's trusting you? Next let's make the line around secrecy just as clear.
