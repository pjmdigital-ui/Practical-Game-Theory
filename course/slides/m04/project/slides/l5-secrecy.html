Where the ethical line falls

Secrecy is not deception

Strategic: legitimate

Varying your launch timing so a competitor can't optimize against a fixed pattern.

Withholding information you aren't obligated to disclose.

Manipulative: out of bounds

Unpredictability used to deceive or destabilize another person.

Deliberately providing false information to induce a decision.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

I'll be honest: I initially hesitated to include unpredictability in this framework at all. I don't anymore, because there's an important distinction between manipulative unpredictability and strategic unpredictability. Suppose your competitor knows you always launch new products at the same time every year. They know your pricing pattern and your promotional schedule. There may be real value in changing those patterns. That's competing, not deceiving. You can compete aggressively and still be honest. You can surprise a competitor without deceiving a customer. Same with secrecy. In a negotiation, you don't have to reveal your reservation price. Withholding information you're not obligated to disclose is one thing. Deliberately giving someone false information to push them into a decision is something else entirely. Game theory teaches you the strategic value of information. What you do with it is an ethical question.
