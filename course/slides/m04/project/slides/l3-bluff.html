Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Let me show you the tax being collected. Imagine a poker player who bluffs frequently. At first his opponents may fold. The bluff works. He bets big with a weak hand and they give up the pot. So he does it again. And again. But eventually the other players notice. "He's bluffing a lot." Now they call more often. And the same bluff that worked earlier stops working. So what changed? Not the words. He's making the same bets. Not the cards. The deck is the same deck. What changed is the other players' beliefs. They learned his pattern. That's the whole lesson in one hand of cards. This is one reason strategic interaction is dynamic. Your behavior doesn't merely affect the immediate outcome. It teaches other players about you. And what they learn changes how they respond in the future. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. When a tactic that used to work stops working, most of us blame the tactic. We think the move was bad. Often the move is fine. The problem is that it became predictable. The other side learned it. So when something that used to work for you suddenly doesn't, ask a different question. What have they learned about me? Think about a line or a move you've leaned on for years. A joke. A threat. A deadline. Does it land the way it used to? Next let's look at what all this teaching adds up to over time.
