Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's the second time you want people to know exactly what you'll do. This is where predictability connects directly to commitment. Suppose you tell your employees something very clear. "If you falsify customer records, you will be terminated." And then you consistently enforce it. Every time. No matter who it is. You've created predictability. And that's good. Why is it good? Because your employees can now anticipate the consequences of their choices. Your predictable response becomes part of their decision-making. They don't have to wonder "What will happen if I do this?" They know. And that knowledge can change behavior before the violation ever occurs. That's the real payoff. The best consequence is the one you never have to use because everyone already believes it. Now notice the contrast with the poker player from Lesson Three. His predictability helped his opponents beat him. Here your predictability helps the whole team stay honest. Same trait. Very different game. This is one of the reasons credible commitments matter so much. We'll devote an entire module to them later in the course. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. A rule you announce but enforce only sometimes isn't a predictable consequence. It's a lottery. People will start to gamble on it. And a rule you announce but never enforce teaches the opposite of what you said. So ask yourself this. Is there a rule in your home or at work that you state but don't enforce?
