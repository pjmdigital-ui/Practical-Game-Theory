Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Think about a stoplight. At an intersection, you want the other drivers to know what you're going to do. Red means stop. Green means go. The other driver will stay in their lane. If everyone suddenly became unpredictable at intersections, traffic would become dangerous. Coordination requires predictability. That's why conventions and norms are so powerful: they let people coordinate because everyone can predict what everyone else is likely to do. So predictability has two very different strategic functions. It can create trust and coordination. Or it can create exploitation and strategic vulnerability. Same phenomenon, different game. The real question isn't "Should I be predictable or unpredictable?" It's "Where does predictability help cooperation, and where does it create an opening for exploitation?"
