Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Think about a stoplight. When you pull up to an intersection, you want the other drivers to know what you're going to do. And you want to know what they're going to do. Red means stop. Green means go. The other driver will stay in their lane. Now imagine everyone suddenly became unpredictable at intersections. Some people stop on green. Some people go on red. Some people drift between lanes just to keep you guessing. Traffic would become dangerous almost immediately. Coordination requires predictability. Shared expectations are what make cooperation possible. That's one reason conventions and norms are so powerful. They let people coordinate because everyone can predict what everyone else is likely to do. Nobody has to negotiate each intersection from scratch. So predictability has two very different strategic functions. On one side it can create trust and coordination. On the other side it can create exploitation and strategic vulnerability. It's the same phenomenon. It's just a different game. The challenge is knowing which environment you're in. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. After the last two lessons some people decide predictability is a weakness. They start trying to hide everything. That's the wrong lesson. Most of your daily life is closer to the stoplight than to the poker table. So ask yourself this. In your most important relationships, is the game more like the intersection or more like the card game? Next let's look at what happens when you get this wrong in the other direction.
