Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

So how do you tell whether a pattern is helping you or hurting you? Here's a simple sorting tool. A pattern can be an asset. It's an asset when it creates trust. It's an asset when it makes cooperation easier. It's an asset when it establishes a credible commitment. It's an asset when it reduces uncertainty for people who depend on you. And it's an asset when it helps others coordinate with you. Think of the stoplight. Think of the manager whose rule everyone believes. But a pattern can also be a liability. It's a liability when people know exactly how to pressure you. It's a liability when your threats are never followed through. It's a liability when your concessions are predictable. It's a liability when your boundaries are predictable but unenforced. That one is sneaky. You keep stating the boundary. Everyone has learned it doesn't hold. And it's a liability when competitors can exploit it or when your strategy can be easily anticipated. Look at both columns together. The underlying thing is identical. Someone can predict you. Same phenomenon. Different game. What makes the difference is what that prediction lets the other person do. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People tend to judge a pattern by how it feels to them. Being reliably available feels generous. Always giving a little on price feels friendly. But the question isn't how it feels. It's what it enables. So take one pattern you identified earlier in this module. Which column does it belong in? Be honest with yourself.
