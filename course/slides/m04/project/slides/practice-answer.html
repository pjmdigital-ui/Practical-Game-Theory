Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's how I'd answer it. The rule Rob has learned is: if I bring Dana an urgent revision on Friday, she'll stay late and do it. Dana never chose that rule. Her repeated behavior established it, and the feedback loop has made Friday requests more frequent. Her predictability tax: the pattern revealed that her evenings are flexible and her complaints carry no consequence. "I won't keep doing this," followed by doing it, taught Rob her warnings aren't credible. The Thursday review is an asset, stoplight predictability that helps the juniors coordinate. The Friday pattern is a liability. So becoming harder to read across the board would be a mistake; it would wreck expectations her team depends on. Instead, keep Thursday predictable, and be deliberately consistent about a clear Friday boundary. For example: revisions that arrive after 2 p.m. Friday get done Monday morning unless the client deadline truly requires otherwise. Then enforce it every time. Staying late only sometimes would keep Rob testing her. And no deception needed. No fake evening plans. She states the rule honestly and lets consistent follow-through teach the new pattern.
