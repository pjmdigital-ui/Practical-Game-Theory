Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's how I'd answer the first three questions. Start with the rule. The rule Rob has learned is simple. If I bring Dana an urgent revision on Friday, she'll stay late and do it. Did Dana choose that rule? No. Her repeated behavior established it. And look at what's happened since. Rob has adapted. The feedback loop has kicked in. He acted. She responded. He learned and adapted. And Friday requests became more frequent. Remember those requests that sat in his inbox since Tuesday? That's the loop at work. Rob has no reason to send them earlier. He's learned that Friday works fine for him. Now the predictability tax. Her pattern revealed two things. It revealed that her evenings are flexible. And it revealed that her complaints carry no consequence. She said "I won't keep doing this." Then she did it anyway. That taught Rob that her warnings aren't credible. Her behavior communicated more than her words. That's Lesson Three playing out exactly. Now asset versus liability. The Thursday review is an asset. It's the stoplight kind of predictability. It helps the juniors coordinate. Both sides benefit from knowing exactly what she'll do. The Friday pattern is a liability. It shows exactly how to pressure her. It also reveals a boundary that's stated but unenforced. So Dana has one pattern in each column. That's the key insight. She doesn't have a predictability problem. She has one specific pattern that's working against her. Here's a question to check yourself. Did your answer treat both patterns the same way? If so, take another look.
