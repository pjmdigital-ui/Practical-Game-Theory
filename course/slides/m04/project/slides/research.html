Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Two research spotlights this time, and they point in opposite directions, which is the point. The first: Chiappori, Levitt, and Groseclose, in 2002, analyzed 459 penalty kicks from the top French and Italian professional soccer leagues. Penalty kicks are a clean strategic setting: opposing goals, known tendencies, high stakes. They found that the testable predictions of mixed-strategy theory fit the data surprisingly well, and a later analysis with a different statistical method reached broadly consistent results. What it suggests for us: when someone's gain depends on anticipating you, your predictability changes the payoff of your action. A concession is worth less if they know it's coming. What it doesn't prove: soccer is close to a pure zero-sum contest. It doesn't show that every conversation works like a penalty kick, that unpredictable people win, or that you should randomize how you communicate. And related research on baseball pitches, by Bhattacharya and Howard, found professionals only approximate the mixed-strategy benchmark, more closely as stakes rise.
