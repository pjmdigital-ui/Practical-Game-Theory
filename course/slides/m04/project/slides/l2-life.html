Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

This is where we need to be careful. It would be a real mistake to take a formal idea like mixed strategies and turn it into a life rule. The rule people reach for is "always keep people guessing." That's not what game theory says. Randomization is useful under particular strategic conditions. And in many human relationships excessive unpredictability is actually destructive. Let me make that concrete. Imagine your spouse never knew whether you were going to keep your promises. Imagine your employees never knew whether you'd enforce a rule. Imagine your business partners never knew whether you'd honor an agreement. Would any of that make you more strategic? Of course not. That's not strategic sophistication. That's a lack of reliability. In relationships where trust matters, predictability can be enormously valuable. Your spouse needs to plan around you. Your team needs to know the rules are real. Your partners need to know a deal is a deal. So the real question isn't "Should I be predictable or unpredictable?" That question is too blunt to be useful. The better question is this. Where does predictability help cooperation? And where does it create an opportunity for exploitation? That's a much more useful question. It forces you to look at the game you're actually in before you choose. Hold that question. We'll build on it through the rest of the module. For now let's go back to rock-paper-scissors and play it out.
