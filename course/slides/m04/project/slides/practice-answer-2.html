Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Now the last two questions. Is becoming harder to read across the board a good plan? No. That plan confuses unpredictability with strategic intelligence. And it would destroy expectations her team depends on. Imagine the juniors showing up Thursday morning and not knowing whether a review is coming. That punishes the people who did nothing wrong. So here's what I'd recommend instead using the four moves. Be predictable about the Thursday review. Keep it exactly as it is. Then be deliberately consistent about a clear Friday boundary. For example, revisions that arrive after 2 p.m. on Friday get done Monday morning. The one exception is when the client deadline truly requires otherwise. And then she enforces it every time. That part is critical. Staying late only sometimes would be the wrong kind of unpredictability. It would keep Rob testing her every single Friday. A boundary that holds most of the time is still a boundary worth pushing on. Now the last question. How does she do this without deception? It's easier than people think. She doesn't need fake evening plans. She doesn't need a made-up excuse. She simply states the rule honestly. Then she lets consistent follow-through teach the new pattern. She also doesn't owe Rob an explanation of her whole schedule. Withholding what she doesn't owe isn't lying. Over time Rob learns something new. Her words predict her actions.
