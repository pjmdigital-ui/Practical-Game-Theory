Three meetings, one rule

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's a simple example. Someone repeatedly interrupts you in meetings. The first time you let it go. Maybe you're being gracious. Maybe it just isn't worth the fuss. The second time you let it go again. The third time you let it go again. Now they've learned something. Interrupting you has no cost. Notice that you may never have consciously decided to establish that rule. You never sat down and thought "I'll allow this person to talk over me." But your repeated behavior established one anyway. That's the trap. Each single moment felt small. Each one felt like a reasonable choice in the moment. But the other person isn't experiencing three separate choices. They're experiencing a pattern. And a pattern reads like a rule. Once people learn the rule, they can act accordingly. They interrupt a little more freely. They don't check whether you were finished. They have no reason to. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. This doesn't mean you should explode at the fourth interruption. We'll see in Lesson Four why that backfires too. The point is simply to notice the rule you're teaching. So the question isn't only "Should I let this go?" The better question is "What rule am I teaching if I let this go again?" Think about a meeting you sit in every week. Is there a rule like this running in it? Let's put the lesson into one line.
