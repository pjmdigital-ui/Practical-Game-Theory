Three meetings, one rule

Meeting one: They interrupt you. You let it go.

Meeting two: They interrupt you. You let it go.

Meeting three: They interrupt you. You let it go.

What they've learned: interrupting you has no cost.

Letting it go once is a choice. Letting it go every time is a policy.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's a simple example. Someone repeatedly interrupts you in meetings. The first time, you let it go. The second time, you let it go. The third time, you let it go. Now they've learned something: interrupting you has no cost. You may never have consciously decided to establish that rule. But your repeated behavior established one anyway. That's the trap. Letting something go once is a choice. Letting it go every time is a policy, and other people read policies. Once they've learned the rule, they can act accordingly. So the question isn't just "Should I let this go?" It's "What rule am I teaching if I let this go again?"
