Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's why this module matters. If someone knows exactly how you'll respond, they can start planning around you. Your behavior stops being just your behavior. It becomes part of their strategy. Think about a coworker who knows that every time they ask you for a favor, you say yes. At first that's simply your personality. You're helpful. You don't mind the extra work. But eventually your coworker learns something. If I ask Paul, he'll probably say yes. They don't have to wonder whether to ask anymore. They already know the answer. The same thing happens in negotiation. Say the other side knows you always make a concession after their first counteroffer. They don't have to wonder whether they should push. They know. Push and he'll probably move. You've taught them the game. Now I want to be clear about something right at the start. This is not a module about becoming erratic. It's not about being deceptive or hard to read. Predictability is also what makes trust possible. It's what makes coordination possible. It's what makes a consequence believable. The skill is knowing when each one is strategically valuable. So the shift I want you to practice is one new question. What are they learning about me from what I repeatedly do? Keep that question in your pocket. Let's look at what you'll be able to do by the end.
