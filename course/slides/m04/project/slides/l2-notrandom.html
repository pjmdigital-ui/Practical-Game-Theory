Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Now I don't want you to reduce this to random behavior. A good mixed strategy is not "I'll just do something random." It's often a carefully chosen probability distribution. It's built to make the opponent indifferent among their responses. Or it's built to keep them from exploiting your pattern. The formal math behind that can get complicated. And here's the good news. You don't need it. You don't have to calculate probabilities to appreciate the idea underneath. Sometimes uncertainty itself has strategic value. That's the whole insight. Sometimes the best strategy isn't "always do X." It's "do X sometimes and Y sometimes" in a pattern the other player can't exploit. But I want to be just as careful in the other direction. The mental model is not "randomize everything." That would be a terrible lesson to take from this. Randomization is useful under particular strategic conditions. It helps when another player gains by correctly anticipating you. Rock-paper-scissors is built that way. Every gain for your opponent is a loss for you. Most of your relationships aren't built that way at all. In fact excessive unpredictability in a relationship is often destructive. We'll see why soon. So keep both halves in your head. Uncertainty can be valuable. And it's valuable only in the right kind of game. Here's a question for you. When you think of someone who is "hard to read," do you trust them more or less? Hold on to your answer. Let's look at why life isn't rock-paper-scissors.
