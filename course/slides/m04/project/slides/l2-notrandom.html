Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Now, don't reduce this to random behavior. A good mixed strategy is not "I'll just do something random." It's often a carefully chosen probability distribution, one that makes the opponent indifferent among their responses or keeps them from exploiting your pattern. The formal math behind that can get complicated, and you don't need it. You don't have to calculate probabilities to appreciate the idea underneath: sometimes uncertainty itself has strategic value. But I want to be just as careful in the other direction. The mental model is not "randomize everything." Randomization is useful under particular strategic conditions, when another player gains by correctly anticipating you. In lots of human relationships, as we'll see, excessive unpredictability is actually destructive.
