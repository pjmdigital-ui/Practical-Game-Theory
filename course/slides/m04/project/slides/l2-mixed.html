Definition · Mixed strategy

Choose among actions by probabilities, not one action every time.

Always rock

Your opponent learns to throw paper.

Always paper

Your opponent learns to throw scissors.

The fix

Not one best action. Keep them from knowing which one you'll choose.

They don't need your exact next move. A tendency is enough.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Game theory has long studied situations where players can't predict exactly what another player will do. That leads to a mixed strategy. A player chooses among actions according to probabilities. The classic example is rock-paper-scissors. If you always throw rock, your opponent eventually learns to throw paper. Here's the part people miss. Your opponent doesn't need your exact next move. A lean in one direction is enough. So the solution is keeping the opponent from knowing which action you'll choose. Next let's clear up the most common misreading of this idea.
