Example · The concession pattern

$100, then $90, then $80

Round one: You offer $100. They push.

Round two: You move to $90. They push again.

Round three: You move to $80.

They never needed your true minimum. Your pattern said: there's more room.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Let's make the tax concrete. Imagine a negotiator who always makes three concessions. First, a hundred dollars. Then ninety. Then eighty. The other side learns the pattern. They don't need to know your true minimum. They just need to know one thing: you'll move again. So instead of accepting your first offer, they push. You move. They push again. You move. By the third round, your pattern has effectively revealed information about your limits. You never said a word about your floor. Your behavior said it for you: there's more room. That's the predictability tax in action. The cost isn't in any single concession. It's in what the sequence teaches.
