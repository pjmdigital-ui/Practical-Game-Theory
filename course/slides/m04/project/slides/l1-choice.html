Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Let me give you two rules that people learn all the time. Nobody announces either of them. The first is the helpful coworker. Imagine a coworker knows that every time they ask you for a favor, you say yes. At first that's simply your personality. You're helpful. You don't mind doing the extra work. There's nothing wrong with that. But eventually your coworker learns something. If I ask Paul, he'll probably say yes. Now your predictability has become part of their strategy. They don't have to wonder whether to ask. They already know. And that changes the interaction. Maybe they stop asking anyone else. Maybe they stop planning ahead because you're always there as the backup. The second is the negotiator. Say the other person knows you always make a concession after their first counteroffer. They don't have to wonder whether they should push. They know the answer already. Push and he'll probably move. You've taught them the game. Here's the line I want you to remember. Doing something once is a choice. Doing it every time is a policy. And other people read policies. They read them even when you don't know you have one. So here's the question for you. What do you do every single time in some part of your life? Write down one. Before we go further I need to be clear about what this module is not.
