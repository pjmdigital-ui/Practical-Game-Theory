Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

So how do you actually choose? Here's the toolkit. There are four moves. The first move is be predictable. Use it when trust matters. Use it when coordination matters. Use it when credibility or cooperation is valuable. This is the stoplight. It's also the person rebuilding trust by keeping every promise. The second move is protect your predictability. Use it when revealing your pattern would give another player leverage. This is the negotiator who says "I'm not prepared to disclose my bottom line." You're not lying. You're just not handing over the map. The third move is vary your behavior. Use it when someone could otherwise exploit a fixed pattern. This is rock-paper-scissors. It's the business that stops launching on the same date every year. It's the negotiator who stops using the same three-step concession sequence. The fourth move is be deliberately consistent. Use it when you want someone to understand that a consequence is real. This is the manager whose rule everyone believes. It's the person who finally enforces a boundary every single time. Notice that two of these moves make you more predictable. Two of them make you less predictable. None of them is the right answer in general. Every one of them is the right answer somewhere. And the question that picks between them is always the same. What does my predictability enable the other player to do? Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Varying your behavior doesn't mean becoming erratic. You vary one specific pattern that is being used against you. Everything else can stay steady. Let's see two players side by side.
