The four moves

Be predictable

When trust, coordination, credibility or cooperation are valuable

Protect your predictability

When revealing your pattern gives another player leverage

Vary your behavior

When someone could otherwise exploit a fixed pattern

Be deliberately consistent

When you want them to understand a consequence is real

Ask first: who benefits from knowing what I'm going to do?

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

So how do you choose? Start by not romanticizing surprise. Surprise is useful only when the uncertainty it creates is valuable. If your spouse doesn't know whether you'll keep a commitment, or your customer doesn't know whether your product will work, that's not strategic. So ask: who benefits from knowing what I'm going to do? If the answer is "both of us," predictability probably helps. If it's "they can use it to consistently take advantage of me," some uncertainty may be valuable. Then pick one of four moves. Be predictable, when trust, coordination, credibility or cooperation matter. Protect your predictability, when revealing your pattern gives someone leverage. Vary your behavior, when someone could exploit a fixed pattern. And be deliberately consistent, when you need someone to understand a consequence is real.
