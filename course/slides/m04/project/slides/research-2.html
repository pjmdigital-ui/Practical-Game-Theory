Research spotlight · 2 of 2

Trust games: when being predictable creates value

What it shows

In Bracht and Feltovich's experiment, seeing one past action of a partner produced substantially higher cooperation. Cheap-talk messages alone had little effect.

What it doesn't prove

It's a simple lab game. It doesn't show that more transparency is always better, or that you should disclose everything. The result depends on context.

Taken together: a liability in one setting, an asset in the other.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

The second spotlight goes the other way. Bracht and Feltovich ran a trust-game experiment in which players could see one previous action of the person they were interacting with. Simply making past behavior observable produced substantially higher cooperation and efficiency than the basic trust game. Cheap-talk messages on their own had little effect. Past cooperative behavior predicted current cooperative behavior, and it influenced whether others were willing to trust. What it suggests: when cooperation depends on trust, being readable is an asset, and what you've done outweighs what you say. That's why I tell you to rebuild trust by becoming more predictable. What it doesn't prove: it's a simple laboratory game. It doesn't show that more transparency is always better, or that you should disclose everything. Put the two studies together and you get the lesson of this module: behavior others can anticipate is a liability in one setting and an asset in another.
