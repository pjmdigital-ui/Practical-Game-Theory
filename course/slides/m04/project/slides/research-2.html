Research spotlight · 2 of 2

Trust games: when being predictable creates value

What it shows

In Bracht and Feltovich's experiment, seeing one past action of a partner produced substantially higher cooperation. Cheap-talk messages alone had little effect.

What it doesn't prove

It's a simple lab game. It doesn't show that more transparency is always better. It doesn't say to disclose everything.

Taken together: A readable pattern is a liability in one setting. It's an asset in the other.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

The second spotlight goes the other way. Bracht and Feltovich ran a trust-game experiment. One player decides whether to trust the other. The other decides whether to honor that trust. Here players could see one previous action of their partner. That alone produced substantially higher cooperation than the basic trust game. Cheap-talk messages on their own had little effect. A visible track record did. It doesn't prove you should disclose everything. But put the two studies together. In the penalty kick a readable pattern gets punished. In the trust game it creates value. Next let's turn this into a tool.
