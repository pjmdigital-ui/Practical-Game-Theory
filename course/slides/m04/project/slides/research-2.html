Trust games: when being predictable creates value

What it shows

In Bracht and Feltovich's experiment, seeing one past action of a partner produced substantially higher cooperation. Cheap-talk messages alone had little effect.

What it doesn't prove

It's a simple lab game. It doesn't show that more transparency is always better. It doesn't say to disclose everything.

Taken together: A readable pattern is a liability in one setting. It's an asset in the other.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

The second spotlight goes the other way. Bracht and Feltovich ran a trust-game experiment. In a trust game one player decides whether to trust the other. The other decides whether to honor that trust. In this version players could see one previous action of the person they were interacting with. That's all. Just one look at what that person had done before. Simply making past behavior observable produced substantially higher cooperation and efficiency than the basic trust game. Here's the part I find striking. Cheap-talk messages on their own had little effect. Words alone didn't move people much. A visible track record did. Past cooperative behavior predicted current cooperative behavior. And it influenced whether others were willing to trust. So what does it suggest for us? When cooperation depends on trust, being readable is an asset. What you've done outweighs what you say. That's exactly why I told you to rebuild trust by becoming more predictable. Call when you say you'll call. Let the record do the talking. Now what it doesn't prove. It's a simple laboratory game. It doesn't show that more transparency is always better. It doesn't show that you should disclose everything. Observable track records support cooperation where trust matters. That's a context-dependent result. Now put the two studies together. In the penalty kick a readable pattern gets punished. In the trust game a readable pattern creates value. Behavior others can anticipate is a liability in one setting and an asset in another. That's the whole lesson of this module in two studies. It's why we ask what your predictability enables instead of following a universal rule. Next let's turn this into a tool you can use this week.
