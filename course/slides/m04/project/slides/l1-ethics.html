Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Before we go any further, I want to be clear about what this module is not. It's not about being deceptive. It isn't about lying. It isn't about manipulating people. And it certainly isn't about becoming erratic just so nobody knows what you're going to do. I say this up front because the topic can sound slippery. When people hear "manage your predictability," some of them picture a poker face and a lot of mind games. That's not where we're going. The principle is much more subtle than that. Don't allow your patterns to become so predictable that other people can consistently use them against you. Read that slowly. The word that matters is "against." A pattern people can count on isn't a problem by itself. It becomes a problem when someone can use it to take advantage of you over and over. Sometimes the answer will be to become less predictable in one narrow area. And sometimes the answer will be to become extremely predictable. You'll see later in this module that rebuilding trust depends on exactly that. So the skill we're building is not "be mysterious." It's knowing when each one is strategically valuable. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. If you leave this lesson thinking the goal is to keep everyone guessing, you've missed it. We'll come back to why that backfires.
