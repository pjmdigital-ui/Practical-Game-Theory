Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

There are times when you want people to know exactly what you'll do. Two of them. First, rebuilding trust. Imagine someone doesn't know whether you'll keep your word anymore. Should you become unpredictable? Probably not. You want to become more predictable. If you say you'll call, call. If you say you'll show up, show up. If you say you'll do something, do it. Over time, they learn: his words predict his actions. That's what trust is made of. Second, making a consequence real. Suppose you tell your employees, "If you falsify customer records, you will be terminated," and then you consistently enforce it. They don't have to wonder what will happen. They know. And that can change behavior before the violation ever occurs. This is where predictability connects to commitment, which gets its own module later.
