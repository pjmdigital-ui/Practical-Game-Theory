Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's a situation where predictability is exactly what you need. Imagine you're trying to rebuild trust with someone. Maybe you've missed commitments. Maybe you've let them down a few times. Right now they don't know whether you'll keep your word. So what should you do? Should you become unpredictable? Keep them guessing? Probably not. That would make things worse. You want to become more predictable. And the way you do it is almost boringly simple. If you say you'll call, call. If you say you'll show up, show up. If you say you'll do something, do it. No exceptions. No "I meant to." Over time the other person learns something. His words predict his actions. That's what trust is made of. It isn't built by a big speech or a grand gesture. It's built by your words and your actions lining up again and again until they can rely on it. So the strategic lesson here isn't "hide your intentions." It's "make your predictability work for you." Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People trying to rebuild trust often promise big. They make five commitments to show they're serious. Then they keep three. That teaches the old pattern all over again. It's usually better to promise less and keep every single one. Each kept promise is a data point. You're building a track record they can read. So here's a question for you. Is there someone whose trust you'd like to rebuild? What's one small promise you could make and keep this week?
