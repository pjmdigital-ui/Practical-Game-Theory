Common mistakes

"Keep them guessing"

Where trust matters, excess unpredictability is destructive. Surprise helps only when the uncertainty is valuable.

Variety mistaken for uncertainty

Rock, then paper, then scissors is still a pattern. If they can learn it, they can exploit it.

Unenforced boundaries

A boundary you state but don't enforce teaches the opposite of what you said.

Secrecy sliding into deception

Declining to share your bottom line is legitimate. Giving false information is not.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here are the mistakes I see most. First, treating "keep them guessing" as the lesson. Game theory doesn't say that. Where trust matters, excessive unpredictability is destructive, and surprise is useful only when the uncertainty it creates is valuable. Close cousin: tolerating something one day and exploding the next. That doesn't make you strategic; it makes you hard to trust. Second, confusing variety with unpredictability. A rigid sequence of different moves, rock, then paper, then scissors, is still a pattern. Third, stating a boundary but never enforcing it. Your behavior creates more information than your words. Fourth, crossing from secrecy into deception. You don't have to answer every question about your limits; giving away your bottom line tells them how far to push. Declining honestly is fine. Deliberately providing false information to induce a decision is not.
