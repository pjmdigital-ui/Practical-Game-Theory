The tax: Your actions reveal information you didn't intend to reveal.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's one of the hidden costs of predictable behavior. Your actions can reveal information you didn't intend to reveal. I call that the predictability tax. You pay it quietly. Most of the time you don't even notice the bill. Here's how it works. Every action you take communicates something. People watch what you do. Then they draw conclusions. If you always answer emails immediately, people may infer that you're highly available. If you always agree to last-minute requests, they may infer that your schedule is flexible. If you always threaten to quit but never do, people learn that your threats aren't credible. And if you repeatedly say you'll enforce a rule and then don't, people learn something about the rule. Notice that none of these people needed you to tell them anything. You never said "my schedule is flexible." You never said "my threats are empty." Your behavior said it for you. That's the key idea of this lesson. Your behavior creates information. Sometimes it creates more information than your words do. And once people learn your pattern, they can adapt to it.
