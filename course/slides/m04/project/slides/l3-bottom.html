Protecting your limits

Giving away your bottom line tells them how far they need to push.

The question

"What's the lowest price you'll accept?" You give them the number.

What changed

You think you answered a question. You changed the information structure.

The honest option

"I'm not prepared to disclose my bottom line." No lie required.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Here's where the tax gets expensive. Imagine you're negotiating and the other person asks a simple question. "What's the lowest price you'll accept?" And you give them the number. Now they know something extremely valuable. They know your boundary. You may think you've simply answered a question. It felt polite. It felt honest. Strategically you've changed the information structure of the whole negotiation. You've told them how far they need to push. Every offer they make from now on can sit right on top of your floor. There's no reason for them to go one dollar higher. Now this doesn't mean you should lie. Please hear that. You don't need to invent a fake number. You might legitimately say "I'm not prepared to disclose my bottom line." That's not deception. Every word of it is true. It's protecting strategically valuable information. And look at what it does to the other player. Now they have to make their decision without knowing exactly where your boundary lies. That's the position they'd be in if they'd never asked. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. A lot of people feel that declining to answer is somehow dishonest. It isn't. Withholding information you don't owe someone is very different from giving them false information. We'll draw that line carefully in Lesson Five. For now here's a question.
