Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Go back to the person who keeps interrupting you in meetings. In Lesson One you let it go every time. That taught a rule. Now imagine you overcorrect and do the opposite. One day you tolerate an interruption. The next day you explode. The next day you ignore it. The next day you punish the person. Nobody knows what will happen. Well, congratulations. You may have made yourself unpredictable. But you've also made yourself difficult to trust. Look at the cost. Your employees can't coordinate around you. Your family can't anticipate your reactions. Your partners can't make plans. Everybody around you is spending energy guessing your mood. That's energy they're not spending on the work or on the relationship. Unpredictability has now destroyed something valuable. It has destroyed reliable expectations. And reliable expectations are what people build on. That's why strategic sophistication isn't "Never let people know what you're going to do." That's the movie version. Real strategic sophistication is understanding the strategic consequences of being predictable. Sometimes that understanding tells you to change a pattern. Often it tells you to keep one. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People sometimes mistake volatility for strength. They think being hard to read makes them powerful. Usually it just makes them hard to work with. Think about the most erratic boss or relative you've known. Did you respect them more for it? Or did you just learn to tiptoe? Next let's look at when you want people to know exactly what you'll do.
