The wrong kind of unpredictable

Tolerating it one day and exploding the next isn't sophistication.

It makes you difficult to trust, and it destroys something valuable: reliable expectations.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Go back to the person who keeps interrupting you. Now imagine you do the opposite of letting it go. One day you tolerate an interruption. The next day you explode. The next day you ignore it. The next day you punish the person. Nobody knows what will happen. Congratulations, you've made yourself unpredictable. But you've also made yourself difficult to trust. Your employees can't coordinate around you. Your family can't anticipate your reactions. Your partners can't make plans. Imagine a spouse who never knew whether you'd keep your promises, or a business partner who never knew whether you'd honor an agreement. That's not strategic sophistication. That's a lack of reliability. Strategic sophistication isn't "Never let people know what you'll do." It's understanding the consequences of being predictable.
