Example · Two players, two patterns

Trapped vs. protected

The situation

Player one always answers a price cut with another price cut.

Player two is asked: "What's the lowest price you'll accept?"

What happened

Player one can be trapped into a predictable escalation.

Player two answers: "I'm not prepared to disclose my bottom line."

The lesson

One is trapped by a fixed rule.

The other protects information without saying anything false.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Let's put two cases side by side. The first player is a competitor who always responds to a price cut with another price cut. Everyone in the market knows it. It's a fixed rule. And a fixed rule like that means they can be trapped into a predictable escalation. Think about what that hands to everyone else. Somebody else gets to decide when the price war starts. They know exactly how this player will respond. So they can pull the trigger whenever it suits them. The competitor's own rule has become someone else's weapon. The second player is a negotiator. Someone asks "What's the lowest price you'll accept?" And the negotiator answers "I'm not prepared to disclose my bottom line." No lie. No fake number. Just a calm refusal to hand over strategically valuable information. Now the other side has to decide without knowing where the boundary is. Look at the contrast. One player is trapped by a pattern anyone can read. The other is managing predictability. They protect what matters and they stay honest while they do it. Notice which of the four moves each one needed. The competitor needed to vary their behavior. Their fixed response was being exploited. The negotiator was protecting their predictability. And here's the deeper point. Changing a pattern isn't valuable because randomness is powerful. It's valuable because predictability gives the other player information they can use. So here's a question for you. Do you have an automatic response that someone else could trigger on purpose? Let's close the lesson with the one line to remember.
