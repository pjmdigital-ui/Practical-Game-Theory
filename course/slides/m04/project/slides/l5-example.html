Example · Two players, two patterns

Trapped vs. protected

Trapped by a fixed rule

A competitor who always answers a price cut with another price cut can be trapped into a predictable escalation.

Protected, honestly

Asked "What's the lowest price you'll accept?", a negotiator answers: "I'm not prepared to disclose my bottom line."

One is trapped by a pattern. The other protects information without saying anything false.

Internomics · Module 4 — Manage Predictability

Let's put two cases side by side. First, a competitor who always responds to a price cut with another price cut. Everyone in the market knows it. That fixed rule means they can be trapped into a predictable escalation. Somebody else gets to decide when the price war starts, because they know exactly how this player will respond. Second, a negotiator who gets asked, "What's the lowest price you'll accept?" and answers, "I'm not prepared to disclose my bottom line." No lie. No fake number. Just a refusal to hand over strategically valuable information. One player is trapped by a pattern anyone can read. The other is managing predictability: protecting what matters, honestly. Changing a pattern isn't valuable because randomness is powerful. It's valuable because predictability gives the other player information they can use.
