Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

We've all heard "Actions speak louder than words." Sometimes. But that phrase is still too simple. Actions can be strategic too. Suppose a company makes a large public investment in a new product. That could signal "We have tremendous confidence in this." Or maybe management is trying to convince investors that the product has a future. Or maybe they're already too committed to the project and don't want to admit they were wrong. Same action. Several possible interpretations. So the right question isn't "Words or actions?" It's "What does this particular action actually tell me, given the incentives surrounding it?" That's why I want you to treat behavior as evidence. Not as proof. Here's when behavior becomes really useful. Suppose someone tells you "I value honesty." Then you watch how they behave when someone tells them an uncomfortable truth. Do they punish the person? Do they get defensive? Do they retaliate? Now you've received real information. Not because actions always beat words. Because the behavior happened in a situation where the stated value was actually tested. That's what makes it relevant evidence. It's the same logic as the warranty. Saying "I value honesty" costs nothing. Staying calm when honesty stings costs something. And it costs more for someone who doesn't really mean it. Here's a question for you. Whose stated values have you actually seen tested? And whose have you only heard? Now let's look at what happens when you bring in someone else to check the claim.
