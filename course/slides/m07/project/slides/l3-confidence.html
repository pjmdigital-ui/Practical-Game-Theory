Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

This deserves special attention. Human beings are easily influenced by confidence. Someone speaks decisively. They make eye contact. They never hesitate. They sound certain. And we interpret all of that as "They know what they're talking about." Maybe they do. But confidence is not evidence of accuracy. And here's the part people miss. Confidence itself can be strategic. Look at the three cards. A negotiator may sound completely certain because uncertainty would weaken their position. A salesperson may speak emphatically because hesitation would reduce the chance of a purchase. A leader may project certainty because they believe the group needs confidence. In every one of those cases the confidence is a response to incentives. It isn't a readout of what they know. None of that makes the underlying claim true or false. It just means the confidence isn't telling you much. And the reverse happens too. Someone can be awkward. Someone can be nervous. Someone can be poor at explaining themselves. And they can still be completely right. If you reward polish and punish awkwardness you'll systematically listen to the wrong people. So here's the mental habit. Separate the delivery of a message from the evidence behind the message. When someone sounds very sure, ask yourself one quiet question. What would they sound like if they weren't sure but needed me to believe them? Often the answer is exactly the same. And confidence is only one of the ways a message can move you without informing you.
