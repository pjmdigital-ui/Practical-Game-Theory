Credibility is not confidence

Don't confuse the delivery of a message with the evidence behind it.

Certainty and eye contact aren't evidence of accuracy. Confidence can itself be strategic, and a nervous, awkward person can be completely correct.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

This deserves special attention, because human beings are easily influenced by confidence. Someone speaks decisively. They make eye contact. They never hesitate. They sound certain. And we interpret that as: they know what they're talking about. Maybe they do. But confidence is not evidence of accuracy. And confidence itself can be strategic. A negotiator may sound completely certain because uncertainty would weaken their position. A salesperson may speak emphatically because hesitation would reduce the chance of a purchase. A leader may project certainty because they believe the group needs it. None of that makes the claim true or false. And the reverse happens too: someone can be awkward, nervous, poor at explaining themselves, and still be completely right. So separate the delivery of the message from the evidence behind it.
