Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Your tool for this module is the Credibility Test. It's in your workbook. Use it for high-stakes claims. Not for directions to the restroom. Start by writing the claim exactly as they said it. Then write down the decision of yours it affects. That second line matters. It reminds you why the claim deserves the work. Then go through the eight questions. One. Do they know? Are they actually in a position to have this information? Two. What do they want me to believe? Name the message plainly. Three. What happens if I believe them? How does my response affect their payoff? Four. Would they say this even if it were false? If yes, the words alone may not be very informative. Five. What evidence supports the claim? Here I want you to split it in two. What's assertion and what's verification? Six. What does their behavior suggest? Treat it as evidence. Not proof. Seven. What does their history suggest? Reputation can matter a lot. And eight. What would make this claim costly to fake? That's where you look for genuine signals. You won't always have answers to all eight. That's fine. The blanks are information too. If you can't answer "Do they know?" then you've found the weak link. Here's how I'd suggest using it the first few times. Do it on paper. It feels slow at first. After a few weeks it becomes instinct. And then you'll run it in your head in the middle of a conversation. The workbook has three more parts. Here they are.
