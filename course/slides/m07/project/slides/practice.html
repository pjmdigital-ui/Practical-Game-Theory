Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Let's practice. Dana runs a small marketing agency. She's choosing a web developer for a large client project. Two candidates. First there's Marcus. He's polished and energetic. He says "I've never missed a deadline. And I'm the best React developer in this city." He never hesitates. He has a beautiful slide deck. And he offers ten percent off if Dana signs this week. When she asks for references he says past clients are under NDA. "But trust me. They were thrilled." Then there's Priya. She's quieter and a bit awkward. She says "I think I can hit your timeline. But the payment integration is the risky part. I'd want a week to scope it." She sends three live sites she built. She names two former clients Dana can call. And she proposes milestone payments tied to working deliverables. One more voice. Dana's business partner knows Marcus socially. The partner says "Marcus is a great guy. He'd never lie to you." Notice how this scenario is built. Marcus wins on delivery. Priya wins on something else. And the partner adds a third kind of claim to sort out. So here are your four questions. One. Apply "Would they say this anyway?" to Marcus's two main claims. Two. Which parts of each pitch are cheap talk? And which are signals or verification? Three. Separate honesty and knowledge and incentives in the partner's endorsement. Four. What should Dana do next without becoming cynical? Pause the video now and write your answers down.
