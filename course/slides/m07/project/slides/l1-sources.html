Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

So where does credibility come from? A statement doesn't exist by itself. Its credibility depends on things we've already learned to notice in this course. Start with payoffs. What does the speaker gain if I believe this? Then information. Are they actually in a position to know? Then history. What happened when they made similar claims before? Then constraints. What prevents them from behaving differently later? We'll go deep on that one in Module Eight. Then evidence. Can the claim be independently verified? Then signals. Does their behavior contain information related to the claim? And finally reputation. What does their previous behavior suggest? Now go back to "Trust me. I'll take care of this." From your partner of fifteen years the history card is full. So is the reputation card. From the stranger asking for a wire every card is empty. All you have is the sentence. That's the whole point. The sentence was identical. The game around it was not. This is a much richer way of evaluating communication than asking "Did they sound convincing?" Here's a question for you. Think of a claim someone made to you this week that really mattered. How many of these seven cards could you actually fill in? If the answer is one or two, you were mostly trusting the sentence.
