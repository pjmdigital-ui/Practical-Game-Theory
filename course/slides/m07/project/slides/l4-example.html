Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Imagine two contractors. Both say "We stand behind our work." Contractor A requires full payment upfront. Provides no references. Offers no warranty. And disappears after the job. Contractor B has hundreds of verifiable reviews. Provides a written warranty. Has operated in the same community for twenty years. And fixes problems at their own expense. The verbal claim is identical. The surrounding signals are very different. You probably don't need any game theory training to feel the difference. You'd hire B. What game theory does is help us understand why. Contractor B has created evidence that would be harder to maintain if their claim were repeatedly false. That makes the information more diagnostic. Walk through it with the tools from this lesson. Would A say "We stand behind our work" anyway? Of course. It costs nothing. What about the warranty? A contractor who cuts corners would keep paying to fix things. So a warranty is far cheaper for someone whose work really is good. The reviews are hard to build up over years if the work is bad. And twenty years in one community is reputation. It's a summary of a lot of previous rounds. Now flip it. What does A's setup tell you? Full payment upfront moves all the risk onto you. Disappearing after the job means there's no next round to protect. Notice that A might still be a fine contractor. You just have no way to tell. Here's a question for you. What would you need to see before you'd hire A? Now let's look at the flip side. Behavior that destroys a stated claim.
