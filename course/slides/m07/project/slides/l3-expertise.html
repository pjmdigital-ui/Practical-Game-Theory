Knowledge and alignment are independent

Expertise without alignment

A salesperson for a complex financial product knows far more than you, and earns a large commission if you buy.

Alignment without expertise

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Let's take the two cases where those questions come apart. Expertise without alignment: you're buying a complicated financial product. The salesperson knows far more about it than you do, so the knowledge advantage is theirs. But they also receive a large commission if you buy, so incentive alignment is uncertain. That doesn't mean ignore the salesperson. They may be extremely useful. It means separate "How much do they know?" from "How much does their payoff depend on what I decide?" Now reverse it. Alignment without expertise. Your brother genuinely wants what's best for you. You trust him completely. But he knows nothing about the medical decision you're considering. So don't ask "Do I trust my brother?" and think you've solved it. Ask: trust him to do what? To want the best for you? Probably. To diagnose a complex condition? Maybe not.
