Example · Same words. Different game.

"Turn left."

The situation

We're driving to an unfamiliar place. You can see the GPS. I can't. You say "Turn left."

What happened

If we share a destination the words are highly informative. If we're racing to get there first I have to ask what you gain if I believe you.

The lesson

The words cost nothing either way. Their meaning depends on whether our interests line up.

Communication didn't change. The game did.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's why cheap talk can work. Imagine you and I are driving to an unfamiliar location. You can see the GPS. I can't. You say "Turn left." Those words cost you nothing. Yet they're highly informative. Why? Because we both want to arrive at the same destination. Our interests are aligned. You gain nothing by sending me the wrong way. In fact you lose. You're in the car too. So I turn left without a second thought. And I'm right to. Now change the game. We're in separate cars. We're competing to reach the destination first. You say "Turn left." Same words. Different strategic meaning. Now I have to consider something new. What do you gain if I believe you? Maybe you're still telling me the truth. Some people would. But I can't treat your words the way I did a minute ago. Notice what happened there. The communication didn't change at all. Not one word. The game did. And that's exactly why you can't judge a sentence without looking at the game it was spoken inside. This shows up everywhere. A teammate who shares your bonus tells you the client is happy. A rival for the same promotion tells you the client is happy. Same sentence. You should hold it differently. Here's a question for you. Who in your life is in the same car as you? And who just looks like they are? Here's the takeaway.
