Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Game theorists have studied this problem formally. Imagine two players. One knows something the other doesn't. We call the informed player the sender, and the other one the receiver. The sender communicates a message, and the receiver then makes a decision. But here's the important part: the sender cares about what decision the receiver makes. Now communication becomes strategic. Vincent Crawford and Joel Sobel's classic model of strategic information transmission formalized exactly this situation. One of its central insights is that the amount of information that can be credibly transmitted depends partly on how closely the two players' interests are aligned. That's an enormously useful idea, because it tells us you shouldn't evaluate a message without considering the sender's incentives.
