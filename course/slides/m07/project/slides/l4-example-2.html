Example · "Challenge me"

The manager who wants honest feedback

The situation

A manager tells the team: "I want everyone to challenge me when you think I'm wrong."

What happened

An employee disagrees in a meeting. The manager gets angry. Later that employee is left off an important project.

The lesson

Next month the manager asks again. People believe the observed consequence. Not the words.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's the flip side. Imagine a manager tells the team "I want everyone to challenge me when you think I'm wrong." Great. An employee takes them at their word. They disagree with the manager during a meeting. The manager becomes angry. Later that employee gets excluded from an important project. The following month the manager says it again. "I really want more honest feedback." What do employees believe? Probably not the words. Because another piece of information has entered the game. The observed consequence of disagreement. Here's the part I find most interesting. The manager may sincerely want honest feedback. They may not even connect the anger and the project decision. But their behavior has created a different incentive structure. Their actions are communicating something their words aren't. Disagreement may be costly. And everyone on that team now has to decide whether to speak up. They're going to decide based on the payoff they saw. Not the invitation they heard. This is where credibility connects back to payoffs. You can't talk your way out of this one. The manager could give a beautiful speech about psychological safety. It wouldn't matter much. What would matter is the next time someone disagrees. If that person is rewarded the game starts to change. So here's a question for you. If you lead anyone, what have your reactions taught people about the cost of telling you the truth? Let's bring this lesson together.
