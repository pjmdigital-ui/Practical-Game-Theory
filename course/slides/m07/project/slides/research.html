Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Two research spotlights this module. The first is cheap talk. Crawford and Sobel modeled a better-informed sender talking to a receiver whose later action affects them both. In their model, how much information can be credibly passed depends on how aligned the players' interests are, and informative communication can still happen when interests partly conflict. Farrell and Rabin reviewed the literature and argued cheap talk is neither automatically truthful nor automatically meaningless. And in Cox and Stoddard's experiment, players with private information could send costless messages before contributing to a public good. Communication was largely truthful and improved efficiency. When the incentive to exaggerate increased, truthfulness declined and the benefits shrank. What it doesn't prove: that people always lie when interests conflict, that costless words are meaningless, or that aligned interests guarantee the truth.
