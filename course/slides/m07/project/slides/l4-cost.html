Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's an important mistake to avoid. People hear the idea of costly signaling. Then they conclude "If someone pays a large cost they must be telling the truth." No. Imagine a terrible company spends twenty million dollars on advertising. The advertising was costly. That doesn't prove the product is good. A person can spend a fortune creating an image. A dishonest company can have an impressive office. Someone can make a dramatic gesture for strategic reasons. All of those cost something. None of them proves the claim. So what's missing? Cost matters only if the structure of the signal helps distinguish between different underlying types. The cost has to fall differently on the true type and the false type. Here's the better question. Why would this signal be easier, cheaper or more worthwhile for someone whose claim is true than for someone whose claim is false? That's the deeper logic of signaling. Go back to the contractor idea for a second. A written warranty is a good signal. Why? Because a contractor who does sloppy work will pay out on that warranty again and again. A contractor who does good work rarely pays at all. Same promise. Very different cost depending on the truth. Compare that to a flashy truck with the company logo on it. Expensive? Sure. But a bad contractor can buy one just as easily as a good one. So here's what I want you to ask. Not "How expensive was it?" Not "How dramatic was it?" But "Who could afford to fake this?"
