A common mistake

Cost alone doesn't make a signal credible.

A terrible company can spend $20 million on advertising. Ask why the signal would be easier, cheaper or more worthwhile for someone whose claim is true.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's an important mistake to avoid. People hear the idea of costly signaling and conclude: if someone pays a large cost, they must be telling the truth. No. Imagine a terrible company spends twenty million dollars on advertising. The advertising was costly. That doesn't prove the product is good. A person can spend a fortune creating an image. A dishonest company can have an impressive office. Someone can make a dramatic gesture for strategic reasons. Cost matters only if the structure of the signal helps distinguish between different underlying types. So the better question is: why would this signal be easier, cheaper, or more worthwhile for someone whose claim is true than for someone whose claim is false? That's the deeper logic of signaling.
