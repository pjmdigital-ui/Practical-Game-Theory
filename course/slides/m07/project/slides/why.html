Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Imagine someone says "Trust me." What information did you actually receive? Very little. The words tell you the person wants you to trust them. They don't tell you whether you should. Now picture two situations. Your longtime business partner has kept every major commitment to you for fifteen years. They say "Trust me. I'll take care of this." Then a stranger you're about to wire fifty thousand dollars to says exactly the same thing. Same words. Completely different credibility. Why? Because credibility isn't contained inside the sentence. It comes from the game surrounding the sentence. History. Incentives. Evidence. Knowledge. Reputation. Consequences. Available verification. What the person stands to gain. What they stand to lose. That's what this module trains. One habit. Separating what someone says from what makes it credible. It replaces the question "Do I believe them?" with a better one. "What makes this believable?" And it turns the lens on you too. Maybe you've been in this spot. You were telling the truth and the other person still didn't believe you. Your first instinct was probably to find better words. Often the wording isn't the problem. The other person may simply have no good reason to believe you yet. We'll work on that in the last lesson.
