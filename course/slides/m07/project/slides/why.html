Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Imagine someone says, "Trust me." What information did you actually receive? Very little. The words tell you the person wants you to trust them. They don't tell you whether you should. Now picture two situations. Your longtime business partner, who has kept every major commitment to you for fifteen years, says, "Trust me. I'll take care of this." And a stranger you're about to wire fifty thousand dollars to says exactly the same thing. Same words. Completely different credibility. Why? Because credibility isn't contained inside the sentence. It comes from the game surrounding it: history, incentives, evidence, knowledge, reputation, consequences, verification, what the person stands to gain and what they stand to lose. This module trains one habit: separating what someone says from what makes it credible. And it turns the lens on you, too, because when you're telling the truth and still not being believed, the problem often isn't your wording.
