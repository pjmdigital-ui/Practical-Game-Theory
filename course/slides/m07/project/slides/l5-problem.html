Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

So far we've mostly looked at credibility from the listener's side. Now let's flip it. Suppose you're the one communicating something true. This matters a lot. Because when we aren't believed our instinct is almost always the same. We reach for better words. We polish the pitch. We add a story. We handle every objection. We get more emphatic. And sometimes the other person still doesn't move. At that point most people conclude "I need a more persuasive argument." Maybe not. What if you've missed the real problem? Here's the test. Did the other person understand you? If they misunderstood you then yes, work on the message. Clarity is a real problem and better words can fix it. But if they understood you perfectly and still don't believe you, more rhetoric may accomplish nothing. You've got a credibility problem. Not a message problem. And those need completely different tools. The strategic question isn't "How do I say this better?" It's "How do I make this claim more credible?" Those are very different problems. One is about the sentence. The other is about the game around the sentence. Everything we've learned in this module applies to you as a speaker. Your incentives. Your history. Your evidence. Whether you'd say this anyway. Here's a question for you. Think of a time you weren't believed. Did they misunderstand you? Or did they understand you and just not buy it?
