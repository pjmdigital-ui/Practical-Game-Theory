Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Michael Spence's work on signaling was recognized as part of the 2001 Nobel Prize in economics. It addressed a fundamental problem created by asymmetric information. How can someone who holds private information communicate something meaningful about themselves? Especially to someone who can't directly observe it? Spence's famous example involved education and employment. Employers can't perfectly observe an applicant's underlying productivity before hiring them. The applicant knows more about themselves than the employer does. And every applicant will say they're productive. So words won't solve it. Education can function as a signal under certain conditions. It can help employers distinguish among workers. I want to be careful here. The claim is not that a degree proves you're good. The claim is narrower. Under certain conditions an observable action can carry information about something hidden. For us the broader idea is what matters. Sometimes behavior carries information that words alone do not. That's the jump from the last slide. Words like "I'm hardworking" are available to everyone. Behavior that's harder to produce when the claim is false is not available to everyone. That's what makes it informative. Here's a question for you. In your work, what's the equivalent of the work sample? What could someone show you that a weaker candidate or vendor couldn't easily show? Once you know that, you know what to ask for.
