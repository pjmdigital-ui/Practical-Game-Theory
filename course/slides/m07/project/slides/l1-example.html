Example · One stranger. Two games.

Directions versus a price

The situation

You're in a city you don't know. You ask a stranger the way to the train station. Later you're negotiating to buy their car.

What happened

"Two blocks east." Their payoff barely depends on it. Then: "The best offer so far is $20,000." That answer affects what they receive.

The lesson

The price claim isn't a lie. It's just weaker evidence than the directions. You have less reason to update.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's how that plays out. You're visiting a city you've never been to. You ask a stranger which way the train station is. They say "Two blocks east." Why would they lie? Maybe they could. But under normal circumstances their payoff doesn't depend much on whether you walk east or west. You want accurate directions. They have little reason to give you bad ones. Your interests are basically aligned. So you take the words at face value and you start walking. Now imagine you're negotiating to buy that same person's car. You ask "What's the most anyone else has offered you?" They say "Twenty thousand dollars." Should you treat that exactly like the directions to the train station? Probably not. Why? Because now the answer affects how much money they may receive. Their incentives are different. Same person. Same honesty. Different game. And that does not mean they're lying. That's very important. It means the statement alone gives you less reason to update than it would if their incentives were neutral. So what do you do with it? You don't call them a liar. You just don't let that one sentence carry the whole weight of your offer. You look for something else. How long has the car been listed? What are similar cars selling for? Is there another buyer you can actually see? Here's a question for you. Where in your life do you treat a price claim like a set of directions? Let's lock in the takeaway.
