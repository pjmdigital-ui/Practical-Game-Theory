Example · One stranger. Two games.

Directions versus a price

The situation

You're in a city you don't know. You ask a stranger the way to the train station. Later you're negotiating to buy their car.

What happened

"Two blocks east." Their payoff barely depends on it. Then: "The best offer so far is $20,000." That answer affects what they receive.

The lesson

The price claim isn't a lie. It's just weaker evidence than the directions. You have less reason to update.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

You ask a stranger which way the train station is. They say "Two blocks east." Their payoff doesn't depend much on whether you walk east or west. So you take the words at face value. Now imagine you're negotiating to buy that same person's car. You ask what anyone else has offered. They say "Twenty thousand dollars." Now the answer affects how much money they may receive. Same person. Different game. That does not mean they're lying. It means the statement alone gives you less reason to update. Let's lock in the takeaway.
