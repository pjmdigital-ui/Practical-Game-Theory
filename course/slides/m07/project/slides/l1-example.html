Example · Same stranger, two games

Directions versus a price

"Two blocks east."

You ask the way to the train station. Their payoff barely depends on which way you walk, so the words are informative.

"Best offer so far: $20,000."

Now they're selling you their car, and the answer affects what they receive. Not a lie. Just less reason to update.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's how that plays out. You're visiting a city you've never been to, and you ask a stranger which way the train station is. They say, "Two blocks east." Why would they lie? Maybe they could. But under normal circumstances their payoff doesn't depend much on whether you walk east or west. You want accurate directions, and they have little reason to give you bad ones. Now imagine you're negotiating to buy that same person's car. You ask, "What's the most anyone else has offered you?" They say, "Twenty thousand dollars." Should you treat that exactly like the directions to the train station? Probably not, because now the answer affects how much money they may receive. That does not mean they're lying. It means the statement alone gives you less reason to update than it would if their incentives were neutral.
