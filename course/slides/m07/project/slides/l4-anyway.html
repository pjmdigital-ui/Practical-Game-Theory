Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

This may be one of the most useful questions in the whole module. Suppose a job candidate tells you, "I'm extremely hardworking." Ask: would an unmotivated candidate also have an incentive to say that? Of course. Almost everyone benefits from saying it, so the statement doesn't distinguish very well between hardworking and non-hardworking candidates. Now suppose the candidate gives you a record of completed projects, references from previous employers, years of observable performance, a demanding work sample completed successfully. Those may be much more informative, because they're harder for different types of candidates to produce equally. That's the move from cheap talk to signaling. Michael Spence's work on signaling, recognized in the 2001 Nobel Prize in economics, showed how behavior can carry information that words alone do not.
