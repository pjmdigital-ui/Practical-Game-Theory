If everyone would say it anyway, the words can't tell the cases apart

Easy for anyone to say

"I'm extremely hardworking." An unmotivated candidate has every reason to say it too.

Hard to produce equally

A record of completed projects. References from past employers. Years of observable performance. A demanding work sample.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Would they say this anyway? A job candidate tells you "I'm extremely hardworking." Would an unmotivated candidate also have an incentive to say that? Of course. So the statement doesn't distinguish between them. The sentence just can't show you. Now suppose they give you a record of completed projects. References. Those are harder for different types of candidates to produce equally. A lazy candidate can't easily produce five years of finished projects. Information lives in the difference between what the true type can do and what the false type can do. This takes us to signaling.
