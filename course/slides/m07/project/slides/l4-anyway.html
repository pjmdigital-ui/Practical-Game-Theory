Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

This may be one of the most useful questions in the whole module. Would they say this anyway? Suppose a job candidate tells you "I'm extremely hardworking." Ask yourself one thing. Would an unmotivated candidate also have an incentive to say that? Of course. Almost everyone benefits from saying "I'm hardworking." So the statement doesn't distinguish very well between hardworking and non-hardworking candidates. Notice I'm not saying the candidate is lying. They may be the hardest worker you'll ever hire. The point is that the sentence can't show you that. Both types say it. So hearing it doesn't move you much. Now suppose the candidate gives you something else. A record of completed projects. References from previous employers. Years of observable performance. A demanding work sample completed successfully. Those may be much more informative. Why? Because they're harder for different types of candidates to produce equally. A lazy candidate can say "hardworking" just as easily as a diligent one. A lazy candidate can't easily produce five years of finished projects. That's the key. Information lives in the difference between what the true type can do and what the false type can do. This question works everywhere. "Our product is the best on the market." Would they say this anyway? Yes. "I'm committed to this relationship." Would they say this anyway? Often yes. Try it on the next three claims you hear. This takes us from cheap talk to signaling.
