Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

There's one more distinction we need before the next module. Suppose I tell you "I will not accept less than one hundred thousand dollars." What makes that statement credible? Maybe my history. Maybe my alternatives. Maybe my incentives. Maybe nothing at all. Right now it's mostly cheap talk. Now suppose I take an action that makes it impossible to accept less. Or at least extremely costly for me to accept less. Something has changed. I haven't simply signaled what I'm likely to do. I've changed what I can rationally do later. That's commitment. The difference is subtle but important. Signaling asks "What does my action tell you about me?" It's about revealing something hidden. My type. My quality. My confidence. Commitment asks "How does my action change my future options?" It's about removing choices so the other person can predict what I'll do. Sometimes one action does both. The contractor's written warranty tells you something about the quality of their work. That's a signal. It also locks them into fixing problems later. That's a commitment. But they're not the same idea. And it's worth keeping them apart in your head. When you're trying to be believed about who you are, think signals. When you're trying to be believed about what you'll do, think commitment. Module Eight is entirely about the second one. It's where we'll see why sometimes having fewer options makes you stronger.
