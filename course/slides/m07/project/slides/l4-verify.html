Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Sometimes the solution is verification. Suppose I tell you "The roof is in excellent condition." You're buying my house. I benefit from you believing me. You could trust my statement. Or you could hire an independent inspector. Now you've changed the information structure. The inspector doesn't eliminate all uncertainty. Inspectors miss things. But you've brought in verification from a player whose incentives may be better aligned with an accurate assessment. Here's a second one. Imagine I tell you "I'm one of the best surgeons in the country." Maybe I am. But I have an obvious incentive to make that claim. Now imagine independent medical boards support it. Peer-reviewed outcomes support it. Hospital credentials and other surgeons support it. The claim has changed. Not because my sentence changed. Because the information environment changed. That's why the things along the bottom of this slide exist. Audits. Certifications. Inspections. References. Independent reviews. Third-party measurements. They aren't merely additional words. They alter the credibility structure. They help reduce uncertainty when people can't simply rely on self-report. And here's something worth noticing. Whole industries exist to solve this exact problem. Reputation systems. Professional licenses. Guarantees. Somebody realized that "trust me" wasn't enough and built a way to check. So when a claim matters, ask a simple question. Is there a third party who could check this? And is it worth the cost to ask them? Next we'll look at the verification that builds up over time.
