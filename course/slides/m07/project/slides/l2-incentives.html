Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

This isn't only theory. Caleb Cox and Brock Stoddard ran an experiment on exactly this. Players received private information about whether contributing to a public good would be valuable. Before deciding they could send costless messages to one another. There was a tension built into the game. The group benefited from honest information. But individual players sometimes had an incentive to exaggerate. So what happened? Communication was largely truthful. And it improved efficiency. People used cheap talk well. Then the researchers increased the incentive to exaggerate. Truthfulness declined. The benefits of communication got smaller. That result is almost a perfect illustration of the principle on this slide. Credibility is partly a property of incentives. People didn't suddenly become different human beings. The payoff for exaggerating changed. And communication changed with it. I love this finding because it cuts against both extremes we just talked about. The cynic would predict lies from the start. That's not what happened. The naive view would predict honesty no matter what. That's not what happened either. People were mostly honest. And their honesty bent when the incentives bent. So here's how to use it. When you hear a claim, ask how much the speaker gains from moving your decision. Small gain? The words probably carry a lot. Big gain? The words carry less. Not zero. Less.
