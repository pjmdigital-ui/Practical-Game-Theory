What the lab shows

Credibility is partly a property of incentives.

In Cox and Stoddard's experiment, communication was largely truthful. When the incentive to exaggerate rose, truthfulness declined.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

This isn't only theory. Caleb Cox and Brock Stoddard ran an experiment in which players received private information about whether contributing to a public good would be valuable. Before deciding, they could send costless messages to one another. There was a tension built into the game: the group benefited from honest information, but individual players sometimes had an incentive to exaggerate. What happened? Communication was largely truthful, and it improved efficiency. Then the researchers increased the incentive to exaggerate, and truthfulness declined, and the benefits of communication got smaller. People didn't suddenly become different human beings. The payoff for exaggerating changed, and communication changed with it. That's what I mean when I say credibility is partly a property of incentives.
