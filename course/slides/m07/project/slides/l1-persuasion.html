Two different things

Persuasive and credible are two separate questions

How convincing was the message?

Charisma. Confidence. Articulate delivery. Emotional pull.

How credible is the underlying claim?

Knowledge. Incentives. Evidence. History. Verification.

A message can be persuasive without being credible. It can be credible without being persuasive.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's the first big idea. Persuasion and credibility are not the same thing. A message can be extremely persuasive and still not be very credible. And a message can be highly credible without being persuasive at all. Persuasiveness is about the delivery. Was the person charismatic? Confident? Articulate? Emotionally compelling? None of those qualities tells you whether what they're saying is accurate. Credibility is about the claim underneath. Does the person know? What do they gain if you believe them? Is there evidence? Can anyone check it? The reverse happens too. Someone can be awkward and nervous. They can be poor at explaining themselves. And they can still be completely correct. So I want you to keep two questions apart. How convincing was the message? And how credible is the underlying claim? Mixing them up is behind a tremendous number of bad decisions. Think about the last time you bought something because the pitch was great. Was the pitch evidence? Or was it just a great pitch? There's a related trap we'll come back to in Lesson Three. People confuse "It changed my mind" with "It gave me good reason to change my mind." Those aren't the same thing. So if credibility doesn't come from the delivery, where does it come from?
