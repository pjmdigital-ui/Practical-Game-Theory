Two different things

A message can be persuasive without being credible, and credible without being persuasive.

Credibility comes from history, incentives, evidence, knowledge, reputation, consequences and verification.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

That's why persuasion and credibility are not the same thing. A message can be extremely persuasive and still not be very credible. And a message can be highly credible without being persuasive at all. Persuasiveness is about the delivery: how convincing it felt, how confident, how charismatic, how emotionally compelling. Credibility is about the game the sentence was spoken inside. What's the speaker's history? What are their incentives? What evidence exists? Are they in a position to know? What's their reputation? What happens to them if they're wrong? Can the claim be checked? Keep those two questions apart. "How convincing was the message?" and "How credible is the underlying claim?" Mixing them up is behind a tremendous number of bad decisions.
