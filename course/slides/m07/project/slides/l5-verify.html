Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's the constructive principle. When you're communicating truthfully, make the truth easier for the other person to evaluate. That may mean evidence, demonstrations, references, guarantees, documentation, independent verification, and consistency between your words and your behavior. Not because every truthful claim has to be proven, but because sometimes the listener's skepticism is strategically reasonable, especially when you benefit from being believed. One distinction before Module Eight. Signaling asks, "What does my action tell you about me?" Commitment asks, "How does my action change my future options?" Sometimes one action does both, but they're different ideas, and next module is all about the second. And the ethical line: this module could easily become "how to make yourself look credible." That would miss the point. Fake reviews, fabricated evidence, staged signals: that's not strategic excellence. It's deception.
