Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's the constructive principle for this whole lesson. When you're communicating truthfully, make the truth easier for the other person to evaluate. What does that look like? It may mean evidence. Something they can check for themselves. It may mean a demonstration. Show it working instead of describing it. It may mean references. People they can call who aren't you. It may mean a guarantee. You put something behind the claim. It may mean documentation. A record they can review in their own time. It may mean independent verification. And it always means consistency between your words and your behavior. Notice that every one of these moves the claim away from your word alone. That's the point. Your word is the one thing the other person can't check. Now why should you bother? Not because every truthful claim has to be proven. That would be exhausting. You don't need a certificate to tell your friend where you parked. But sometimes the listener's skepticism is strategically reasonable. That's especially true when you benefit from being believed. In those moments a little evidence goes a long way. And here's a nice side effect. When you offer verification before anyone asks, you're sending a signal. Someone who's bluffing doesn't usually volunteer an inspection. Someone telling the truth can afford to. Here's a question for you. Pick one claim you'll need someone to believe this month.
