Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

In Module Six we briefly met a term game theorists use: cheap talk. It sounds dismissive, but technically it has a specific meaning. Cheap talk is communication that is generally costless and nonbinding. The sender can say something without the message itself directly changing the material structure of the game. The temptation is to assume: if talk is cheap, talk is meaningless. But that isn't what the research says. Joseph Farrell and Matthew Rabin reviewed the game-theory literature on cheap talk and argued that both common extremes are wrong. Costless communication does not automatically produce efficient truth-telling. But neither is it necessarily meaningless. Under the right incentive structure, ordinary talk can transmit substantial information.
