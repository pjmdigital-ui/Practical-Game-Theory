Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

In Module Six we briefly met a term game theorists use. Cheap talk. It sounds dismissive. But technically it has a specific meaning. Cheap talk is communication that is generally costless and nonbinding. The sender can say something without the message itself directly changing the material structure of the game. Think about what that means. When I tell you "I'll have it done by Friday" nothing about the world changes when I say it. My options are the same. My payoffs are the same. I haven't put up money. I haven't signed anything. I've just said words. Most of what we say every day is cheap talk in this sense. A recommendation. A status update. A compliment. A promise made over coffee. Directions to the train station. So here's the temptation. If talk is cheap then talk must be meaningless. Why would costless words ever tell you anything? I want you to notice that this is the cynic's position. And it doesn't survive contact with real life. You rely on cheap talk constantly and it works most of the time. You ask a coworker where the meeting is. They tell you. You go there. It's the right room. So the question isn't whether cheap talk can be informative. It obviously can. The question is when.
