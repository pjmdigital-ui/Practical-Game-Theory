Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's one of the hardest things for people to accept. You know you're telling the truth. That doesn't mean the other person should automatically believe you. You say "But I'm telling the truth!" Fine. How would they know? Look at it from their position. There are at least four reasons a perfectly reasonable person might hold back. First. You may have an incentive to say it. They can see that you benefit from being believed. Remember the job candidate saying "I'm hardworking." From the outside your true claim can look exactly like someone else's false one. Second. They may lack access to your information. You know the transmission is fine because you've driven the car for years. They haven't. Third. They may have been misled before. Maybe not by you. But someone said these exact words to them once and it cost them. Fourth. They may have competing evidence. Someone else told them something different. Or they saw something that doesn't fit your story. None of those reasons is an insult to you. Each one is just the game they're sitting in. So instead of becoming frustrated, ask a much more useful question. What reason do they have to believe me? Then go through the four cards. Which one is the obstacle? If it's your incentive, bring in someone who doesn't share it. If it's their lack of information, show them what you see. If they've been burned, give them a way to check.
