Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

This module could easily become "How to make yourself look credible." That would miss the entire point. We're not trying to manufacture the appearance of truth. Our ethical standard is the same one we've used all along. Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice. So here's the line. If you're the speaker, the goal is to make truthful claims easier to evaluate. If you're the listener, the goal is to evaluate important claims intelligently. Fake reviews are not strategic excellence. Neither is fabricated evidence. Neither are staged signals. Neither is deliberately exploiting someone's trust. That's deception. Game theory can explain why deception sometimes works. That doesn't make it ethical. Now there's a second warning. And it goes the other way. Once you learn to analyze incentives it can become easy to distrust everything. Someone compliments you and you think "What do they want?" Someone helps you and you think "What's their angle?" Someone makes a promise and you think "They're strategically signaling." That's not the goal. Most human communication is not some elaborate deception game. People cooperate. People tell the truth. People help each other. People speak honestly even when lying might benefit them. So the mental model is not "Trust nobody." It's "Don't confuse trusting someone with failing to think." You can trust and still understand the game. Here's a question for you. Which way are you more likely to drift after this module? Toward naive or toward cynical?
