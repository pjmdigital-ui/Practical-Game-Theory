Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Imagine you're selling your car. You know the transmission is perfectly fine. The buyer asks, "Any transmission problems?" You say, "None." And then you get offended when they want a mechanic to inspect it. From your side: why don't they trust me? From theirs: the seller knows more about this car than I do and benefits from the sale. Both perspectives make sense. The inspection isn't necessarily an accusation. It's a response to an information asymmetry. Now the client. You explain beautifully that you'll deliver on time. You tell a story, you handle every objection, and they still don't believe you. Maybe the problem is that you were late on the last three projects. They understand your words perfectly. They just don't believe them. A better story won't fix that. Milestones they can check might.
