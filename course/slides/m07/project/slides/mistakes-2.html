Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here are four more. Fifth. Treating cheap talk as meaningless. This is the cynic's version of the first mistake. When interests align, costless words are highly informative. Directions to the train station. "Turn left" from the person in your car. If you throw those away you're not being smart. You're just losing information. Sixth. Reducing credibility to "Are they honest?" Ask about honesty and knowledge and incentives separately. The sincere friend with the investment tip passes the honesty test and fails the knowledge test. The brother passes on alignment and fails on medicine. So always ask "Trust them to do what?" Seventh. Assuming any costly signal is credible. A twenty million dollar ad campaign doesn't prove a product is good. An impressive office doesn't prove a company is honest. Cost only matters if it falls harder on someone whose claim is false. Ask why the signal would be easier for someone telling the truth. And eighth. Being offended by verification. This is the speaker's mistake. Someone asks for an inspection or a reference and it stings. But an inspection is a response to an information asymmetry. It isn't an accusation. The better move is to welcome the check. Or offer it before they ask. Notice that these eight mistakes come in pairs. Some push you toward being naive. Some push you toward being cynical. The goal sits between them. Look at the game.
