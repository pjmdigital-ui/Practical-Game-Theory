Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Suppose you've known someone for fifteen years. They've consistently kept their word. They've shown up. They've honored agreements. They've told you uncomfortable truths. They've admitted mistakes. Now they make a claim you can't immediately verify. Their history matters. Why? Because history gives you evidence about how this person behaves across situations. Reputation is in one sense a summary of information from previous rounds of the game. We'll explore that much more deeply when we reach The Shadow of the Future. For now remember two things. Credibility can accumulate. And it can collapse. In fact it can collapse faster than it builds. Imagine someone has told you the truth a hundred times. Then you catch them fabricating something important. What happens? You don't merely update your belief about that one statement. You may update your model of the person. Earlier claims get reconsidered. Future claims may need verification. The whole informational structure of the relationship has changed. That's why trust is so strategically valuable. When trust is high people don't need to verify everything. Transactions get easier. Communication gets faster. Cooperation needs less monitoring. Destroy credibility and all of those costs come back. So here's a question for you. Look at the relationships where you never have to prove anything. What did it take to build that? And what would it take to lose it?
