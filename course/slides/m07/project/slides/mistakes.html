Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

A few mistakes I see all the time. First, treating "they benefit from saying it" as "it's false." That's paranoia. The right conclusion is that the statement alone is weaker evidence. Second, confusing confidence with evidence. Smooth delivery can be strategic, and an awkward, nervous person can be completely correct. Third, answering a credibility problem with more rhetoric. If they understood you and still don't believe you, make the claim more credible instead of more eloquent. And fourth, becoming cynical. Once you learn to analyze incentives, it's easy to start asking "What's their angle?" every time someone helps you. That's not the goal. Most people cooperate and tell the truth. A few more to watch for: treating cheap talk as meaningless, assuming any costly signal is credible, and being offended when someone asks to verify your claim. The mental model isn't "trust nobody." It's: don't confuse trusting someone with failing to think.
