Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Let me walk you through the mistakes I see most often. There are eight. Here are the first four. First. Treating "they benefit from saying it" as "it's false." That's paranoia. The correct conclusion is that the statement alone is weaker evidence. Remember the car seller and the twenty thousand dollar offer. Not a liar. Just weaker evidence. Second. Confusing confidence with evidence. Smooth delivery can be strategic. The negotiator sounds certain because doubt would cost them. And an awkward or nervous person can be completely correct. Think of Priya in the practice scenario. Third. Answering a credibility problem with more rhetoric. If they understood you and still don't believe you, the words aren't the problem. Make the claim more credible instead of more eloquent. Bring milestones. Bring evidence. Bring someone they can call. And fourth. Becoming cynical. Once you learn to analyze incentives it's easy to start asking "What's their angle?" every time someone helps you. That's not the goal. Most people cooperate and tell the truth. People help each other. People speak honestly even when lying might benefit them. The mental model isn't "Trust nobody." It's "Don't confuse trusting someone with failing to think."
