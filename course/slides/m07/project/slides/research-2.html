Research spotlight · Signaling

Signals inform. Imperfectly.

What it shows

Spence (Nobel Prize, 2001) showed how education can help employers tell workers apart. In Kübler, Müller and Normann's lab tests more productive types invested in education more often. They were paid more.

What it doesn't prove

Actions don't perfectly reveal hidden qualities. A costly action doesn't prove a claim true. Separation was incomplete. Wages didn't match the theory exactly.

Use it: a work sample or a warranty tells you more than self-description.

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

The second spotlight is signaling. Michael Spence showed how education can help employers distinguish among workers under certain conditions. Dorothea Kübler, Wieland Müller and Hans-Theo Normann tested that game in the lab. More productive types invested in education more often. Employers paid higher wages to those who invested. But separation was incomplete. This does not show that observable actions perfectly reveal hidden qualities. And it does not show that a costly action proves a claim. Treat a good signal as strong evidence and never as a guarantee. Now let's build a tool.
