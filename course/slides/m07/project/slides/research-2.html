Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

The second spotlight is signaling. Michael Spence's work, recognized in the 2001 Nobel Prize in economics, showed how education can, under certain conditions, help employers distinguish among workers whose productivity they can't observe directly. Dorothea Kübler, Wieland Müller and Hans-Theo Normann tested versions of that job-market signaling game in the lab. More productive worker types invested in education more often, and employers paid higher wages to those who invested. So the basic mechanism showed up. But separation was incomplete, and wages didn't converge perfectly to the theoretical equilibrium. That's exactly the kind of evidence I want you to hold onto. The theory gives us a lens; real people approximate it imperfectly. Signals contain information. They don't perfectly reveal reality, and a costly action doesn't prove a claim is true.
