Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's another important distinction. A statement can change your behavior even if it isn't credible evidence. Someone shouts "If you don't agree you'll regret it." You may be intimidated. You may comply. But compliance doesn't mean you believed the factual content of the statement. You just didn't want the fight. The same thing happens more quietly all the time. Repetition can build familiarity. Hear a claim enough times and it starts to feel true. Authority can influence behavior. Emotion can influence behavior. Social pressure can influence behavior. None of those is evidence about whether the claim is accurate. So here's the principle. A message can influence without informing. That's important because people often confuse two sentences. "It changed my mind." And "It gave me good reason to change my mind." Those aren't necessarily the same. The first one just reports that something moved you. The second one says the thing that moved you was actually evidence. I'd like you to try a small habit this week. When you notice you've changed your mind about something, stop and ask which of those two sentences is true. What moved you? Was it new information? Or was it pressure? Was it the tenth time you heard it? Was it the person's title? There's no shame in being influenced. Everyone is. The goal is just to know the difference.
