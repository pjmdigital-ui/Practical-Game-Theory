Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

The workbook has three more parts to the Credibility Test. Part Two separates the dimensions. You rate honesty. Then knowledge. Then incentive alignment. High, medium or low. And unknown is a perfectly valid answer. Write down why for each one. This is the "Trust them to do what?" question turned into a worksheet. You'll often find one high score hiding a low one. Your brother scores high on honesty and alignment. He scores low on medical knowledge. Part Three is persuasive versus credible. How convincing did the claim feel? Think about confidence and charisma and emotion. Then ask how credible the underlying claim actually is. Then the question I love most. Did it change my mind? Or did it give me good reason to change my mind? And finally ask what verification is available and whether it's worth getting. Not every claim deserves an inspector. Part Four flips everything. It's for when you're the one making the claim. What reason does the other person have to believe me? Is this a message problem or a credibility problem? And how can I make my truthful claim easier to evaluate? Be honest with yourself on this part. Ask whether someone in your position would say this even if it were false. If the answer is yes, your words alone won't carry it. You'll need to bring something they can check. Let's put the whole test to work on a real scenario.
