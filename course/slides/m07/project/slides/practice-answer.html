Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Here's how I'd read it. One: almost any developer chasing this contract, reliable or not, benefits from saying "never missed a deadline" and "best in the city." That doesn't make Marcus a liar. It means the claims alone give Dana little reason to update. Two: Marcus's pitch is mostly cheap talk delivered with confidence. The deck took effort, but a weak developer could make one just as polished, and the signing discount is pressure, not evidence. Priya offers things that are harder to produce if her claims were false. Milestone pay is far more attractive to someone who expects to deliver, and volunteering the risk works against her short-term interest, which makes her other statements more believable. Three: the partner is probably honest, but "he'd never lie" addresses honesty, not accuracy. Trust him to do what? Four: ask Marcus for a public portfolio or work sample, offer both the same milestone structure, and actually call Priya's references. That's not distrust. It's a reasonable response to an information asymmetry.
