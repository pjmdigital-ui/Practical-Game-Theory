"It's weaker evidence than it looks."

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Now here's where people go wrong as soon as they learn this. They hear "consider the sender's incentives." Then they jump straight to "They benefit from saying it. So it must be false." That's not strategic thinking. That's paranoia. The correct conclusion is more precise. They benefit from me believing this. So the statement by itself gives me less reason to update my belief. It gives me less than it would if their incentives were neutral or aligned with mine. Notice what that does and doesn't say. It doesn't make the claim false. It makes the words on their own weaker evidence. Those are very different conclusions. The first one makes you insulting and cynical. You end up dismissing honest people who happen to benefit from the truth. And plenty of honest people benefit from the truth. A good contractor benefits when you believe their work is good. It also happens to be good. The second conclusion keeps you open. It just tells you to look for something beyond the words. History. Evidence. Verification. That's a much more precise way of thinking. It protects you from the dishonest people without insulting the honest ones. Here's a misunderstanding to watch for in yourself. If you catch yourself saying "Of course they'd say that" and stopping there, you've done half the job. The other half is asking what would actually tell the cases apart.
