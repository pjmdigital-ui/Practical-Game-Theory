Strategic thinking isn't paranoia

From

"They benefit from saying it, so it's false."

To

"It's weaker evidence than it looks."

Internomics · Module 7 — Separate Words From Credibility

Now, here's where people go wrong as soon as they learn this. They hear "consider the sender's incentives" and jump to: "They benefit from saying it, therefore it must be false." That's not strategic thinking. That's paranoia. The correct conclusion is more precise: they benefit from me believing this, so the statement by itself gives me less reason to update my belief than it would if their incentives were neutral or aligned with mine. It doesn't make the claim false. It makes the words, on their own, weaker evidence. That's a much more precise way of thinking, and it keeps you from insulting honest people while still protecting you from the dishonest ones.
