Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's the example for this lesson. Picture a small company. In a meeting everyone agrees. We need to follow up with that customer today. Heads nod. The meeting ends. Tomorrow someone asks, "Did anyone call them?" Silence. Here's what happened. The sales manager thought customer service would do it. Customer service thought sales owned the relationship. The owner thought the sales manager had assigned it. Look at what's missing from that story. Nobody opposed calling the customer. Nobody was unwilling to do the work. Nobody necessarily forgot. The group simply failed to establish who was expected to act. That's coordination failure. And notice how easy it would be to misdiagnose. The owner might say that nobody takes initiative. The employees might say that management is disorganized. Those might contain some truth. But both of them are character judgments. And both of them skip the more useful question. Before making a character judgment, ask this. Was there actually a mutually understood assignment? That's a different question. And it points to a very different fix. You don't need a culture overhaul here. You need one sentence at the end of the meeting. Something like "Jordan owns the call by 3 PM," said in front of the team. Now the owner knows. Everyone else knows. And the owner knows everyone else knows. So think about the last thing that fell through the cracks around you. Before you decide who was lazy, ask whether anyone ever actually said who owned it. Let's put this lesson on one line.
