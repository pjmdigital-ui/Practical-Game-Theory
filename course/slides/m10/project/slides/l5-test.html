Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's the simple test. It's the single most useful question in this module. Ask yourself: if I knew for certain that everyone else was going to cooperate, would I want to cooperate too? Read that slowly. The test removes the strategic uncertainty on purpose. It says imagine you're sure the others will act. Now what do you want to do? If the honest answer is "Yes, absolutely" then you're probably dealing with coordination. The only thing stopping you was not knowing what others would do. So clarity and visibility will help. If the honest answer is "I'd still be better off letting everyone else do the work," you have a social dilemma. A free-rider problem. Knowing what others will do doesn't make you want to join. It makes you want to sit back. That's a very different game. Here's the important part. Answer it honestly for yourself first. It's easy to run this test on other people and decide they're the free riders. It's harder to admit that you'd happily let someone else clean the kitchen. But if your own honest answer is "I'd rather someone else did it," say so. It changes the diagnosis. Then ask the second question. What would the others honestly answer? You may not know for sure. But you can often make a good guess from what people say and do. In the Dana practice case later, people say they keep their spreadsheets because that's where everyone else's dates are. That tells you a lot about their answer. Notice too that a group can be mixed. Some people answer yes and some answer no. So what does each answer mean for what you should do? Let's look at that.
