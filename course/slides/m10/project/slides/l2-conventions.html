Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's something we often overlook. Every time a group lacks a convention, people have to solve the same coordination problem again. And again. Look at these questions. Who runs the meeting? Who follows up? Where do files go? Who makes the final decision? When does the meeting actually start? How quickly should an email be answered? None of these are deep questions. But if they stay ambiguous, everyone spends energy predicting everyone else. You walk into the meeting wondering whether someone else has the agenda. You wonder whether you should send the follow-up or whether someone already did. That's real effort. And it's wasted effort. A convention removes the ambiguity. It says one simple thing. This is what we do here. Once everyone expects everyone else to follow it, coordination becomes much easier. Think about driving again. You don't wake up and decide which side of the road to use today. The rule has already coordinated expectations for you. That's why organizations create processes. Families create routines. Communities create customs. Societies create institutions. Processes aren't automatically good. They can become bureaucratic. But the reason we build them is important. Repeated coordination is costly. When the same uncertainty shows up every day, eventually someone writes a rule and the rule becomes a shortcut. So here's a question for you. What question does your team or your family answer from scratch every single week? That might be a convention waiting to be written. And sometimes the fastest way to write one is just to pick something.
