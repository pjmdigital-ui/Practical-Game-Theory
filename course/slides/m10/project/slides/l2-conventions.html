Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Every time a group lacks a convention, people have to solve the same coordination problem again. Who runs the meeting? Who follows up? Where do files go? A convention removes the ambiguity. It says: this is what we do here. That's why organizations create processes, families create routines and communities create customs. Fifty-fifty is interesting here. It may reflect fairness, but it's also extremely easy for both sides to anticipate, so it works as a coordination device too. And sometimes the best choice is simply to pick something. If two people have three equally acceptable options and spend twenty minutes debating, the cost of not coordinating is bigger than the difference among the options. Alternate. Rotate. Flip a coin. The rule doesn't need to reveal a profound truth; it just needs to be a mutually understood way to select one. One caution: bad conventions can be stable too. Everyone may follow a rule only because everyone expects everyone else to.
