Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Let's practice. Here's the scenario. Dana runs a twelve-person marketing team at a regional credit union. Three months ago leadership approved a shared content calendar. It was meant to replace the team's spreadsheets and email threads. Dana sent a kickoff email. It said, "Everyone should start using the new calendar when you can." Today only two people use it. Here's the interesting part. The others say they like the tool. But they keep their spreadsheets because that's where everyone else's dates are. Hold on to that sentence. It's a clue. Then last week two things went wrong. Two staff members scheduled competing promotional emails for the same morning. And a member newsletter went out without its compliance review. The writer assumed the designer had sent it for sign-off. The designer assumed the writer had. Now Dana's director weighs in. "Your team doesn't take ownership and resists change. Motivate them." And Dana is drafting a speech about accountability. Before you jump in, notice how familiar this sounds. Resistance to change. Lack of ownership. A speech to fix it. We've heard every one of those phrases in this module. And each time we asked whether something else was really going on. So read the scenario again with your new lenses. Where is there strategic uncertainty? Where is there a handoff? Is anyone actually opposed to anything? And is there any hint of a free-rider problem hiding in here?
