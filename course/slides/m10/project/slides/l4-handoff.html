Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Think about a relay race. The fastest runners in the world can still lose if they can't pass the baton. Human systems work the same way. Sales does its job. Operations needs information. Operations assumes sales sent it; sales assumes operations can see it in the system. The information never transfers, and the customer suffers. Nobody failed at their own task. The connection between tasks failed. So when a system breaks, inspect the handoffs. And notice that roles, deadlines, agendas and chains of command are coordination technology. A role answers who does what. A deadline answers when. An agenda answers what we're coordinating around. A chain of command answers who decides when disagreement remains. That's also why leadership often involves less persuasion than people think. In a crisis, a leader says: you call them, you stay here, everyone meets back here at six. They haven't changed anyone's motivation. They've created a shared coordination structure.
