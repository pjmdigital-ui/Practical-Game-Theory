Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Think about a relay race. The fastest runners in the world can still lose if they can't pass the baton. Every runner can run a great leg and the team can still fail. Human systems work the same way. Here's the chapter's version. A sales team does its job. Then operations needs some information. Operations assumes sales sent it. Sales assumes operations can see it in the system. The information never transfers. And the customer suffers. Now ask yourself who failed. Sales did their job. Operations did their job. Nobody necessarily failed at their individual task. The connection between tasks failed. That's a coordination problem. And it's invisible if you only look at people one at a time. So when a system breaks, don't just inspect each person's performance. Inspect the handoffs. There are three questions on the slide to help you do that. Where does one person's action depend on another person's? Where do expectations have to align? Where can ambiguity create mismatches? That's where coordination problems often hide. Here's the common mistake. When something breaks we look for the person who dropped it. Performance reviews and blame both work one person at a time. But the baton didn't fall out of one hand. It fell between two hands. If you only coach the runners you'll never fix the exchange. So here's a question for you. Pick one process at your work that keeps failing. Where exactly does the baton pass? Who thinks they handed it off, and who thinks they're still waiting?
