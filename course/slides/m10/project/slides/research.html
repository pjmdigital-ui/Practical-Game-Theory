Research spotlight · Focal points

People coordinate far above chance

What was done: Schelling posed matching puzzles with no communication. Mehta, Starmer and Sugden later tested focal points in pure coordination games.

What it shows

People coordinated far above random chance. They used shared culture and context and salience. Later experiments found people use salience rather than choosing randomly.

What it doesn't prove

That the most salient option is the best one. Salience also has limits. When players prefer different outcomes, coordination drops.

Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Our first spotlight is about focal points. Thomas Schelling posed matching puzzles with no communication allowed. People coordinated far above random chance using shared culture and salience. Later Mehta and Starmer and Sugden found people really do use salience in pure coordination games. So the option everyone recognizes most easily has real strategic value. Here's what it does not prove. The most salient option isn't necessarily the best one. And once conflicts of interest were built in higher stakes or stronger salience didn't reliably help. A focal point settles uncertainty. It doesn't settle conflict. Now the second spotlight.
