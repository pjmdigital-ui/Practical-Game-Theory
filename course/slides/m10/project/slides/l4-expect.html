Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Managers say this constantly. They know what they need to do. Parents say it. He knows the rules. Partners say it. She knows what I expect. Maybe. But the strategic question isn't "Have I said it?" It's "Do we actually share the same expectation about what happens next?" Those aren't the same thing. Here's the example from the chapter. You believe everyone knows the deadline means the work needs to be finished Thursday. Someone else believes Thursday is when we review the draft. Both of you heard Thursday. You both nodded. You both left the meeting confident. And you are not coordinated. Thursday arrives. You expect a finished product. They show up with a draft and a list of questions. Now it feels like a conflict. You think they dropped the ball. They think you moved the goalposts. But nobody did anything wrong on purpose. The word was shared. The expectation wasn't. This is why ambiguity is so dangerous in interdependent work. When my next step depends on your step, a small gap in meaning becomes a big gap in outcome. And here's the misunderstanding to watch for. We tend to believe that once we've said something clearly, our job is done. But "clear to me" is not the same as "shared by us." The test isn't whether the words left your mouth. It's whether everyone would describe what happens next the same way. So here's a quick check for you. Think of a deadline you set recently. Would everyone involved define "done" the same way you do? Sometimes the fix is remarkably boring. Let's look at it.
