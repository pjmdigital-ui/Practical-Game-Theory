"Everyone knows what to do" is often false

Not "Have I said it?" but "Do we share the same expectation?"

Two people both hear "Thursday." One means finished; the other means draft review. Compare: "Paul owns this by 3 PM." Paul knows, everyone knows, and Paul knows everyone knows.

Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Managers say it constantly: they know what they need to do. Parents say: he knows the rules. Partners say: she knows what I expect. Maybe. But the strategic question isn't "Have I said it?" It's "Do we actually share the same expectation about what happens next?" You believe the deadline means the work is finished Thursday. Someone else believes Thursday is when we review the draft. Both heard Thursday. They are not coordinated. Sometimes the fix is remarkably boring. Not motivation, not persuasion, not incentives. Just: Paul owns this by 3 PM. That one sentence changes the game, because now Paul knows he owns it, everyone else knows Paul owns it, and Paul knows everyone knows. A surprising amount of organizational dysfunction comes from compatible goals and incompatible expectations.
