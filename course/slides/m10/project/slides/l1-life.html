Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

That evening-out structure looks a lot like ordinary life. Here are three versions straight from the chapter. Two founders both want the company to succeed. That's aligned. But each one wants their own strategy adopted. That's the divergence. Two parents both want consistency for their kids. They agree on that completely. But each prefers a different parenting rule. Two departments both want the project finished. Nobody wants it to fail. But each wants the other department to absorb more of the workload. In every one of these the people really do share a goal. And they still have something real to settle. This is why the phrase "We all want the same thing" doesn't settle the strategic problem. I hear it in meetings all the time. It's usually said to end a disagreement. But it skips the important part. You need to ask a sharper question. Where exactly do our interests align? And where exactly do they diverge? When you can name both you can work on both. The aligned part needs coordination. The divergent part needs some kind of fair bargaining or a rule for choosing. If you only name the aligned part you'll be confused when people keep pushing back. If you only name the divergent part you'll treat a partner like an opponent. So here's a question for you. Pick one ongoing disagreement in your life. Can you write one sentence for where you align and one sentence for where you diverge? Now let's look at the example the chapter opens with.
