Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's why this module matters. When an interaction goes badly, most of us ask one question. Why won't they cooperate? This module trains you to stop asking that and to separate three possibilities instead. The first is conflict. We genuinely want different outcomes. The second is coordination failure. We could both benefit from compatible actions. We just aren't aligned on what each of us will do. The third is a social dilemma. We all like the collective outcome. But each of us has an incentive to let others carry more of the cost. From the outside those three look remarkably similar. Somebody isn't doing what you hoped. The result is bad. Everybody is frustrated. But they are not the same game. And they do not have the same solution. Here's what misdiagnosis costs you. Treat a coordination problem as a conflict and you can spend enormous energy persuading someone who already wants essentially what you want. Treat a free-rider problem as coordination and you can write the clearest plan in the world and watch it fail. Everyone knows exactly what to do. They still have a reason not to do it. So I want you to think of one situation right now where people can't seem to get on the same page. Hold it in mind. By the end of this module you'll know which of these three games it really is.
