Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Once you've diagnosed the game, you choose the intervention. Here's the menu from the chapter. A shared plan. A public commitment. A clear owner. A standard. A deadline. A focal point. Or information about what everyone else intends to do. Every one of these is a coordination tool. They work when the problem is that people are willing but unsure what everyone else will do. That's why the diagnosis comes first. If step two told you it's a social dilemma, this menu may not be enough. You'd be looking at incentives or accountability instead. And if it's a genuine conflict, you're in bargaining territory. So match the tool to the game. Here's how they map to what we've seen. The five departments needed a shared plan and a deadline, announced in public. The customer who never got a call needed a clear owner. The project-management system needed a standard. The Senegal farmers needed information about what everyone else intended. Then comes the most practical part of the whole tool. Write one sentence that makes the next move obvious to everyone. Think "Paul owns this by 3 PM." A name. A task. A time. Said where everyone can hear it. If you can't write that sentence, you probably haven't finished diagnosing. And finally run the ethics check. Does this respect truth and autonomy and dignity and informed choice for everyone affected? Remember that coordination is morally neutral. The same tools that align a team on good work can align it on something harmful. Now let's practice the whole thing on a real scenario.
