Three games, three different fixes

Conflict

We want incompatible outcomes.

Needs: bargaining. Or a fair rule for choosing between options.

Coordination

We'd both benefit. We just aren't aligned.

Needs: clear expectations. Shared information. A focal point. A standard.

Social dilemma

Each would rather others pay.

Needs: incentives. Monitoring. Reputation. Repeated play. Commitments. New rules.

Diagnosing the game comes before choosing the intervention.

Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

This is why diagnosis comes before the intervention. The fix changes with the game. Let's take them one at a time. If it's coordination, people are willing but not aligned. So you may need clearer expectations. Shared information. A focal point. An agreed standard. Those tools work because they remove the uncertainty about what everyone else will do. If it's free riding, clarity may not be enough. Everyone may know exactly what they're supposed to do and still have an incentive not to do it. A clearer chore chart won't change that. That kind of problem may require changing incentives. It may need monitoring or reputation. It may need repeated interaction, which we covered in Module Nine. It may need commitments. Or it may need changing the rules altogether. And if it's a real conflict, people want incompatible outcomes. Coordination tools alone won't settle that. You're in bargaining territory. Sometimes a fair way of choosing helps, like the alternating and rotating rules we saw earlier. But somebody has something real at stake. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Each fix looks reasonable on its own. That's what makes the wrong one so tempting. Put monitoring on a coordination problem and you insult people who were willing all along. Put a clearer plan on a free-rider problem and nothing changes, because clarity was never the issue. Give a pep talk to a real conflict and you just get a more polite version of the same fight. So before you pick a tool, name the game. Which of the three is the situation you thought of at the start of this module? Now a word on the limits of focal points.
