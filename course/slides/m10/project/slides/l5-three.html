Three games, three different fixes

Conflict

We want incompatible outcomes. A focal point can't erase that; it's a bargaining problem.

Coordination

We'd both benefit but aren't aligned. Fix: clear expectations, shared information, a focal point, a standard.

Social dilemma

Each would rather others pay. Fix: incentives, monitoring, reputation, repeated play, commitments, rules.

Coordination is morally neutral: groups can coordinate around harm. Truth, autonomy, dignity and informed choice still apply.

Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

This is why diagnosis comes before the intervention. The fix changes with the game. If it's coordination, you may need clearer expectations, shared information, a focal point or an agreed standard. If it's free riding, clarity may not be enough. Everyone may know exactly what they're supposed to do and still have an incentive not to do it. That may require changing incentives, monitoring, reputation, repeated interaction, commitments, or changing the rules. And if it's a real conflict, be careful with focal points. Once people strongly disagree about which outcome they prefer, the thing that seems obvious may not be enough. A focal point can help solve uncertainty. It cannot magically erase incompatible interests. One more thing: coordination is morally neutral. Groups can coordinate around harm just as easily as around good. So our foundation stays the same: truth, autonomy, dignity and informed choice.
