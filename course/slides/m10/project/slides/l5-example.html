Example · Four roommates

"Great. Then I don't have to."

The situation: Everyone wants a clean apartment. Three roommates will definitely clean it.

What happened: The fourth thinks, "Great. Then I don't have to."

The diagnosis: They coordinated just fine. Everyone knew the plan. The individual incentive is the problem.

The lesson: Same desired group outcome. Different strategic problem. A clearer plan won't fix it.

Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Back to the roommates. Three are definitely going to clean the apartment. There's no uncertainty. And the fourth thinks "Great. Then I don't have to." That isn't a failure to coordinate. The individual incentive is the problem. Fischbacher and Gächter found that people often cooperate conditionally. They contribute if others contribute. But over time contributions can decline and free riding can become widespread. That happened even though most participants weren't pure selfish maximizers. The clearest chore chart might not fix this house. They need something that changes the incentive or the accountability. Let's close the lesson.
