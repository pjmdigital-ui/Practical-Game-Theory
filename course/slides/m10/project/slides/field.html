Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's your assignment for this week. Pick one recurring friction point you've been treating as a conflict or a motivation problem. A task that keeps falling through the cracks. A handoff that keeps failing. A schedule you renegotiate every week. Before you say anything, run the Coordination Diagnostic and commit to a diagnosis. If it's coordination, make one expectation mutually clear. Name an owner. Name a time. Name a standard. Say it somewhere everyone can see everyone else receive it. Or propose a simple convention. If it turns out to be a social dilemma or a real conflict, don't force a coordination fix. Just note what kind of intervention it really needs. Then record what you said and where you said it. Write down what happened over the following days and whether the problem came back. Next week ask yourself a few questions. Did your first assumption match your final diagnosis? What changed your mind? Did a public and explicit expectation work differently from all those private reminders? And if the problem came back despite clear and mutually known expectations, what does that suggest? A hidden incentive? A real conflict? Or a game that needs redesigning?
