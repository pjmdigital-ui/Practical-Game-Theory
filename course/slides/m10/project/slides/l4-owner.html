Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Sometimes the solution to a human problem is remarkably boring. Not motivation. Not persuasion. Not incentives. Just one sentence. Paul owns this by 3 PM. That one sentence can change the game. Why? Look at the three layers on the slide. Paul knows he owns it. Everyone else knows Paul owns it. And Paul knows everyone else knows he owns it. That third layer is the one people forget. It's the difference between "I think this might be mine" and "everyone is now looking at me for this." The expectations have become aligned. The ambiguity is gone. Notice what's packed into that short sentence. A name. A task. A time. That's an owner and a deadline at once. And when you say it where everyone can hear, it also becomes common knowledge. Here's a line from the chapter I want you to remember. A surprising amount of organizational dysfunction comes from people having compatible goals but incompatible expectations. Nobody disagrees about the goal. Without realizing it they disagree about who does what and when. The common mistake is to reach for the dramatic fix. We redesign the culture. We run a workshop on accountability. We give a speech about ownership. Meanwhile the actual gap could have been closed by one plain sentence with a name and a time in it. So try this. Think of one task that's floating in your team right now. Can you write the "Paul owns this by 3 PM" sentence for it? Who would you say it to, and where would they all hear it?
