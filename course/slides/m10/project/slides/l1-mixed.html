Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Real interactions are often messier than pure coordination. They contain coordination and conflict at the same time. The classic game-theory example is called the Battle of the Sexes. The name is dated, but the structure is useful. Two people want to spend the evening together. That's aligned. But one prefers the concert and the other prefers the football game. That's conflict. Their worst outcome may be each going to a different event, so they need to coordinate, but they disagree over which outcome to coordinate on. That looks a lot like ordinary life. Two founders who both want the company to succeed, but each wants their own strategy adopted. Two parents who both want consistency, but prefer different rules. Two departments that both want the project finished, but each wants the other to absorb more of the workload. That's why "we all want the same thing" doesn't settle anything. You have to ask where exactly your interests align, and where they diverge.
