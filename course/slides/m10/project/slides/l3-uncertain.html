Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

There's a useful term for a problem we run into constantly. It's called strategic uncertainty. Let me separate it from the ordinary kind. Ordinary uncertainty is "Will it rain?" It's about the world. The weather doesn't care what you decide. You just make your best guess and carry an umbrella. Strategic uncertainty is different. It's "What are the other players going to do?" It's about people whose choices depend on yours, just as yours depend on theirs. And that loop is what makes it hard. I'm waiting to see what you do. You're waiting to see what I do. Nobody is wrong to wait. Here's the key point. This second kind of uncertainty is what blocks coordination. Not a lack of motivation. Not a disagreement about the goal. Just not knowing whether anyone else is going to move. The Senegal farmers from our research spotlight are a perfect case. They generally understood the advantages of selling together through their cooperative. But a farmer's willingness to take part depended partly on whether enough other farmers would take part too. That's strategic uncertainty in one sentence. The common mistake is to read this kind of waiting as a character problem. People call it laziness or resistance. Sometimes it's neither. It's a perfectly reasonable response to not knowing what everyone else will do. So when you see a group stuck, ask this. Is anyone actually opposed? Or is everyone waiting for someone else to go first? Let's see what that looks like inside a company.
