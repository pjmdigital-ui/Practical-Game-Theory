Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

So what is a coordination game? It's a situation in which players benefit when their actions fit together. That's the whole definition. And in a pure coordination game something interesting happens. Which outcome you land on may matter less than the fact that everyone chooses the same one. Driving is the obvious example. You could build a perfectly good traffic system where everyone drives on the right. You could build an equally good one where everyone drives on the left. Neither is the disaster. The disaster happens when half the people choose one convention and half choose the other. Game theorists describe this as a game with more than one equilibrium. There are several stable places to land. The problem is getting everyone to land on the same one. So the question often isn't "Which option is objectively better?" It's "Which option can we all reliably choose together?" That is a different kind of strategic problem. Here's where people go wrong. They treat a coordination problem like a debate about quality. They argue for weeks about the best option. Meanwhile nobody is aligned and everybody loses. So think about your own team or your own household. Where are you arguing about which option is best when the real need is just for everyone to choose the same one? Now real life is usually messier than driving.
