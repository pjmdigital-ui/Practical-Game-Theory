Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Thomas Schelling made this idea famous with a wonderfully simple question. You and another person are told: meet somewhere in New York City tomorrow. You can't communicate. Where do you go? There are thousands of possible places, and yet people don't choose randomly. They look for something obvious. A famous landmark. A central station. A conventional time. They ask themselves: what place will the other person think I will think is obvious? Schelling called these focal points. And here's the fascinating part: the players don't need to prefer the focal point. They only need to believe the other person will recognize it too. Try this one. Pick a positive whole number; if you match another person, you both win. Many people pick 1. It isn't mathematically superior. It stands out, and you can imagine the other person thinking the same thing.
