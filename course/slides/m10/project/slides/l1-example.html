Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's the example the chapter opens with. Imagine you and I are trying to meet for dinner. There are two restaurants. We both prefer eating together to eating alone. I slightly prefer the Italian place; you slightly prefer the steakhouse. What's the most important thing? Probably not getting our first choice. It's ending up at the same restaurant. If I go to the Italian place and you go to the steakhouse, we both lose. That's a coordination problem. Now compare that with a situation where there's only one reservation available and we both want it for separate events. Now our interests are directly opposed. In both cases you'd call it a disagreement. Same word. Very different game.
