Research spotlight · Communication as coordination

Revealing intentions changed behavior

What was done: Lab games with nonbinding messages (Cooper et al.). A field study with groundnut cooperatives in Senegal that revealed members' combined selling intentions.

What it shows

Nonbinding messages often improved coordination substantially. In Senegal treated farmers contributed more than twice as much. No new incentive was added.

What it doesn't prove

That talking more fixes every group problem. It helps when it resolves the blocking uncertainty. It won't dissolve a real conflict or a free-rider incentive.

Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

The second spotlight is communication as coordination. In the lab Cooper and DeJong and Forsythe and Ross found that nonbinding messages often improved coordination substantially. In Senegal Aflagah and Bernard and Viceisza revealed groundnut cooperative members' combined selling intentions. Treated farmers contributed more than twice as much toward collective selling as the control group. No new incentive was added. Only information about what everyone else intended. Here's what it does not prove. Neither result supports "talk more and every group problem disappears." Communication won't fix a free-rider incentive. Let's turn this into a tool.
