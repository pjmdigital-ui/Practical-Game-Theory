Everyone waits

"Employees resist change." Or was each one waiting for everyone else?

When switching alone is costly, everyone waits. A shared goal tells people where they want to end up. It doesn't tell them how everyone will get there.

Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's how it plays out. Your company introduces a new software system. Everyone thinks it could be better, if everyone uses it. But you aren't sure your coworkers will adopt it, and switching alone would make your work harder, because you'd still be dealing with everyone on the old system. So you wait. Your coworker is thinking exactly the same thing. They wait. Everyone waits. Months later, management concludes: employees resist change. Maybe. But perhaps every employee was willing to change, conditional on everyone else changing too. The barrier wasn't motivation. It was strategic uncertainty. And notice: a shared goal didn't solve it. A shared goal tells you where people want to end up. It doesn't tell them how everyone is going to get there. That's what coordination solves.
