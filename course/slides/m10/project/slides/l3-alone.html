Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's how strategic uncertainty plays out at work. Suppose your company introduces a new software system. Everyone thinks it could be better if everyone uses it. Hold on to that little condition. If everyone uses it. But you aren't sure whether your coworkers will adopt it. And switching alone would make your work harder. You'd still have to deal with everyone who's on the old system. So you wait. Your coworker is thinking exactly the same thing. So they wait. Everyone waits. Months later management looks at the numbers and concludes that employees resist change. Maybe that's true. But perhaps every single employee was willing to change. Conditional on everyone else changing too. That's a completely different diagnosis. The barrier wasn't motivation. It was strategic uncertainty. And notice how badly the wrong diagnosis would go. If management thinks it's resistance, they schedule a motivational talk. They explain again why the new system is good. But people already believed that. The talk answers a question nobody was asking. What people actually needed to know was whether everyone else was really going to switch. So here's what to watch for. When you hear "they resist change" or "nobody takes ownership," pause. Ask whether each person would happily act if they were sure the others would act too. If the answer is yes, you don't have a motivation problem. You have a coordination problem.
