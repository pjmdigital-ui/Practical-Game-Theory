Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here are the mistakes I see most often. First, treating every disagreement as a conflict, and then spending your energy persuading someone who already wants what you want. Second, calling coordination failure a character flaw. Before you say "nobody takes initiative," ask whether there was a mutually understood assignment, and whether people were simply unwilling to act alone. Third, telling people privately what needs to be public. Private messages leave each person wondering about the others. Fourth, prescribing "better communication" without a diagnosis. Ask what the communication needs to accomplish. A few more to watch for: assuming a shared goal settles it, when it can still hide a free-rider problem. Saying "everyone knows what to do" instead of checking whether you share the same expectation. Over-optimizing among acceptable options when failing to coordinate costs more than the difference. And assuming a stable convention is a good one. Sometimes the system needs redesigning, which is exactly where Module Eleven picks up.
