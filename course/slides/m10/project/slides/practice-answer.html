Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Here's how I'd read it. Question one. This is mostly coordination failure. Run the simple test. If I knew everyone else would use the calendar, would I? The team's own words answer it. They keep spreadsheets "because that's where everyone else's dates are." That's a yes. They aren't opposed to the tool. They said they like it. They're unwilling to switch alone. And that's reasonable. A calendar that only one person uses is worse than spreadsheets that everyone checks. Question two. The barrier is strategic uncertainty. Look at that kickoff email again. "When you can." It gave no shared date. It gave no plan. It gave no visible sign that anyone else was switching. And it arrived as a private message in each person's inbox. Everyone read it alone. Nobody could tell whether the others would act. So everyone waited. From the outside that looks exactly like resistance to change. It's the software story from lesson three all over again. Question three. The newsletter broke at the handoff. Nobody opposed the compliance review. Nobody thinks skipping it was a good idea. The writer did their job. The designer did their job. But there was no clear owner for sending it. Each one held a reasonable but incompatible expectation about who sends it for sign-off. The individual tasks were done. The connection between them failed. That's the relay race and the dropped baton. Notice that none of these three answers needed a word about anyone's character.
