Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Question four. Is a motivational speech the right intervention? No. Motivation isn't the bottleneck. People already like the tool. A speech about accountability answers a question nobody was asking. Here's what I'd do instead. Announce it in a team meeting so everyone sees everyone hear it. Starting Monday the 3rd, the shared calendar is the only calendar. Spreadsheets are retired. That one move does several jobs at once. It gives a shared date. It removes the private uncertainty. And it creates common knowledge. Then name an owner for keeping the calendar current. Define what "scheduled" actually means, so nobody hears "Thursday" two different ways. And review the calendar briefly at each weekly meeting so people can see others using it. Now the calendar becomes the focal point. It's the one obvious place everyone looks. And that also stops the colliding sends, because both promotions would now be sitting on the same screen. For the newsletter the fix is one plain sentence. The writer submits for compliance review. Nothing is released without sign-off. That's "Paul owns this by 3 PM" for a compliance step. Question five. Could part of this turn out not to be coordination? Possibly. Suppose the expectations are now public and clear. And some people still skip the calendar because updating it is extra work that mostly helps others. That looks like a social dilemma. Then clarity may not be enough. Dana may need to build updating into the workflow or make it visible. So diagnose first. And re-diagnose if the fix doesn't hold. Let's look at the mistakes that trip people up most.
