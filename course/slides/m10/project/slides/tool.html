Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Your tool for this module is the Coordination Diagnostic. It's in your workbook. Use it when a group or relationship keeps producing an outcome nobody seems to want. Start by writing down the bad outcome that keeps happening. Do it without any character judgments. Not "they're lazy." Just what happens. Then work through the questions. One. Do we actually want incompatible things? If yes, you may have a conflict or a bargaining problem. If it's partly true, write down where you align and where you diverge. Two. Would I take the desired action if I knew the others would too? Answer honestly for yourself. Then write what you suspect the others would honestly answer. Three. Commit to a diagnosis. Conflict. Coordination failure. Social dilemma. Or mixed. Write down why. Four. What am I uncertain about? Their actions? Their intentions? Their knowledge? Their commitment? Five. What are they uncertain about? This one gets skipped a lot. Your own intentions may be part of the problem. Six. What expectation needs to become mutually clear? Who acts? When? According to what standard? And where's the handoff? Seven. Is there a focal point or convention you can coordinate around? Sometimes something simple solves the whole thing. Eight. Does everyone know that everyone knows? Would making it public change the game? The common mistake with this tool is jumping straight to six. People love fixing expectations. But if step two tells you it's a social dilemma, a clearer expectation won't be enough. So go in order. Then you choose the fix. Let's look at that last step.
