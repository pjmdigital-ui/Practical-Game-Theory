Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Now let's go one level deeper. Sometimes everyone has the necessary information and coordination still fails. Why? Because there's a difference between everyone knowing something and everyone knowing that everyone knows it. Game theorists call the stronger condition common knowledge. Let's make it ordinary. A manager tells you privately: we're moving the meeting to three. You know. But you don't know whether everyone else was told, and you may wonder, what if everyone else comes at two? Now imagine the manager says it to the whole team in one room. You saw everyone hear it. They saw you hear it. Nothing about the meeting changed. What changed was the mutual awareness. That's why public announcements, shared dashboards, visible schedules and rituals matter. They're not just transmitting information. They create shared expectations.
