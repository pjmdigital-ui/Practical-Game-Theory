Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

This may seem surprising. You'd think that if everybody wants the same result, cooperation should be easy. If we all agree on the destination, why would we get stuck? Here's the answer. A shared goal tells you where people want to end up. It doesn't necessarily tell them how everyone is going to get there. Those are two different pieces of information. The first is about preferences. The second is about expectations. Who moves first? When? What does everyone else plan to do? A shared goal leaves all of that open. That's what coordination solves. The research we'll look at later makes this concrete. Researchers worked with groundnut-producing cooperatives in Senegal. The farmers broadly saw the benefits of selling collectively through their cooperative. They agreed on the goal. And yet beliefs about whether others would take part created a real barrier. People held back. Not because they disagreed. Because they weren't sure enough others would act. When the researchers revealed what the group as a whole intended to sell, behavior changed. Nobody got a new incentive. They got information about everyone else. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Leaders often think the job is done once everyone agrees on the vision. They hold the offsite. They get the nods. Then nothing moves and they're baffled. The vision answered "where." It never answered "how" or "who goes first." So ask yourself. Is there a goal your group already agrees on that still isn't happening? What part of the "how" has nobody made clear? Next let's look at a tool that can help, and one that's often misunderstood.
