Internomics · Module 10 — Diagnose the Coordination Problem

Before we look at the example, I want to be honest about the limits of everything we've learned. First, focal points are powerful. They help when several outcomes would work and nobody knows which one everyone else will choose. A focal point gives everyone something mutually recognizable to organize around. But focal points have limits. Once players strongly disagree about which outcome they prefer, the thing that seems obvious may no longer be enough. Experiments where players prefer different outcomes find lower coordination than in pure coordination settings. We'll see that in the research spotlight. So here's the line to remember. A focal point can help solve uncertainty. It cannot magically erase incompatible interests. If two people really want different things, pointing at the obvious option won't make one of them stop wanting theirs. Second, coordination is morally neutral. That's easy to forget because the examples so far have been friendly. Meeting for dinner. Calling a customer. But groups can coordinate around harm just as easily as around good. Everything in this module can be used to align people on something that hurts others or hurts themselves. Making an expectation public works whether the expectation is good or bad. So our foundation stays exactly where it was in every module. Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice. When you use these tools, run that check. Does the plan respect the people affected? Are they choosing with real information? Or are you just using visibility and pressure to herd them? That's the difference between leading coordination and manipulating it.
