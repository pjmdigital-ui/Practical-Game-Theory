Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

We need to be careful with the word forgiveness. Here's the point. Strategic forgiveness means one specific thing. Don't allow every deviation to make future cooperation impossible. That's it. Look at the first two cards. Forgiveness does not mean pretending nothing happened. You can remember exactly what happened. You can talk about it. Nor does it mean letting someone exploit you indefinitely. Forgiveness is not a blank check. It simply means one mistake doesn't end the game. Now look at the two dark cards. This is the real tension. Be too forgiving and you become exploitable. People learn that crossing you costs nothing. Be too unforgiving and you can get trapped in endless conflict. Every slip becomes a war. Neither extreme works. So a good repeated-game strategy has to do two things at once. It has to discourage exploitation. And it has to allow recovery from mistakes. That tension is very real. There's no formula that settles it for every relationship. But history helps. One slip from someone reliable for years probably deserves room. The same slip as part of a long pattern is different. That's information too. Here's the common misunderstanding. People think they have to choose between being a pushover and holding a grudge. You don't. You can forgive the mistake and still keep the boundary. Forgiveness and boundaries work together. So ask yourself which way you lean. Are you usually too forgiving? Or are you usually too unforgiving? And who in your life has paid the price for that lean? Now let's see what happens when nobody repairs anything for years.
