This gives us a concept that matters enormously in real relationships. Repair. Here's the point. In a noisy world the ability to recover from mistakes is a strategic asset. Suppose I fail you. If there's no route back to cooperation, then once the relationship breaks it stays broken. Every mistake becomes permanent. Now suppose the game has two things built in. It has a way to tell mistakes from exploitation. And it has a way to restore cooperation after a mistake. Then the relationship becomes much more robust. Look at the five cards. Repair might be an apology. It might be a clarification. "Here's what actually happened." It might be restitution. You make the other person whole. It might be forgiveness. Or it might be a renewed commitment about what happens next time. Those aren't just soft skills. People sometimes talk about them as if they were nice extras. In a repeated game with noise they do a hard strategic job. They stop errors from becoming permanent retaliation loops. Remember the chain on the last slide? Repair is the thing that breaks it. Somebody steps in at the "misread" stage and says, "Wait. That wasn't intentional." Or "I'm sorry. Let me fix it." The chain stops right there. Here's the common misunderstanding. People think repair is about being nice. It's really about keeping a valuable game alive. So here's a question. In your closest relationships is there a clear route back after a mistake? Or does every slip get remembered forever? But we need to be careful with one of these words. Let's talk about forgiveness.
