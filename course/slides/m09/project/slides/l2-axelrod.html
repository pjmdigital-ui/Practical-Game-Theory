Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Robert Axelrod explored this with computer tournaments built around the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma. Researchers submitted strategies that played against each other again and again. Some were complicated. They modeled opponents and tried to exploit patterns. Yet the winner of the first tournament was remarkably simple: Tit for Tat. It cooperated first, then did whatever the other player did on the previous round. In a larger second tournament, it won again. It didn't win by dominating anyone. It created an environment where cooperation could continue while still responding to exploitation. But be careful. The lesson is not that Tit for Tat is the best strategy for life. Its success depended on the environment, the other strategies, the payoffs, and how mistakes were handled. The broader lesson is this: once people expect a future together, reciprocity becomes strategically possible.
