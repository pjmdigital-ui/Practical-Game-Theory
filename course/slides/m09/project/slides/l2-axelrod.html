Axelrod's tournaments · Tit for Tat

The winner was simple: cooperate first, then copy their last move.

Round one

Cooperate.

Every round after

Do what the other player did last round. Cooperation gets cooperation. Defection gets defection.

The result

It won the first tournament. It won again in a larger second one with 62 entries.

Why it worked: It didn't dominate anyone. It let cooperation continue while still responding to exploitation.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. A very simple reciprocal strategy did remarkably well when the game repeated. Robert Axelrod ran computer tournaments built around the iterated Prisoner's Dilemma. Some strategies were complicated. Yet the winner of the first tournament was Tit for Tat. In round one it cooperated. After that it copied the other player's last move. In a larger second tournament with 62 entries it won again. It didn't dominate anyone. It let cooperation continue and still responded to exploitation. In your closest relationships do you start by cooperating? Now let me warn you about how people misread this result.
