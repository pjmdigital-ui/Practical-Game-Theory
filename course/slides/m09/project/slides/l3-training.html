You are always training the repeated game

The child who asks

You say no. They complain. You still say no. They escalate. Eventually you give in.

They learn: "Escalation can work."

The honest mistake

Someone makes an honest mistake. You punish them mercilessly.

They learn: "Never admit mistakes."

You solved today's problem. You optimized for this round. You may have made the next round harder.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. You train the repeated game in small everyday moments. A child asks for something. You say no. They escalate. Eventually you give in. The tantrum stops. But the child learns that escalation works. Now the opposite move. Someone makes an honest mistake and you punish them mercilessly. They learn never to admit mistakes. So you won't hear about the next one until it's much bigger. One move is too soft and one too harsh. Both focus only on this round. So ask before you respond. What am I teaching this person to expect from me next time? Next let's look at moves that lose today and pay off for years.
