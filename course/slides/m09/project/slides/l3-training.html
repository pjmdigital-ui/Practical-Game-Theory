Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. You're training the repeated game in small everyday moments. And the lesson you teach is often the opposite of the one you meant. Look at the left card. A child asks for something. You say no. They complain. You still say no. They escalate. Eventually you give in. What happened? You solved the immediate problem. The tantrum stopped. But you may also have changed the next interaction. The child has learned that escalation can work. So next time they'll escalate sooner and harder. You optimized for this round. You may have made the next round harder. Now look at the right card. It's the opposite move. Someone makes an honest mistake. You punish them mercilessly. They learn one thing. Never admit mistakes. Again you may feel like you dealt with today's problem. But you also shaped tomorrow's incentives. The next mistake still happens. You just won't hear about it until it's much bigger. Notice that these two moves look completely different. One is too soft and one is too harsh. But they share the same flaw. Both focus only on this round. Neither asks what the other person will learn from it. Here's the common misunderstanding. People think the lesson is in the speech they give afterward. It isn't. The lesson is in what actually happened. So this gives us a powerful question. What am I teaching this person to expect from me next time? Ask it in the moment before you respond.
