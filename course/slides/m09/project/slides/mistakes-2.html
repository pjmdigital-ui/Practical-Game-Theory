Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here are two more mistakes. Here's the point. Both come from misreading what patience is for. The fifth mistake is confusing forgiveness with forgetting. Some people think forgiving means acting as if nothing happened. So they either refuse to forgive because they don't want to forget. Or they forgive and then get exploited again because they wiped the record clean. Strategic forgiveness is different. You can remember exactly what happened. You can keep your boundary. Forgiveness simply means you don't let every deviation make future cooperation impossible. Think about the dinner example. One cancellation from a friend who's been reliable for ten years deserves room. You can forgive that and still notice if it becomes a pattern. The sixth mistake is using "the long term" to justify endless losses. This one sounds wise. Someone treats you badly and you tell yourself you're playing the long game. They do it again. And again. At some point the long-run data are telling you something. Repeated exploitation is data. It's not a reason to try harder. The fix is to change your strategy. Or change the rules. Or leave the game. Remember the bottom line here. The shadow of the future matters only if the future game is worth playing. A long relationship isn't automatically a good one. Here's a question for you. Is there a relationship where you've been calling it patience when it's really just absorbing losses? What would conditional cooperation look like there? Now let's take this into your week.
