The reputation that matters

"This person is safe to play repeated games with." Squeezing every round teaches the opposite.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

We tend to think of reputation as something vague. But strategically it does a practical job. It helps other people answer, "What is this person likely to do next?" If you've kept your promises for twenty years, that history is evidence. Reputations can be wrong, manufactured, or unfair, but when people lack information about you, your past shapes their expectations. Trust does something similar. Deal with someone you completely distrust and you want contracts, deposits, verification, monitoring, constant follow-up. Deal with someone reliable across hundreds of interactions and many of those costs fall away. That's not sentimental. It makes cooperation cheaper for both of you. Often the most valuable reputation is simply: this person is safe to play repeated games with. Squeeze every round, and people learn the opposite: every interaction with you requires protection.
