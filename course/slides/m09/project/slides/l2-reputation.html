Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Reputation isn't a vague cloud of opinion. It's a prediction. We tend to talk about reputation in fuzzy terms. "He has a good reputation." But strategically reputation serves a very practical function. It helps other people answer one question. What is this person likely to do next? Look at the left card. If you've kept your promises for twenty years, that history becomes evidence. If you've broken every agreement you've ever entered, that becomes evidence too. People use that evidence because they usually can't see inside your head. They only see what you've done. Now look at the right card. Reputation is not perfect. People can change. Reputations can be wrong. A good reputation can be manufactured. A bad reputation can be unfair. I'm not claiming reputations are accurate. I'm claiming they work. When people have incomplete information about how you'll behave, your past behavior shapes their expectations. And their expectations shape how they treat you. That's why reputation becomes an economic and relational asset. Here's the common misunderstanding. People ask, "What do people think of me?" That's the wrong question. It's about image. The strategic question is different. What future behavior do they expect from me? That's the part of reputation that changes the game. So try this. Pick one colleague. If they had to predict what you'll do the next time you disagree with them, what would they predict? And would you like that prediction? Reputation tells people what to expect.
