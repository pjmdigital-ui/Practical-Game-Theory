Research spotlight · Aoyagi, Bhaskar & Fréchette

When monitoring is noisy, leniency helps

What it shows

Players sustained cooperation with perfect and with noisy monitoring. Under noise, they used more complex, more lenient strategies, which limited the damage from errors.

What it doesn't prove

"Always forgive." A too-forgiving strategy can make exploitation profitable. Nor do Axelrod's tournaments make Tit for Tat best everywhere.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

The second study is about noise. Masaki Aoyagi, V. Bhaskar, and Guillaume Fréchette compared repeated Prisoner's Dilemma games where players could see exactly what happened with games where past actions were observed imperfectly. People sustained cooperation in all the conditions, but their strategies changed when monitoring got noisy. They became more complex and more lenient, which helped limit the damage from observational errors. That's a deeply useful finding. When misunderstandings are possible, a completely unforgiving strategy is fragile. Some tolerance, and a path to repair, can be strategically valuable. But it isn't evidence for "always forgive." Leniency helps in noisy settings, and exploitation is still possible. In the same way, Axelrod's tournaments don't show Tit for Tat is best everywhere. Its success depended on the mix of strategies, the payoffs, and how errors were handled.
