Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's how I'd read it. The big idea is boundaries plus repair. Dana needs both. She just needs them in different places. One. The retailer relationship has the long shadow. Marcus leaving shortens his personal horizon but not the account's. How Dana handles this becomes history that his replacement inherits. The game has an endpoint for one player. It doesn't have one for the relationship. Two. Priya's approach wins the round. Dana gets four thousand dollars and a record showing the error wasn't the agency's. But it may teach the client that every mistake with this agency produces a bill and an itemized accusation. Then every interaction requires protection. Dana can still protect herself. She can clarify how approvals work going forward. She might split the rework cost. She doesn't need to turn an honest error into a punishment. Three. With Theo, Dana has been cooperating unconditionally with someone who keeps defecting. She's been teaching him that missed deadlines cost him nothing. She absorbs the cost. A conditional boundary might sound like this. "I want to keep working with you. But missed deadlines change what I can do next. Before we talk about rates, I need the next three projects on time." If the pattern continues, the long-run data are telling her something. Four. On noise, Marcus's error looks like one mistake inside four years of reliability. Same action. Different history. Theo's three out of four is a pattern. Apologies without changed behavior are not repair. Five. A hundred more rounds of absorbing Theo's misses means more overtime and growing resentment. It means rising rates for unreliable work. And it likely ends in a collapse on worse terms. A clear boundary now gives the relationship a better chance. Or it ends it before more value is lost.
