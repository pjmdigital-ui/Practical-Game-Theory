Boundaries plus repair

1. The shadow: The account has the long shadow. Marcus's exit shortens his horizon, not the account's; his replacement inherits this history.

2. The round: Priya wins $4,000 and a clean record, but teaches that every mistake brings a bill and an accusation. Clarify approvals; perhaps split the cost.

3. Theo: Unconditional cooperation taught him misses cost nothing. Boundary: "Before we talk about rates, I need the next three projects on time."

4. Noise: Marcus erred once inside four years of reliability. Theo's three of four is a pattern. Apologies without changed behavior aren't repair.

5. Hundred rounds: More overtime, resentment and rising rates, then likely collapse on worse terms. A clear boundary now is the better bet.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's how I'd read it. The retailer relationship has the long shadow. Marcus leaving shortens his personal horizon, but not the account's, and how Dana handles this becomes history his replacement inherits. Priya's approach wins the round: four thousand dollars and proof the error wasn't the agency's. But it may teach the client that every mistake with this agency produces a bill and an itemized accusation. Dana can protect herself, by clarifying approvals or splitting the cost, without punishing an honest error. With Theo, Dana has been cooperating unconditionally with someone who keeps defecting, teaching him that missed deadlines cost him nothing. A conditional boundary: "Before we talk about rates, I need the next three projects on time." On noise: Marcus's error is one mistake inside four years of reliability. Theo's three out of four is a pattern. Apologies without changed behavior are not repair. And a hundred more rounds of absorbing Theo's misses means more overtime, growing resentment, and a likely collapse on worse terms. A clear boundary now gives the relationship a better chance, or ends it before more value is lost.
