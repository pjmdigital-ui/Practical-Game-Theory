Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Trust isn't just a warm feeling. It changes the cost of doing anything together. Imagine doing business with someone you completely distrust. Look at the left panel. You'd want detailed contracts. You'd want deposits. You'd want verification and monitoring. You'd want documentation and maybe third parties involved. And you'd need constant follow-up. Every one of those things costs time or money or attention. Every one of them slows the work down. Now imagine doing business with someone who has shown they're reliable across hundreds of interactions. Perhaps you still use contracts. But many of those transaction costs fall away. You don't need to verify every small statement. You don't need to protect yourself against every possible betrayal. Trust makes interaction easier. And that's not merely sentimental. It has strategic value. The more reliable my behavior has been, the less uncertainty you face in dealing with me. Less uncertainty means less protection. Less protection makes cooperation cheaper for both of us. Notice that both sides win here. The trusted person saves effort. So does the person trusting them. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People sometimes think being trustworthy is a cost they pay to be good. In a repeated game it's closer to an investment. It lowers the price of every future round. So here's a question for you. Think of the person you trust most at work. How much time do you save because you don't have to check their work? Now let's see what kind of reputation earns that trust and what kind destroys it.
