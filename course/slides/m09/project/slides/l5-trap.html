Reputation can trap you too

What reputation do I want to be bound by?

"I never back down." "I'm always the nice one." A reputation changes how others expect you to act, and how you feel obligated to act.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

A good reputation can create value. But it can also create constraints. Suppose you've built your identity around "I never back down." Now every conflict puts pressure on you to maintain it, even when backing down would be wise. Or "I'm always the nice one." Now asserting a legitimate boundary feels inconsistent with who you've been. This connects back to Module Four. Reputation is a form of strategic predictability. "Keeps promises" is useful predictability. "Always gives in eventually" is exploitable predictability. So the question isn't just, "How do I build a strong reputation?" It's, "What reputation do I want to be bound by?" That's a much more interesting question.
