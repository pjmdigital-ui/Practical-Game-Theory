Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Don't judge a strategy by one round. Suppose your strategy loses today. Does that make it bad? Not necessarily. Look at the three cards. In the first one you refuse to exploit someone. You leave five hundred dollars on the table. Later they send you fifty thousand dollars of business. In the second one you enforce a boundary. That creates an uncomfortable conversation. But the relationship improves afterward. In the third one you admit a mistake. You lose face for ten minutes. And you gain credibility that lasts for years. In every one of those cases the move looks like a loss if you only score today. Each one looks like a win if you score the relationship. The repeated-game lens teaches us that a strategy should sometimes be judged across the relationship. Not the incident. That's a huge mental shift. Most of us were trained to score every round. Did I win the argument? Did I get the better deal? Did I come out ahead today? Those questions aren't wrong. They're just incomplete. Now here's the misunderstanding to avoid. This doesn't mean every loss today is secretly a win tomorrow. Some losses are just losses. The question is whether the move improves the future game. Leaving money on the table for someone who will never deal with you again isn't strategy. It's just generosity. That may be fine. Just know which one you're doing. So ask yourself this.
