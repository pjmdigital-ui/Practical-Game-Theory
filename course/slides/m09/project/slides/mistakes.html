Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Let's look at the mistakes people make most often with this model. Here's the point. Each one takes a true idea and pushes it too far. The first is treating a relationship like a transaction. You optimize every round as if it were the last. You might win a lot of rounds. But you can make the game itself less valuable. Remember the hard bargainer whose counterparties start adding contract language and deposits. The fix is simple to say. Give the future a place in the decision. The second runs the other way. It's assuming "long term" means "always be nice." Unconditional cooperation can make exploitation profitable. Remember Dana and Theo. Her patience taught him that missed deadlines cost nothing. The fix is conditional cooperation and credible boundaries. The third is treating every apparent defection as intentional. In a noisy world rigid retaliation turns mistakes into feuds. Remember the two families. The fix is to separate mistakes from exploitation. And build in a path to repair. The fourth is treating cooperation as automatically good. Cartels and corrupt groups cooperate too. Game theory explains which patterns can persist. Ethics decides whether they deserve to. Notice how the second and third mistakes pull in opposite directions. One is too soft. The other is too harsh. Most of us lean one way by habit. Here's a question for you. Which of these four is your default? And where did it cost you last? There are two more mistakes worth naming.
