Common mistakes

Treating a relationship like a transaction

Optimizing every round can make the game itself less valuable. Give the future a place in the decision.

"Long term" means "always be nice"

Unconditional cooperation can make exploitation profitable. Use conditional cooperation and credible boundaries.

Every defection is intentional

Rigid retaliation turns mistakes into feuds. Separate mistakes from exploitation. Build in a path to repair.

Cooperation is automatically good

Cartels cooperate too. Ethics decides which patterns deserve to persist.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here are the mistakes people make most often with this model. Each takes a true idea too far. The first is treating a relationship like a transaction. You optimize every round and make the game less valuable. The second is assuming "long term" means "always be nice." Dana's patience taught Theo that missed deadlines cost nothing. The third is treating every apparent defection as intentional. In a noisy world that turns mistakes into feuds. The fourth is treating cooperation as automatically good. Cartels cooperate too. Which of these four is your default? There are two more worth naming.
