Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Imagine you're buying something from a street vendor in a city you'll never visit again. Now imagine buying the same thing from the store around the corner from your house. You expect to shop there every week for the next twenty years. Same transaction. Same product. Same price. But not necessarily the same game. In the first one you can both reasonably think this is probably the only time you'll ever deal with each other. In the second one whatever happens today may affect what happens next week. Your behavior today can influence how they treat you tomorrow. Their behavior today can influence whether you ever come back. Trust matters. Reputation matters. Retaliation matters. Repair matters. The future has entered the payoff. Game theorists call this the shadow of the future. Once that shadow gets long enough, behavior that makes little sense in a one-time interaction can make a great deal of sense in a relationship that continues. Earlier in the course you learned to ask "Then what?" Now we're stretching the horizon. The new question is "What happens next time?" A choice can be excellent in a one-shot game and terrible in a repeated one. And most of the important parts of your life are repeated games. Your marriage is one. So are your kids and your closest friends. So are your team and your business partners. You're always teaching the people in them what to expect from you. You do it whether you intend to or not. So here's a question to hold as we go. Which of your important relationships have you been treating like a one-time transaction? Let's look at what you'll be able to do by the end of this module.
