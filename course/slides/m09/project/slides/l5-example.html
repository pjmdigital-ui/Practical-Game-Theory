Example · A repeated bad game

"Why am I still playing this game?"

The situation: A business relationship keeps producing broken promises and growing distrust.

What happened: Monitoring escalates. Conflict grows. Outcomes stay poor. You keep optimizing each new round and telling yourself, "I'm thinking long term."

The better question: Why am I still playing this repeated game?

The lesson: A future doesn't obligate you to preserve the relationship. Change your strategy. Change the rules. Or leave the game.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Don't use "the long term" to justify endless losses. Someone treats you badly. You tell yourself you're thinking long term. They do it again and again. At some point the long-run data are telling you something. Picture a business relationship that keeps producing broken promises and escalating monitoring. You could write a tighter contract. Or you could ask why you're still playing this repeated game. The lesson may be to change your strategy or the rules. Or to leave the game. People think leaving means they failed at the long game.
