Example · A repeated bad game

"Why am I still playing this game?"

The situation: A business relationship keeps producing broken promises and growing distrust.

What happened: Monitoring escalates. Conflict grows. Outcomes stay poor. You keep optimizing each new round and telling yourself, "I'm thinking long term."

The better question: Why am I still playing this repeated game?

The lesson: A future doesn't obligate you to preserve the relationship. Change your strategy. Change the rules. Or leave the game.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

There's an opposite mistake to everything we've said about patience. Here's the point. Don't use "the long term" to justify endless losses. Someone treats you badly. You tell yourself, "I'm thinking long term." They do it again. And again. And again. At some point the long-run data are telling you something. A repeated game gives you chances to learn. Use them. Now picture a business relationship like the one on the slide. It keeps producing broken promises. It keeps producing distrust. The monitoring keeps escalating. There's constant conflict. The outcomes stay poor. You could keep optimizing each new round. You could write a tighter contract. You could add another check-in meeting. You could find a slightly better way to handle the next broken promise. Or you could step back and ask a different question. Why am I still playing this repeated game? That's an important strategic question. If the other player repeatedly exploits cooperation and never reciprocates, the lesson may not be to cooperate harder. It may be to change your strategy. It may be to change the rules. Or it may be to leave the game. The existence of a future doesn't obligate you to preserve the relationship. The shadow of the future matters only if the future game is worth playing. Here's the misunderstanding. People think leaving means they failed at the long game. Sometimes leaving is the long game. We'll come back to changing the rules in Module Eleven. It's called Change the Game. So here's a question for you.
