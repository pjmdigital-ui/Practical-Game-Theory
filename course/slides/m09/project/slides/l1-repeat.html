Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

So what exactly does repetition add? Here's the point. In a repeated game my choice today doesn't only determine today's payoff. It reaches forward into five things. First, it can change what you expect from me. Second, it can change how you treat me next time. Third, it can change whether you trust me. Fourth, it can change whether you punish me. And fifth, it can change whether you cooperate with me again. None of those five show up in today's payoff. They all show up later. That's why a one-shot analysis can look smart and still be wrong. Go back to that store around the corner. Suppose they overcharge you today. In that one exchange they won. But you'll be back next week. Now you expect something different from them. You'll check the receipt. You'll trust them less. Maybe you'll start shopping somewhere else. Their behavior today influenced whether you ever return. The payoff from that one exchange keeps changing for months. The mistake people make here is to look only at the first column. They ask whether the move worked today. The better question is what the move did to all five of these. So pick a relationship you care about. Which of these five did your last important move touch? In a one-shot game today's move is just today's move. In a repeated game today becomes history. And history can affect tomorrow. Let's look at how that works in practice.
