Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the core mechanism in one line. In a repeated game I can respond tomorrow to what you did today. That's really what repetition gives us. Look at the four cards. If you cooperate with me, I can reward that cooperation later. If you exploit me, I can protect myself later. If you keep your promise, I can trust your next promise more. And if you keep breaking promises, I can stop relying on them. None of that is possible in a one-shot game. When there's no tomorrow there's nothing to reward and nothing to protect. Today's move is just today's move. Add a tomorrow and today becomes history. History is what lets consequences reach back and shape the present. Think about the promises people have made to you over the years. You probably have a few people whose word you'd take without a second thought. You probably have others whose promises you've quietly stopped counting on. Nobody announced that change. It happened because you remembered. That's memory creating consequences. And it runs both ways. The people around you are keeping the same kind of record on you. So here's a question. Who in your life has stopped relying on your promises? And who has learned they can rely on them completely? Repeated interaction creates memory. Memory creates consequences. Now let's ask what that does to the reasons people cooperate.
