Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Think about two families who have hated each other for years. Ask either side why, and they'll describe something the other side did in response to something their side did in response to something that happened years earlier. At some point, the original event barely matters. The retaliation has become the game. Each side sees its own behavior as a justified response, and the other side's as aggression. That's a repeated-game trap, because the relationship now contains history, and history changes the meaning of every new move. Think about someone cancelling dinner with you. If they've been reliable for ten years, you think, "Something must have come up." If they've cancelled the last six times, you think, "Here we go again." Same action. Different history. Different belief. Actions never arrive alone. They arrive attached to everything that came before them.
