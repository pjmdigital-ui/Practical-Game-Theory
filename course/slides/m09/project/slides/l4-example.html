Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's what it looks like when a relationship has no repair at all. The point is that retaliation can become the game itself. Think about two families who have hated each other for years. Ask either side, "Why?" They may start describing something the other side did. It was in response to something their side did. That was in response to something that happened years earlier. Follow it back far enough and you'll find something small. Maybe it was a misunderstanding. Maybe it was a real wrong. At some point the original event barely matters. The retaliation has become the game. Look at the third line on the slide. Each side sees its own behavior as a justified response. And each sees the other side's behavior as aggression. Both stories feel completely true from the inside. That's what makes this so hard to escape. This is a repeated-game trap. The relationship now contains history. And that history affects the meaning of every new move. A neutral gesture from the other family gets read as a trick. A real attempt at peace gets read as weakness. Nobody can make a fresh move because no move arrives fresh. Now you probably don't have a family feud. But you may have a smaller version. Maybe it's a coworker you've been trading small slights with for a year. Or a relative who always says the thing that sets you off. Here's a question for you. If someone asked you why that relationship is so tense, would you describe a response to a response? Let's look at how history changes the meaning of the very same action.
