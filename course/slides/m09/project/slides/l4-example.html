Example · The feud

Ask either family: "Why?"

The situation: Two families have hated each other for years.

What happened: Each side describes a response to a response to something that happened years earlier.

How each side sees it: Our behavior is a justified response. Theirs is aggression.

The lesson: The original event barely matters anymore. History now colors every new move.

The retaliation has become the game.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Without repair retaliation can become the game itself. Think about two families who have hated each other for years. Ask either side why. They describe something the other side did in response to something their side did. Follow it back far enough and you'll find something small. Each side sees its own behavior as a justified response. Each sees the other's as aggression. That history changes the meaning of every new move. A real attempt at peace gets read as weakness. You may have a smaller version with a coworker or a relative. Is it a response to a response? Let's see how history changes the meaning of one action.
