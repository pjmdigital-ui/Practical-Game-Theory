Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. A repeated game is not just the same game played more often. The future changes the math of today. Let's go back to the Prisoner's Dilemma. In the basic one-shot version the payoffs make defection individually attractive. If you cooperate, I can do better by defecting. If you defect, I protect myself by defecting. So we can end up at defect and defect. And that happens even though cooperate and cooperate would leave both of us better off. We've used this game before to understand payoffs and equilibrium. Now change one thing. Instead of playing once we're going to play again tomorrow. And the day after that. And perhaps again after that. Notice what did not change. The immediate payoff is exactly the same. Defecting today still pays more today. What changed is the continuation of the relationship. My choice today now carries information about how I'll behave next time. You can respond to it. And that can completely change the strategy. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to avoid. People hear this and think repeated games make people nicer. That's not the claim. The claim is that the structure now contains a tomorrow. Tomorrow gives both of us something to protect. So think of one interaction you had this week. Was it really a one-shot game? Or did it have a tomorrow you weren't counting? Let's look at exactly what today's move can now change.
