Three limits of the long game

Endings change incentives

"Friday is my final day." Values may still hold. But some future consequences disappear.

No guarantee

The future creates possibilities for cooperation. It doesn't guarantee outcomes.

Not the same as goodness

Cartels cooperate. Corrupt organizations cooperate. Two people can cooperate to deceive someone else.

Game theory tells us how patterns become sustainable. Ethics tells us whether they're worth sustaining. Our standard stays the same: Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. The shadow of the future has three real limits. First, the future only matters if players believe there is one. Someone who knows Friday is their final day may still behave well. But certain incentives have changed. Second, the future creates possibilities. It doesn't guarantee outcomes. Third, cooperation is morally neutral. A cartel can cooperate. Two people can cooperate to deceive someone else. Game theory tells us how patterns become sustainable. Ethics tells us whether they're worth sustaining.
