Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

We need to put firm boundaries around this model. Here's the point. The shadow of the future is powerful. But it has three real limits. First, the future only matters if players believe there is a future. Imagine someone who's worked at a company for twenty years. They plan to stay another ten. Their reputation inside the company matters a lot. Now imagine they know that Friday is their final day. Some future consequences disappear. They may still behave well because of values or conscience. They may care about relationships or their wider reputation. But certain incentives have changed. The game has an endpoint. When people believe there's no next round, their incentives may change. Second, it would be easy to conclude that people who interact repeatedly will cooperate. That's false. The future creates possibilities. It doesn't guarantee outcomes. We'll see the research on that in a few minutes. Third, we shouldn't romanticize repeated games. The future can sustain cooperation. But cooperation itself is morally neutral. A cartel can cooperate. A corrupt organization can cooperate. Two people can cooperate to deceive someone else. Members of a criminal group can stay loyal through repeated interaction. Cooperation is not the same thing as goodness. Game theory tells us how patterns become sustainable. Ethics tells us whether those patterns are worth sustaining. Our standard stays the same. Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice. Here's a question for you. Is there a cooperative pattern in your world that's stable but not worth sustaining? Next let's look at how reputation itself can become a trap.
