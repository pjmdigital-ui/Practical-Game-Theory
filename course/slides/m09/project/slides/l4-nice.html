The long game is not "always be nice"

Unconditional cooperation can make exploitation profitable.

A boundary can protect cooperation: "I want to keep cooperating with you, but this behavior changes what I will do next."

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

This is important. Repeated-game theory does not teach "always cooperate." If you cooperate unconditionally with someone who repeatedly exploits you, you may simply make exploitation profitable. Remember, Tit for Tat was cooperative, but it wasn't passive. It responded to defection, and that mattered. People often treat boundaries as the opposite of cooperation. But picture a relationship where one person keeps taking advantage of the other and the cooperative person never responds. Resentment builds, and eventually the relationship may collapse. A credible boundary changes the payoff: "I want to keep cooperating with you, but this behavior changes what I will do next." Thinking long term does not mean refusing to protect yourself. Conditional cooperation is often more durable than unconditional cooperation.
