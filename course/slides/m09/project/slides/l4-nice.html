Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

This is important. Here's the point. Repeated-game theory does not teach "always cooperate." If you cooperate unconditionally with someone who repeatedly exploits you, you may simply make exploitation profitable. The possibility of a future creates room for many things. It creates room for reward and for punishment. It creates room for reciprocity. It creates room for boundaries and for repair. Look at the first card. Axelrod's Tit for Tat was cooperative. But it was not passive. It responded to defection. That mattered. Now look at the second card. People often treat boundaries as the opposite of cooperation. But picture a relationship where one person keeps taking advantage of the other. The cooperative person never responds. What happens? Exploitation becomes profitable. Resentment builds quietly. Eventually the relationship may collapse. The nice person thought they were protecting the relationship. They were actually starving it. Now look at the bottom line. A credible boundary changes the payoff. It sounds something like this. "I want to keep cooperating with you. But this behavior changes what I will do next." That can make a healthy repeated relationship more sustainable. Notice that it starts with the relationship. It isn't a threat to leave. It's a statement about what happens next round. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Thinking long term does not mean refusing to protect yourself. Sometimes a firm boundary preserves the relationship better than endless accommodation. Cooperation does not require defenselessness. So who in your life might need to hear a sentence like that one? But punishment brings its own problem. Let's look at it.
