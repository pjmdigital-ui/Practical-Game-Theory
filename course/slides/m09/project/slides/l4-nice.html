Conditional lasts longer

Conditional cooperation is often more durable than the unconditional kind.

A boundary that protects cooperation: "I want to keep cooperating with you. But this behavior changes what I will do next."

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Repeated-game theory does not teach "always cooperate." Cooperate unconditionally with someone who keeps exploiting you and you make exploitation profitable. Tit for Tat was cooperative but not passive. It responded to defection. When one person keeps taking advantage and the other never responds, resentment builds quietly. Eventually the relationship may collapse. A credible boundary changes the payoff. "I want to keep cooperating with you. But this behavior changes what I will do next." Thinking long term does not mean refusing to protect yourself. But punishment brings its own problem. Let's look at it.
