Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Let's put this together with two restaurants. The point is that structure decides how long the shadow is. Picture a restaurant beside a highway. It serves mostly travelers who will never come back. Now picture a neighborhood restaurant. Its customers live within a mile. All else equal, the second one has stronger repeated-game incentives. Mistreat someone today and they may never return. They may tell their neighbors. Their family may stop coming. Every meal is a round in a long game. Now be careful here. That doesn't mean the highway restaurant will behave badly. Morality matters. So do law and competition. So do reviews and institutional reputation. But structurally the local restaurant has a longer direct shadow of the future. The game shapes incentives. Now here's where it gets interesting. Technology can stretch the shadow. Suppose you buy something online from a stranger you'll never meet. At first glance that looks like a one-shot game. But then add ratings. Add reviews and seller histories. Add public complaints and reputation scores. Now how you treat this stranger can affect how future strangers treat you. The repeated game has been extended beyond the same two people. The platform has created memory. That's what institutions can do. They turn behavior in one interaction into information for later ones. We'll come back to that kind of game redesign in Module Eleven. So think about your own work.
