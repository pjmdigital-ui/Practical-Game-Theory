Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Picture a restaurant beside a highway that serves mostly travelers who'll never come back. Now picture a neighborhood restaurant whose customers live within a mile. All else equal, the second one has stronger repeated-game incentives. Mistreat someone today and they may never return, they may tell their neighbors, and their family may stop coming. That doesn't mean the highway restaurant will behave badly. Morality, law, competition and reviews all matter. But structurally, the local restaurant has a longer direct shadow of the future. And technology can stretch that shadow. Buying from a stranger online looks like a one-shot game, until you add ratings, reviews and seller histories. Now how you treat this stranger affects how future strangers treat you. The platform has created memory.
