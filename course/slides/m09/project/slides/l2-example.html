Example · Two restaurants

Who has the longer shadow?

The situation: A highway restaurant serves mostly travelers who will never return. A neighborhood restaurant serves customers who live within a mile.

What's at stake locally: Mistreat someone and they may never return. They may tell the neighbors. Their family may stop coming.

The online twist: Ratings and seller histories extend the shadow to strangers. The platform has created memory.

The lesson: The highway place won't necessarily behave badly. But the local one has the longer direct shadow. The game shapes incentives.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Structure decides how long the shadow is. Picture a highway restaurant serving travelers who never come back. Now picture a neighborhood restaurant whose customers live within a mile. All else equal, the second has stronger repeated-game incentives. That doesn't mean the highway restaurant will behave badly. Morality and law matter too. Technology can also stretch the shadow. Buying online from a stranger looks like a one-shot game. But add ratings and seller histories. Now how you treat this stranger can affect how future strangers treat you. The platform has created memory. Here's the takeaway.
