Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

The point of this slide is that cooperation can come from structure and not only from character. When people talk about cooperation they usually explain it with personality. "She's cooperative." "He's selfish." "They're trustworthy." Those things may matter. But repeated-game theory shows us another possibility. Sometimes cooperation is a strategic response to the structure of the relationship. Look at the mini-example. Suppose I could make a hundred dollars by betraying you today. But doing it causes me to lose a thousand dollars of future business with you. Even if I cared only about my own payoff, cooperation could make sense. I don't need to be a saint. I just need to be able to count. Now here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Some people hear this and get cynical. They say that means the cooperation is fake. It isn't. It simply means there's more than one path to cooperative behavior. Look at the five cards. Someone might cooperate out of generosity. Or morality. Or loyalty. Or friendship. Or because future consequences make cooperation strategically valuable. Real human behavior often contains several of those motives at once. Your best friend may be loyal to you and also know that the friendship is worth protecting. Both are true. Neither cancels the other. Here's why this matters for you. If cooperation only came from personality you could only hope for good people. If it also comes from structure you can design for it.
