Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

So here's what the thousand dollars may really cost. The point is simple. Future consequences are part of the price of today's move. Look at the cards. Taking the loophole may produce less trust. My partner now wonders what else I'll take. It may produce more monitoring. They start checking my numbers. It may produce tighter contracts. Every future deal gets more lawyering and more fine print. It may produce fewer opportunities. They stop bringing me into their best deals. It may produce resentment. That doesn't show up on any spreadsheet but it shapes every meeting. It may produce retaliation. Maybe they find their own loophole next quarter. And finally it may produce the end of the partnership. Add those up across ten years of business. A thousand dollars starts to look like a terrible trade. Notice something about this list. Almost nothing on it happens today. Every item lands in a future round. That's why a one-shot analysis misses it. You can't see the price if you only look at the current round. The common misunderstanding is to treat these as unlikely side effects. They aren't side effects. In a repeated game they're the main event. They're simply delayed. So here's a question for you. Is there a move you're tempted to make right now that looks like free money? Run it through this list. Which of these cards would it put on the table? That brings us to the central idea of the lesson.
