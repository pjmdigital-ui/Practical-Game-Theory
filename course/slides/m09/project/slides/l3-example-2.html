Example · The argument

Winning the round can lose the game

The situation: You're arguing with your spouse. They once told you something painful and deeply personal.

What happened: You use it against them. It works. The argument ends. Maybe you "win."

What they learn: Vulnerability is dangerous with you. Next time they disclose less. They trust less.

The lesson: The immediate move succeeded. The repeated-game strategy failed.

Strategic thinking sometimes means resisting a short-term win.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Winning the round can lose the game. Suppose you're arguing with your spouse. Once they trusted you with something painful and deeply personal. Now you realize you could use it against them. And it would work. But what game exists tomorrow? They learn that vulnerability is dangerous with you. So next time they disclose less and trust less. The win didn't cost one argument. It cost every future conversation where they might have told you the truth. So what's the move you reach for when you want to win an argument? And what is it teaching? Carry this lesson in one question.
