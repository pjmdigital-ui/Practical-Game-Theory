Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Now we need to be careful. Here's the point. Tit for Tat teaches us something real. But it is not a rule for living. The lesson is not that Tit for Tat is the universally best strategy for life. It isn't. Its success depended on the environment. Look at the cards. It depended on the other strategies present in the tournament. It depended on the payoff structure. It depended on how mistakes were handled. And it depended on whether the interactions continued. Change any of those and the result can change. Axelrod himself and later researchers explored the limits of simple Tit for Tat. That was especially true once misunderstandings or errors entered the game. Think about what happens when Tit for Tat meets an honest mistake. You meant to cooperate. I see a defection. So I defect. Now you see my defection and you copy it. We can end up trading blows for a long time over something nobody intended. We'll dig into that problem in Lesson Four. So what is the lesson worth keeping? It's broader and more useful. Once people expect a future together, reciprocity becomes strategically possible. Rewarding cooperation and answering exploitation only work when there's a next round to do it in. That's the real gift of the shadow of the future. Here's the common mistake. Someone learns about Tit for Tat and decides to match every slight exactly. That's a rule built for a clean tournament. Real relationships are messier. So ask yourself this. Where are you applying a rigid rule to a messy relationship? Next let's look at the other thing a shared future makes possible: reputation.
