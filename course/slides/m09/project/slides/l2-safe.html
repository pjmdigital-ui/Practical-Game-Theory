Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's a consequence people often underestimate. Being easy to deal with increases the value of every future game. Look at the left card. Suppose you're known as someone who keeps agreements. You handle disagreements fairly. You don't exploit other people's mistakes and you own your own. You respond firmly but proportionately to genuine exploitation. Over time people become more willing to trust you. They introduce you to others. They enter deals with you. They share information. They cooperate without excessive protection. You've increased the value of future games. And it doesn't require manipulation. Quite the opposite. Now look at the right card. Suppose you squeeze every penny out of every transaction. You exploit every mistake. You change terms whenever you can get away with it. You use private information opportunistically. You "win" a lot. But eventually people learn that every interaction with you requires protection. So they add contract language. They require deposits. They document everything. They hold back information. They raise their prices to cover the risk. Or they simply refuse to deal with you. You optimized every round. And you made the game itself less valuable. That's winning the battle and losing the war. Here's the misunderstanding. The hard bargainer often believes they're the smart one. They see the wins. They don't see the deals that never came to them. So which card describes how the people around you would describe you? Often the most valuable reputation is simply this. This person is safe to play repeated games with.
