Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the simplest way to carry this model into your life. Before you make an important move, figure out what you're dealing with. Is it a transaction or a relationship? A transaction may end when the exchange ends. A relationship creates another round. And another. And another. This is how you estimate the length of the shadow. Ask whether there's likely to be a next round. Then ask how valuable that next round is to you and to them. Now look at the cards. Most of the important parts of life are repeated games. Your marriage. Your children. Your closest friends. Your employees and your employer. Your business partners. Your community. Every one of those relationships creates another round tomorrow. And here's the part people forget. Even your reputation among people you haven't met yet can create future rounds. The way you treat one client becomes a story another client hears. Now I want to be careful. This doesn't mean every interaction should be calculated. I'm not asking you to run a spreadsheet on your marriage. It means the future deserves a place in the decision. The common mistake is to treat a relationship like a transaction. You optimize this round as if it were the last one. You might even win it. But you've used the tools of a one-shot game inside a repeated one. So take a moment with this question. Which relationship in your life have you been handling like a transaction lately?
