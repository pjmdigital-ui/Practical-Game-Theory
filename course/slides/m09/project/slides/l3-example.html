Example · Words versus history

The lesson nobody meant to teach

The situation: A manager tells the team: "Bring me bad news early."

What happened: An employee reports a serious problem right away. The manager becomes furious. They humiliate and blame the employee. Next month nobody reports anything.

The words said: "Report problems."

The behavior said: "Reporting problems is dangerous."

The lesson: people respond to the history of the game. Not to your words.

Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Remember our manager from earlier modules? Here's the point of this example. People respond to what you did last time. Not to what you said. The manager tells employees, "Bring me bad news early." That sounds great. It's exactly what a good leader should say. Then an employee discovers a serious problem and reports it right away. They did exactly what they were asked. And the manager becomes furious. They humiliate the employee. They blame them for the problem. Now what happens next month when another employee discovers bad news? The manager may be surprised that nobody says anything. They might even repeat the speech. "I told you all to bring me bad news early!" But the employees aren't simply responding to the manager's words. They're responding to the history of the game. Look at the two lines on the slide. The manager's words said, "Report problems." The manager's behavior said, "Reporting problems is dangerous." Which message will shape future behavior? Probably the second. That's the shadow of the future. Today's response is teaching tomorrow's players what to expect. And notice who learned the lesson. It wasn't only the employee who got blamed. Everyone who watched learned it too. One response taught the whole team. Here's the misunderstanding. The manager thinks the team lacks courage or integrity. It doesn't. The team is reading the game accurately. So ask yourself this. Is there something you keep asking people to do that your own reactions punish? Now let's see the same pattern at home.
