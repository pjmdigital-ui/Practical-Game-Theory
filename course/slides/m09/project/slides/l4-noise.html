Internomics · Module 9 — The Shadow of the Future

Here's the point. Punishment can protect cooperation. But punishment also creates its own problem. Follow the chain on the slide. Suppose you betray me accidentally. I think it was intentional. So I punish you. You believe my punishment was unprovoked. So you punish me. Now I see your punishment as a further betrayal. So I punish you again. What began as a mistake has become a feud. And notice that each step made sense to the person taking it. Nobody in this chain thinks they're the aggressor. Everyone thinks they're responding. This is where real relationships get much harder than the clean models. In a tournament every move is clearly visible. In life we don't observe each other perfectly. We misunderstand. We forget. We mishear. We misread motives. Game theorists call that noise. And noise changes which strategies work well. In a quiet world a strict rule like "punish every defection" can work fine. In a noisy world that same rule will eventually punish an honest mistake. Then it starts a cycle nobody wanted. Here's the common misunderstanding. People assume they can tell the difference between a mistake and a betrayal just by looking. Often you can't. You're seeing the outcome. You're guessing at the intent. Think about a text message that sounded cold. Or a meeting invite that left you off. How often did you assume the worst? And how often were you right? Here's a question for you. Where in your life might a feud have started with somebody's honest mistake? So what stops a mistake from turning into a feud? The answer is repair.
