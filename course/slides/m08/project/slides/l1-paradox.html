Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

This is Schelling's paradox. You can sometimes strengthen your position by openly making your own alternatives worse. Thomas Schelling made this idea central to the study of strategic commitment. When he received the Nobel Prize the committee summarized one of his key insights in almost those exact words. A bargaining player can sometimes strengthen its position by openly making its own alternatives worse. Let's unpack the three pieces on screen. First, it's not about tougher words. You aren't just sounding more serious. Your actual options change. Second, tomorrow shapes today. The other player makes a decision now based on what they expect you to do later. Change what's possible later and you change their best move now. Third, notice the word "openly." The other side has to see the change. If they can't see it they can't respond to it. We'll come back to that in the next lesson. Go back to our negotiation. If I really can't go below a hundred thousand and you can see that, you stop asking "Will he cave?" Pushing lower just wastes your time. Here's a question to sit with. Where in your own life have you watched someone gain leverage by giving something up? Once you look for it you find it everywhere.
