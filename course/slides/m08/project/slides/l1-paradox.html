Schelling's paradox

Openly making your own alternatives worse can strengthen your position.

Thomas Schelling made this paradox central to the study of strategic commitment.

Not tougher words

You don't just sound more serious. Your options really change.

Tomorrow shapes today

They act now on what they expect you to do later.

Openly

It works because the other side can see the change.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

This is Schelling's paradox. You can sometimes strengthen your position by openly making your own alternatives worse. When Thomas Schelling received the Nobel Prize the committee summarized one of his key insights in almost those words. Three pieces matter. It's not about tougher words. Your actual options change. Tomorrow shapes today. The other player decides now based on what they expect you to do later. And notice the word "openly." If they can't see the change they can't respond to it.
