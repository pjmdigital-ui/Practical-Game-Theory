Research spotlight · Swope, Cadigan & Schmitt

Commitment in the bargaining lab

What it shows

Credible commitment could raise the committer's payoff. This was especially true where others had reasons to hold out. It could also raise the chance of bargaining failure and reduce efficiency.

What it doesn't prove

That commitment helps in every negotiation. That a "final offer" works because it sounds firm. These are specific and structured lab games.

The evidence doesn't say "commit harder and you'll win." Commitment trades off credibility and power against flexibility and agreement.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

The second spotlight is experimental. Kurtis Swope and John Cadigan and Pamela Schmitt ran bargaining games where players could credibly commit to a position. Commitment could increase the payoff to the person making it. But it could also increase the probability of bargaining failure and reduce efficiency. That's one mechanism with two sides. You gain power by eliminating flexibility. But flexibility might have let a deal happen. The book also cites a 2026 experiment where people used commitment less aggressively than theory predicts. Reciprocity and equal opportunity seemed to matter. So the evidence doesn't say "commit harder and you'll win." Now let's turn this into a tool.
