Three things we call "commitment"

Intention

"I plan to do something."

"I'm going to do it." Sincere. Changes nothing about the game.

Promise

"I tell you that I plan to do something."

"I promise I'll do it." Communication. May or may not be backed by anything.

Strategic commitment

I take an action that changes the incentives or options around what I can do later.

"I've arranged things so that doing anything else would be extremely costly."

Only the third changes the future game.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's the point of this slide. Only one of these three things actually changes the game. We say things like "I'm committed to getting in shape." Or "I'm committed to this business." Or "I'm committed to making this relationship work." Those statements can be completely sincere. But that's not the game-theoretic meaning we're after. So let's separate three things. The first is an intention. I plan to do something. "I'm going to do it." It may be heartfelt. But nothing about the game has changed. The second is a promise. I tell you that I plan to do something. "I promise I'll do it." That's communication. It may or may not be backed by anything. The third is a strategic commitment. I take an action that changes the incentives or the options around what I can do later. Now doing something else becomes difficult or costly or impossible. In plain words it sounds like this. "I've arranged things so that doing anything else would be extremely costly." Here's why that matters. The third statement can affect behavior before the future even arrives. Other people know something about what you're likely to do when that moment comes. So they adjust now. The common mistake is to think a louder promise becomes a commitment. It doesn't. Volume doesn't change the payoffs. Only an action does. Ask yourself this. Which of your own "commitments" are really just intentions?
