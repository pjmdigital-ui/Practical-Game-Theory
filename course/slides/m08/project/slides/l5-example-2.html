Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's the second version of the trap. This one happens inside teams all the time. The point is the same. A rule that leaves no room for judgment can force you into a bad choice later. Here's the situation. A manager wants to send a strong message. So the manager declares "Anyone who makes this mistake is automatically fired." It's clear. It's credible. Everyone hears it. Then what happens? The best employee in the company makes an honest mistake under unusual circumstances. Now look at the manager's options. There are only two. Break the commitment. Or carry out a punishment that no longer makes sense. If the manager breaks it everyone learns that the rule wasn't real. Every future rule from this manager carries less weight. That's the cost we talked about in Lesson Three. If the manager follows through the company loses its best person over an honest mistake. Either way it costs something. That's the commitment trap. Here's the lesson. This is why strategic commitment should never be confused with stubbornness. A good commitment considers in advance what happens if the world changes. What happens if the situation is unusual? What happens if the person involved is your best performer? Here's the misunderstanding. People think firmness means having no exceptions. But a well-designed commitment can be firm on the things that matter and still leave room for circumstances nobody predicted. So think about the rules you've set for your team or your kids. Does any of them have no room for an honest mistake? That brings us to this lesson's takeaway.
