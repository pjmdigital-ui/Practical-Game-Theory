Example · The $100,000 negotiation

Same number, different game

The situation

I say $100,000 is my absolute minimum. You offer $85,000. I say no.

What happened

You wait. I come back at $95,000. My "absolute minimum" wasn't absolute.

The lesson

If a binding agreement truly stops me below $100,000, pushing stops making sense.

Fewer options. A stronger position. No need to ask "Will he cave?"

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let's play it out. I say a hundred thousand is my absolute minimum. You offer eighty-five. I say no. You wait. I come back at ninety-five. Now you'll treat every "final" number from me as an opening bid. Now imagine I genuinely cannot accept less. Maybe I've entered an agreement that makes a lower price impossible. You no longer ask "Will he cave?" If the constraint is real and visible, pushing lower stops making sense. I have fewer options. Yet my position may be stronger. It works because the constraint is real and not because I sound confident. That's our first takeaway.
