Example · The $100,000 negotiation

Same number, different game

The claim

I say $100,000 is my floor. You offer $85,000. I say no.

The test

You wait. I come back at $95,000. My "absolute minimum" wasn't absolute.

The real constraint

A binding agreement means I genuinely can't take less. Pushing below $100,000 stops making sense.

Fewer options, and a stronger position.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let's play it out. I say a hundred thousand is my floor. You offer eighty-five. I say no. You wait. And I come back at ninety-five. What did you learn? My absolute minimum wasn't absolute. Now imagine a different situation. For some reason I genuinely cannot accept less than a hundred thousand. Maybe I've entered an agreement that makes a lower price impossible. Maybe another party has an enforceable claim. Maybe I've delegated the decision to someone with no authority to go lower. Whatever the mechanism, my future choice set is different. You no longer have to ask, "Will he cave?" If that constraint is real and visible to you, continuing to push below a hundred thousand may no longer make sense. I have fewer options, and yet strategically my position has become stronger.
