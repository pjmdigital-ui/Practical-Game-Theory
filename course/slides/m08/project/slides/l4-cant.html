Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

There's a major strategic difference between "I don't want to" and "I can't." When you say "I'd rather not," you invite pressure. Why? Because the other side knows your preference can change. Push hard enough, wait long enough, and maybe it will. But when you can truthfully say, "My authority stops at this number," something different has happened. It's not your mood that's changed. It's your available actions. This is one reason delegation can create commitment. A decision-maker can sometimes strengthen a bargaining position by transferring authority to someone whose choices are more restricted. Schelling's commitment logic has been applied to exactly this kind of delegation and institutional constraint. The key word, though, is truthfully. We'll come to the fake version in a moment.
