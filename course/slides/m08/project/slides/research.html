Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Two research spotlights this week. The first is theory. When Thomas Schelling received the Nobel Prize, the committee summarized one of his key insights this way: a bargaining player can sometimes strengthen its position by openly making its own alternatives worse. The committee's materials also noted applications to delegating decision power, which is exactly the "my authority stops at this number" logic from Lesson Four. What it suggests for you: ask how your future options look to the other side, because changing them can change their best response today. What it does not prove: that commitment always creates power, that you should never leave yourself an escape route, or that the most extreme commitment wins. Schelling showed the mechanism. He didn't promise it works every time.
