The strongest commitment isn't always the best

Two credible, incompatible commitments create no deal.

"Nothing under $100." "Nothing above $90." Evaluate a commitment not by how strongly it binds you, but by the game it creates.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Suppose two countries are in a confrontation. Country A publicly commits: "If you cross this line, we will retaliate." Country B publicly commits: "We will cross the line." Both commitments may be highly credible. That doesn't mean the outcome is good. It may mean both sides have removed every path that would have let them de-escalate. You can see the same thing at a much smaller scale. I commit: "I will accept nothing under a hundred." You commit: "I will pay nothing above ninety." Congratulations. We've created no deal. Both commitments are perfectly credible. The problem is that they're incompatible. So game theory doesn't teach "commit more." It teaches: understand what commitment does to the game. Never evaluate a commitment solely by how strongly it binds you. Evaluate it by the game it creates.
