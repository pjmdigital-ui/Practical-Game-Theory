Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let's practice. Meet Dana. Dana runs a small design studio. She's renewing a contract with her largest client. The client's procurement lead is Marcus. He asks for a fifteen percent cut. Dana says "Our rates are final. We can't go lower." Then when he pushes she agrees to five percent off. Now he wants another five percent. And Dana says this time she really means it. Marcus also mentions something. His authority caps the budget at a set number. Anything above it goes to his CFO. Meanwhile at home Dana has a teenage son. Three times this month she's told him that if he misses curfew again he loses the car "for the rest of the year." And each time he kept the keys. And there's one more thing. Dana is drafting a public post. It promises that her studio will "never charge clients for revisions." But her biggest project next quarter has a scope that's hard to predict. So that's four commitments in one person's week. One is a price. One is someone else's claimed constraint. One is a threat at home. And one is a public promise. As you read the cards ask yourself the module's question for each one. What makes this commitment real? Or what would? Then go to the next slide for the questions.
