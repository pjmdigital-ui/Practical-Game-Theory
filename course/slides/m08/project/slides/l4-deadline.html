Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's something people don't expect. A real commitment isn't always a weapon. Sometimes it helps both sides by making the future clearer. Suppose you're negotiating with someone who keeps saying "Maybe I'll accept. Maybe I won't." Think about where that leaves you. You don't know how long they'll keep bargaining. You don't know whether the next offer will be final. You don't know whether waiting will improve your outcome. So you hedge. You stall. You test. Everyone burns time. Now suppose they credibly commit. "This offer expires Friday at noon, and after that the asset goes to another buyer." If that's true uncertainty drops. You know more about the future game. And that can actually make agreement easier. You can stop guessing and just decide. This is another reason commitment is not simply an aggressive bargaining weapon. It can also provide clarity. The same bargaining research we'll look at later makes this point too. Credible ultimatums can reduce uncertainty in some settings. They can also remove the incentive for strategic delay. Why wait if waiting gets you nothing? Notice the word "credibly" though. A fake deadline does the opposite. If Friday comes and goes and the offer is still there, you've learned the deadline was just pressure. And the next deadline will mean less. So here's the question. When you set a deadline is it real?
