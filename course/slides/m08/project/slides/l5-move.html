Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Remember Module Three? Think in responses. That applies here too. A commitment is itself a move. And the other player gets to respond to it. Sometimes they respond before you expect. Here's the point. The whole reason you commit is to change their decision problem. So their response is the entire game. Look at the options on screen. Maybe they concede. That's what you were hoping for. Maybe they walk away. Now there's no deal at all. Maybe they make their own commitment. Then you're in the hundred-versus-ninety standoff we just saw. Maybe they search for an alternative. You've just pushed them to your competitor. Maybe they retaliate. Maybe they simply stop negotiating. Notice that only one of those six is the outcome you wanted. The other five are real possibilities too. And a hard commitment can make several of them more likely. If you don't think about their response you're missing half the game. So before you lock anything in ask this. How will they respond to the fact that I'm limiting my options? And then ask the follow-up. What happens if they refuse to move? If your honest answer is "then we're both stuck" you may want a smaller commitment. Or a more flexible one. Here's the misunderstanding. People picture the other side as a wall that will simply bend. But they're a player too. They're running the same kind of analysis you are. So ask yourself.
