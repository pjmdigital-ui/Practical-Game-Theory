Example · Boundaries are commitments

"I'm ending the conversation"

The situation

Someone keeps speaking disrespectfully to you. You say: "If this continues, I'm ending the conversation."

What happened

It happens again. You stay. It happens again. You stay. They learn it's cheap talk.

The lesson

Calmly say "We'll continue this when we can talk respectfully." Then leave. Every time.

A boundary is "If this occurs, here is what I will do."

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Now let's bring this into everyday adult life. Boundaries are commitments. That's one of the clearest applications of everything in this module. Here's the situation. Someone repeatedly speaks disrespectfully to you. You tell them "If this continues, I'm ending the conversation." Then what happens? It happens again. You stay. It happens again. You stay. What has the other person learned? They've learned that your statement doesn't constrain your behavior. It's cheap talk. And notice that you didn't just fail once. You trained them. Now imagine a different version. When the behavior happens you calmly say "We'll continue this when we can talk respectfully." And you leave. No drama. No speech. You just go. If you do that consistently the other person begins to understand something. The boundary changes the game. Their behavior now has a predictable cost. They lose the conversation. Here's the most common misunderstanding about boundaries. People think a boundary is a demand. "I demand that you behave differently." That's not a boundary. You can't control what another person does. A boundary is this. "If this occurs, here is what I will do." It's about your action. Not theirs. And it becomes strategically meaningful only when your future behavior makes it credible. So the hard part isn't saying it. The hard part is doing it calmly every single time.
