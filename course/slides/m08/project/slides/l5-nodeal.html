Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let's bring that down to the size of an everyday negotiation. The point is that the strongest commitment isn't always the best commitment. Suppose we're negotiating. I commit "I will accept nothing under a hundred dollars." You commit "I will pay nothing above ninety." Congratulations. We've created no deal. Now here's what makes this interesting. Both commitments may be perfectly credible. Neither of us is bluffing. Each of us really is bound. The problem isn't credibility at all. The problem is that the two commitments are incompatible. There's no number left that works for both of us. And because we both locked ourselves in we can't go looking for one. So here's a crucial lesson. Never evaluate a commitment solely by how strongly it binds you. Evaluate it by the game it creates. A commitment that's rock solid but leaves no room for agreement may be worse than having no commitment at all. This is exactly why the experiments we'll look at in a few minutes find two results at once. Commitment can increase bargaining power. And commitment can increase bargaining failure. Those aren't contradictions. They're the same mechanism working in two directions. Here's the common misunderstanding. People assume the person who commits first and hardest will win. Sometimes that's true. But if the other side commits too you both lose the deal. So before you lock in a number ask yourself a simple question. What happens if they've already locked in theirs?
