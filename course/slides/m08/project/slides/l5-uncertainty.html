Uncertainty makes commitment riskier

The less you know about the future, the more flexibility is worth.

Public commitment creates discipline and rigidity at the same time.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Suppose you're entering a game where the payoffs may change, new information may emerge, the other players may change, the rules may change, even your own preferences may change. Committing strongly today may make tomorrow's decision worse. So ask: how much uncertainty am I locking myself into? Public commitment is a good example of both sides. If I privately plan to finish a project by October first and I miss it, maybe nobody knows. If I announce the launch date to my customers, reversal now carries reputational and financial costs, and embarrassment. I've made failure more expensive, which builds discipline. But if new evidence shows the launch would be unsafe, that same public commitment may pressure me to do something foolish just to save face. There's no universal answer here. That's why this is a mental model, not a rule.
