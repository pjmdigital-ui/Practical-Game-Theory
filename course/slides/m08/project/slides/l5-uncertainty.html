Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's another cost of commitment that's easy to miss. The less you know about the future the more valuable flexibility can become. Suppose you're entering a game where a lot could shift. Look at the list on screen. The payoffs may change. New information may emerge. The other players may change. The rules may change. Even your own preferences may change. In a game like that committing strongly today may make tomorrow's decision worse. You'd be locking in an answer before you even know the question. So here's another strategic question to add to your toolkit. How much uncertainty am I locking myself into? If the future is reasonably predictable commitment may be valuable. You know roughly what you're signing up for. If the future is extremely uncertain flexibility may be worth more. You'll want room to adapt when things move. Now I want to be honest with you. There's no universal answer here. I can't give you a rule that says "commit when uncertainty is below this line." That's why this is a mental model and not a rule. You have to look at the specific game you're in. Here's the common misunderstanding. People often commit hardest when they feel most anxious. A firm stance feels safe when the future feels scary. But that's often exactly when flexibility is worth the most. So think about a commitment you're considering right now. What's the one thing most likely to change before you have to deliver?
