Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let's put this lesson into one negotiation. The point is that the same request can meet three very different answers. And only one of them truly changes the game. Here's the situation. You're negotiating on behalf of a company. The other side asks you to approve another ten percent discount. Answer one is "I'd rather not." What happens? That invites a push. They know your preference can change. So they'll lean on it. Answer two has to be true to work. "My authority stops at this number. Anything beyond it requires approval from the board." That's different. It changes their decision problem. Not because the board is magical. It's because your available actions really are different. Pushing you harder won't produce the discount. They'd have to go another route. Now answer three. It's the one you've probably heard yourself. "I'd love to help you, but corporate won't let me." Maybe that's completely true. Or maybe corporate is sitting ten feet away and would approve it instantly. Same kind of words. Very different games. So what's the lesson? On your side only claim the constraint when it's real. On their side don't take "I can't" at face value. Ask what actually binds them. Is there a written limit? Is there a person who approves exceptions? Could we move on scope or timing instead? Here's the question for you. Which of these three answers do you give most often? And which one do you hear most? That leads right to this lesson's takeaway.
