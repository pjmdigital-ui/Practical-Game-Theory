Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let me name the mistakes I see most often. First is confusing severity with credibility. People make big threats and then don't follow through. A huge threat you won't carry out does less than a small one you will. And every unenforced threat teaches the other person that your words don't predict your behavior. If you won't follow through don't create the threat. Second is keeping the commitment hidden. A private constraint may shape your own behavior. But it can't change their present decision unless they know about it. Or unless they can credibly infer it. If you want it to affect them make it visible. Third is believing every "my hands are tied." Sometimes it's true. Sometimes corporate is sitting ten feet away. Don't argue and don't just fold. Ask what actually binds them. Fourth is mistaking rigidity for strength. People judge a commitment by how strongly it binds them. But two credible and incompatible commitments produce no deal. And under deep uncertainty flexibility may be worth more than credibility. Refusing to adapt with no strategic benefit is just rigidity. And "we've invested too much to quit" is the sunk-cost fallacy. Know when a past commitment has become a liability. Here's a question for you. Which of these four do you fall into most often? Be honest. Most of us have a favorite. Once you know yours you can watch for it. Now let's take this into your week.
