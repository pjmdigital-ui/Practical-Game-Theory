Common mistakes

Big threats, no follow-through

Severity isn't credibility. Each unenforced threat teaches them your words don't predict your behavior.

Keeping it hidden

A private constraint can't change their decision unless they know about it or can infer it.

Believing every "my hands are tied"

Sometimes corporate is ten feet away. Ask what actually binds them.

Mistaking rigidity for strength

Judge a commitment by the game it creates. Not by stubbornness. Not by sunk costs.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here are the mistakes I see most often. First is confusing severity with credibility. A huge threat you won't carry out does less than a small one you will. Second is keeping the commitment hidden. A private constraint can't change their decision unless they know about it. Third is believing every "my hands are tied." Don't argue and don't just fold. Ask what actually binds them. Fourth is mistaking rigidity for strength. Two credible and incompatible commitments produce no deal. And "we've invested too much to quit" is the sunk-cost fallacy.
