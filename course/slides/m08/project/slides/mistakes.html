Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let me name the mistakes I see most often. First, confusing severity with credibility, and threatening what you won't do. A huge threat you won't carry out does less than a small one you will, and every unenforced threat teaches the other person that your words don't predict your behavior. Second, keeping the commitment hidden. A private constraint may shape your behavior, but it can't change their present decision unless they know about it or can credibly infer it. Third, believing every "my hands are tied." Sometimes it's true. Sometimes corporate is ten feet away. Ask what actually binds them. And fourth, judging a commitment by how strongly it binds you. Two credible, incompatible commitments produce no deal, and under deep uncertainty flexibility may be worth more than credibility. Refusing to adapt with no strategic benefit is just rigidity, and "we've invested too much to quit" is the sunk-cost fallacy. Know when a past commitment has become a liability.
