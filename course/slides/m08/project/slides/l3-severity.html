A threat is only as good as the future decision

Severity is not credibility.

The wrong question

"How severe is the threat?"

The right question

"Will carrying it out still make sense when the time comes?"

If following through will be too costly for you, the other side can predict that you'll back down.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's where most of us get threats wrong. We think the bigger the consequence the more seriously the other person will take it. But severity is not the same thing as credibility. Think about it from their side. They aren't asking how scary your threat sounds. They're asking what you'll actually do when the moment arrives. And they can reason about that. Go back to the bargaining table. Someone says "If you reject my offer, I'll walk." But when the time comes walking away would be extremely costly for them. The other player can reasonably think "You say you'll leave. When you actually have to decide you probably won't." The size of the threat didn't help at all. In fact a huge threat can be less believable. The bigger the consequence the more it often costs you to carry it out. So the important question is never how severe the threat is. It's this. Will carrying it out still make sense when the time comes? If the answer is no then a big threat is just a big statement. And people can usually tell. Here's a quick test you can use. Before you threaten anything picture the moment you'd have to follow through. Picture how you'd feel. Picture what it would cost you. If you flinch the other person will probably see that coming. So ask yourself. What's a threat you've made that you knew deep down you'd never carry out? Next let's see how threats and promises share the same problem.
