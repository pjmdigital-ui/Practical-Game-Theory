Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

This connects straight back to last module. The point is that signaling and commitment answer two different questions. And one action can do both at once. Imagine I put fifty thousand dollars into a new business. What does that tell you? It says I believe in this business. That's signaling. It reveals something about me. Now suppose that investment is irreversible. The money is in and I can't get it back. That gives me a strong incentive to keep supporting the business. That's commitment. It changes what I'm likely to do later. Same action. Two different effects. So here are the two questions to keep apart. Signaling asks what this action tells the other player about me. Commitment asks how this action changes what I can or will do later. Why bother separating them? Because real actions often serve more than one strategic purpose at the same time. They can reveal information. And they can change future incentives. If you blur the two you'll misread what's going on. You might think someone is just showing off when they've actually locked themselves in. Or you might think they're locked in when they've only sent a message. Here's a question for you. Think of something you did recently that cost you something. Did it mostly tell people about you? Or did it mostly change what you'll do next?
