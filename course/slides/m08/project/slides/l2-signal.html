Signal or commitment?

One action can do both

Signaling asks

What does this action tell the other player about me?

Commitment asks

How does this action change what I can or will do later?

And to change their decision, a commitment usually has to be observable.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

This connects straight back to last module. Imagine I put fifty thousand dollars into a new business. That action may tell you: I believe in this business. That's signaling. But suppose the investment is irreversible and now gives me a strong incentive to keep supporting the business. That's commitment. One action can do both, but they answer different questions. Signaling asks what this action tells the other player about me. Commitment asks how it changes what I can or will do later. Keeping those separate makes your analysis much cleaner. And one more thing: suppose I secretly make it impossible for myself to accept less than a hundred thousand. You don't know. Will your behavior change? Probably not. My private commitment may affect my future behavior, but it can't affect your present decision unless you know about it, or can credibly infer it.
