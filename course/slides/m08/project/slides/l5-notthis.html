Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Three things commitment is not. First, it's not the sunk-cost fallacy. Suppose I invest a hundred thousand in a project, and six months later we discover it's terrible. Should we continue because "we've already invested too much to quit"? No. A strategic commitment uses today's action to deliberately change tomorrow's incentives. The sunk-cost fallacy lets yesterday's irreversible cost distort today's decision. A smart strategist makes commitments, and also knows when a past commitment has become a liability. Second, it's not toughness. A person who never changes position may simply be stubborn. If refusing to adapt has no strategic benefit and enormous cost, that's not sophistication. That's rigidity. And third, it's not a license to trap people. Someone who deliberately creates a crisis and says, "I've made it impossible for either of us to turn back, so now you have to do what I want," is drifting into coercion. Truth, autonomy, dignity, informed choice. A contract, a boundary, a transparent deadline, a public promise: those help people coordinate. Destroying someone's meaningful ability to choose does not.
