What strategic commitment is not

Not sunk cost

"We've invested too much to quit" lets yesterday's cost distort today's choice.

Not toughness

Someone who never changes position may just be stubborn. No strategic benefit means rigidity.

It has a purpose

It changes incentives or expectations or options. The gain must justify the lost flexibility.

A smart strategist makes commitments. A smart strategist also knows when a past commitment has become a liability.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Two things get confused with commitment. First, it's not the sunk-cost fallacy. Say I invest a hundred thousand dollars in a project. Six months later we learn the project is terrible. Should we continue because "we've already invested too much to quit"? No. A strategic commitment uses today's action to change tomorrow's incentives. The sunk-cost fallacy lets yesterday's irreversible cost distort today's decision. Second, commitment is not toughness. Someone who never changes position may simply be stubborn. Commitment is valuable only when the constraint improves the game enough to justify losing flexibility. Next let's talk about ethics.
