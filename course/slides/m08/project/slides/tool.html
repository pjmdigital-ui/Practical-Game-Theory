Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Your tool this week is the Commitment Test. You'll find it in your workbook. Use it before you make a threat or a promise or a deadline. Use it before you set a boundary or name a "final" position. And use it to evaluate one that someone makes to you. Start by writing the commitment down. Then classify it. Is it an intention, a promise or a strategic commitment? Part one asks what actually binds it. Is it a contract? A rule? An authority? Or merely words? And can the other player observe or verify it? Part two asks what future option you're giving up. Be precise. Then ask what strategic benefit the constraint creates. Part three is for threats and promises. When the moment arrives will carrying it out still make sense? Is there a smaller consequence you'd enforce every time? Part four is for boundaries. What will you actually do when it's crossed? Calmly. Consistently. Part five is their response. How will their best response change? Could this produce an impasse? What if they refuse to move? Part six is the cost of commitment. What happens if new information appears? What flexibility will you wish you still had? And be honest here. Is this strategy? Or is it stubbornness and sunk cost? Part seven is ethics. Does it preserve truth and autonomy and dignity and informed choice? Then decide. Commit as planned. Commit to something smaller or more enforceable. Or stay flexible. And write down why. Here's the common mistake. People skip straight to the decision. The value is in the questions. Let's practice with a scenario.
