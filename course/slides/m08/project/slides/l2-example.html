Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's the example from the book. It puts all three ideas side by side. The point is simple. Words describe what you hope to do. A commitment changes what it costs to do something else. Here's the situation. I say "I'm definitely waking up at five tomorrow." That's an intention. It's a plan in my head. Then I tell my training partner "I promise I'll meet you at the gym at five-thirty." That's a promise. Now I've said it out loud to someone. It might help a little. But nothing has really changed about tomorrow morning. Now here's what happened in the third version. I arrange for my training partner to pick me up at five-fifteen. And I've already paid for a session that I lose if I don't show up. I've altered the future decision. At five in the morning getting out of bed now has a different payoff. Staying in bed means someone is waiting in my driveway. It means losing money I already spent. I didn't just become more motivated. I changed the game my future self will face. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Most of us treat these problems as willpower problems. We try to feel more committed. But feeling committed is still just intention. Changing the payoffs is what moves behavior. So try this. Take one goal you keep "committing" to. Is it an intention, a promise or a real commitment? What would it take to make it the third one? That brings us to this lesson's takeaway.
