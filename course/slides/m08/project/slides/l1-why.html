Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

So why would less freedom ever help? The short answer is this. A commitment changes what the other player expects you to do later. And what they expect tomorrow shapes what they do today. Picture two players across a bargaining table. Player A can accept. A can reject. A can delay or compromise or walk away. Player B knows all of that. Now A says "If you reject my offer, I'll walk." Sounds tough. But suppose walking away would be extremely costly for A when the time comes. B can reasonably think "You say you'll leave. But when you actually have to make that decision you probably won't." A's flexibility undermines A's own threat. Now change one thing. Suppose A genuinely eliminates the ability to keep negotiating after a deadline. Maybe the offer expires because another commitment becomes binding. Now B's decision changes. Here's what I want you to notice. A didn't get louder. A didn't sound more serious. The future game itself is different. That's the logic behind Thomas Schelling's insight. And here's a common misunderstanding to watch for. People think commitment is about attitude. It isn't. It's about what options actually exist when the moment arrives. So ask yourself.
