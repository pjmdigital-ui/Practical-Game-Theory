Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

This is one of the simplest practical lessons in the whole module. Don't threaten what you won't do. People often make threats in moments of emotion. "I'll never speak to you again." "I'm quitting." "I'm cancelling everything." "If this happens again, you're done." Then they don't follow through. And each time that happens something changes. The other person updates. The next threat carries less information. This isn't only about personal integrity. Integrity matters. But this is game theory too. You're teaching the other player that your stated consequence does not predict your actual behavior. And once they learn that they'll discount everything you say. Even the threats you really mean. So the rule is simple. If you're not prepared to follow through don't create the threat. Use a consequence you can actually enforce. Promises work exactly the same way. Picture a manager who keeps saying "If you do this, I'll reward you." Then doesn't. What happens? Eventually employees stop responding to the promise. Now here's the trap. That manager might decide the team has a motivation problem. Or a communication problem. But the real problem may be credibility destroyed by failed commitments. This shows up in parenting. It shows up in partnerships and in sales. It shows up in leadership and friendships. Every promise that isn't kept alters the future game. So here's a question to be honest with yourself about.
