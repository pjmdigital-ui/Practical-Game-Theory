Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's the first version of what I call the commitment trap. The point is that a commitment can be smart when you make it and costly when the world moves. Here's the situation. A business owner publicly announces "We will never raise our prices." It sounds admirable. Customers love it. And honestly it probably works for a while. It's a clear and credible promise. People can plan around it. It sets the business apart. Then what happens? Years later costs double. And now the company has a problem. Every option left is painful. Keep the promise and absorb costs that may sink the business. Or break a public promise and pay the reputational price. What's the lesson? The original commitment created credibility. But it also destroyed flexibility. And nobody asked the key question at the start. What happens if the world changes? That's the question a good commitment always asks before it's made. Notice how this ties back to uncertainty. "Never" is a very long time. The longer a commitment runs the more things can change underneath it. Payoffs shift. Costs shift. Customers shift. Here's the common misunderstanding. People think the problem was making a promise at all. It wasn't. The problem was making a promise with no room for a world that changes. A narrower promise might have kept most of the credibility. So here's your question. Is there a "never" or an "always" you've said at work or at home that might not survive the next few years?
