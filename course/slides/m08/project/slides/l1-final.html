The problem with "final"

"This is my absolute minimum" is only a statement.

If you can still accept less later, the other side can test you. And when you move, they learn your words didn't bind you.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Imagine we're negotiating, and I tell you, "A hundred thousand is my absolute minimum. I will not accept a dollar less." What do you do? That depends on whether you believe me. Maybe a hundred thousand really is my floor. Or maybe I'd take ninety if you held firm long enough. Or eighty. You don't know. My statement is only a statement. So you might test me. And here's the part people miss: if I move, you don't just get a better price. You learn something. You learn that my words don't bind me. That lesson carries into every "final" number I give you after that. So the question isn't whether I sound firm. It's whether anything actually stops me from changing my mind.
