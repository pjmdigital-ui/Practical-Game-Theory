What makes Dana's positions real

The fake "can't": she moved, so Marcus learned her words don't bind her. Published rates or a partner who approves exceptions would.

The curfew: a year without the car punishes Dana too. Try "Home after curfew, no car next weekend," every time.

The pledge: uncertain scope makes it a trap. "Two rounds of revisions included" keeps most of the credibility.

Marcus's cap: may be real. Ask what binds it: is it documented, can it go to the CFO, is there room on scope or timing?

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's how I'd see it. One: Dana said "can't" and then moved, so Marcus learned her words don't bind her, and "this time I mean it" is still only words. A real position needs something he can see that makes reversal costly, like published rates she applies to every client, or a partner who must approve exceptions. If nothing like that exists, an honest "I'd rather not, and here's why" beats a fake constraint. Two: severity isn't credibility. Taking the car for a year would be miserable for Dana too, and three unenforced threats have taught her son her words don't predict her behavior. Something smaller, enforced every time: home after curfew, no car next weekend. Three: going public builds credibility, but with an unpredictable scope she risks the commitment trap. "Two rounds of revisions included" keeps much of the credibility without locking in all that uncertainty. Four: delegated authority can be real, and if it is, pushing above it accomplishes nothing. But she should ask, respectfully, what actually binds him. Five: she can use transparent deadlines, published rates, and enforceable boundaries. She should not invent constraints or engineer a crisis that leaves Marcus no meaningful choice.
