What makes Dana's positions real

The fake "can't": she moved. Marcus learned her words don't bind her. Published rates or a partner who approves exceptions would.

The curfew: a year without the car punishes Dana too. Try "Home after curfew means no car next weekend." Every time.

The pledge: uncertain scope makes it a trap. "Two rounds of revisions included" keeps most of the credibility.

Marcus's cap: it may be real. Ask what binds it. Is it documented? Can it go to the CFO? Is there room on scope or timing?

Ethics: transparent deadlines and real boundaries are fine. Invented constraints and engineered crises are not.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's how I'd see it. One. Dana said "can't" and then she moved. So Marcus learned her words don't bind her. "This time I mean it" is still only words. A real position needs something Marcus can see that makes reversal costly. That could be published rates she applies to every client. Or a partner who must approve exceptions. And if nothing like that exists an honest "I'd rather not, and here's why" beats a fake constraint. Two. Severity isn't credibility. Taking the car for a year would be miserable for Dana too. So her son predicts she'll back down. And three unenforced threats have taught him that her words don't predict her behavior. A credible version is smaller and enforced every time. "If you're home after curfew, you don't have the car next weekend." Three. Going public raises the cost of reversal. That builds credibility. But her next big project has an unpredictable scope. If revisions balloon she faces the commitment trap. She'd have to break a public promise or absorb costs that no longer make sense. A narrower version keeps much of the credibility. "Two rounds of revisions included." Four. Delegated authority can be a real commitment. If it is then pushing above it accomplishes nothing. But "my hands are tied" can be fake. So Dana should ask respectfully what actually binds him. Is the cap documented? Can it go to the CFO? Is there room on scope or timing instead? Five. Dana can use transparent deadlines and published rates and enforceable boundaries. She should not invent constraints. And she should not engineer a crisis that leaves Marcus no meaningful choice.
