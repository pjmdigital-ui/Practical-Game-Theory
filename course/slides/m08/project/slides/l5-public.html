Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Public commitment is a perfect example of how one tool can cut both ways. Here's the point. Going public raises the cost of reversal. That creates discipline. It also creates rigidity. Let me walk you through it. Suppose I privately decide I'm going to finish a project by October first. If I miss it maybe nobody knows. There's no real cost to slipping. Now suppose I publicly announce the launch date to my customers. Reversal now carries reputational costs. It carries financial costs. There's embarrassment and customer disappointment. I've made failure more expensive. And that can increase my incentive to follow through. That's the discipline side. It's why people announce goals to friends. It's why companies publish dates. But notice the danger. Suppose new evidence appears showing the launch would be unsafe. Now that same public commitment may pressure me to do something foolish just to save face. I'd be choosing between breaking my word and shipping something I shouldn't. That's the rigidity side. So public commitment creates discipline and rigidity at the same time. Both need to be considered before you go public. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People treat "announce it publicly" as pure upside. Accountability sounds like a good thing and it often is. But accountability to a promise can turn into a trap when the facts change. So before you announce anything ask yourself this. If the facts change next month will I be able to change course without losing face? Next let's clear up what commitment is not.
