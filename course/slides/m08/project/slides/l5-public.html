Discipline and rigidity

Public commitment cuts both ways

I privately plan to finish a project by October 1. If I miss it maybe nobody knows. Then I announce the launch date to my customers.

Discipline

Reversal now carries reputational and financial costs. Embarrassment too. I've made failure more expensive.

Rigidity

New evidence shows the launch would be unsafe. The public promise pressures me to do something foolish to save face.

Both need to be considered.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Public commitment cuts both ways. Going public raises the cost of reversal. That creates discipline and rigidity. If I privately decide to finish a project by October first and miss it, maybe nobody knows. Now suppose I publicly announce the launch date to my customers. Reversal now carries reputational and financial costs. But suppose new evidence shows the launch would be unsafe. That same public commitment may pressure me to do something foolish to save face. People treat "announce it publicly" as pure upside. But it can turn into a trap when the facts change. Next let's clear up what commitment is not.
