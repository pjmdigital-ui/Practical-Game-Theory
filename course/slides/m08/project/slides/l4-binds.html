Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Of course, people know that "I can't" is more powerful than "I don't want to." Which means they sometimes pretend to be constrained. This takes us right back to Module Seven. A claimed commitment is only useful if it's credible. So ask: what actually binds them? Is there a contract? A rule? A third-party authority? A legal restriction? A public commitment, a financial consequence, a reputational cost, an irreversible investment? Or merely words? And notice that a real constraint isn't always a weapon. Suppose someone keeps saying, "Maybe I'll accept, maybe I won't." You don't know if waiting helps you. Then they credibly tell you, "This offer expires Friday at noon, and after that the asset goes to another buyer." If that's true, uncertainty drops. You know more about the future game, and that can actually make agreement easier. Commitment can provide clarity, not just pressure.
