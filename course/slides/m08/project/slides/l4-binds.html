Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Of course people know that "I can't" is more powerful than "I don't want to." Which means they sometimes pretend to be constrained. You've probably heard this one. "I'd love to help you, but corporate won't let me." Maybe that's completely true. Or maybe corporate is sitting ten feet away and would approve the request instantly. This takes us right back to Module Seven. A claimed commitment is only strategically useful if it's credible. So here's the question to ask. What actually binds them? Look at the list on screen. Is there a contract? A rule? A third-party authority? A legal restriction? Is there a public commitment they'd have to walk back? A financial consequence? A reputational cost? An irreversible investment they've already made? Or is it merely words? That distinction matters a lot. If something real binds them then pushing harder accomplishes nothing. You should work around it. Maybe on scope or timing. If nothing binds them then "I can't" is really "I'd rather not" in a nicer outfit. Now here's the common mistake. People hear "my hands are tied" and they do one of two things. They argue. Or they give up. Neither one is smart. The better move is to ask respectfully what the constraint actually is. Real constraints usually have answers. Fake ones tend to get vague. So think about the last time someone told you they had no choice. Did you ask what bound them?
