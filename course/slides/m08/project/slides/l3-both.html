Threats and promises share one problem

Threat

"If you do X, I will do Y."

Promise

"If you do X, I will reward you with Y."

Will they believe you'll follow through? A contract, published criteria, or money already in an incentive pool makes the future action harder to reverse.

Don't threaten what you won't do.

Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Commitment works in both directions. A threat says, "If you do X, I will do Y." A promise says, "If you do X, I will reward you with Y." Both create the same problem: will the other player believe you'll follow through later? Imagine a company tells an employee, "Hit this target and we'll promote you." The employee hits it, and now promotion is inconvenient. If management has a history of changing its mind, future employees stop responding to those promises. A commitment mechanism fixes that: the bonus written into a contract, promotion criteria that are published and externally reviewed, money already placed in an incentive pool. And the flip side: people make threats in moments of emotion. "I'm quitting." "If this happens again, you're done." Then they don't follow through, and the next threat carries less information. That isn't just about integrity. It's game theory. You're teaching the other player that your words don't predict your behavior.
