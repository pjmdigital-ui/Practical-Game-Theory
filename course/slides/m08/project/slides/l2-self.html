Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Here's a form of commitment you already use. Sometimes the other strategic player is your own future self. You know that tonight you'll want one thing. Tomorrow morning you'll want another. Tonight you say "I'm getting up early." Tomorrow morning you say "Five more minutes." Same person. Two different preferences. So what do smart people do? Tonight they change tomorrow's game. You put the alarm across the room. You arrange to meet someone. You prepay for the session. You remove the tempting food from the house. Those are commitment devices. You're using your current preferences to limit the options available to your future self. Notice how this lines up with everything else in this lesson. You didn't just try harder. You changed the payoffs your future self will face. Snoozing now means walking across a cold room. Skipping now means losing money or letting a friend down. The book mentions a field experiment with university students too. Offering them a nonbinding "soft commitment" to stay on track toward graduation increased exam registration and participation and passing. Simple reminders didn't have the same effect. Students who tended to procrastinate benefited most. That's not exactly the same problem as bargaining with another person. But the underlying insight is related. Sometimes constraining future freedom improves the outcome. So here's your question. What's one place where your evening self and your morning self keep disagreeing? What could you set up tonight?
