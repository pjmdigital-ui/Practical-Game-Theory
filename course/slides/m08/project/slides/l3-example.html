Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

Let's bring this home. Literally. The point of this example is that a smaller consequence you'll enforce beats a huge one you won't. Here's the situation. A parent tells a child "If you don't clean your room, you're grounded for six months." The punishment is severe. And what happens? The child immediately knows it's never happening. Why? Because when the moment arrives six months of punishment would be miserable for the parent too. Six months of a bored and angry kid in the house. Six months of enforcing it every single day. So the child anticipates the parent will back down. And usually the child is right. Now compare that with a smaller and clearly defined consequence. "If the room isn't cleaned by six, there's no gaming tonight." And the parent enforces it consistently. That consequence is much less dramatic. But if the child believes it will actually happen it can have far more strategic effect. The child does the math. Clean the room or lose tonight. Not maybe. Definitely. That's the whole lesson of this module in a kid's bedroom. The question isn't how severe the threat is. The question is whether carrying it out will still make sense when the time comes. A common misunderstanding is that small consequences feel weak. So parents escalate. But escalation without follow-through just teaches the child to wait it out. So here's the question.
