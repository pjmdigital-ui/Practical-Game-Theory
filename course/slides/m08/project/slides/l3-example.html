Internomics · Module 8 — Commitment Changes the Game

A parent tells a child, "If you don't clean your room, you're grounded for six months." The child immediately knows: that's never happening. Six months of punishment would be miserable for the parent too, so the child anticipates they'll back down. Now compare: "If the room isn't cleaned by six, there's no gaming tonight." Consistently enforced. Less dramatic, but if the child believes it will actually happen, it has more strategic effect. Boundaries work the same way. Someone keeps speaking disrespectfully to you. You say, "If this continues, I'm ending the conversation." It happens again. You stay. It happens again. You stay. What have they learned? That your statement doesn't constrain your behavior. Now imagine you calmly say, "We'll continue this when we can talk respectfully," and you leave, every time. A boundary isn't "I demand that you behave differently." It's "If this occurs, here is what I will do," and it only matters when your behavior makes it credible.
