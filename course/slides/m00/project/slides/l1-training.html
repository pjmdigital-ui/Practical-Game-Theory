Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Let me start with a confession. In 2011 I started a publishing company focused on influence and persuasion. Since then I've created and published close to a hundred different trainings. And for most of that time I focused on the same thing almost everyone in this field focuses on. Strategies and tactics. What words should you use? What questions should you ask? How should you handle an objection? How do you build rapport? How do you persuade? How do you negotiate? Those are the four cards you see here, plus a few more. I'm not here to tell you those things don't matter. They do. I taught them for years and I still use them. But there was something underneath them that I had never really named. It was something I'd been doing since I was a kid. I wasn't only thinking about what to say. I was trying to understand the situation surrounding what was being said. That's the part most training skips. It jumps straight to the visible layer. The words. The technique. And when the technique doesn't work, we assume we need a better technique. So here's a question for you. Think of the communication advice you've picked up over the years. How much of it was about the words? And how much of it was about reading the situation before the words? For most people it's almost all words. Let's look at what sits underneath them.
