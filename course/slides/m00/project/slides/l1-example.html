The classroom: learning to read a room

The situation

An only child on a busy block. Everyone else went home to brothers and sisters. I went home alone.

What happened

I wanted friends. So I tried to be funny. Sometimes it worked. Sometimes I took it much too far.

The lesson

The same joke could make one group laugh and annoy another. I had to feel the room before I spoke.

Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Let me tell you where this came from for me. It happened in three rooms. The first room was childhood. I grew up as an only child with two loving parents on a dead-end block in the 1980s. It was a great childhood. Our block was always alive. My mother is one of sixteen children. At one point two of her brothers lived on our block with their eight kids. My grandparents lived just down the street. There was always something happening. But at the end of the day everybody else seemed to go home to brothers and sisters. I went home alone. So I became very aware of my need to connect with people. I wanted friends. And I realized that if I wanted people to want me around, I had to figure out how to get them to like me. The problem was I didn't really know how. When you don't have siblings teaching you the unwritten rules, you improvise. For me that meant trying to be funny. I acted out. I got in trouble in school. Sometimes it worked. Other times I became incredibly disruptive. But underneath all of that I was learning to read people. I noticed when a joke would work and when it wouldn't. The exact same thing could make one group laugh and annoy another. I learned about timing. Delivery. Tone. I learned to feel what was happening in a room before deciding what to say. I didn't have language for any of it. I was just learning the unwritten rules of human interaction. Think back to your own childhood for a second. Where did you first learn to read a room? Then life gave me a much more serious test.
