Research spotlight

Most real games are mixed

What it shows

Thomas Schelling (Nobel Prize, 2005) emphasized that most real-world interactions mix conflicting and common interests. They're rarely pure competition.

What it doesn't prove

Game theory isn't a theory of better communication, and people aren't perfectly rational. Research disciplines our thinking; it doesn't guarantee any technique works.

Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Every module has a research spotlight, and every spotlight has two sides: what the research shows, and what it does not prove. Here's the first one. Thomas Schelling, who shared the 2005 Nobel Prize in economics, emphasized that most real-world interactions contain both conflicting and common interests. They're rarely pure competition. That's why we don't treat the other person as an opponent by default. And what it doesn't prove: game theory was never designed as a theory of communication, and people are messier than the models. In this course, research disciplines our thinking. It never promises that a technique will work on a particular person.
