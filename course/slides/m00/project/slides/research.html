Research spotlight

Most real games are mixed

What it shows

Thomas Schelling received the 2005 Nobel Prize in economics. He emphasized that most real interactions mix conflicting and common interests. They're rarely pure competition.

What it doesn't prove

Game theory isn't a theory of better communication. People aren't perfectly rational. Research disciplines our thinking. It doesn't guarantee any technique works.

Why it matters: We don't treat the other person as an opponent by default.

Human beings are messier than the rational players in mathematical models. Research does not promise that any technique will work on any particular person.
