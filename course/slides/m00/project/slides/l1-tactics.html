Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Here's the point of this slide. Underneath every tactic is a set of judgments about the situation. You make them whether you notice them or not. Who are the people involved? And that includes people who aren't in the room. What does each person want? Not just what they say they want. What they actually value. What choices do they really have? What do they know? What do they believe? What are they expecting you to do right now? And if you make this move, how are they likely to respond? Then if they respond that way, what happens next? Those are the questions I was asking myself my whole life without knowing it. Here's why they matter so much. A tactic is a tool for carrying out a plan. The plan comes from your read of the situation. If you think your boss is worried about the budget, you'll pick one approach. If your boss is really worried about losing you to a competitor, that same approach misses completely. The tactic was fine. The judgment underneath it was wrong. And this is the common misunderstanding I want you to watch for. When a conversation fails, most of us blame the delivery. We think we should have said it better. Sometimes that's true. But very often the delivery was fine and the read was off. Get these six judgments wrong and the best tactic in the world won't save you. Which brings us to a puzzle you've probably lived through yourself.
