Here's how I'd read it. Start with the players. Dana and Marcus are obvious. But the client contact is a hidden and very powerful player. That person isn't in any of Dana's conversations with Marcus. Yet they're shaping his behavior every week. And the bonus system is shaping the game too. So is whoever designed it. Now why would better wording fail? Because Marcus's incentives reward client satisfaction. The client rewards his last-minute responsiveness. Clear words don't change what Marcus gains by submitting late. In fact finishing early might cost him. Think about it. If he submits two days early, the client will probably request changes anyway. So he does the work twice. From where Marcus stands, waiting until the night before is a sensible strategy. It's not defiance. It's a reasonable response to the game he's in. That's why Dana's colleague gave the wrong advice. Working on her delivery won't touch any of this. So what question should Dana ask instead? Something like this. What game is Marcus actually in? And what makes his current behavior sensible from where he stands? That question opens up options that better wording never could. Dana could involve the client in the timeline. She could look at adjusting what the bonus rewards. She could agree on a change-request process so the late edits stop landing on Marcus. Notice that each of these changes the game rather than the words. This is a preview of where the course is going. You'll see these ideas again in Modules One, Two and Eleven.
