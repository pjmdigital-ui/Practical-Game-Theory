Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Here's a puzzle you've probably lived. You use an approach with one person and it works beautifully. You use the exact same approach with someone else and it falls flat. Sometimes it even makes things worse. If communication were only about the words, that wouldn't happen. Same words. Same delivery. Different result. So what changed? The game changed. Every important interaction contains more than words. There are people with different goals. Different incentives. Different information. Different options. Different expectations. And every move one person makes can change what the other person wants to do next. That's what I mean by the structure of the interaction. It's the set of things around the words that decide how the words land. This puzzle bothered me for years. It sits right next to a few others. Why do people sometimes stay stuck in situations nobody actually wants? Why can a promise be persuasive in one situation and meaningless in another? Why does cooperation show up in some relationships while conflict seems almost inevitable in others? Tactics don't answer any of those questions. Structure does. The common mistake here is to conclude that the second person was just difficult. Maybe they were. But more often they were sitting in a different game. They had different stakes. They had different alternatives. So I want you to think of an approach that works for you with one person and never works with another. What's different about the game each of them is in? That question leads to the shift this whole course is built on.
