Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Now look at the questions underneath the words. What does your boss actually want? What do you want? What happens if your boss says no? And what happens if you say no to a counteroffer? What alternatives do you have? What alternatives does your boss have? What does your boss know that you don't? What do you know that your boss doesn't? Who has the authority to make the decision? Who else might influence it? What will your boss probably do after you make your request? And what are you likely to do after your boss responds? Suddenly this isn't simply a conversation. It's a game. Notice how many of these questions have nothing to do with you. They're about your boss's wants. Their options. Their information. The people behind them. Most of us walk into that office having thought hard about our own side and almost nothing about theirs. And notice the players who aren't in the room. Maybe your boss needs approval from someone above them. Maybe there's a budget cycle you don't know about. One of the first mistakes we make is assuming the person we're talking to is the entire game. Sometimes they aren't. Sometimes the people who matter most aren't even in the room. Here's my question for you. Think of a request you're planning to make in the next month. Pick any two of these questions. Can you actually answer them? If you can't, that's not a failure. That's exactly what you need to find out before you speak. And that leads us to the big idea of this lesson.
