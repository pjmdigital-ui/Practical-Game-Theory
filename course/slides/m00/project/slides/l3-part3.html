Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Part Three is about thinking beyond the moment. Parts One and Two mostly look at a single interaction. Part Three steps back. It looks at relationships over time. It looks at groups trying to line up. And it looks at the rules of the game itself. Model nine is the Shadow of the Future. Its question is what changes because there will be a next time? When you know you'll deal with someone again, everything shifts. Reputation matters. Trust becomes valuable. Short-term wins can become long-term losses. Model ten is Diagnose the Coordination Problem. Its question is are we in conflict, or are we failing to coordinate? This is one of my favorites. Two people can look like they're fighting when they're not actually opposed. They just don't know what the other one is going to do. Fixing that takes a completely different move than fixing a real conflict. Model eleven is Change the Game. Its question is do I need a better move, or a better game? Sometimes you keep losing because the game itself is badly designed. Maybe the incentives reward the exact opposite of what everyone wants. Then no clever move will fix it. You need to change the structure. Look at the progression. The future. Coordination. The design of the game itself. Each one zooms out a little further. Here's a question to sit with. Think of a problem that keeps coming back in your work or your home. Are you trying to find a better move? Or is it time to change the game? Now there's a big risk with eleven models, and I want to address it directly.
