Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

Here's the standard. Every strategy you build in this course has to pass four tests. The first is truth. No deception. No creating false impressions. The second is autonomy. The other person still makes their own choice. You're not cornering them or tricking them into it. The third is dignity. People are never just pieces on a board. The fourth is informed choice. They decide with the information they need. Now here's something important. Game theory itself doesn't supply these values. It's a tool for understanding strategic situations. A tool can be used well or badly. So we bring the values. Why does this matter so much? Because strategic intelligence without humanity becomes manipulation. And manipulation is not what we're building here. The goal isn't to control people. The goal is to understand interactions well enough to make wiser choices inside them. Here's a simple way to use these four tests. Before you act on any strategy from this course, run it through them. Am I being truthful? Am I leaving them free to choose? Am I treating them with dignity? Do they have what they need to decide? If any answer is no, change the strategy. The book puts it simply. Does it treat people as decision-makers rather than objects to be manipulated? There's a common misunderstanding to watch for here. Some people think ethics and effectiveness pull in opposite directions. In most real relationships they don't. You'll deal with these people again. Trust is valuable. So ask yourself this. Is there a tactic you use today that would fail one of these four tests? Now there's one more distinction I need to make.
