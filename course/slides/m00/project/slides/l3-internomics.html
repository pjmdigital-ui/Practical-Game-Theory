Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

So what is Internomics? It's the name I gave to this way of thinking. It's the study of what happens between people. It applies the principles of game theory to communication and human interaction. Its purpose is not to teach you what to say. Its purpose is to teach you to understand the game you're already playing. And you are already playing. Every day at work and at home you're making moves inside games you may never have consciously identified. You're negotiating chores. You're deciding whether to speak up in a meeting. You're responding to a customer who's pushing back. Each of those has players and payoffs and responses. This course makes those games visible. Here's what that means in practice. There are no scripts in this course. I'm not going to hand you seven phrases for every situation. There's no math either. You won't draw a single game tree. What you'll get instead is eleven mental models. Each one gives you a better question to ask before you speak. Why questions instead of scripts? Because a script only works in the situation it was written for. A good question works in situations nobody anticipated. That's the real advantage here. Now here's the misunderstanding I hear most often. People assume that seeing the game means trying to win it at someone else's expense. It doesn't. The goal isn't to turn you into a mathematician. And it certainly isn't to teach you to manipulate people. The goal is to help you see the game behind the conversation. Once you see it differently, you have far more choices about how you play.
