Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

So here's the shift this whole course is built on. It's simple to say and hard to practice. Stop starting with "What should I say?" Start with "What game am I actually in?" Most communication advice begins with that first question. It sounds like the obvious place to start. But it's the wrong place to start. Think about walking into your boss's office to ask for a raise. Before you say anything you're probably thinking about the words. How much to ask for. How to show your value. Which objections your boss will raise. All of that matters. But there's another way to look at it. What does your boss actually want? What happens if your boss says no? What alternatives does each of you have? Once you ask those questions it stops being a script problem. It becomes a game. We'll come back to that example in the next lesson. Now I want to be careful here. I'm not saying the words don't matter. They do. They just come second. Once you can see the game, choosing the words gets much easier. You know what they need to accomplish. You know who they need to reach. You know what response you're hoping to create. Without that you're polishing sentences in the dark. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. This isn't about overthinking every small exchange. Most conversations don't need a strategic audit. The shift is about the conversations that matter. So the next time one of those comes up, notice which question you ask first. And the book gives us a few more questions that work the same way. Let's look at them.
