Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

So where did the name come from? After about two years of copywriting I burned out. I didn't want to spend the rest of my life writing sales letters for other people. I wanted to build something of my own and teach. So I asked myself a question. What do I actually know well enough to teach? The answer was obvious. Communication. In 2011 I started a publishing company focused on influence and persuasion. And as I told you a few slides ago, for years I taught strategies and tactics. Then I discovered game theory. As I began studying it, something strange happened. I kept recognizing ideas. Players. Payoffs. Best responses. Strategic moves. Information. Signals. Credibility. Commitment. Repeated interactions. Cooperation. Coordination. Equilibrium. Again and again I found formal concepts describing patterns I'd spent much of my life noticing by intuition. The kid reading the classroom. The young man reading the unit. The salesperson reading resistance. They all had names. That led me to a question. Could the concepts of game theory teach people a fundamentally different way of thinking about communication? Not another collection of scripts. Not another list of persuasion tricks. Something deeper. A way of looking at an interaction and seeing the structure underneath it. I dug into the research to find out if the connection I was seeing was really there. The deeper I went, the more convinced I became that it was. That's where this course comes from. Don't worry if some of these words on the screen are new to you. We'll build every one of them together. And here's the one thing I want you to take from this lesson.
