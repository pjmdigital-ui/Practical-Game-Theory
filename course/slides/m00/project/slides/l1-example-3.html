Internomics · Module 0 — Orientation

The third room was sales. When I came home I was still only twenty. I had a federal conviction. I had no college degree. I had almost no meaningful professional experience. My options were limited. The jobs I could get were mostly sales jobs. Nobody cared much about my résumé as long as I could produce results. If I wanted to make money, I had to convince people to buy. So once again my livelihood depended on understanding human beings. I had to listen. I had to read people. I had to understand what mattered to them. I had to recognize resistance. I had to know when to push and when to stop. I had to know when to ask a question and when to explain something differently. And I had to understand that what I said was only part of the equation. What really mattered was how the other person responded to it. For years I did all of this mostly by intuition. Then in 2004 a friend started an online business and needed a copywriter. He couldn't afford an experienced one. I was a sales guy, so he figured I knew how to sell. I had no formal training. I simply wrote the way I thought people needed to be spoken to. And it worked. Eventually I was making more from copywriting than from my sales job in Manhattan. So I went all in. Over roughly two years I wrote more than a hundred sales letters. In all three rooms the same thing was true. What mattered most wasn't the words. It was the situation surrounding the words. I just didn't have a name for it yet.
