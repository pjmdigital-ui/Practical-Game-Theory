Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's why this approach is different from most communication advice. Most communication instruction starts with an assumption. The problem is mainly linguistic. If you say the right thing in the right way you'll get the right result. Sometimes that's true. But it isn't always true. Your words enter a system that already holds incentives and expectations and information. It holds relationships and constraints and alternatives. It holds history and timing and other decision-makers. John Nash's equilibrium concept captures the core idea. Each player's behavior must be considered in relation to the behavior of the others. So here's the shift in practice. Look at the table. Instead of asking only "What should I say?" start asking "What situation am I saying it in?" Instead of "How do I get them to agree?" ask "What does agreement mean to them?" Instead of "How do I win this interaction?" ask "What outcome does each of us actually want?" Instead of "Why did they respond that way?" ask "What about the structure of this interaction made that response reasonable from their side?" And instead of "What should I do next?" ask "What will my move cause them to do next?" Read those right-hand questions again. Not one of them tells you what to say. That's on purpose. They don't give you a script. They give you something more valuable. They give you a way of seeing. Here's a quick exercise. Pick one row that fits a conversation you have coming up.
