The "Wall Street Game" vs. the "Community Game"

What it shows

Same Prisoner's Dilemma, different name. The label changed cooperation substantially, more than observers expected, while reputations predicted poorly. What game people think they're in shapes how they play.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's our first research spotlight for this module. In 2004, Liberman, Samuels and Ross had participants, including American college students and Israeli pilots and instructors, play essentially the same Prisoner's Dilemma. The only thing they changed was the name. Some were told they were playing the "Wall Street Game," others the "Community Game." The label had a substantial effect on whether people cooperated or defected, larger than observers expected. Meanwhile, judgments of each participant's reputation did a poor job of predicting who would cooperate. What it suggests: what kind of game people believe they're in can change how they behave inside it. What it doesn't prove: that mapping a game consciously leads to optimal decisions. And it doesn't mean the game is only perception. There's still a real strategic structure, and our reading of it can be right or wrong.
