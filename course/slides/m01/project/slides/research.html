Research spotlight · Liberman, Samuels & Ross (2004)

The "Wall Street Game" vs. the "Community Game"

What it shows

Same Prisoner's Dilemma. Different name. The label changed cooperation more than observers expected. Reputations predicted poorly. The game people think they're in shapes how they play.

What it doesn't prove

That consciously mapping a game leads to optimal decisions. Or that the game is "only perception." A real structure sits underneath. Our readings of it can be right or wrong.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's our first research spotlight. In 2004 Liberman, Samuels and Ross ran a study with a simple twist. Participants played essentially the same Prisoner's Dilemma. Some were told it was the "Wall Street Game." Others were told it was the "Community Game." Same rules and payoffs. The label had a substantial effect on whether people cooperated. So what kind of game people believe they're in can change how they behave. Here's what it doesn't prove. It doesn't show that mapping a game leads to optimal decisions.
