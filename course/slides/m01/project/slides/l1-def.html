What game theory studies

Strategic interaction: one person's choices shape the outcomes open to others.

The formal framework: players, strategies, payoffs, outcomes. A game needs no winner and no loser.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

When people hear "game theory," they picture chess, poker, business competition, maybe two countries threatening each other. Those are games. But at its most fundamental level, game theory studies strategic interaction: situations where the choices of one decision-maker affect the outcomes available to others. The formal framework describes the players, their available strategies, their preferences or payoffs, and the outcomes that result. Notice what's missing from that definition: a winner and a loser. Cooperation is a game. So is coordination, negotiation, compromise, friendship, a business partnership, a family deciding where to go on vacation. Thomas Schelling emphasized that real interactions usually contain both common and conflicting interests. That's the world we're working in.
