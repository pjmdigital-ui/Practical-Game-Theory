Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's something else that's easy to miss. A game isn't always static. Your own actions can change the situation itself. Look at the four cards. You ask someone for a favor and they say yes. Now you've established something about your relationship. You break a promise. Now they may behave differently the next time. You reveal information. Now the other person knows something they didn't know before. You publicly commit yourself to a position. Now backing away may become more costly. In each case the board itself shifted. Your move can change what the other person knows. It can change what they believe. It can change what they expect from you. It can change which choices are still available. And it can change what happens next. That's why game theory isn't simply about picking the best move from a fixed menu. It's about understanding interdependent decisions over time. Here's a practical example. Say you announce your position in a team meeting. Before the meeting you had room to adjust quietly. After it, changing course means walking it back in front of everyone. You didn't just state a view. You changed your own options. Sometimes that's exactly what you want. Sometimes it's a trap you set for yourself. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. We treat each conversation as a fresh start. It rarely is. The other person is carrying forward everything you've done before. So ask yourself this. What did my last move teach them to expect from me? Now let's look under the surface of the conversation.
