Ask: Who moves when? Sometimes the key part of your strategy isn't what you do. It's when you do it.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's something that becomes obvious once you start seeing interactions this way. When you act matters. Look at the four cards. Suppose I make you an offer and you have to respond on the spot. That's one game. Now suppose I make an offer and wait for your response. Then I decide whether to improve it. That's another game. Suppose you get to see my offer before you decide on yours. Different game. And suppose neither of us knows what the other is going to do. Different game again. Same two people. Same thing at stake. Four different games. Game theorists separate two big families here. There are simultaneous-move games where people choose without seeing each other's move. And there are sequential games where one person moves first and the other responds. They keep them apart for a reason. The timing of moves changes what's possible. So does what each person can observe before they choose. Think about what that means day to day. If someone pushes you for an answer right now, they've chosen the first card for you. You can often move the game to the second card just by saying "Let me come back to you tomorrow." If you show your number first, the other side gets to respond to it. Sometimes that helps you. Sometimes it doesn't. Here's the common mistake. We obsess over what to say and almost never think about when to say it or who should go first. So add this to your toolkit. Who moves when?
