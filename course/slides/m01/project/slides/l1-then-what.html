Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's the question I want you to put in front of every other question. What happens after I say it? Your sentence doesn't exist in isolation. Your boss hears it. Then your boss makes a decision. Look at the options on the slide. Your boss might agree. Your boss might reject the request. They might ask for more information. They might make a counteroffer. They might delay the conversation. They might consult someone else. They might test how badly you want the raise. Every one of those is a different branch. And each branch puts you in a different spot. That's the line at the bottom. Your move changes their decision problem. And their response changes yours. That's the essence of strategic interaction. Game theorists have a name for this kind of situation. They call it a sequential game. The order in which people act matters because later decisions depend on what happened earlier. In the formal version they draw it as a game tree. Each move opens new branches. Now you don't need to draw a tree to use this. You just need one habit. Plan a move. Then ask "Then what?" If they counter, what will I do? If they delay, what will I do? Here's the common mistake. We prepare for the yes we want. We rarely prepare for the five other responses we might actually get. So which of these responses would catch you most off guard? That's the branch to think through first. Let's lock in the lesson.
