Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Some of you may be getting nervous about now. Game theory. Isn't that a lot of math? There is a tremendous amount of mathematics behind game theory. There are formal models of equilibrium. There are Bayesian games and sequential games and repeated games. There's bargaining and mechanism design and much more. University courses can spend whole semesters on these models. We're not doing that here. We're extracting something different. We're looking for the mental models underneath the mathematics. The ideas that help you understand people. Let me show you what I mean. The goal isn't for you to calculate a Nash equilibrium before you ask your spouse where she wants to eat. That would be ridiculous. The goal is for you to notice something like the line on this slide. "We're not actually disagreeing about the restaurant. We're both worried about choosing something the other person won't like." Think about how many dinner standoffs that one sentence explains. Each person keeps saying "I don't mind. You pick." It looks like indecision. It looks like neither of you cares. But the structure is something else. You both care a lot. You care about the other person's happiness. And that shared worry is what's stalling you. Once you notice it you can break it. One of you names a choice. Or you each name one place you'd genuinely enjoy. No equations. Just a noticing. That's game theory becoming useful. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People think they need the formal tools before they can use the ideas. You don't. You need the habit of looking. And the next slide gives you a simple way to build that habit.
