Research spotlight · Charness & Sontuoso, 2023

Awareness of the frame changes strategic play

What it shows

In lab coordination games, changing which features players were made aware of changed how they played. We don't notice every relevant feature on our own.

What it doesn't prove

It was a lab task, not a workplace or family conversation. It doesn't show any habit of mind improves everyday outcomes.

The Game Map is a practical synthesis, not a tested intervention.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

The second study, from Charness and Sontuoso in 2023, looked at coordination games built around visual objects. The researchers varied which features of the choices players were made aware of, and that changed their strategic behavior. The takeaway for us: people don't necessarily notice every strategically relevant feature in front of them, which is a strong reason to ask on purpose, "What am I failing to notice about the game?" But it was a controlled lab task, not a family conversation, and it doesn't show that any habit of mind improves everyday outcomes. Colin Camerer's review of behavioral game theory is a useful counterweight: framing, social preferences and learning push real behavior away from simple predictions. So here's the honest standard. The evidence that construal affects play is strong. Evidence that teaching people to see the game improves real-world communication has not been established. The Game Map is a practical synthesis, not a tested intervention.
