Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here are four more traps. The first is assuming "game" means "competition." It doesn't. Most interactions mix common and conflicting interests. If seeing the game makes you more combative, something's gone wrong. It should make cooperation easier. The second is getting stated constraints wrong in either direction. Some people assume every stated constraint is a lie. Others assume every one is true. Remember "I can't go below ten thousand dollars." It could be a bluff. It could be a real policy. It could be a preference dressed up as a rule. Ask what actually constrains them. The third is ignoring what happens if there's no agreement. Alternatives and dependence shape the conversation itself. They don't only matter after it fails. But don't overcorrect either. A better alternative doesn't automatically win. It depends on the structure of the game. The fourth card holds two mistakes that are mirror images. One is becoming paranoid about hidden motives. Sometimes the stated issue really is the issue. Stay curious instead of suspicious. The other is running the Game Map as a rigid script. It's there to train your attention. Over time you want to notice these things automatically. You don't want to fill out a form before every conversation. Here's the thread that runs through all eight mistakes. Each one is a way of seeing less of the game than is really there.
