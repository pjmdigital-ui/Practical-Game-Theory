Four more mistakes to watch for

"Game" means "competition"

Most interactions mix common and conflicting interests. Seeing the game should make cooperation easier.

Stated constraint: lie or truth?

It could be a bluff or a real policy or a preference. Ask what actually constrains them.

Ignoring "no agreement"

Alternatives shape the conversation itself. A better alternative still doesn't automatically win.

Paranoia or a rigid script

Sometimes the stated issue is the issue. And the Game Map trains attention. It isn't a form.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here are four more traps. The first is assuming game means competition. Most interactions mix common and conflicting interests. The second is getting stated constraints wrong in either direction. "I can't go below ten thousand dollars" could be a bluff or a real policy. The third is ignoring what happens if there's no agreement. The fourth card holds two mirror images. One is becoming paranoid about hidden motives. The other is running the Game Map as a rigid script. Each mistake is a way of seeing less of the game than is really there.
