Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

This distinction is so important that I want to make it explicit one more time. A game doesn't have to be competitive. Look at the three cards. You can think about your marriage through game theory without believing your spouse is your opponent. You can think about a business partnership through game theory without believing your partner is trying to beat you. You can think about a friendship through game theory without turning it into a calculation. If anything the opposite happens. Understanding the strategic structure of a relationship can make cooperation easier. Here's why. When someone you love does something that frustrates you, the easy story is about them. They don't listen. They don't care. They're being difficult. Seeing the game gives you a second story. What about their position makes this choice reasonable? What are they worried about? What do they expect you to do? That second story doesn't excuse everything. But it gives you something to work with. You can change a structure. It's much harder to change a person's character. In the book I put it this way. You begin to understand why another person's behavior makes sense from their position. That's not cold analysis. That's empathy with a map. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Some people hear "game theory in a marriage" and imagine scorekeeping. That's not it at all. Scorekeeping treats the relationship as a contest. Seeing the game treats it as a shared structure you can both improve. So think about one close relationship. Where might a little structure explain a lot of friction?
