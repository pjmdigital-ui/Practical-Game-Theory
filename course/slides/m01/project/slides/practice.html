Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Let's practice. Dana runs operations at a regional distribution company. For six weeks she's been pushing Marcus to adopt a new scheduling system. Marcus is the warehouse lead. He keeps saying the system "isn't ready." He says his team "doesn't have time to learn it." Dana has started to see him as stubborn. At Thursday's one-on-one she plans to tell him the switch is happening next Monday. No exceptions. Now here's what Dana hasn't thought about. Marcus's quarterly bonus is tied to on-time shipment rates. Any slowdown during the learning period will hit his numbers. The software vendor's onboarding team is booked for the next three weeks. Two of Marcus's most experienced pickers have quietly told him they'd rather leave than learn "another system." Dana's own boss is the VP who approved the system. That VP has asked her for a progress update at the end of the month. And Marcus knows other warehouses in the area are hiring leads. Notice how Dana framed this to herself. It's a person problem. Marcus is stubborn. That's exactly the kind of read this module asks you to check. So here's what I want you to do. Pause the video. Pull out your Game Map. Work through the five questions on the slide. Who are the players and who's missing from the meeting? Where are the interests aligned and where do they differ? What constrains Marcus? What are each side's alternatives? What happens after Monday's announcement? And then what? Finally, is this a person problem or a game problem? Write short answers. Then come back and compare yours with mine.
