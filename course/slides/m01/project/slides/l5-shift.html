Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Let me pull this whole module into one picture. Before you met this way of thinking you probably saw an interaction like the left side of the slide. Me. What should I say? Them. A straight line. All the effort goes into the middle box. The words. Now I want you to start seeing it like the right side. Me. My move. Their response. My next move. Their next response. Outcome. It's not a line anymore. It's a chain of decisions. Each link depends on the one before it. And look at the row at the bottom. Underneath that chain sits everything we've talked about. Their incentives and my incentives. Our information. Our expectations. Our alternatives. Our constraints. Our history. That's the structure of the interaction. That's the game. Once you begin seeing it, something interesting happens. You stop treating every interaction as a hunt for the perfect words. Here's the honest part. Sometimes the words really are the problem. A clumsy sentence can do real damage. I'm not telling you words don't matter. But often the words are merely the most visible part of a much larger system. Think of a conversation that went badly recently. Did it go badly because of one sentence? Or did the sentence land on top of incentives and history you hadn't accounted for? Here's the common mistake. People hear this and decide they should stop preparing what to say. Keep preparing. Just do it second. First see the game. Then choose your words for the game you're actually in. Next let's swap out the questions we usually start with.
