The word player can sound cold when we're talking about human relationships. It shouldn't. A player is simply a decision-maker inside the interaction. That's all the word means here. Look at the slide. Your spouse is a player. Your business partner is a player. Your customer is a player. Your employee is a player. Your boss is a player. And notice the dark card at the end. You are a player too. That one matters more than people expect. We tend to analyze everyone else and forget that our own choices are part of the structure. Now here's the misunderstanding I want to head off. Recognizing someone as a player doesn't mean you reduce them to a selfish calculation machine. It doesn't mean you treat them as a piece on a board. It means the opposite. It means you take their choices seriously. You accept that they're weighing things just like you are. They have their own reasons. They have their own options. They have their own pressures. That's actually a respectful way to see people. Think about the last time someone surprised you in a conversation. Maybe a coworker pushed back when you expected a yes. In that moment did you treat them as a decision-maker with their own reasons? Or did you treat them as an obstacle? The first view gives you something to work with. The second view just makes you frustrated. So who are the players in the conversation you're thinking about right now? Write them down. Include yourself. Next let's look at what real players actually care about.
