Example · A game inside the game

On the surface: "Which strategy should we use?"

The situation

Two people argue about a business decision. They seem to be debating which strategy the company should use.

What happened

Underneath sits another game. Who gets final authority? Who gets credit if it works? Who gets blamed if it fails? Who sets the precedent?

The lesson

The visible disagreement is only one layer. People can carry several overlapping incentives at once.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Picture two people arguing about a business decision. On the surface they're debating option A or option B. But look at the middle card. Underneath there may be another game. Who gets final authority? Who gets the credit if this works? Who gets the blame if it fails? If that's the real game then no amount of data will settle it. So you might ask who owns the final call before you debate the options. You might find a way to share credit. Each of those works on the game underneath.
