Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Picture two people arguing about a business decision. That's the situation. On the surface they're debating which strategy the company should use. Option A or option B. They each bring numbers. They each bring arguments. It looks like a debate about the best plan. But look at the middle card. Underneath that debate there may be another game going on. Who gets final authority here? Who gets the credit if this works? Who gets the blame if it fails? Is one of them trying to set a precedent for future decisions? Any one of those can matter more to a person than the strategy itself. And if that's the real game, then no amount of data on option A will settle it. You could bring the perfect spreadsheet. The argument would just move to new ground. That's the lesson on the right. The visible disagreement is only one layer of the interaction. Human interaction can contain several overlapping incentives at the same time. Again, this doesn't mean you should become obsessed with hidden motives. It means you widen your view. So what would you actually do? You might ask who will own the final call before you debate the options. You might find a way for both people to share credit. You might agree that this decision doesn't set a rule for the next one. Each of those moves works on the game underneath. Here's the common mistake. We argue the surface question harder and harder while the real question goes untouched. So which disagreement in your life might have a second layer like this? Let's lock in the takeaway.
