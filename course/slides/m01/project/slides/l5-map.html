Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

So here's your first tool. It's called the Game Map. When you enter an important interaction, pause and look at the structure. These nine questions help you do that. Let's walk across the grid. Who? Who are the relevant players? Who can actually affect the outcome? What? What does each person want? Where are your interests aligned and where are they different? Options? What can each player actually do? Constraints? What limits those choices? Information? Who knows what? Who doesn't know what? Timing? Who moves first? Who responds? What can each person observe? Alternatives? What happens if there's no agreement? Dependencies? Who needs whom? Who has alternatives? And finally then what? If I make this move, what is the other person likely to do next? You'll notice every one of these came from something we covered in this module. The map just puts them in one place. Now here's the most important thing I'll say about it. This isn't meant to be a rigid nine-step procedure. You don't run it mechanically every time you speak to someone. That's not the point. The point is to train your attention. At first you'll go through the questions on paper. That's good. Over time you stop consciously running the list. You start noticing these things automatically. That's what a mental model does. Here's the common mistake. People treat the map like a form to fill out perfectly. Then it feels like homework and they drop it. Keep your answers short. A few words each is plenty.
