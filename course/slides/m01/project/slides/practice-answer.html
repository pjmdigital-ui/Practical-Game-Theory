Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's how I'd read it. Players: in the room, Dana and Marcus. Outside it, the VP who wants an update, the two senior pickers who could quit, the vendor's onboarding team whose availability sets the real start date, and the local employers hiring leads. Dana's been treating this as Dana versus Marcus. It's a much bigger game. Their interests are mixed, not opposed: both want the warehouse to run well and keep good people, but Dana needs visible progress by month's end while Marcus needs to protect the on-time rates that drive his bonus. "Next Monday" may not even be feasible, since the vendor can't onboard anyone for three weeks. If Dana forces it, Marcus might comply on paper while shipments slip, escalate, start interviewing, or watch his pickers walk. Every one of those makes her update worse. So this is mostly a game problem. Marcus still has responsibility, but his "stubbornness" is a reasonable response to incentives that punish the change. A better first move: ask Marcus what would make the rollout workable, check the vendor's timeline, and raise the bonus conflict with the VP. That changes the game instead of escalating inside it.
