A much bigger game than Dana vs. Marcus

Players: Dana and Marcus. Outside the room: the VP and the two pickers. Also the vendor's team and local employers.

Interests: Both want a working warehouse and good people. Dana needs visible progress. Marcus needs his on-time rates.

Constraints: The bonus punishes a slowdown. The vendor can't start for three weeks. Marcus has more alternatives than Dana assumed.

Then what: He complies on paper or escalates or starts interviewing. Pickers may resign. Each makes her update worse.

Diagnosis: Mostly a game problem. Ask what would make it workable. Check the vendor. Raise the bonus conflict with the VP.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's how I'd read it. Start with the players. In the room there's Dana and Marcus. Outside it there's the VP who wants an update. There are the two senior pickers who could quit. There's the vendor's onboarding team. Their schedule sets the real start date. And there are the local employers hiring leads. They're part of Marcus's outside option. Dana has been treating this as Dana versus Marcus. It's a much bigger game. Now the interests. They're mixed, not opposed. Both want the warehouse to run well. Both want to keep good people. Neither wants a failed rollout. But Dana needs visible progress for her VP by month's end. Marcus needs to protect the on-time rates that drive his bonus. He also needs to keep his best pickers. Next the constraints and alternatives. Marcus's bonus structure penalizes a slowdown. The vendor can't onboard anyone for three weeks. So "next Monday" may not even be feasible. Marcus can't force veteran pickers to stay. If there's no agreement, Marcus can leave for another warehouse. Dana's alternative to Marcus is replacing a lead mid-rollout. That's costly. Dependence runs both ways. And Marcus has more alternatives than Dana has assumed. Then what? If Dana forces it, Marcus might comply on paper while shipments slip. He might escalate. He might start interviewing. His pickers might resign. Every one of those makes her month-end update worse. So this is mostly a game problem. Marcus still has responsibility. But his "stubbornness" is a reasonable response to incentives that punish the change. A better first move is to ask Marcus what would make the rollout workable. Check the vendor's timeline. Raise the bonus conflict with the VP. That changes the game instead of escalating inside it.
