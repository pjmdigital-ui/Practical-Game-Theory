Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Real human beings have complicated motivations. That's the point of this slide. People care about money. But they also care about fairness. They care about winning. But they also care about relationships. They care about status and identity. They care about loyalty and security. They care about principles and reputation. And they care about countless other things that never show up on a price tag. Why does this matter for seeing the game? Because if you assume everyone wants the same thing you'll misread almost every interaction. You'll offer money to someone who wants recognition. You'll push a quick win on someone who's protecting a relationship. You'll argue the numbers with someone who's really worried about how they'll look. Picture the raise conversation again. You might assume your boss only cares about the budget. But maybe your boss also cares about fairness across the team. Maybe they worry about how a raise for you will look to others. Maybe they care about their own reputation with their manager. Each of those is part of their payoff. Miss them and your perfect argument lands in the wrong place. Here's the common misunderstanding. People hear "game theory" and assume it means everyone is chasing money. It doesn't. A game simply asks what each player values. The answer is often surprising. So here's a question. Think of someone you're in a tough conversation with right now. Which of these six cards matters most to them? Are you sure? We'll go much deeper on this in Module Two. For now let's look at how interests line up between people.
