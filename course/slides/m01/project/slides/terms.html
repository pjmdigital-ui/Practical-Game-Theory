Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here are the key terms from this module. You'll find all of them in your workbook. A game is a situation where the outcome depends at least partly on the decisions of more than one person. It can be cooperative. It can be competitive. Most often it's mixed. A player is any decision-maker who can affect the outcome. That includes people who aren't in the room. Mixed interests are interests that are neither fully aligned nor fully opposed. Think of the two business partners. Available actions are what each player can actually do. Game theorists also call them strategies. They're distinct from what the player wants to do. A constraint is anything that limits a player's choices. It might be a policy or a budget. It might be an approval requirement or a veto. And remember that a stated constraint and a real constraint aren't always the same. An outside option is what happens to each player if the interaction produces no agreement. You'll also hear it called an alternative. Here's a quick check for you.
