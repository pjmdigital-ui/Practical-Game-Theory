Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Most communication advice begins with a deceptively simple question. What should I say? Think about asking for a raise. Before you walk in you rehearse your words. You pick your number. You think about how to show your value. You try to guess the objections. All of that matters. But it skips what comes first. What does your boss actually want? What happens if they say no? What alternatives does each of you have? What will your boss probably do after you speak? Once you ask those questions the conversation becomes a game. I don't mean a contest against your boss. I mean a game in the game-theoretic sense. That's a situation where the outcome depends at least partly on the decisions of more than one person. Look at the cards on this slide. Your words don't land in empty space. They enter a system that already holds incentives and expectations. It holds information and constraints. It holds alternatives and history. It holds timing. And it holds other people making their own decisions. Here's the part I love. Seeing the game isn't a cynical view where everyone is manipulating everyone. It's simpler than that. Other people are decision-makers. Their decisions affect your outcomes. And when you see that clearly, cooperation often gets easier. You start to understand why the other person's behavior makes sense from where they stand. So here's a question for you. Think of a conversation you're dreading right now. Have you spent more time on the words or on the situation? Let's look at what you'll be able to do by the end of this module.
