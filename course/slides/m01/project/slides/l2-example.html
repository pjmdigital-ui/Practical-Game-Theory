Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

One of the first mistakes we make is assuming the person we're talking to is the entire game. Sometimes they aren't. Here's the situation. You're negotiating with a salesperson. In your head the game is simple. It's you versus the salesperson. Two players. One price. Now look at the middle card. Maybe the salesperson has to get approval from a manager. Maybe the company has a pricing policy. Maybe another customer is interested in the same product. And maybe you have three alternative suppliers. Suddenly the strategic situation looks very different. The manager is a player. The policy is a constraint. The other customer changes how much the salesperson needs you. And your three suppliers change how much you need them. None of that shows up in the words being spoken. It sits under the surface. That's the lesson on the right. The visible conversation is only the surface. There may be other people whose decisions affect the outcome. And sometimes the people who matter most aren't even in the room. Think about what this means for your approach. If the salesperson can't go lower without a manager, then a brilliant argument aimed at them alone may go nowhere. You might do better to ask who else needs to sign off. Or to make it easy for them to take your case upstairs. Here's the common misunderstanding. We assume the loudest person in the conversation is the most important player. Often they're not. So in your next negotiation ask yourself this. Who isn't here but still has a say? Let's lock that in.
