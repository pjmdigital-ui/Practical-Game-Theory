Other people are decision-makers. Their decisions affect your outcomes.

Not this

"Everyone is trying to manipulate everyone else."

This

"My choice makes sense only in light of theirs."

Nash's insight: each person's behavior has to be understood in relation to everyone else's.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

I want to be really clear about the premise here. It's easy to hear "game theory" and think I'm handing you a cynical philosophy. You know the one. Everyone is trying to manipulate everyone else. That's the left side of the slide. It's not what I'm proposing. What I'm proposing is much simpler. Other people are decision-makers. Their decisions affect your outcomes. That's it. That's the whole premise. John Nash gave us a way to say this precisely. His equilibrium concept captures a basic idea. Each player's behavior has to be considered in relation to the behavior of the other players. Your choice makes sense only in light of theirs. Their choice makes sense only in light of yours. Here's how that plays out. Think back to the raise. Your boss isn't a vending machine where the right words drop out a yes. Your boss is weighing a budget. They're weighing what you might do if they say no. They're weighing what their own boss will think. And you're weighing what they'll do. Each of you is reacting to the other. Once you see that, communication starts to look very different. You stop asking only "What's wrong with them?" You start asking "What about this situation makes their choice reasonable?" Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. This premise isn't about suspicion. It's about respect. You're treating the other person as someone who makes real choices.
