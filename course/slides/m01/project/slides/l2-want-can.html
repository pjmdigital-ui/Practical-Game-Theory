Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's a question we overlook all the time. We tend to think about what we can do. Game theory makes us look at what everyone can do. Look at the three cards. Suppose the person at the counter genuinely wants to give you a refund. But company policy doesn't allow it. Suppose your boss really wants to give you a raise. But the budget has already been approved. Suppose your business partner wants to accept your proposal. But their other partner has veto power. In each case the person's preference is only part of the picture. Their available actions matter too. Formal game theory keeps these two things apart on purpose. A player's strategies are what they can choose. Their preferences are how they rank the outcomes. Those are separate pieces of the model. In plain language it comes down to one line. Wanting something and being able to do something are not the same thing. Why does this matter in a real conversation? Because when we hear "no" we usually assume the other person doesn't want to say yes. So we push harder on their motivation. We make a better argument. We get more emotional. But if the real problem is what they're able to do, none of that helps. You're pushing on the wrong lever. A better question is this. If you wanted to say yes, what would stop you? That question invites them to show you their constraints. Then you can work on the constraint together. Here's one for you. Think of a "no" you got recently. Was it really a "won't"? Or was it a "can't"? Let's see all of this in one example.
