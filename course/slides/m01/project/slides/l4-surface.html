Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's where it gets even more interesting. Sometimes the obvious subject of a conversation isn't the whole problem. Look at the three cards. Someone says "I don't care about the money." But perhaps what really matters to them is recognition. Someone says "I just want to know why you didn't tell me." But perhaps the deeper issue is trust. Someone says "I don't care who gets credit." But perhaps they care a great deal about status. In each case there's a stated topic. And there may be a second game running underneath it. If you only answer the stated topic you can win the point and still miss what the person needed. Now I need to be careful here. This is where people take the idea too far. The lesson is not "assume everyone is lying about their motives." That's not strategic thinking. That's paranoia. And paranoia makes you worse at reading people. You start inventing hidden agendas that aren't there. The lesson is narrower than that. Don't assume the surface description of an interaction tells you everything about its strategic structure. Sometimes the stated issue really is the issue. Sometimes it isn't. Your job is to stay curious. So how do you stay curious without getting suspicious? Ask open questions. Notice when the energy in a conversation doesn't match the size of the topic. A small issue with a big reaction is often a clue that something else is at stake. Here's a question for you.
