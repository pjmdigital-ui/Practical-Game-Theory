Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Imagine you're negotiating. The other person says this. "I would love to give you that price. But I simply can't go below ten thousand dollars." What's your first instinct? For a lot of us it's to assume they're lying. That's just what people say, right? Or we swing the other way and accept it as a fact. Both of those reactions skip a better question. What actually constrains them? Look at the three cards. Maybe nothing does and they're bluffing. Maybe the company really does have a minimum price. Or maybe they have the authority to go lower but don't want to. Here's the key point. Those three situations sound exactly the same. The words are identical. The tone might be identical. But strategically they're completely different games. If it's a bluff there's room to move. If it's a real floor then pushing harder just burns goodwill. You'd be better off changing what you're buying or how you pay. If they're able but unwilling then the question is what would make going lower worth it to them. Each one calls for a different move. So the skill here is separation. Separate what people say constrains them from what actually constrains them. Don't assume a stated constraint is a lie. Don't assume it's true either. Treat it as a claim to understand. Here's the common mistake. We decide in the first two seconds which of these three it is. Then we spend the rest of the conversation acting on a guess. We'll come back to this hard in later modules on credibility. For now let's ask what happens if you don't reach a deal at all.
