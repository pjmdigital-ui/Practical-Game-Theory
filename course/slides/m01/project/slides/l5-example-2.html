Example · The stalled project

"My coworker is procrastinating."

The situation

You and a coworker both want a project finished. It isn't moving. You think they're procrastinating.

What happened

They think you'll take over if they start. You both want the same outcome. Your expectations of each other create the problem.

The lesson

That isn't necessarily a character flaw. It may be a game. Once you see the structure you may be able to change it.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Here's a smaller version of the same idea. I think almost everyone has lived this one. Here's the situation. You and a coworker both want a project completed. It matters to both of you. But it isn't moving. And from where you sit the explanation is obvious. They're procrastinating. Now look at the middle card. From where they sit the picture is different. They think you're going to take over if they start. Maybe they've seen you jump in before. Maybe they don't want to do work you'll redo. So they wait. And because they wait, you get more convinced they're procrastinating. Notice what's going on. You both want the same outcome. There's no conflict of goals at all. But your expectations about each other are creating the problem. Each of you is responding reasonably to what you expect the other to do. And the result is a project nobody's moving. That's the lesson on the right. That's not necessarily a character flaw. It may be a game. And once you see the structure, you may be able to change it. How? You could talk about the expectation directly. Something like "I won't take this over. It's yours from here." Or you could split the work so each person clearly owns a piece. Either move changes the game instead of blaming the player. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. When a shared goal stalls we assume someone doesn't care. Often both people care. They're just stuck in each other's expectations. So think of something that's stalled in your life right now. What might the other person expect you to do? Now let's put the whole shift into one picture.
