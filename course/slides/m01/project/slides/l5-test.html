Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Let's put this into practice with a simple test. Imagine your boss sends you a text. "We need to talk about your performance." What do you do? Be honest. Most people immediately start preparing what they're going to say. They build a defense. They list their wins. They rehearse. But stop. Try seeing the game first. Look at the cards. What do you know? Not much. What don't you know? Almost everything important. What does your boss want? You don't know yet. What options does your boss have? Several. What options do you have? Also several. What happens if the conversation goes badly? Potentially quite a lot. What happens if it goes well? Potentially quite a lot. Then there's information. What might your boss know that you don't? What might you know that your boss doesn't? And who else could influence the outcome? And then the question at the bottom. Perhaps the most important one. Why did your boss choose those particular words? Was it a warning? Was it a rushed message? Was it pressure from someone above them? You can't know yet. But simply asking changes how you walk in. You're not preparing a speech anymore. You're trying to understand the game. That usually means you ask before you defend. You find out what this is about. Here's the common mistake. We treat an ambiguous message as a verdict and walk in braced for a fight. That can turn a neutral conversation into a hostile one. You'll actually get to role-play this exact scenario in the workbook. For now let's lock in the lesson.
