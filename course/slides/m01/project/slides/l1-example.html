Example · The raise request

Before you rehearse a word

The situation

You plan to say: "I've been here three years. I've increased sales by 30%. I'd like to discuss my compensation." Then you polish every word.

What happened

You ask what comes first. What does your boss want? What if they say no? What if you turn down a counteroffer? Who decides? Who else influences it?

The lesson

The "conversation" is really a structure. It has several players and options. Its consequences run well past your first sentence.

Internomics · Module 1 — See the Game

Let's make this concrete with the raise. Here's the situation. You've decided to ask. You plan to walk in and say something like this. "I've been here for three years. I've increased sales by 30%. I'd like to discuss increasing my compensation." Most of us would spend the next few days polishing that sentence. And most communication books would jump right in to grade it. Was it the right thing to say? Should you have phrased it differently? Should you have built more rapport first? Should you have asked a question instead? Those can be worthwhile questions. But look at the middle card. Here's what happens when you see the game first. You start asking different questions. What does your boss actually want? What happens if your boss says no? What happens if you say no to a counteroffer? What alternatives do you have? What alternatives does your boss have? What does your boss know that you don't? Who has the authority to make the decision? Who else might influence it? Suddenly this isn't simply a conversation. It's a game. Not a game where you're trying to beat your boss. A game where the outcome depends on more than one person's decisions. That's the lesson on the right. The conversation turns into a structure. It has several players and several options. And its consequences run well past your first sentence. So here's my question for you. The last time you asked for something important, how much of your prep went into the words? How much went into the situation? Next let's look at the one question that comes before all the others.
