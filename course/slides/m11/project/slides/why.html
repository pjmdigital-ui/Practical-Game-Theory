Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Picture a sales organization. Management keeps telling people to collaborate. Share leads. Help one another. Teach each other what's working. Don't compete internally. But every month the company publicly ranks each salesperson against the others. Bonuses go only to the top performers. Promotions lean heavily on individual ranking. The lowest performers fear losing their jobs. Then management is shocked that salespeople guard information. So they hold a workshop. They talk about teamwork and company values and abundance. Nothing changes. Why? Because the game is saying something different from the speech. Management says cooperate. The incentive system says your coworkers are your competitors. Guess which message wins. At some point the strategic question is no longer how to persuade these people to collaborate. It becomes a different question. What behavior does our system actually reward? That's the beginning of game design. This module moves you from fixing people to examining systems. It moves you from reacting to bad outcomes to redesigning the structure that produces them. And there's one more thing I want you to hold from the start. Designers usually hold more power than players. So every lever we cover ties back to the foundation of this course. Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice. Here's a question to carry through the next hour. Where in your own life is the speech saying one thing while the game says another? Let's look at the signs that you're inside that kind of game.
