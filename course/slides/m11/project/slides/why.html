Why this module matters

The game is saying something different from the speech.

Management says "cooperate." Monthly rankings and top-only bonuses say "your coworkers are your competitors."

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Picture a sales organization. Management keeps saying, "We want everyone to collaborate. Share leads. Help one another. Don't compete internally." But every month the company publicly ranks each salesperson against the others. Bonuses go only to the top performers. Promotions lean heavily on individual ranking, and the lowest performers fear losing their jobs. Then management is shocked that people guard information. So they hold a workshop on teamwork and values. Nothing changes. Why? Because the game is saying something different from the speech. At some point the strategic question stops being "How can we persuade these people to collaborate?" and becomes "What behavior does our system actually reward?" That's the beginning of game design. And because designers usually hold more power than players, everything in this module ties back to truth, autonomy, dignity, and informed choice.
