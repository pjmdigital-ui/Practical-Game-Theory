Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here's one strong answer. The game rewards closing lots of tickets quickly and not helping others, because mentoring or untangling a hard ticket lowers your count while your coworkers are your rivals. It punishes complex work, mentoring, and thorough fixes. The workshops are speeches; the leaderboard is the game. The metric is tickets closed; the goal is solved problems and a stronger team. Because reopened tickets count as new, a shallow fix earns credit twice, and ticket-splitting is people playing the metric. Levers: a team-level reward, or one weighted toward problems that stay solved; count a reopen against the original resolution; publish reopen rates; let senior engineers escalate within limits so tickets don't wait on Dana's calendar. Then stress-test: reward low reopen rates and people might discourage customers from reopening. That's design, not accusation. Finally, firing people fixes the player before inspecting the game. Accountability can still matter, but a new hire faces the same payoffs. Dana should name the mechanism, make the smallest testable change, explain it openly to the team, then observe and adapt.
