Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Even something as simple as a default can change the strategic environment. Here's the point. What happens when nobody chooses is itself a rule. And rules influence outcomes. Look at the two cards. Imagine every project automatically continues unless someone explicitly stops it. Inertia favors continuation. Projects that should have died years ago keep going. Nobody wants to be the one to kill them. Now flip it. Imagine every project automatically expires unless someone actively renews it. Inertia now favors termination. Somebody has to stand up and say this one is worth keeping. Neither rule forces anyone to decide anything. But each one structures what happens when no player acts. And in most organizations most players don't act most of the time. They're busy. So the default quietly decides a huge share of outcomes. Defaults are easy to underestimate because they don't look like strategy. Nobody argues about them in meetings. They just sit there doing their work. Here's where this shows up in your life. A recurring meeting that stays on the calendar until someone cancels it. A subscription that renews unless you remember to stop it. A weekly family plan that happens unless someone objects. Each default tilts the game in one direction. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People think a default is neutral because it doesn't force anything. It isn't neutral. It decides who has to spend effort to change the outcome. So ask yourself this. What default in your work or home life is quietly making decisions for you?
