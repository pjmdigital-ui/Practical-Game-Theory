Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Two families of problems explain a lot of this drift. The first is the principal-agent problem. Suppose you own a business. You hire someone to manage it. You want long-term business value. The manager may care about that too. But perhaps their bonus depends mainly on this year's revenue. Now you and the manager are not perfectly aligned. You are the principal. You want one thing. The agent acts on your behalf and may have somewhat different incentives. And here's the crucial part in the dark card. The agent often knows more about their own actions than you do. You can't see every decision they make. So you get two things at once. A difference in payoffs. And a gap in information. Look at the bottom of the slide. This pattern shows up everywhere. Shareholders and executives. Managers and employees. Clients and advisors. Voters and politicians. Patients and medical providers. Homeowners and contractors. Think about hiring a contractor to redo your kitchen. You want it done right. They may be paid the same whether they cut a corner behind the wall or not. And they know what's behind that wall far better than you do. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. The solution is not always to hire better people. Sometimes the challenge is design. You shape the incentives and the monitoring. You shape the authority and the accountability. You shape the information. You do it so the agent's behavior lines up better with what the principal wants. So ask yourself this. Where are you the principal right now? And where are you the agent?
