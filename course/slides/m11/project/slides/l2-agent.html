Two problems to watch for

Why good people drift from the goal

The principal-agent problem

You (the principal) want one thing. The agent acting for you has somewhat different incentives, and often knows more about their own actions than you do.

Multitask incentives

Most jobs have several valuable tasks. Strongly reward the easiest one to measure, and effort can drift away from work that's harder to see.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Two families of problems explain a lot of this drift. The first is the principal-agent problem. Suppose you own a business and hire someone to manage it. You want long-term value. The manager may care about that too, but perhaps their bonus depends mainly on this year's revenue. You, the principal, want one thing; the agent acting on your behalf has somewhat different incentives, and often knows more about their own actions than you do. It shows up with shareholders and executives, clients and advisors, homeowners and contractors. The answer isn't always "hire better people." Sometimes it's designing incentives, monitoring, authority, and information so they line up. The second is multitask incentives, from Bengt Holmström and Paul Milgrom. A teacher has to teach, develop reasoning, encourage curiosity, mentor. Reward only the easiest thing to measure, and effort can shift away from the rest.
