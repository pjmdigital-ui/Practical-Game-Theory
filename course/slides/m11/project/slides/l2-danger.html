The warning

People optimize against the scoreboard.

Tie a measurement to money, status, promotion or punishment, and it may stop representing the thing you cared about.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Changing an incentive can change behavior substantially. We'll look at a striking study of tax inspectors in Pakistan in the research spotlight. But here's the warning: designing incentives is dangerous precisely because incentives work. Imagine I tell my team, "I want higher-quality leads." Then I pay them based entirely on the number of leads generated. What will tend to increase? Probably the number of leads. Quality may fall. Then I accuse the team of missing the point. But the scoreboard told them what the point was. Once a measurement is tied to money, status, promotion, punishment, or reputation, people begin optimizing against it. And the measurement may stop being a good representation of the thing you originally cared about.
