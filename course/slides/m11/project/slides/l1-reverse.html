Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Most of the game theory we've explored so far asks one kind of question. Given the game, what are the players likely to do? We take the rules as fixed. Then we work out the smartest move inside them. Mechanism design reverses the question. It asks something different. Given the outcome we want, how should we design the game? That's the whole shift on this slide. On the left the rules are a given and the players are the variable. On the right the outcome is the given and the rules become the variable. Now you don't need the mathematics for this. The mental shift is what matters. Instead of saying "Here are the rules. What should I do?" we say "Here is the outcome we want. What rules would make that outcome more likely?" That's a radically different way to think. Think about the meetings you've sat in where the same problem came up again. Most of the energy usually goes into the left-hand question. How do we get this person to behave differently? Which argument will finally land? Very little energy goes into the right-hand question. What would the rules have to look like for the behavior we want to make sense? Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Mechanism design isn't only for economists and governments. You design games all the time. You do it every time you set a deadline or a bonus or a house rule. The only question is whether you design them on purpose.
