Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

More levers. Players: two people can't agree on a property's value, so bring in an independent appraiser. A buyer doesn't trust the seller, so bring in an inspector. A third party changes the information structure. Observability: if nobody can see who produced poor work, that's one game; if defects can be traced to the stage where they happened, that's another. Defaults: if a project continues unless someone stops it, inertia favors continuation; if it expires unless someone renews it, inertia favors termination. What happens when nobody chooses is itself a rule. Feedback: a lesson that arrives six months later is much harder to learn from than one that arrives right away. Then two levers that subtract. Sometimes the better design is removing the incentive that's distorting things. People who already took pride in their work get a competitive bonus for every tiny output, and suddenly nobody helps anyone. Not every problem needs another rule; sometimes the accumulated rules are the problem. And finally, exit, which deserves its own slide.
