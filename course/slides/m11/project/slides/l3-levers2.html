Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here are six more levers. Players first. Sometimes a third person transforms an interaction. Two people can't agree on the value of a property. So bring in an independent appraiser. Two employees can't resolve a conflict. Bring in a mediator. A buyer doesn't trust a seller's claims. Bring in an inspector. Each time it's a different game. Remember Module Seven. A third party can change credibility because they change the information structure. Players are part of the architecture. Observability is next. Imagine nobody can see who produced poor work. That's one incentive structure. Now imagine defects can be traced back to the stage where they happened. That's a different game. Visibility affects reputation and accountability. It affects signaling and reciprocity too. Defaults and feedback get their own moment in a second. Feedback is simple to state. A lesson that arrives six months later is much harder to learn from than one that arrives right away. Sometimes redesign means shortening the distance between action and consequence. Now notice the two dark cards. They subtract. We usually assume game design means adding things. More rules. More rewards. Sometimes the better design is removing the incentive that's distorting behavior. And sometimes the best move is to stop playing a game you can't redesign. That's exit. We'll look at both of these in detail. Here's a question for you. When you last tried to fix a recurring problem, did you add something or remove something? Most of us only ever add. Let's start with the lever that doesn't look like strategy at all.
