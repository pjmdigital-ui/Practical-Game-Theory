When players are unequal

Sometimes the rules should protect the weaker player

The used-car problem: the seller knows far more about the car than the buyer. Rules can change that game.

Disclosure

The seller has to share what they know.

Inspection rights

The buyer can have the car checked.

Warranties

The seller stands behind the claims.

The answer isn't always "Buyer, become smarter."

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Mechanism design matters especially when players are unequal. They may have unequal information or expertise or bargaining power. Sometimes the rules themselves should protect the weaker player. In the used-car problem a seller knows far more about the car than the buyer does. A rule requiring disclosure changes the game. So do inspection rights and warranties. Each one moves information or risk back toward the side that lacked it. In which of your games are you holding most of the cards? Now a real system redesigned for exactly this reason.
