Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

That gives us one of the most important questions in this module. What exactly am I rewarding? Notice the wording. Not what do I intend to reward. What behavior actually improves the metric? Those two can be very far apart. Here's an example. Suppose you tell a teacher their bonus depends on their students' test scores. What do you want? Probably better education. What did you actually reward? Higher scores on a particular measurement. Those aren't necessarily the same thing. The teacher can raise the score by teaching better. The teacher can also raise it by drilling the test. Your reward can't tell the difference. So you have to walk the incentive forward yourself. Look at the four cards. Every one is a payoff someone attached to a number. You create a bonus for the number of calls. Ask whether people could make useless calls. You reward the number of bugs fixed. Ask whether people could generate easy bugs. You reward customer reviews. Ask whether employees could pressure only happy customers to leave one. You reward quarterly profit. Ask whether managers could sacrifice long-term investment. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. Asking these questions isn't cynical. You're not accusing anyone. You're stress-testing the game before real people start playing it. It's much cheaper to find the loophole on a whiteboard than to discover it six months later. So try it now. Pick one thing you reward at work or at home. Write down the laziest way someone could hit that number. Now let's see this question play out in a real department.
