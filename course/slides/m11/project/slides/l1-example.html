Example · The cake

One cuts, the other chooses

The situation

Two children. One cake. Each wants the larger piece. Even a perfect split by the parent draws complaints.

What happened

The parent changes the rule. One child cuts. The other chooses first. The cutter makes the pieces as equal as possible.

The lesson

No new personalities. No lecture on fairness. The rules made self-interest produce the fair outcome.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here's a simple example often used to illustrate mechanism design. Two children need to divide a cake. Each wants the larger piece. Even a perfect split by the parent draws complaints. So the parent changes the mechanism. One child cuts. The other chooses first. Suddenly the cutter has every reason to make the pieces as equal as possible. If one piece is clearly larger, the other child takes it. The parent didn't give a lecture on fairness. Both still want the bigger piece. The rules made their own incentives produce the fair outcome. There's a name for what this achieves.
