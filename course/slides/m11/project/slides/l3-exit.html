Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

There's one more form of game change that people often overlook. You can sometimes stop playing. Imagine you've spent years trying to optimize your behavior inside a relationship or an organization or a market. Look at the dark card. The incentives remain destructive. Commitments aren't credible. Trust cannot be restored. Every equilibrium is bad. And you lack the authority to redesign the rules. At some point you have to ask a hard question. Why am I trying to become better at this game? Exit changes the game. If a business relationship is consistently destructive, you can leave. If a negotiation can't produce an acceptable agreement, use your outside option. If a market is structurally unattractive, compete somewhere else. If a conversation becomes abusive, end it. Not every game deserves another move. But here's the other half. Look at the second card. Leaving isn't magically correct. Exit carries costs. You may lose money. You may lose relationships and status. You may lose opportunity and future upside. So we go back to the earlier models. Module Two asks what the payoffs are. Module Three asks the next question. Then what? Module Nine asks what future you're giving up. That's why these models aren't isolated tricks. They work together. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Some people never consider exit and keep polishing their moves in a hopeless game. Others treat exit as the answer to every frustration. Both skip the analysis. So think of a game you've been playing for a long time with no improvement. Have you honestly priced the exit? Now let's see the levers at work in everyday life.
