Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

We often assume game design means adding rewards. Here's the point of this slide. Sometimes the better design is removing the thing that's creating the distortion. Walk through the three cards with me. Before. Employees already take pride in doing good work. They help each other. Nobody had to pay them to care. Then management introduces a competitive bonus for every tiny output. That's the new game. And after? Coworkers stop helping one another. Helping a colleague now costs you points. What happened here? Perhaps the organization didn't need stronger motivation at all. It already had motivation. It introduced the wrong game. The new bonus turned colleagues into rivals and pushed out the pride that was already working. So the solution could be to simplify. Remove the competition. Reduce the measurement. Restore discretion. Let people use their judgment again. This idea matters because our instinct runs the other way. When something goes wrong we add a rule. Then another rule to close the gap in the first one. Then an exception. Over time the rules pile up. And people spend more energy navigating the rules than doing the work. Not every problem requires another rule. Sometimes the accumulated rules are the problem. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. Removing an incentive isn't the same as not caring about results. It's recognizing that a measurement can crowd out the very thing it was meant to encourage. So ask yourself this. Is there a rule or reward in your world that you'd remove tomorrow if you could? What would happen if you did? Now for the most dramatic way to change a game.
