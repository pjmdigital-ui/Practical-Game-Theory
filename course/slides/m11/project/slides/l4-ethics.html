Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Throughout this course we've used four principles: truth, autonomy, dignity, informed choice. They matter especially here, because when you design the game you usually have more power than the people playing it. You control the rules, the information, the choices, the defaults, sometimes the consequences. That power creates responsibility. So a good mechanism doesn't only ask, "Will this produce the behavior I want?" It asks: does the player understand the game? Do they keep meaningful choice? Are important facts hidden? Does the system exploit confusion? Does it treat people as decision-makers rather than objects to be manipulated? A dark pattern is still game design. It's just game design serving the wrong ethical objective. And sometimes the rules should protect the weaker player. Remember the used-car problem: disclosure, inspection rights and warranties change the game, so the answer isn't always "buyer, become smarter."
