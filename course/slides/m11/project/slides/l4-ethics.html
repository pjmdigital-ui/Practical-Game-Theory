Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Throughout this course we've used four principles. Truth. Autonomy. Dignity. Informed choice. They matter especially here. Here's why. When you design the game you usually have more power than the people playing it. You control the rules. You control the information and the choices. You control the defaults. Sometimes you control the consequences too. That power creates responsibility. So a good mechanism doesn't only ask whether it will produce the behavior you want. It asks four more questions. Look at the cards. Clarity. Does the player understand the game they're in? Choice. Do they keep meaningful choice? Or has the design quietly taken it away? Openness. Are important facts hidden? Does the system exploit confusion? Respect. Does it treat people as decision-makers? Or does it treat them as objects to be manipulated? Now look at the line at the bottom. A dark pattern is still game design. It's just game design serving the wrong ethical objective. That's an important thing to admit. The techniques in this module are neutral tools. The same lever that makes honesty easy can make a bad deal hard to escape. What separates them is the objective. And whether the people inside the game would agree to it if they understood it. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Some people think ethics is a brake on good design. In this framework it's part of the design brief. The goal was never the outcome by any means. So ask yourself this. If the people inside your system could see exactly how it works, would they feel respected? Let's look at what informed choice means in practice.
