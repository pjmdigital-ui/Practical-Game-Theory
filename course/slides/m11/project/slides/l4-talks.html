Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Remember Module Three and its question. Then what? Mechanism design needs the same discipline. Let's walk the chain on the slide. You change a rule. Then what? People respond. Then what? They learn how the rule works. Then what? They adapt. And then what? They may discover a way to produce the metric without producing the outcome you wanted. So game design isn't "set rule, get outcome." That's the fantasy version. The real version is the chain you see here. You set a rule. Players respond. Players learn. Players adapt. The system evolves. Which means something important. Designers are players too. You don't stand outside the game controlling everything like a chess player moving pieces. Once you create rules, everyone else begins responding strategically to them. And you have to respond to their responses. Think about the tickets example we'll see in the practice exercise. A manager ranks engineers by tickets closed. That's the rule. The engineers respond by closing fast. They learn that reopened tickets count as new. They adapt by splitting issues into extra tickets. The system has evolved into something nobody designed on purpose. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Many people treat a new policy as the end of the work. They announce it and move on. In fact the announcement is the start of a new round. The players are about to move. So here's a question for you. Think of the last rule you set for a team or a family. What did people learn about it in the first month? And what did they do with what they learned? When the system responds, it often surprises you. Let's look at how.
