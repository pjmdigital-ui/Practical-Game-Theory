Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

So how do you know you're dealing with a game problem and not a move problem? Here are the signs I look for. The point is simple. When the same bad outcome keeps coming back no matter who's involved, suspect the structure before you suspect the people. Look at the cards. The rules produce the very behavior you're trying to stop. People are rewarded for competing when what you need is cooperation. The information is distributed badly. Some people know things that others need to know. Authority sits in the wrong place. The person who has to fix the problem can't decide anything. And everyone has learned to game the metric. Then comes the ritual in the dark card. Every time the bad outcome appears, somebody calls another meeting. And somebody says we need people to communicate better. Maybe. Communication does matter. But maybe you've built a game that keeps producing exactly what it was built to produce. In that case better wording won't save you. You can say the perfect thing inside a broken game and still get the broken result. Think of the sales team from a moment ago. They didn't need a better workshop. They needed a ranking system that didn't turn coworkers into rivals. Here's a question for you. Pick one problem in your life that keeps returning. How many of these six signs does it show? If it shows two or three, you may be working on the wrong level. Now let me tell you what you'll be able to do by the end of this module.
