Six levers that change the game

Rules

A clear sales-to-customer-success handoff

Payoffs

Compensation, payment schedules, a refundable deposit

Information

Dashboards, transparent criteria, independent verification

Timing

Sequential moves, immediate feedback, staged payments

Options

Trial periods, phased rollout, partial agreements

Who decides

Assigned decision rights, an independent expert

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Let's walk the levers. Rules: two departments keep fighting over who owns a customer after the sale. You can run conflict training every quarter, or you can say, "Sales owns the relationship until this event, then it transfers to customer success through this handoff." That doesn't guarantee harmony, but it removes one source of strategic ambiguity. Payoffs: a vendor has no reason to fix a problem after full payment, so change the payment schedule; a customer has no reason to return equipment, so add a refundable deposit. Not every behavior should be bought, but payoffs matter. Information: who knows what, and when. Timing: sometimes the problem isn't the players, it's the sequence. Options: when every conflict only allows yes or no, adding a trial period or a partial agreement creates new moves, and new options can alter the equilibrium. And who decides: two partners who need unanimous agreement on everything are in a different game from partners where A decides marketing and B decides operations. Who gets to decide is part of the game.
