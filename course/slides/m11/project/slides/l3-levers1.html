Six levers that change the game

Rules

Define what players can do. A clear handoff from sales to customer success.

Payoffs

Change what pays. A new payment schedule. A refundable deposit.

Information

Change who knows what and when. Dashboards. Transparent criteria.

Timing

Change who moves when. Sequential moves. Feedback now instead of yearly.

Options

Create new moves. Trial periods. Partial agreements.

Who decides

Assign decision rights. Partner A runs marketing. Partner B runs operations.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Let's walk the levers. Each one is a different part of the game you can redesign. Rules first. Two departments keep fighting over who owns a customer after the sale. You can run conflict training every quarter. Or you can set a rule. Sales owns the relationship until a certain event. Then ownership transfers to customer success through a defined handoff. That doesn't guarantee harmony. But it removes one source of strategic ambiguity. Payoffs next. A vendor has no reason to fix a problem after receiving full payment. So change the payment schedule. A customer has no reason to return equipment. So add a refundable deposit. The point isn't that every behavior should be bought. The point is that payoffs matter. Information is about who knows what and when. Remember the farmers from Module Ten. Making everyone's intentions visible changed behavior. Timing is about the sequence. Two people deciding at the same time are in one game. One moving first while the other responds is a different game. Annual feedback and immediate feedback are different games too. Sometimes the problem isn't the players. It's the sequence. Options create new moves. If every conflict only allows yes or no, add a trial period or a partial agreement. New options can change the equilibrium. And who decides. Two partners who need unanimous agreement on everything are in one game. Partners where A decides marketing and B decides operations are in another. Who gets to decide is part of the game. Which of these six have you never thought of as something you could change?
