Design for the behavior people are likely to have

For saints

"Everyone will behave perfectly. We don't need safeguards."

For monsters

"Everyone is trying to cheat us. Build a prison."

For people

Self-interest and morality. Loyalty and ambition. Error and creativity. All at once.

A robust design doesn't assume everyone is selfish. It also doesn't require everyone to be a saint.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here's a balance to hold. Don't design for the behavior you wish people had. Design for the behavior they're likely to have. Suppose an expense policy relies entirely on everyone behaving reasonably. But if abuse brings large benefits with no meaningful cost, somebody may exploit it. One extreme designs for saints. The other designs for monsters and builds a prison. Real human beings contain self-interest and morality at the same time. Is a policy you've written designed for saints or for monsters? Next let's talk about a design tool that's easy to abuse.
