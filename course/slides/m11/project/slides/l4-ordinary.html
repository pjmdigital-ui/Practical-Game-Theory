Design for the behavior people are likely to have

For saints

"Everyone will behave perfectly. We don't need safeguards."

For monsters

"Everyone is trying to cheat us. Build a prison."

For people

Self-interest, morality, fairness, loyalty, error and creativity, all at once

A robust design doesn't assume everyone is selfish, and doesn't require everyone to be a saint.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here's a balance to hold. Don't design for the behavior you wish people had; design for the behavior they're likely to have. Suppose you write an expense policy that relies entirely on "everyone will behave reasonably." Maybe they will. But if abusing it brings large benefits with no meaningful cost, eventually somebody may exploit it, everyone else watches, and if nothing happens the behavior can spread. One extreme says we don't need safeguards. The other says everyone's trying to cheat us, so build a prison. Neither is wise. Real human beings contain self-interest, morality, reciprocity, fairness, loyalty, ambition, error, and creativity. You want a system that works reasonably well with ordinary people. Not only with saints, and not only against villains.
