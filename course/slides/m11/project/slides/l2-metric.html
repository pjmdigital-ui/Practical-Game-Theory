Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

This principle applies everywhere. The metric is not the goal. Let's read the table together. You want student learning. You measure test scores. You want healthy customers. You measure the number of appointments. You want useful customer service. You measure call duration. You want good content. You measure clicks. You want productive employees. You measure hours online. And my favorite. You want a healthy relationship. You measure who apologized first. Every one of those measurements may be useful. I'm not telling you to stop measuring. Measurement helps you see. But the measurement is not necessarily the goal. It's a stand-in for the goal. Here's how it works. The left column is what you care about. It's usually hard to see directly. So you pick something on the right that's easy to count and that tends to move with it. That works fine until you attach a payoff to the number. The moment the metric affects payoffs, people may begin playing the metric instead of pursuing the underlying objective. Look at that last row again. If a couple keeps score on who apologized first, the apology stops being about repair. It becomes a move in a contest. The number took over the goal. Now look at your own life for a second. What number are you watching at work or at home? Has it quietly started to stand in for the thing you actually care about?
