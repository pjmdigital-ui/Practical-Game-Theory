Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here's a simple principle. Incentives speak louder than mission statements. Let's walk down the table. An organization can say quality matters. But it rewards volume. It can say think long-term. But it measures only this quarter. It can say experiment. But it punishes every failure. It can say tell us the truth. But it retaliates against bad news. It can say work as a team. But it rewards people only for individual results. Every row is a contradiction between the words and the payoffs. And people live inside the payoffs. The mission statement is read once a year at an offsite. The reward shows up in every paycheck and every promotion decision and every performance review. When the stated value and the rewarded behavior conflict, don't be surprised when the reward wins. Think about that fourth row for a moment. Tell us the truth. Imagine you're the person who has to deliver bad news in that place. You've seen what happened to the last person who did it. What's your smart move? Silence. Nobody had to be a coward for that to happen. The game made silence the safe choice. Now here's a question for you. Look at your own team or your own family. Which row of this table do you recognize? Is there something you say you value while you quietly reward the opposite? Most of us have at least one. Before we go further though, I want to be fair about what incentives can and can't explain.
