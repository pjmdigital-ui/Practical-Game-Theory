Research spotlight · Khan, Khwaja & Olken

Tax inspectors in Punjab, Pakistan

What it shows

525 property-tax inspectors. Performance rankings set the order in which they chose future postings. Annual tax-revenue growth rose by roughly 30 to 41 percent.

What it doesn't prove

Not that any incentive redesign will help. Not that this one transfers unchanged to your team. Not that people will respond as the designer intended.

For your conversations: before another speech, ask what people genuinely value. Does your system connect the behavior you want to it?

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Two research spotlights pull in opposite directions. The first is a field experiment by Adnan Khan and Asim Khwaja and Benjamin Olken. It looked at property-tax administration in Punjab in Pakistan. In one study 525 property-tax inspectors took part. Performance rankings set the order in which they could choose future job postings. The system connected performance with something they valued. Annual tax-revenue growth rose by roughly thirty to forty-one percent. The structure changed. But it doesn't prove people will respond the way the designer intended. The next study shows incentives can move behavior the wrong way too.
