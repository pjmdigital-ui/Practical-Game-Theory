Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Two research spotlights this time, and they pull in opposite directions on purpose. The first is a field experiment by Adnan Khan, Asim Khwaja, and Benjamin Olken in Punjab, Pakistan. Five hundred twenty-five property-tax inspectors took part in a system where performance rankings determined the order in which they could choose their future job postings. Instead of telling bureaucrats to work harder, the design connected performance to something many of them genuinely valued: where they'd be assigned to work. The result was substantial: annual tax-revenue growth increased by roughly thirty to forty-one percent. The people weren't replaced. The structure changed, and behavior changed with it. So before your next speech, ask what people actually value, and whether your system connects the behavior you want to it. What it doesn't prove: that any incentive redesign will improve outcomes, that this design transfers unchanged to your team or family, or that people will respond the way the designer intended.
