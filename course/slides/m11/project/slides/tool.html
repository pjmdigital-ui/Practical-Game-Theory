Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Your tool for this module is the Game-Change Audit. It's in your workbook. Use it when the same bad outcome keeps coming back. You've tried better arguments and better wording. You've tried more motivation and more pressure. Nothing has fixed it. That's your signal. Let me walk you through the six steps. Step one is naming the problem. Write down the outcome that keeps returning. Write what you've already tried inside the current game. And be honest about your current character explanation. Are you telling yourself they're lazy or selfish or gaming it? Step two asks what the game actually rewards. Not what the policy claims to reward. What actually pays? Then ask what it punishes. Could the desirable action be costly right now? What metric are people optimizing? Step three is the levers. The workbook has a table for them. Rules. Payoffs. Information. Who decides. Timing. Options. Players. Observability and feedback. The default. Note what's easy and what's hard. And ask whether something should be removed instead of added. Step four is the stress test. How could a smart player game your new version? Then what? What harder-to-measure work might lose effort? Step five is the ethics check. Does the player understand the game? Do they keep meaningful choice? Are facts hidden? Are people treated as decision-makers? Step six is the smallest testable change. Name the mechanism you think is producing the behavior. Make the smallest change that tests it. Decide what you'll observe and when. And if exit is on the table, write down what you'd lose. Let's practice with a real-feeling case.
