Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

This reversal became important enough to win a Nobel Prize. In 2007 Leonid Hurwicz, Eric Maskin and Roger Myerson received the Nobel Prize in Economic Sciences. They got it for laying the foundations of mechanism design theory. I'm showing you this for one reason. I want you to know this isn't a clever trick I made up for a communication course. It's a serious field with serious foundations. Each of them added a different piece. Hurwicz introduced key ideas. One of them is incentive compatibility. We'll meet that idea in a few minutes with a cake. Maskin developed implementation theory. Myerson extended mechanism design to situations involving private information. That's when people know things the designer doesn't. Now please don't worry about the technical side. You won't need any equations in this module. What I want you to take from their work is the stance. They treated the rules of an interaction as something you can design. They didn't treat the rules as weather that just happens to you. Here's why that matters in ordinary life. By the time anyone opens their mouth in a meeting, the rules have already done a lot of work. Who gets paid for what. Who decides. Who knows what. Those choices shape behavior long before anyone gives a speech. So ask yourself this. In the groups you belong to, who set the rules? And did anyone design them on purpose? Before we design anything though, we need to stop blaming the wrong thing.
