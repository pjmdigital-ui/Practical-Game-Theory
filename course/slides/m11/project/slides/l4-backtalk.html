Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here's what it looks like when the system talks back. Suppose you redesign compensation. You expect more collaboration. Instead people start forming small alliances and excluding outsiders. That's data. You redesign a customer policy. You expect fewer complaints. And complaints do fall. But they fall because customers stop bothering and simply leave. That's data too. You introduce a productivity metric. Productivity rises. Quality collapses. That's data. In every case the system told you something about the game that you didn't know when you designed it. The question is how you respond. Look at the bottom of the slide. The wrong response is to ask why people aren't behaving according to your design. That response treats the players as the problem again. It's Lesson One all over. The right response is to ask what you misunderstood about the game. That's strategic humility. Here's why it matters. You can't perfectly control a complex human system. People adapt. Contexts change. Unexpected incentives appear. New players enter. Rules interact. And the designer's model can be wrong. So a better sequence is this. Design. Observe. Test. Update. Adapt. That should sound familiar. It's the same loop we've used through this whole course. Your first design is a hypothesis. Then reality responds. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. Don't confuse control with design. Mechanism design can sound like a way to control people. That's not what I want you to take from it. It's a way to learn about a system by changing it carefully. That's also why you start small. Make the smallest change that tests your idea. Then watch. Next let's ask who exactly we're designing for.
