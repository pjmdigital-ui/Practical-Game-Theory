Research spotlight · Glewwe, Ilias & Kremer

Teacher incentives in Kenya

What it shows

Rewards tied to test scores raised scores on the linked exams. Most of it came through test prep. Little sign of better teaching. No lasting learning gains.

What it doesn't prove

Not that incentives "don't work." Experiments in India and Tanzania found different results. The design of the incentive changes the game.

For your conversations: when you attach a payoff to a number, ask what behavior moves it. Then ask what harder work might be quietly abandoned.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

The second spotlight is the warning. Paul Glewwe, Nauman Ilias and Michael Kremer studied a program in Kenyan primary schools. It rewarded teachers based on students' test performance. Scores on the incentivized exams improved. But there was little evidence that attendance or classroom teaching improved. The gains were concentrated on the linked exams and didn't persist as broader learning. The incentive worked. Just not the way the designers hoped. It doesn't prove incentives don't work. In Tanzania a design with multiple thresholds produced greater learning gains at lower cost than one based on rank. Now the tool.
