Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

I want to be careful here. I'm not telling you that people are just machines that follow rewards. Incentives don't explain everything. People have values. People sometimes do what's right even when it costs them. You've seen it. Someone tells the truth knowing it will hurt their career. Someone helps a colleague when nobody is keeping score. People resist bad incentives all the time. So the point of this module isn't cynicism. It's something more practical. If you repeatedly create a payoff for a behavior, you shouldn't be shocked when more of that behavior appears. Both things are true at once. Some people will rise above a bad game. But you can't build a team or a family or a business that depends on everyone rising above it every single day. That's asking for heroism as a standing requirement. Here's the way I'd put it. Good people can survive a bad game. They shouldn't have to. And a well-designed game lets ordinary people do the right thing without a constant fight against their own interests. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Some people hear "incentives matter" and conclude that values don't. Others hear "people have values" and conclude that incentives don't matter. Both are wrong. The designer's job is to stop putting values and incentives at war with each other. Ask yourself this. Where are you counting on people's character to overcome a reward that points the other way? Now let me show you the cleanest example of putting incentives to work. It involves two children and a cake.
