Example · Boston school choice

Tell the truth or game the system?

The situation

Boston's old system let families gain by ranking schools strategically. Parents who listed their true wishes could lose out.

What happened

In 2005 Boston switched to a deferred-acceptance mechanism. It was designed to remove much of the incentive to misreport.

The lesson

Don't just say "Don't game the system." Ask why the system makes gaming useful.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

School choice gives us a much more consequential example. Boston once used a school-assignment system with a hidden problem. Families could benefit from strategically ranking schools instead of simply listing their true preferences. Think about what that meant for a parent filling out the form. You had to ask yourself something strange. Should I list the school I actually want? Or should I list something else because of how the mechanism works? Sophisticated parents could try to game the mechanism. Less sophisticated parents might simply report what they genuinely wanted. And they could be disadvantaged for doing so. Honesty itself could cost them. Four researchers studied it. Atila Abdulkadiroğlu and Parag Pathak. Alvin Roth and Tayfun Sönmez. They documented both kinds of behavior. Some families paid close attention to capacity limits. Others made mistakes that left them disproportionately unassigned. In 2005 Boston replaced the old system with a deferred-acceptance mechanism. It was designed to remove much of the incentive for that kind of strategic ranking. Now think about the lesson. You could tell parents not to game the system. Or you can ask why the system makes gaming strategically useful. That second question is mechanism design. A strategy-proof mechanism tries to make truthful reporting a good strategy. That's better than teaching every parent advanced game theory just to survive the rules. The design carries some of the strategic burden for them. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. It's tempting to call the strategic parents cheaters. They were responding to the rules they were given. So ask yourself this. Where are people around you being punished for simply telling the truth?
