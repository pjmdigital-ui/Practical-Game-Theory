Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

School choice gives us a much more consequential example. Boston once used an assignment system in which families could benefit from strategically ranking schools instead of simply listing their true preferences. Sophisticated parents could try to game it. Less sophisticated parents might just report what they genuinely wanted, and could be disadvantaged for it. Researchers Atila Abdulkadiroğlu, Parag Pathak, Alvin Roth, and Tayfun Sönmez documented both kinds of behavior. In 2005, Boston replaced the old system with a deferred-acceptance mechanism designed to remove much of that incentive. Think about the lesson. You could tell parents, "Don't game the system." Or you can ask, "Why does the system make gaming strategically useful?" That second question is mechanism design. A strategy-proof design carries some of the strategic burden for people, which is better than teaching every parent advanced game theory just to survive the rules.
