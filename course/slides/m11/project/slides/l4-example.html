Example · Boston school choice

Tell the truth or game the system?

The situation

Boston's old system let families gain by ranking schools strategically. Parents who listed their true wishes could lose out.

What happened

In 2005 Boston switched to a deferred-acceptance mechanism. It was designed to remove much of the incentive to misreport.

The lesson

Don't just say "Don't game the system." Ask why the system makes gaming useful.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

School choice gives us a much more consequential example. Boston once used a school-assignment system with a hidden problem. Families could benefit from strategically ranking schools instead of listing their true preferences. Parents who simply reported what they wanted could be disadvantaged. Four researchers documented both kinds of behavior. Atila Abdulkadiroğlu and Parag Pathak. Alvin Roth and Tayfun Sönmez. In 2005 Boston replaced it with a deferred-acceptance mechanism. It was designed to remove much of the incentive for strategic ranking. Instead of telling parents not to game the system, ask why the system makes gaming useful. That's mechanism design.
