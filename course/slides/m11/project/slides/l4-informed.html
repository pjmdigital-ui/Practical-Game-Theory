Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Let's make informed choice concrete. Suppose a customer is choosing between three plans. Both kinds of design will affect what that customer does. The question is how. Look at the dark card. Ethical design makes the price reasonably understandable. It makes the terms clear. It shows the tradeoffs between the plans. And it makes the cancellation rules easy to find. The customer may still pick the plan you hoped they'd pick. But they pick it knowing what they're getting. Now look at the light card. Manipulative design may bury the real price. It may bury the renewal terms. It may hide the exit mechanism. That design also shapes behavior. It may even shape it more powerfully in the short run. But only one of the two respects informed choice. Here's the standard I want you to carry. Our goal is not to get the outcome by any means available. That's a low bar and anyone can clear it. Our goal is to create a game in which good outcomes emerge without violating the people playing it. That's a much higher standard. And it's the one this whole course has been built on. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Some people think that if a term is technically disclosed somewhere, the choice was informed. Disclosure buried on page nine isn't the same as understanding. The test is whether a reasonable person playing your game knows what game they're in. So think about something you sell or offer or ask of people. Could a newcomer explain the real terms back to you after one look? If not, what would you need to bring to the surface? Sometimes the rules have to go further than clarity. They have to protect the weaker player.
