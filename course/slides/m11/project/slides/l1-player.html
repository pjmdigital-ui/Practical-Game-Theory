Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

This connects all the way back to Module One. We have a strong tendency to explain behavior through character. Look at the four cards. Someone isn't cooperating. We say they're selfish. Employees aren't taking initiative. We say they're lazy. Customers aren't telling us the truth. We say they're dishonest. Parents are working a school-choice system. We say they're gaming it. Maybe. Sometimes that's exactly right. But strategic thinking asks one more question before it settles on character. What does the game make attractive? Here's why that question matters. Sometimes perfectly ordinary people behave differently when the rules change. That's one of the great lessons of game theory. Behavior doesn't come only from personality. It emerges from the interaction between people and structure. Take that last card about parents gaming a school system. We're going to see a real case of it in Lesson Four. In Boston the old rules made strategic ranking useful. Parents who simply listed what they wanted could lose out. Calling those strategic parents dishonest misses the point. The rules invited the behavior. Now here's the misunderstanding I want you to avoid. I'm not saying structure explains everything. People still make choices and people are still responsible for them. I'm saying inspect the game before you decide the player is the problem. If you replace the person and keep the game, you may just get the same behavior from someone new. So think of someone you've labeled lazy or selfish lately. What does their situation actually reward? Next let's look at the loudest signal in any organization.
