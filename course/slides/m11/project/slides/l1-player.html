Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

This connects all the way back to Module One. We have a strong tendency to explain behavior through character. Someone isn't cooperating: "They're selfish." Employees aren't taking initiative: "They're lazy." Customers aren't telling us the truth: "They're dishonest." Parents are working a school-choice system: "They're gaming it." Maybe. But strategic thinking asks one more question: what does the game make attractive? Because sometimes perfectly ordinary people behave differently when the rules change. That's one of the great lessons of game theory. Behavior doesn't come only from personality. It emerges from the interaction between people and structure. I'm not saying structure explains everything. I'm saying inspect the game before you decide the player is the problem.
