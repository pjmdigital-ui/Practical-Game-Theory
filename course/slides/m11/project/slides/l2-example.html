Example · The customer-service department

The system may be rewarding the problem

The situation

Management says: "Our number-one priority is solving the customer's problem." Reps are judged mainly on average call time.

What happened

Complicated customers became expensive. People rushed and transferred calls. Then management asked: "Why won't anyone take ownership?"

The lesson

This isn't a communication problem. It's a game-design problem.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Imagine a customer-service department. Management says their number-one priority is solving the customer's problem. That's the speech. But representatives are evaluated primarily on average call time. That's the game. Employees may transfer calls or end conversations before problems are resolved. Then management asks why nobody takes ownership. Perhaps because you've rewarded getting customers off the phone. That's not a communication problem. It's a game-design problem. The natural response is another training session on ownership. But the training is a speech and the metric is the game. The game will win. Here's the takeaway.
