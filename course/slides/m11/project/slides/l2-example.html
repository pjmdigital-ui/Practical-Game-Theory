Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Imagine a customer-service department. Management says, "Our number-one priority is solving the customer's problem." But representatives are evaluated primarily on average call time. What happens? Shorter calls become valuable. A complicated customer is now strategically expensive. So employees may rush, transfer calls, avoid complicated issues, and end conversations before problems are fully resolved. Then management complains: "Why aren't our people taking ownership?" Perhaps because you've rewarded them for getting customers off the phone. That's not a communication problem. That's a game-design problem. And notice: another training session on ownership won't touch it, because the training is a speech and the call-time metric is the game.
