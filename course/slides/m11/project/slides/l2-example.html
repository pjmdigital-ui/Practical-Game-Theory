Example · The customer-service department

The system may be rewarding the problem

The situation

Management says: "Our number-one priority is solving the customer's problem." Reps are judged mainly on average call time.

What happened

Complicated customers became expensive. People rushed and transferred calls. Then management asked: "Why won't anyone take ownership?"

The lesson

This isn't a communication problem. It's a game-design problem.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Imagine a customer-service department. Management says their number-one priority is solving the customer's problem. That's the speech. But representatives are evaluated primarily on average call time. That's the game. What happens? Shorter calls become valuable. A complicated customer is now strategically expensive. Every extra minute with that customer costs the rep on the scoreboard. So employees may rush. They may transfer calls. They may avoid complicated issues. They may end conversations before problems are fully resolved. Then management complains. Why aren't our people taking ownership? Perhaps because you've rewarded them for getting customers off the phone. Look at how this connects to the key question. What exactly am I rewarding? Management intended to reward solved problems. What actually improved the metric was a short call. Those aren't the same thing. And here's the lesson on the third card. That's not a communication problem. That's a game-design problem. Here's the trap I want you to see. The natural response is another training session on ownership. Maybe a poster about customer care. But the training is a speech and the call-time metric is the game. The game will win. A design response asks different questions. Should call time be one measure among several? Should we track whether the problem came back? Who is allowed to keep a hard call open? So here's a question for you. Have you ever been on the customer side of this game? Bounced from rep to rep with nobody owning your problem? Now you know what they were probably being paid for. Here's the takeaway for this lesson.
