Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Sometimes you're the designer and the player. You keep telling yourself you need more discipline. Maybe. But inspect the game first. Look at the first card. Your phone is beside you. Notifications are on. Every distracting app opens instantly. And the difficult work takes six steps just to begin. Look at what that game rewards. Distraction costs nothing. Focus costs effort before you've even started. Then you blame your character when distraction wins. That's the same mistake we made with employees in Lesson One. We fixed the player before inspecting the game. You can spend your life trying to win a willpower contest. Or you can change the environment. Remove the app. Turn off notifications. Make the productive action easier. Make the distracting action harder. Schedule another person to work with you. Use a commitment device like we discussed in Module Eight. Now you're no longer relying entirely on making a better decision every five minutes. You've redesigned the choice environment. Notice the levers here. Friction is the big one. You made one choice easier and the other harder. Players is another. Adding a work partner changes the game. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. This isn't a trick for weak people. It's what strong people do. They don't waste willpower on fights they could design away. One more principle applies here. If you're designing for yourself, design for the behavior you're likely to have. Not the behavior you wish you had. So ask yourself this. What's one thing you keep promising yourself to do with more discipline? What would make it easier than the alternative? Let's pull these examples together into one principle.
