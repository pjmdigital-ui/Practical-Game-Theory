Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Let's practice. Dana manages a twelve-person software support team. A leaderboard ranks engineers by tickets closed per week. The top three each quarter get a bonus. They also get first choice of vacation weeks. Dana keeps saying that customer satisfaction and knowledge-sharing matter most. She's even run two mentoring workshops. Yet senior engineers rarely help juniors. Complex tickets sit untouched until someone is assigned them. Customers keep reopening tickets marked resolved within days. And here's a detail to notice. Reopened tickets are counted as new tickets. There's another one. Escalations to engineering need Dana's personal sign-off. She's often in meetings until late afternoon. Last month two engineers were caught splitting single customer issues into several tickets. Dana's boss has a suggestion. Fire them and hire people with better values. Here's how I'd like you to approach it. Don't start with the people. Start with the game. Read every detail in the scenario as a possible lever. Who gets paid for what? What counts and what doesn't? Who decides? Who moves when? Then work through the five questions on screen. Take your time with the last one. It's tempting to agree with the boss. Two engineers really did split tickets. But ask yourself what a brand-new engineer would do in the same game after a month. And notice the common mistake. Many people jump straight to a fix without stress-testing it.
