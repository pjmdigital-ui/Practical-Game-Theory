Friction is part of the game

Being able to make a choice hard doesn't mean you should.

One click to sign up, twenty to cancel. Technically they can leave. Strategically, you've made one choice costly.

Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Suppose you want customers to cancel a subscription honestly if they no longer want it. Then you make cancellation require eight screens, a phone call, a retention conversation, and a written request. Technically they can cancel. Strategically, you've made one choice costly. Or the reverse: signing up takes one click, cancelling takes twenty. The architecture is influencing behavior. And this is exactly where we need our ethical standard. Game design is powerful precisely because environments shape choices. So here's the line: the fact that you can make something strategically difficult does not mean you should. Friction isn't neutral. When you decide what's easy and what's hard, you're making a design choice, and you're responsible for it.
