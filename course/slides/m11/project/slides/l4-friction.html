Internomics · Module 11 — Change the Game

Here's a lever that deserves special care. Friction. How easy or hard each choice is made to be. Friction is part of the game. Suppose you say you want customers to cancel a subscription honestly if they no longer want it. Then you make cancellation require eight screens. A phone call. A retention conversation. And a written request. Technically they can cancel. Strategically you've made one choice costly. Or look at the version on the slide. Signing up takes one click. Cancelling takes twenty. The architecture is influencing behavior. And notice what the customer experiences. They're still free to leave in theory. In practice some may give up and keep paying for something they don't want. Nobody lied to them. The design did the work. This is exactly where we need our ethical standard. Game design is powerful precisely because environments shape choices. Everything you've learned in this module can be used to make good behavior easy. It can also be used to make a fair choice painful. So here's the line I want you to hold. The fact that you can make something strategically difficult does not mean you should. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People often think friction is neutral. They say the option is there and nobody's stopping anyone. But friction isn't neutral. When you decide what's easy and what's hard, you're making a design choice. And you're responsible for it. So think about something you've designed. A sign-up process. A refund policy. A rule at home. Is the easy path also the honest path? That question leads us straight into ethical design.
