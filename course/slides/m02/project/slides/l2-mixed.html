Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Let's spend a minute on mixed interests. This is where much of human life actually happens. Look at the four cards. We both want the relationship to succeed. But we disagree about how. We both want the company to grow. But we disagree about what to do with the profits. We both want the deal completed. But we disagree about price. We both want the family to be happy. But we have different ideas about what that requires. Notice the shape of every one of those. There's a shared goal on top. And there's a real disagreement underneath. Both parts are true at once. That's what makes mixed-motive games so fascinating. They create room for conflict and cooperation at the same time. And it's why the strategic thinker isn't constantly looking for a winner. They're looking for the structure of the interests. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to avoid. When we're in a disagreement, the disagreement is loud. The shared goal goes quiet. We start to feel like the other person is against us entirely. But usually they're not. They're against one part of our plan. So try this. Next time you're in a disagreement, finish this sentence out loud. We both want this. We disagree about that. You'll be surprised how often the first half is bigger than you thought.
