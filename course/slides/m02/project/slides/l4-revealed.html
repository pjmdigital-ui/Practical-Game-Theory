Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's a subtler lesson. People can tell you what they want. But their behavior gives you additional information. Picture this. Someone tells you, "Price is my only concern." You believe them. So you bring them the cheapest option. Then they reject it because it takes longer to deliver. Now you've learned something. Perhaps price wasn't actually their only concern. Speed mattered too. Maybe a lot. That's the difference between a stated preference and a revealed preference. A stated preference is what someone says they want. A revealed preference is what their choices show they value. Now here's the misunderstanding I really want you to avoid. This doesn't mean people are lying to you. Most of the time they aren't. People often don't fully understand their own preferences until they face an actual choice. The person who said price was everything probably believed it. Then a real tradeoff showed up and they learned something about themselves. But strategically, behavior gives us information about what matters. That's why game theory often looks at observed choices. It doesn't simply accept statements at face value. So listen to the words. And watch the choices. When they line up, you can be more confident. When they don't, get curious instead of suspicious. Here's a question for you. Is there someone whose words and choices don't match right now? What might the choices be telling you?
