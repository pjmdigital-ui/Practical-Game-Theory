Example · A business deal

Grow the pie, then divide it

The situation

You and a seller disagree over price.

What happened

The seller cares most about getting paid quickly. You care more about paying over time.

The lesson

Restructure the deal. The seller gets faster payment. You get better terms.

Total value can rise even while you still disagree about the split.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's the better question from a few slides ago in action. Imagine you're negotiating a business deal. You disagree over price. So far that sounds like the house. But as you talk, you discover something. The seller cares enormously about getting paid quickly. You care more about paying over time. Now something interesting happens. Those two preferences don't collide. They fit together. You may be able to restructure the deal. The seller gets faster payment. You get better terms. The total value of the arrangement goes up. And here's the part I really want you to notice. You still disagree about the final allocation. The price conflict didn't disappear. You'll still have to work that out. But now you're dividing something bigger. That's what it means to create value before dividing it. And how did you find it? Not by arguing harder about price. You found it by learning what the seller actually valued. That's a payoff question. You asked what made the outcome better or worse for them. If you'd stayed locked on the number, you would never have seen it. So here's a question for you. In a deal you're working on right now, what does the other side care about that you haven't asked about yet? Timing? Certainty? Something else? This is one of the reasons mixed-motive interactions are so interesting. And it leads to the big idea of this lesson.
