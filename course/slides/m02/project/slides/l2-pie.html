The strategic thinker isn't looking for a winner. They're looking for the structure of the interests.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's an important consequence of seeing mixed interests. If you assume every interaction is zero-sum, you start seeing everything as one question. Who gets what? There's a fixed pie. Every slice I take is a slice you lose. And if that's the game, the only skill that matters is grabbing. But sometimes there's a better question. Can we create more value before deciding how to divide it? That's the question on the right. It doesn't pretend the split won't matter. The split still matters. It just puts it second. First we look for ways to make the whole thing worth more to both of us. Then we talk about how to divide it. Why does this work? Because people value things differently. Remember the currencies from lesson one. If I care a lot about something you care little about, there's room to trade. I get more of what I want. You give up something that costs you little. Both of us end up better off. That's value creation. Fixed-pie thinking can't see it because it assumes we're counting in the same currency. Here's the question for you.
