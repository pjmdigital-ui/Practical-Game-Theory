Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Let's go back to the raise from Module Zero. You walk into your boss's office and ask for a raise. You want more money. That part seems obvious. But what does your boss want? Most of us answer right away. To save money. Maybe that's true. But look at the list on screen. Perhaps your boss also wants to retain good employees. Perhaps they need to stay within a budget. They may want to avoid setting an expensive precedent. If you get a raise, who else is going to ask? They may want to keep the rest of the team happy. They may want to demonstrate fairness. They may have a profit target to hit this year. And they may want to avoid explaining the raise to their own superior. Now look at the last one. It's the one we almost never consider. Your boss may actually want to give you the raise. They just don't believe they can afford it. Think about how that changes the room. If your boss is mainly trying to save money, you're in a fight. If your boss wants to keep you but is boxed in by a budget, you're on the same side of a hard problem. Same conversation. Completely different game. So here's the question for you. In your last tough conversation, did you stop at the first answer? Let's put this example together in one view.
