Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

I want you to recognize three broad patterns. First, opposed interests: what benefits me hurts you. A zero-sum game, where one player's gain is the other's loss, is the strong form. Chess is the obvious example. If I win, you lose. But pure zero-sum situations are far less common in everyday relationships than we imagine. Second, aligned interests: we both benefit from the same outcome. Two employees pushing a hard project over the line. Their main problem may not be conflict at all. It may be coordination: how do we get our actions lined up? Third, and most common, mixed interests. We both want the relationship to work, but disagree about how. We both want the company to grow, but disagree about the profits. So instead of asking "How do I beat them?", ask "Where are our interests actually aligned, and where are they opposed?" That question opens up possibilities.
