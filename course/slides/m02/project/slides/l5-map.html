Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Now you can add a second layer to the Game Map from Module One. The Game Map told you who's playing. The Payoff Map tells you what they're playing for. When you're heading into an important interaction, ask six questions. First, what do I want? What outcomes matter to me? List more than one currency. You'll be surprised how often you care about more than you realized. Second, what do they want? What outcomes appear to matter to them? Third, where do our interests align? What can we both gain? Fourth, where do they conflict? What does one of us gain at the other's expense? Fifth, what are we each trying to avoid? What losses or risks matter to them? And to me? Sixth, what might I be missing? Could status matter here? Could identity or fairness? Could autonomy or a relationship? Then there's the most important question on the whole map. Am I assuming their payoff is the same as mine? If the honest answer is yes, go back and look again. That's the trap this entire module is built around. Here's how I'd use it in practice. You don't need a worksheet every time. For a big conversation, write it out. For a small one, run the six questions in your head on the way in. It doesn't take long. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. The map isn't a script. It's a way of seeing. So what does seeing this way actually give you? Let's look.
