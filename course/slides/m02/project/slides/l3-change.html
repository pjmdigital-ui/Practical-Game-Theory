Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's where this model gets even more powerful. The payoff structure isn't necessarily fixed. Imagine you have two employees competing for a promotion. The company rewards individual performance. So their incentives are competitive. Every win for one is a step back for the other. Of course they're guarded with each other. Of course they don't share credit. Now management changes the compensation system. Both employees receive a bonus if the entire team hits its target. What happens? The people didn't necessarily change. Their personalities are the same. Their skills are the same. But the game changed. Their incentives changed. And so their behavior has room to change too. Now helping each other pays. That's a big idea. And it's a hopeful one. It means that when you see behavior you don't like, you have more than one lever. You can try to change the person. That's hard and often doesn't work. Or you can look at what the environment rewards. Later in the course we'll look much more closely at how changing the rules can change behavior. For now I just want you to notice one thing. Before trying to change someone's behavior, understand what the current environment rewards. Here's a question for you.
