Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here are the mistakes I see most often with this model. The first is the one this whole module is built around. Assuming they want what you want. It's so natural that you won't notice you're doing it. So build in a check. Before you interpret anyone's behavior, ask yourself if you're assuming their payoff is the same as yours. Second, calling someone irrational too fast. When we say that, it usually means they aren't maximizing what we assumed they cared about. Remember the lucrative job. So pause and ask what payoff would make the behavior make sense. Third, getting the structure wrong. This one cuts both ways. Some people treat every interaction as zero-sum. They see enemies everywhere. Other people insist there's always a win-win. They miss real conflict until it bites them. Most interactions are mixed. So identify conflict accurately. Don't invent it. And don't wish it away. Fourth, confusing understanding with excusing. Seeing why a behavior makes sense doesn't make it good. And there's a darker version of this mistake. Confusing understanding with exploiting. Knowing what someone values is not a hot button for you to push. If you catch yourself thinking that way, stop. That's tactics. It's not what this course is about. Here's a question for you. Which of these four do you fall into most? Be honest with yourself. There are three more mistakes worth naming.
