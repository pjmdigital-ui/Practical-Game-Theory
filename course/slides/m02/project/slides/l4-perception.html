Perception is part of the game

Same deal. Two different payoffs.

One person sees

"I'm getting $10,000."

The other sees

"I'm losing $5,000 compared with what I expected."

Decisions are based on perceived outcomes. Your perception of the game shapes how you play it. Theirs shapes how they play it.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

This connects to something we've talked about before. There's the objective situation. And then there's how each player perceives the situation. Those aren't always the same. Suppose two people are offered the exact same deal. One of them sees it this way. "I'm getting ten thousand dollars." The other sees it this way. "I'm losing five thousand dollars compared with what I expected." Same objective outcome. Different perceived payoff. And because decisions are based on perceived outcomes, their behavior can be completely different. The first person says yes and feels good about it. The second person might push back. They might feel cheated. They might even walk away. Now if you're the one making that offer, this is crucial. You might think you made a generous offer. And by your reference point you did. But the other person isn't using your reference point. They're using theirs. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to avoid. This doesn't mean reality doesn't matter. Reality still matters. Ten thousand dollars is ten thousand dollars. It means your perception of the game influences how you play it. And the other person's perception influences how they play it. So before you make an offer, ask yourself. What are they expecting? What will this feel like next to that? Next let's look at the gap between what people say and what they choose.
