Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

In everyday language we say, "That person isn't being rational." What we frequently mean is, "That person isn't maximizing the thing I assumed they cared about." Those aren't the same. Suppose you turn down a lucrative job because it would mean missing your children's lives. From a purely financial perspective, that looks irrational. But if your priorities include family, it makes perfect sense. The problem wasn't that you made an irrational decision. The observer misunderstood your payoff function. One boundary, though. Understanding someone's incentives doesn't mean their behavior is justified. People can value something unhealthy, or knowingly harm someone because they value something else more. Game theory helps explain why a behavior may make sense from someone's perspective. It doesn't tell us whether it's good, moral, or wise.
