Example · The lucrative job

"Irrational" to whom?

The situation

You're offered a lucrative job. Taking it would mean missing your children's lives.

What happened

You turn it down. From a purely financial view an observer calls it irrational.

The lesson

Your priorities include family. The observer misunderstood your payoff function.

"Not rational" often means "not maximizing the thing I assumed they cared about."

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

In everyday language we often say, "That person isn't being rational." We frequently mean they aren't maximizing the thing we assumed they cared about. Suppose you're offered a lucrative job. But taking it would require you to miss your children's lives. So you turn it down. From a purely financial perspective that looks irrational. But if your priorities include family, it makes perfect sense. The observer misunderstood your payoff function. Rational doesn't mean selfish. It means pursuing what you actually value. Who have you called irrational lately? Now an important boundary.
