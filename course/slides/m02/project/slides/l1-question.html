Whenever you're dealing with another person, ask this. What are they actually trying to get out of this? Now let me tell you what that question is not asking. It's not asking what you think they should want. That's your idea of what's reasonable for them. It's not asking what you would want if you were them. That's just your preferences wearing their clothes. And it's not even necessarily asking what they told you they want. People don't always say everything. Sometimes they don't fully know it themselves. Instead ask this. What outcome appears to matter to them? That distinction is fundamental. Here's why. Each of those three wrong versions puts you at the center. They all start from your view of the world. The right version starts from theirs. Picture a coworker who keeps pushing back on your plan. If you ask what they should want, you'll decide they should want the project to succeed. Then their pushback looks like sabotage. If you ask what appears to matter to them, you might notice something else. Maybe they're the one who has to clean up if it goes wrong. Now the pushback makes sense. So here's a question for you.
