Research spotlight · The Ultimatum Game

Fairness is part of the payoff

What it shows

Güth, Schmittberger and Schwarze (1982) is the classic start. Responders often reject offers they see as unfair. Even when that leaves them with nothing. In one meta-analysis proposers offered about 40% on average. About 16% of offers were rejected.

What it doesn't prove

Not that people always choose fairness over money. Not that money doesn't motivate. The weight on fairness varies across people and cultures. It varies with situations and stakes.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Our first research spotlight is the Ultimatum Game. It began with Güth, Schmittberger and Schwarze in 1982. A later meta-analysis pooled 37 papers with 75 experimental results. On average proposers offered about 40 percent. Responders rejected about 16 percent of offers. Slonim and Roth varied the stakes by a factor of 25 in Slovakia. Higher stakes had only a small effect. So the payoff isn't only "How much did I get?" It can also be "How was I treated?" But it doesn't prove people always choose fairness over money. Our second spotlight is about autonomy.
