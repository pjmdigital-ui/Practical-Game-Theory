Currencies: identity and craft, autonomy and time, respect. He may be avoiding losing the work he's good at.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's a model answer. Dana assumes Marcus values what she'd value: more money and a bigger title. But the evidence says otherwise. He hesitated, he described the role in terms of what he'd lose, and he was hurt by how the offer was handled. "Why is he being so difficult?" is a signal she may be misreading his payoff function, not that he's irrational. The likely currencies are identity and craft, because he sees himself as a designer; autonomy and time, because of all those meetings; and respect, because leadership heard first. The structure is mixed: both want him to stay and the work to be excellent, but Dana needs someone to carry management and Marcus may not want it. His turned-down offer is a revealed preference that backs up his stated one. So another five percent doubles down on the currency that isn't driving his decision, and could even read as "she thinks I can be bought." Better to ask than push. That may reveal something like a senior designer track with a raise and mentoring but no management load. Or it may show the interests really are incompatible, which is better to know than to pretend.
