Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's a model answer. Dana assumes Marcus values what she'd value. More money and a bigger title. But the evidence says otherwise. He hesitated. He described the role in terms of what he'd lose. And he was hurt by how the offer was handled. "Why is he being so difficult?" is a signal. It tells us she may be misreading his payoff function. It doesn't tell us he's irrational. So what currencies are in play? First, identity and craft. He sees himself as a designer. The role takes him away from the actual design work. Second, autonomy and time. More meetings means less control over his day. Third, respect. Leadership heard before he did. And he may be trying to avoid something. Losing the work he's good at. Or struggling in a management role he never asked for. Money matters. But it doesn't look like the binding payoff. The structure is mixed. They're aligned on wanting him to stay and the agency's creative work to be excellent. They're opposed on management. Dana needs someone to carry it. Marcus may not want it. His turned-down offer is a revealed preference. It backs up his stated one. It isn't proof. But it's meaningful. So another five percent doubles down on the currency that isn't driving his decision. It could even read as "she thinks I can be bought." Better to ask than push. That may reveal a senior designer track with a raise and mentoring but no management load. Or it may show the interests really are incompatible. That's better to know than to pretend.
