Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Now I need to give you a warning. I don't want you turning this into a "there's always a win-win" philosophy. Sometimes there isn't one. Two people can genuinely want opposite things. Look at the top row. A company may want to pay less. An employee may want to earn more. A buyer wants the lowest price. A seller wants the highest. Two political groups may want policies that can't both happen. Two competitors may both want the same customer. Those are real conflicts. The point of understanding payoffs isn't to pretend they don't exist. It's to identify them accurately. And here's why accuracy matters. There are two ways to get this wrong. Both are expensive. If you misdiagnose a conflict as cooperation, you'll make bad decisions. You'll trust when you shouldn't. You'll share information that gets used against you. You'll keep waiting for a win-win that isn't coming. And if you misdiagnose cooperation as pure conflict, you'll leave value on the table. You'll fight over things you could have both gained on. You'll burn a relationship you didn't need to burn. So the goal isn't optimism. The goal is accuracy. Here's a question for you. Which mistake are you more prone to? Most of us lean one way.
