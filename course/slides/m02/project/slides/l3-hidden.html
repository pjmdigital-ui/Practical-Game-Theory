Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Sometimes the payoff you can see isn't the payoff that actually drives the decision. Let me give you two pictures. First, imagine someone refuses a perfectly reasonable offer. You think, "They're being irrational." But suppose accepting that offer would make them look weak in front of their colleagues. Now the financial outcome isn't the whole payoff. There's also status. And once you see that, the refusal makes sense. It may not be wise. But it makes sense. Second, imagine someone refuses to apologize. You think, "They don't care about the relationship." Maybe they do care. But they believe apologizing means admitting they were entirely at fault. So their payoff isn't simply relationship versus no relationship. It's more complicated than that. It's relationship plus dignity plus self-image. And notice what that opens up. If the sticking point is "entirely at fault," maybe there's a way to repair things that doesn't require total surrender. You'd never find that if you'd decided they just don't care. Now I need to be careful here. The lesson is not that everyone has a secret hidden motive. That would be an unhealthy way to use this model. You'd start seeing conspiracies everywhere. The lesson is simply this. The visible payoff may not be the complete payoff. So how do you see the rest of it? That's where empathy comes in.
