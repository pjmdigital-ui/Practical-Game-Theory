Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

In game theory, a player's payoff is essentially what they value about an outcome. Economists often put numbers on payoffs, because the math needs some way to compare outcomes. But you don't need numbers to use the idea. A payoff is simply this: what makes this outcome better or worse for this player? And that leads to the most important question you can ask in any interaction: what are they actually trying to get out of this? Not what you think they should want. Not what you would want if you were them. And not even necessarily what they told you they want. What outcome appears to matter to them? That distinction is fundamental, because people don't make decisions based on what would make you happy. They make decisions based on what they value.
