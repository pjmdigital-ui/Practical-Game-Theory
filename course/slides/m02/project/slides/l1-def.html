Definition

A payoff is what makes this outcome better or worse for this player.

What they value

In game theory a payoff is what a player values about an outcome.

Their values

People decide based on what they value. Not on what would make you happy.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Let's start with the core idea. In game theory a player's payoff is essentially what they value about an outcome. That's it. Economists often put numbers on payoffs. They do that because the math needs some way to compare one outcome with another. But you don't need numbers to use this idea in your own life. So here's the working definition I want you to remember. A payoff is what makes this outcome better or worse for this player. Notice the two words "this player." A payoff always belongs to someone. The same outcome can be a great payoff for me and a terrible one for you. Here's why that matters so much. People don't make decisions based on what would make you happy. They make decisions based on what they value. That sounds obvious when I say it out loud. But watch how often we forget it. Think about a time you made someone a generous offer and they turned it down. Your first thought was probably that they were being unreasonable. But the offer was generous by your standards. It was measured in your currency. Maybe it didn't move the thing they actually cared about. So the definition sounds simple. Using it takes discipline.
