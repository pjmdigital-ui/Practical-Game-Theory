Money isn't always the real currency

Two partners. One pile of profit.

Partner A

Wants to reinvest 80% in the company.

Optimizing for: long-term growth

Partner B

Wants to distribute most of the profits.

Optimizing for: financial security

Neither is irrational. They're optimizing for different things.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's a picture of what I mean. Imagine two business partners arguing over how much money to reinvest in their company. Partner A wants to reinvest eighty percent. Partner B wants to distribute most of the profits. On the surface this looks like a fight about money. One wants to keep it in the business. The other wants to take it out. So you might try to solve it like a money problem. Split the difference. Reinvest half. Everybody walks away a little unhappy. But now suppose you look underneath. Partner A is motivated mainly by long-term growth. Partner B is motivated mainly by financial security. Now their disagreement makes a lot more sense. Neither of them is necessarily irrational. Neither is being greedy or reckless. They're optimizing for different things. And that changes the conversation completely. You're no longer haggling over a percentage. You're asking a better question. How do we grow without putting B's security at risk? That question has more possible answers than "split it down the middle." Here's the common misunderstanding to watch for. People think that naming the different payoffs makes the disagreement bigger. Usually it makes it clearer.
