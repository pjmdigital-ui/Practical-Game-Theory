Understanding isn't excusing

An understandable incentive

can still lead to a bad decision.

People can value something unhealthy

and pursue it consistently.

Short-term gratification

can come at enormous long-term cost.

Someone can knowingly harm another

because they value something else more.

Game theory explains why a behavior may make sense. It doesn't tell us whether it's good or moral or wise.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Understanding someone's incentives does not mean assuming their behavior is justified. Someone can have an understandable incentive and still make a bad decision. The behavior makes sense from their perspective. And it's still not okay. Game theory doesn't tell us whether it's good or moral or wise. You still need your own judgment for that. When people first learn to see payoffs, some start excusing everything. "Well, he lied because he was protecting his status." That explains the lie. It doesn't make the lie fine.
