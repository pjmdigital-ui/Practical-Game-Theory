Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Now here's an important boundary. Please don't skip this one. Understanding someone's incentives does not mean assuming their behavior is justified. Those are two different things. Look at the four cards. Someone can have a perfectly understandable incentive and still make a bad decision. Someone can value something unhealthy. Someone can pursue short-term gratification at enormous long-term cost. And someone can knowingly harm another person because they value something else more. In every one of those cases you can see the payoff clearly. The behavior makes sense from their perspective. And it's still not okay. So here's how I want you to hold it. Game theory helps us understand why a behavior may make sense from someone's perspective. It doesn't tell us whether that behavior is good. It doesn't tell us whether it's moral. And it doesn't tell us whether it's wise. You still need your own judgment for that. Here's why I'm making a point of it. When people first learn to see payoffs, some of them swing too far. They start excusing everything. "Well, he lied because he was protecting his status." Maybe so. That explains the lie. It doesn't make the lie fine. The opposite mistake is refusing to understand bad behavior at all. That leaves you blind. The goal is to see clearly and still judge wisely.
