Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

So instead of borrowing their shoes with your own preferences, reconstruct their game. Here are six questions that help. First, what do they value? Ask it in their currencies, not yours. Second, what are they afraid of losing? That's often where the real energy is. Third, what alternatives do they have? Someone with great options behaves very differently from someone with none. Fourth, what constraints are they facing? Think of the boss with the budget. Fifth, what outcome would make them feel they won? And sixth, what outcome would make them feel they lost? Notice those last two say "feel." We're not asking what a neutral judge would call a win. We're asking what it would feel like to them. Let me put this to work. Say a colleague keeps blocking a change you've proposed. Run the questions. Maybe they value stability. Maybe they're afraid of losing a process they built. Maybe they have no good alternative if your change fails. Maybe their manager is breathing down their neck. Suddenly the blocking makes sense. You may still think they're wrong. That's fine. You aren't necessarily agreeing with them. You're trying to understand the incentive structure they're operating within. And once you see it, you have better options than pushing harder. So here's a question for you. Which of these six do you usually skip? Next let's see how the structure of a game can push even decent people in a direction.
