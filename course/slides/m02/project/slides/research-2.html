Research spotlight · Falk and Kosfeld

Autonomy can be part of the payoff

What it shows

In Falk and Kosfeld's 2006 experiment principals could impose a minimum performance requirement. Most agents then performed worse. Many read the control as distrust and a limit on their autonomy.

What it doesn't prove

Not that oversight always backfires. The evidence is good but context-dependent. Treat findings like these as possibilities. Not universal laws.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

The second spotlight comes from Armin Falk and Michael Kosfeld in 2006. In their principal-agent game the principals could impose a minimum performance requirement on the agents. Instead of performing better under control, most agents reduced their performance. So a rule meant to secure performance can read as "I don't trust you." And once it reads that way, the payoff has changed. But it doesn't prove that oversight always backfires. The evidence is context-dependent. Treat these findings as possibilities.
