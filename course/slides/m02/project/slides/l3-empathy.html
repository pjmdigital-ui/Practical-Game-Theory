Where empathy and game theory meet

From

"How would I feel if I were them?"

To

"What does this look like from their position?"

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

This is where empathy and game theory meet. And I think it's one of the most interesting connections in the whole book. People often think empathy means imagining how I would feel if I were them. That's the left side of the screen. Strategic thinking asks for something slightly different. Try to understand what the situation looks like from their position. That's the right side. Those two sound almost identical. They aren't. Here's the difference. On the left you move yourself into their situation. But you bring your own preferences with you. You bring your values. You bring your fears and your priorities. And so your reading of their behavior can be completely wrong. You're still answering the question as you. On the right you try to leave your preferences at the door. You ask what they value. Not what you'd value standing where they stand. Think back to the person who wouldn't apologize. If you imagine yourself in their spot, you might think you'd apologize in a heartbeat. So you decide they must not care. But you're not them. For them an apology may cost dignity in a way it wouldn't cost you. The common misunderstanding is that this kind of empathy means agreeing. It doesn't. You can see someone's position clearly and still disagree with it. So how do you actually reconstruct their game? Here are the questions.
