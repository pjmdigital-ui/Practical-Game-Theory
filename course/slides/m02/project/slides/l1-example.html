Example · The raise you didn't get

Same conversation. Different game.

The situation

You ask for a raise. You think the game is simple: "I want more money. My boss doesn't want to give it to me."

What happened

Your boss has budget and team and precedent to weigh. They may even want to give you the raise.

The lesson

The real game: "We both want me to stay. We have different constraints on how pay is structured."

A completely different problem needs a completely different way of thinking.

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Let's put the raise example together. Here's the situation. You walk in thinking the game is simple. I want more money. My boss doesn't want to give it to me. That framing makes your boss an opponent. It makes the conversation a tug of war. And it pushes you toward tug of war tactics. You argue harder. You bring up what you're worth. Maybe you hint that you'll leave. Now here's what's actually happening on the other side of the desk. Your boss is weighing a budget. They're thinking about the rest of the team. They're thinking about precedent. They're thinking about how they'll explain this upstairs. And they may actually want to give you the raise. They just don't believe they can afford it. So what's the lesson? The actual game might be this. We both want me to stay. But we have different constraints and different preferences about how compensation is structured. That's a completely different problem. And it calls for a completely different way of thinking. In the first game you're trying to beat your boss. In the second game you're trying to solve a shared problem. You might talk about timing. You might talk about what would make the raise easier to justify. Notice that you didn't change your goal. You still want more money. You changed your model of what the other person wants. Here's the big idea of this lesson in one line.
