Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

One of the easiest mistakes is looking only at what someone gains right now. Here are three pictures from the book. Imagine someone refuses a small financial benefit. Why? Because accepting it would damage their reputation. In the moment they look like they're leaving money on the table. Over time they're protecting something worth far more to them. Or imagine someone takes a short-term loss to preserve a long-term relationship. Today they come out behind. But they're playing a longer game than the one you're watching. Or imagine someone agrees to an uncomfortable conversation. Nobody enjoys those. They do it because they believe it will improve the relationship later. In every case the immediate payoff isn't the whole payoff. If you only score the current moment, you'll misread all three of these people. You might even call them irrational. Sound familiar? It's the same mistake we keep running into. We judge the choice by a payoff we picked. Not the one they're actually playing for. This idea becomes central later in the course when we get to repeated interactions. When people expect to deal with each other again, the future starts to shape the present. For now just remember this. Don't evaluate someone's choice only by what they gain or lose in the current moment. Here's a question for you. Whose choice have you been scoring too narrowly?
