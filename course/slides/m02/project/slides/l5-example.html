Example · The curfew

"Be home by midnight." "That's ridiculous."

The situation

A parent sets a curfew. The teenager pushes back hard.

What happened

The parent may be optimizing for safety. The teen may be optimizing for independence.

The lesson

Framed as parent versus teen it's a standoff. Seen as safety versus independence both positions make sense.

Before "How do I convince them?" ask "What payoff are they protecting?"

Internomics · Module 2 — See the Payoffs

Here's an example almost every family has lived. A parent says, "You need to be home by midnight." The teenager says, "That's ridiculous." And off they go. Now if you frame this as parent versus teenager, you get a standoff. Each side digs in. The parent thinks the kid is reckless. The kid thinks the parent is controlling. And you miss the underlying structure completely. So let's ask the payoff question. What is the parent optimizing for? Maybe safety. What is the teenager optimizing for? Maybe independence. Once you see that, something shifts. You can begin to see why both positions exist. Neither one is crazy. The parent isn't trying to ruin the kid's life. The kid isn't trying to give the parent a heart attack. They're each protecting something real. And that opens up a different conversation. Not "midnight or one o'clock." Instead it's how do we protect safety and still give you more independence. Maybe that leads somewhere. Maybe it doesn't. But it's a much better conversation than a shouting match. So the next time someone disagrees with you, don't immediately ask how to convince them. First ask what payoff they're protecting. Maybe it's money. Maybe it's time or status or control. Maybe it's security or identity or fairness. Once you see it, the disagreement may turn out to be smaller than it looked. Or it may not. Either way you've learned something important.
