Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

There's a trap here that all of us fall into. You've heard people say it. You've probably said it yourself. "If I were them, I'd never do that." It sounds like perspective-taking. It sounds like you're stepping into their shoes. But look closely at what that sentence actually does. It puts your preferences into their position. You keep your own values and your own payoffs. You just imagine yourself standing where they stand. So the prediction you get is really a prediction about you. It's not about them. Remember Module Two. Their payoffs may be completely different from yours. What feels costly to you may be cheap for them. What feels obvious to you may never cross their mind. So you don't want to put your preferences into their position. You want to put their preferences into their position. That's the second card. What do they want? What do they know? What can they actually do? Then ask what would make sense for them. That question is much more powerful. And this is where game theory and ordinary empathy begin to overlap. Empathy at its best isn't imagining how you would feel. It's understanding how they see the game. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Some people hear this and think it means agreeing with the other person. It doesn't. You can understand their position completely and still disagree with it. You just predict them better.
