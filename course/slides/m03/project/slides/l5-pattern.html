Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

We're not going to fully explain equilibrium until Module Five. But you can already see where we're heading. Suppose my behavior causes your response. And your response causes my next behavior. Round and round. Eventually we may reach a pattern where neither of us has much reason to change what we're doing given what the other is doing. That's an equilibrium. It's the loop settling down. Now here's the part people find surprising. Look at the four cards. Some equilibria are terrible. Some are excellent. Some are stable but inefficient. Everyone could do better but nobody wants to move first. And some are very difficult to escape. Think about a couple who've fallen into a pattern. One nags. The other withdraws. The withdrawing invites more nagging. The nagging invites more withdrawing. Neither of them likes it. But given what the other one is doing, each response makes a kind of sense. So it holds. That's what a bad equilibrium feels like from the inside. It feels stuck. This is why asking "What's the best thing for me to do?" isn't enough. That question looks at one move. You also need to ask what pattern your action will create. A move that wins today can lock in a pattern that costs you for years. A move that costs a little today can shift the whole loop. Here's a question for you. What pattern in your life is stable but bad? What keeps each person in it? Next let's talk about the limits of all this prediction.
