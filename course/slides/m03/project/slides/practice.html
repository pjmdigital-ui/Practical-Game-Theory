Dana's "no surprises" rule

The rule

Dana leads a five-person operations team. At her first meeting she says: "If something goes wrong, tell me right away. No surprises."

The reaction

Marcus admits a booking error. Dana is under pressure from her boss. She raises her voice. Later she names his mistake in the team meeting.

A month later

A bigger error surfaces only when the client calls. Two people knew for a week. Dana: "I specifically told them to come to me."

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Let's practice with Exercise A. Dana leads a five-person operations team. At her first meeting she says: "If something goes wrong, tell me right away. No surprises." Two weeks later Marcus admits he booked a shipment to the wrong warehouse. Dana was under pressure from her own boss. She called it "exactly the kind of careless mistake I can't afford." She later cited the error by name in the team meeting. A month later a much larger error surfaced only when the client called. Two team members had known for a week. Dana told her boss: "I specifically told them to come to me." What did telling Dana look like from where they sat? Let's look at the questions.
