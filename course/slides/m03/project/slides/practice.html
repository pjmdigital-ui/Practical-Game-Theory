Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Let's practice. This is Exercise A in your workbook. Dana leads a five-person operations team at a logistics company. At her first team meeting she says: "If something goes wrong, tell me right away. No surprises." That's the first card. It's a good rule. Most of us would want our own team to work that way. Two weeks later Marcus comes to her. He's a junior coordinator. He admits he booked a shipment to the wrong warehouse. That delayed a client order by two days. Now look at the second card. Dana was under pressure from her own boss that week. She raised her voice. She called it "exactly the kind of careless mistake I can't afford." And later she cited the error by name in the weekly team meeting. Marcus fixed the booking and apologized. Dana felt she had set a clear standard. Now the third card. A month later a much larger error surfaced. It surfaced only when the client called to complain. Two team members had known about it for a week. And Dana told her boss: "I don't understand it. I specifically told them to come to me." I want you to sit with that last line for a second. Because I think most of us have said something like it. We set a rule. People didn't follow it. And we're genuinely confused. Before you look at the questions, try one thing. Put yourself in the shoes of the two people who knew. What did telling Dana look like from where they sat? Let's look at the questions.
