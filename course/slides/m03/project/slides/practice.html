Dana's "no surprises" rule

Dana told her new team, "If something goes wrong, tell me right away." When Marcus admitted a booking error, Dana, under pressure from her own boss, raised her voice and later named the mistake in the team meeting. A month later, a bigger error surfaced only when the client called. Two people had known for a week.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Let's practice. Dana leads a five-person operations team. At her first meeting she said, "If something goes wrong, tell me right away. No surprises." Two weeks later, Marcus, a junior coordinator, admitted he'd booked a shipment to the wrong warehouse, delaying a client order by two days. Dana was under pressure from her own boss that week. She raised her voice, called it exactly the kind of careless mistake she couldn't afford, and later cited the error, by name, in the weekly team meeting. Marcus fixed it and apologized, and Dana felt she'd set a clear standard. A month later, a much larger error surfaced only when the client called to complain. Two team members had known about it for a week. Dana told her boss, "I don't understand it. I specifically told them to come to me."
