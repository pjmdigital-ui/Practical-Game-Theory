Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

There's another level to all of this. Once you predict their response, ask a second question. How will I respond to that? And then a third. How will they respond to my response? This is where interactions become recursive. You're not simply choosing an action. You're choosing an action while anticipating the other person's anticipation of your action. I know that sounds complicated. But you already do it. Imagine you're negotiating over price. You think: "If I offer ninety dollars, they'll probably counter at a hundred." Then you think: "They probably expect me to counter again." So you might start at eighty-five instead. But then you think: "They know I'm going to start low." And now you're thinking several moves ahead. That's strategic reasoning. You did it without a single equation. You've probably done some version of it the last time you haggled over anything. The point isn't to make your head spin. The point is to notice that the other person is doing the same thing. They're not a vending machine that spits out a response. They're a player who is thinking about you. So when you plan a move, it helps to ask what they expect you to do. Sometimes the strongest move is the one that fits their expectations.
