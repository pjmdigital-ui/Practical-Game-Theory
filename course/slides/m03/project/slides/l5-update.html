Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

So how do you hold a prediction without getting stuck on it? This is where the meta-principle of this whole course comes in. Model. Act. Observe. Update. Look at the four boxes. You make your best prediction. That's the model. You make your move. That's the act. They respond. And you compare their actual behavior with what you predicted. That's the observe step. Then comes the step most people skip. Update. If you were wrong, don't immediately conclude that they're irrational. That's the lazy conclusion. It protects your model and teaches you nothing. Instead ask: what did I misunderstand about the game? Look at the four possibilities at the bottom. Maybe their payoff was different from what you assumed. Maybe they had information you didn't know about. Maybe you misunderstood the options they had. And sometimes maybe they were simply unpredictable. That happens too. But check the first three before you settle on the fourth. Here's the shift I want you to make. The response is data. A surprise isn't a failure. It's information about the game that you didn't have before. People who get good at this aren't the ones who predict perfectly. They're the ones who update fastest when they're wrong. Here's a question for you. Think of someone who surprised you recently. Which of these four was it? And what does that tell you about your next move with them?
