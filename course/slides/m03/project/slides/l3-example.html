The lesson

Look for explanations in the game before you assign a character judgment.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's what this looks like in practice. This is where perspective-taking becomes strategic. Suppose a customer refuses your offer. You put real work into it. And they say no. The easy thought is: "They're being unreasonable." It's a very natural thought. Instead try a different question. What response would make sense if I had their objectives and their constraints? Now look at the list. Maybe they don't trust the delivery date. Maybe they have another supplier. Maybe they're worried about looking foolish if this goes wrong. Maybe their boss has to approve the purchase. Maybe they don't understand the value yet. Or maybe they're testing how flexible you are. You don't know which one is true. That's fine. You're not supposed to know yet. What matters is where you're looking. You're looking for explanations in terms of the game. You're not immediately assigning a character judgment. And notice something useful. Each of those explanations points to a different next move. If they don't trust the delivery date, you address the date. If their boss has to approve it, you help them make the case to their boss. If they're testing your flexibility, you think hard about what giving ground would teach them. "They're being unreasonable" points to no next move at all. It just ends the thinking. So here's the habit. When someone says no, write down three reasons it might make sense for them. Then pick your next move. And that gives us the takeaway for this lesson.
