Research spotlight · 2 of 2

People differ in how many steps ahead they think

What it shows

Stahl and Wilson found wide variety in reasoning depth. A cognitive hierarchy model fit an average of about 1.5 steps. In Nagel's beauty contest people rarely jumped straight to equilibrium.

What it doesn't prove

These figures come from specific games and participant pools. They aren't facts about anyone in your life. Backward reasoning is only as good as your model of the other player.

What it suggests: think one or two responses ahead. Calibrate to the actual person.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

The second spotlight is about depth. A level-one thinker asks "What will they probably do?" A level-two thinker asks "What do they think I will do?" Dale Stahl and Paul Wilson classified roughly a quarter of participants as level-one and about half as level-two. Colin Camerer and Teck-Hua Ho and Juin-Kuan Chong fit an average depth of roughly one and a half steps. So the rule isn't "think ten moves ahead." It's one or two responses ahead. And in centipede-game experiments people kept playing when strict backward induction said stop. Your backward reasoning is only as good as your model of the other player. Now let's build a tool.
