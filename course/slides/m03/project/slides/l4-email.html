Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Let's say someone sends you an angry email. You know the feeling. Your face gets hot. Your first impulse is to reply immediately. You start typing before you've finished reading. But imagine that instead you pause and ask one question. If I respond angrily, what will they probably do? Maybe they'll escalate. You've probably seen that happen before. So then ask the next question. If they escalate, what happens next? Maybe now both of you are defending yourselves. You're arguing about tone. You're arguing about who started it. And nobody is solving the original problem anymore. That problem is still sitting there underneath the whole mess. Look at the chain on the screen. Four steps. And you only chose one of them. But that one choice made the next three much more likely. Here's the point. You haven't just sent an angry email. You've potentially created a sequence. This is the essence of sequential reasoning. Each move sets up the next one. A common mistake is to judge your reply on its own. Was it fair? Was it accurate? Did they deserve it? Those questions all look at one move in isolation. The better question is where the chain is going. Think of the last angry message you answered fast. Where did the sequence end up? Formal game theory has a powerful tool for exactly this kind of situation. It's called backward induction. Let's look at it.
