People aren't perfectly rational

Separate what someone would choose if optimizing from what they're likely to choose.

We get emotional, miscalculate, work with incomplete information and misread the game.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

There's an important qualification. Game theory often begins with models where players choose whatever best serves their preferences. Real human beings don't always do that. We make mistakes. We misunderstand. We get emotional. We have incomplete information. We miscalculate. Sometimes we act against our own interests, and sometimes we simply don't understand the game very well. That doesn't make game theory useless. It means you need to hold two things apart: what someone would choose if they were optimizing based on their information, and what they're actually likely to choose. Those aren't always the same. Understanding that difference is one of the reasons experimental game theory exists.
