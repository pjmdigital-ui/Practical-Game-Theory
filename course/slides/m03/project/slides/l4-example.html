Decide the last move first

1 · The end

If they reject, what will I do with my one final response?

→

2 · Their move

Knowing that, will they accept or reject?

→

3 · Now

So what should my opening offer be?

The game runs forward: offer, accept or reject, one final response. You reason through it backward.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's the example from the chapter. You're negotiating. You make an offer. They can accept or reject it. If they reject, you get one final opportunity to respond. Most of us think only, "What should I offer?" Backward induction asks you to start at the other end. If they reject, what will I do with that final response? Once you know that, ask: what will they do, knowing what I'll do then? And only once you know that do you ask: so what should I offer now? The game runs forward, but you reason through it backward. You don't need to do this for every small decision. But when the stakes are high, deciding your last move first changes your first move.
