Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Your tool for this module is the Response Map. It has two parts. Part one is a rewind. Here's how it works. Pick a recent interaction that didn't go the way you wanted. It might be an argument at home. It might be a meeting that soured. It might be a message thread that got tense. Now here's the important instruction. Don't analyze whether you were right or wrong yet. That's the instinct. Resist it for now. Instead just write down the sequence. Look at the four boxes. What did I do? What did they do? What did I do next? What did they do next? Keep going if it ran longer. Once it's on paper, ask two questions. At which point did the interaction change direction? There's almost always one moment where it turned. And then ask what you could have predicted about their response before you made your move. This is one of the simplest ways to begin developing strategic awareness. You're training yourself to stop seeing an interaction as a series of isolated statements. You're learning to see it as a sequence of interdependent decisions. The common mistake here is skipping straight to blame. The moment you start asking who was right, you stop seeing the sequence. Blame can wait. The sequence is where the learning is. Here's a suggestion. Do this rewind tonight on one interaction from this week. It won't take long.
