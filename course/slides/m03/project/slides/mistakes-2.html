Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here are the other four mistakes. The fifth is overthinking. You don't need the 47-step tree. Most conversations don't deserve it. Focus on the responses that could materially change the outcome. Let the rest go. The sixth is overpredicting. This is the one that catches smart people. Your model of the other person is a hypothesis. When they surprise you, the lazy move is to decide they're irrational. The better move is to ask what you misunderstood about the game. Their payoff. Their information. Their options. The seventh is forgetting that they're predicting you too. If you always give in to pressure, people learn that pressure works. Your predictable response becomes part of their strategy. That's the doorway into the next module. And the eighth is mistaking influence for control. This one matters ethically as well as strategically. Everything in this module helps you shape the environment other people respond to. That's influence. But other people still choose their own responses. Remember Dana. Her goal isn't to control her team. It's to build an honest environment where reporting problems is a reasonable choice for them. That's the right way to hold these tools. Here's a question for you. Where in your life are you trying to control a response instead of shaping the game around it? Now let's take all of this into the real world with your field assignment.
