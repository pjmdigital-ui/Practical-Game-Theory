Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Let's talk about the mistakes I see all the time with this model. There are eight of them. Here are the first four. The first is judging a move by your intention. "I was only being honest" may be completely true. But strategy asks a different question. What does your move make the other person want to do? Your intention is real. It just isn't what they respond to. The second is projecting your preferences. "If I were them, I'd never do that" uses your payoffs. Not theirs. The fix is to use their goals and their information and their options and their constraints. Put their preferences into their position. The third is assuming perfect rationality. This one is the opposite trap. People get emotional. They miscalculate. They misread the game. So separate what would be optimal for them from what they're actually likely to do. Both matter. The fourth is judging threats by how forcefully they're made. A loud threat isn't the same as a credible one. Check whether carrying it out would really be their best response when the moment arrives. And run the same check on your own threats. Here's a question for you. Which of these four did you recognize in yourself? Be honest. Most of us have a favorite. Let's look at the other four.
