Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's a simple example you've definitely lived through. I love it because it's so small. Two people are walking toward each other in a hallway. You move slightly to your right to get out of the way. At the same moment they move slightly to their right. Now you're blocking each other again. So you both move again. And again. Now you're awkwardly bouncing from side to side. Maybe you both laugh. Maybe you both apologize. What happened there? Neither of you wanted that outcome. Nobody walked into the hallway planning to do a little dance. Both of you were just responding to the other person's movement. And each response made sense on its own. You saw them in your path and you moved. That's reasonable. They did the same. Also reasonable. This is a tiny example of strategic interaction. Look at the third card. Your action changed their best response. Their response changed yours. And suddenly a simple interaction became a feedback loop. That's the game. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to avoid. When a loop goes badly, we look for the person who caused it. In the hallway there isn't one. The outcome came from the sequence. Not from anyone's intention. And notice how it usually ends. Someone stops and lets the other person choose. One different response breaks the loop. Keep that in mind. Now let's scale this up to something that isn't funny at all.
