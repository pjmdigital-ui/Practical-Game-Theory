Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Many human interactions are essentially loops. Look at the four boxes. I act. You respond. I interpret your response. I act again. And then it flips around. You interpret my action. You respond again. Round and round it goes. Now I want you to notice one word in that loop. Interpret. It's the most important word on this slide. You're not responding only to what the other person objectively did. You're responding to what you believe their action means. Think about a short reply to a text message. One word. "Fine." The objective action is tiny. But what you believe it means can be huge. Maybe they're busy. Maybe they're annoyed. Maybe they're hurt. Your next move depends almost entirely on which one you believe. And the other person will then interpret your next move in the same way. So the loop isn't just actions bouncing back and forth. It's interpretations bouncing back and forth. That's why two people can describe the same argument in completely different ways. Each one is responding to a different reading of the other. We'll dig into this properly in Module Six on information and beliefs. For now we just need to recognize the loop. Here's the common misunderstanding. People think they're reacting to the facts. Usually they're reacting to their story about the facts. So here's a question for you. In your last tense exchange, what did you assume the other person's move meant?
