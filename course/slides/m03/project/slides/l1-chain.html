From a straight line to a chain

From

I say it → they hear it → outcome

To

I act → they respond → I respond to their response

The question we ask

"What do I want to do?"

The question we skip

"What will this cause the other person to do?"

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Most of us picture communication as a straight line. I say something. They hear it. And there's an outcome. That picture feels natural. It's also wrong in a way that costs us. Real interaction looks more like a chain. I act. They respond. I respond to their response. They respond to mine. And sometimes it keeps going for a long time. Here's why that matters. If the outcome depends on the whole chain, your decision can't rest on one question alone. "What do I want to do?" is the question we all ask naturally. The second question is the one we skip. "What will this cause the other person to do?" Think about a time you pushed back on an idea in a meeting. In the straight-line picture you made your point and that was that. In the chain picture your pushback became the other person's situation. They had to decide what to do with it. Maybe they dug in. Maybe they went quiet. Either way their move then shaped your next move. This sounds obvious when I say it out loud. But it's remarkably easy to forget. And it's easiest to forget at the exact moments it matters most. When you're nervous. When you're angry. When you're in a hurry. So here's a question for you. Think of one conversation from this past week. Where did you treat it like a straight line?
