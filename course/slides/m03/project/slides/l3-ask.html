Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

So how do you predict a response well? It starts before you act. You pause. But the pause only helps if you ask the right question. Look at the three grey cards. These are the questions most of us reach for. "What should they do?" That's a question about your standards. Not their behavior. "What would a reasonable person do?" That's a question about an imaginary person. Not the one in front of you. And "What would I do?" That's a question about you. All three feel like predictions. None of them really is. Here's the question I want you to ask instead. Given their goals and their information and their options and their constraints, what is the most likely response? Look at those four boxes. Their goals are what they're actually trying to achieve. Their information is what they know and what they don't. Their options are the moves really open to them. Their constraints are the things that box them in. A boss to answer to. Money. Time. Reputation. Emotions. When you fill in all four, the prediction gets much sharper. That's a much harder question. It takes more effort than the easy ones. But it's much more useful. Here's a quick exercise. Think of someone whose behavior has been frustrating you. Have you been asking what they should do? Or what's most likely given their four boxes? Next let's look at the trap that hides inside the easy questions.
