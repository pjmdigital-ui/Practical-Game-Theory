Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's the problem this module solves. We think about what we're going to say. We think about what we're going to do. We think about what we want to happen. But if another person is involved, the outcome isn't decided by our choice alone. They get a move too. And their move depends at least partly on ours. When we forget that, we start judging our moves by how they feel to make. You've heard this line. Maybe you've said it. "I was just being honest." Maybe you were. I'm not doubting that. But strategy asks a different question. What did your move cause the other person to do? That's the confusion at the center of so many conversations that go wrong. We confuse intention with incentive. We judge the move by what we meant. The other person responds to what the move made attractive for them. Think about the last time a conversation went sideways and you felt you'd done nothing wrong. You may have been right about your intention. The better question is what your move made them want to do next. So here's the shift I want you to practice. Before an important move, don't stop at "What should I say?" Ask what it's likely to make them do. Ask what you'll do then. And when the stakes are high, ask where the whole sequence leads. It's simple to say. It's hard to practice. Because once another person is involved, your move becomes their situation. Let's look at what you'll be able to do by the end of this module.
