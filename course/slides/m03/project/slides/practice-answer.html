Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's how I'd read it. Dana intended to set a high standard. But her response raised the cost of disclosure: anger plus public embarrassment, in front of everyone. She didn't want concealment, but her move made it rational. The sequence: she says tell me right away, Marcus discloses, she explodes and names him publicly, the team learns disclosure is punished, two people sit on the next error, and the client finds out first. The turning point was her response to the disclosure. That's why "I specifically told them" is beside the point. They heard her perfectly. They responded to the game her reaction rewrote. Reasoning backward: early reporting requires an expectation that disclosure will be heard and worked through, and that expectation comes from her observable responses. So she could tell the team plainly that her reaction made it harder to bring problems forward, then respond differently next time: thank the person, focus on the fix, handle performance privately. Expect skepticism, or a small test disclosure. How she handles that test is what changes the game.
