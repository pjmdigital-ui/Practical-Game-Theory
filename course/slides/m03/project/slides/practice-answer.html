Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's how I'd read it. Start with intention versus incentive. Dana intended to set a high standard. But her response raised the cost of disclosure. Anger plus public embarrassment. Witnessed by everyone. She didn't want concealment. But her move made it rational. Now the sequence. Dana says tell me right away. Marcus discloses. Dana explodes and names him publicly. The team learns that disclosure gets punished. The next error comes along and two people sit on it for a week. The client finds out first. So where did it turn? At Dana's response to the disclosure. That's the moment her stated rule and her actual behavior pointed in opposite directions. That's why "I specifically told them" is beside the point. They heard her perfectly. They responded to the game. And her own reaction rewrote that game. Today's response became tomorrow's incentive. Now reason backward. The behavior she wants is early reporting. That requires an expectation that disclosure will be heard and worked through. Not punished. Where does that expectation come from? From Dana's observable responses. Especially the most recent and most public one. So the question isn't "What phrase will make them open up?" It's "What does my response pattern teach them to expect?" Her next move could be to tell the team plainly that her reaction made it harder to bring problems forward. Then respond differently next time. Thank the person. Focus on the fix. Handle performance concerns privately. She should expect skepticism. Or a small test disclosure. That test is the critical branch. How she handles it is what changes the game. And if reporting stays slow, she updates and asks what else makes silence attractive.
