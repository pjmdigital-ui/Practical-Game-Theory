A shortcut · Rationalizability

Don't waste energy predicting moves that make no strategic sense.

If Choice A is worse for you than Choice B no matter what they do, eliminate A first. No prediction needed.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's a shortcut that saves a lot of energy. Before you try to predict exactly what someone will do, you can sometimes eliminate actions that simply don't make sense. Game theory calls this iterated elimination of dominated strategies, or more broadly, rationalizability. You don't need the math. Here's the practical translation: don't waste energy predicting moves that make no strategic sense. Suppose you have two choices, and Choice A gives you a worse outcome than Choice B no matter what the other person does. Then you don't need to predict them at all. Cross off A. So before asking "What are they going to do?", sometimes ask, "Is there anything I already know I shouldn't do, regardless of what they do?" If yes, eliminate it, and then continue.
