Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's a shortcut that saves a lot of energy. Before you try to predict exactly what someone will do, you can sometimes eliminate actions that simply don't make sense. Game theory calls this iterated elimination of dominated strategies. More broadly it's called rationalizability. MIT treats these as foundational ideas alongside Nash equilibrium. You don't need the formal mathematics. Here's the practical translation. Don't waste energy predicting moves that make no strategic sense. Look at the first card. Suppose you have two choices. Choice A gives you a worse outcome than Choice B no matter what the other person does. If they agree, A is worse. If they refuse, A is still worse. Whatever they do, A loses. Then you don't need to predict the other person at all. Look at the second card. Cross off A. That's a powerful mental shortcut. Most of us skip it. We jump straight to guessing what the other person will do. We burn a lot of energy on that guessing. Sometimes the guessing was never needed. So before asking "What are they going to do?" try a different question first. Is there anything I already know I shouldn't do regardless of what they do? If the answer is yes, eliminate it. Then continue with what's left. The same logic works for them too. If a move would leave them worse off whatever you do, you can usually stop worrying about it.
