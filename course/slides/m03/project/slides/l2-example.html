Example · The sales team

"I want you to collaborate more."

The situation

A sales manager asks the team to collaborate. The pay plan rewards each person for outperforming colleagues.

What happens

Each salesperson's best response may be to protect their own leads. They heard the message perfectly.

The lesson

Their incentives make another response more attractive. Saying it louder won't fix that.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Here's where this gets very practical. Suppose you manage a sales team. At the team meeting you tell everyone: "I want you to collaborate more." You mean it. You sincerely want them sharing leads and helping each other close. But there's a catch. You've structured compensation so that each salesperson gets rewarded individually for outperforming their colleagues. Now put yourself in one salesperson's shoes. Their payoff from the pay plan is clear. Beat the people next to you. So what's their best response? It may be to protect their own leads. Share a lead and you might help a colleague beat you this quarter. Here's the key point. The problem isn't that your team didn't hear you. They may have heard you perfectly. They understood every word. The problem is that their incentives make another response more attractive. And this is where managers often make it worse. They assume the message didn't land. So they say it again. Then they say it louder. Maybe they put it on a poster. None of that touches the pay plan. The words keep pointing one way while the game keeps pointing the other way. Have you ever been on the receiving end of this? A boss who said one thing while the system rewarded something else? You probably followed the system. Most of us do. That's not a character flaw. That's a best response. People don't respond only to your words. They respond to the game those words are entering. And that's our takeaway.
