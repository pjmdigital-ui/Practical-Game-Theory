The gap

You can intend a response without making it rational for them.

What we say after

"I was only being honest." "I just wanted to set a standard." Both may be true.

What they respond to

Not your intention. The situation your move puts them in.

The real test

Did your move make the response you wanted more attractive than their alternatives?

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

This is the difference between intention and incentive. Intention is what you hope your move will produce. Incentive is what your move actually makes attractive for the other person. And here's the uncomfortable part. You can sincerely intend a particular response without creating the conditions that make that response rational for them. When the response doesn't come, it's tempting to defend the move by its intention. "I was only being honest." "I just wanted to set a standard." Look at the first card. Those statements may be completely true. I'm not saying you were lying to yourself. But look at the second card. The other person doesn't respond to your intention. They can't see inside your head. They respond to the situation your move puts them in. That's what they actually have to work with. So the test isn't what you meant. Look at the third card. The test is whether your move made the response you wanted more attractive than the other things they could do. Here's where people go wrong. They treat a good intention as a kind of defense. If I meant well, the bad response must be their fault. Sometimes it is. But often the honest answer is that we built a situation where the response we got was the sensible one for them.
