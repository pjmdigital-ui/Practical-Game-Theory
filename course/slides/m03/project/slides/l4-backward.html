Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Backward induction sounds complicated. I promise you the underlying idea is simple. Instead of only asking what happens next, ask where the sequence eventually leads. Most of us think forward one step at a time. I'll say this. Then they'll say that. Then we'll see. Backward induction flips the direction. In a finite sequence you can start with the final decision and reason backward to the beginning. Look at the three cards. First you start at the end. What's the last decision anyone will make in this sequence? What will they choose there? Second you work back. Once you know what happens at the end, the step before it becomes easier to predict. People make choices based on what they expect to come after. Third you arrive at now. You keep stepping back until you reach the move you're about to make right now. And now you're choosing it with the whole sequence in view. MIT's game-theory courses explicitly teach backward induction as a core method for solving sequential games. You don't need the formal version for daily life. You need the direction of thought. Go back to the angry email. Where does that sequence end if you reply in kind? Probably somewhere neither of you wants to be. Once you see the end, the first move looks very different. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Backward induction doesn't mean predicting everything perfectly. It means letting the likely ending inform the opening. Next let's see why the ending can change the whole game.
