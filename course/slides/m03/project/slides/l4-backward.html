Backward induction

Don't only ask what happens next. Ask where the sequence eventually leads.

Then start from the final decision and reason back to what you should do now.

Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Let's say someone sends you an angry email. Your first impulse is to reply right away. But pause and ask: if I respond angrily, what will they probably do? Maybe they'll escalate. And if they escalate, what happens next? Maybe now you're both defending yourselves instead of solving the original problem. You haven't just sent an angry email. You've created a sequence. Formal game theory has a powerful tool for sequences, called backward induction. It sounds complicated, but the idea is simple. Instead of only asking what happens next, ask where the sequence eventually leads. In a finite sequence, you can start with the final decision and reason backward to the beginning, to the move you're about to make right now.
