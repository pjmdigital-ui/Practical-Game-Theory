Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

This is where a formal idea from game theory becomes incredibly useful. It's called a best response. Roughly speaking it's the action that gives a player the best outcome given what the other player is doing. Notice the last part of that sentence. Given what the other player is doing. A best response is never defined in a vacuum. It's always defined relative to someone else's move. Here's the simplest example I know. Imagine you and a friend are choosing a restaurant. If you know your friend is definitely going to the Italian place, perhaps your best response is to go there too. Now change one thing. Your friend is definitely going to the steakhouse. Your preferences didn't change. The menu didn't change. But your best response just changed. Why? Because their move changed. That's the key. Your best move isn't determined in isolation. It depends on their move. And this is the part people miss in conversation. We ask "What's the best thing to say?" as if there's one answer. There isn't. The best thing to say depends on how the other person is likely to respond. A sentence that works beautifully with one person can backfire badly with another. So when you catch yourself searching for the perfect line, stop. Ask what the other person is likely to do. Your best line depends on that. Now this idea has a famous cousin you may have heard of.
