Internomics · Module 3 — Think in Responses

Best responses are the building block of one of the most famous ideas in game theory. Nash equilibrium. Here's the plain version. A Nash equilibrium is an outcome where each player's strategy is a best response to the others. So no one benefits by changing their strategy alone. Go back to the restaurant. If you're both at the Italian place and neither of you would rather be somewhere else given where the other one is, you've landed on an equilibrium. Nobody has a reason to move on their own. MIT's game-theory curriculum treats best responses and Nash equilibrium as foundational concepts. And I'll be honest with you. The formal versions involve real math. You don't need any of it for this course. You don't need to calculate best-response functions to use the idea. What you need are two habits. The first habit is asking: if I do this, what are they likely to do next? The second habit is asking: if they do that, what will I do? That's it. Those two questions are where strategic thinking really begins. Most people stop before the first one. They decide what to say and then they say it. A few people get to the first question. Very few get to the second. When you ask both you're no longer just making a move. You're thinking in responses. We'll come back to equilibrium in depth in Module Five. For now let's put these two habits to work on a real situation.
