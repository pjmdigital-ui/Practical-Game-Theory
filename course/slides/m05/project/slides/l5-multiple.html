Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's where things get even more interesting. Some games have one equilibrium. Others have several. That means the same underlying game can support more than one stable pattern. Let me show you the simplest version. Imagine two people trying to coordinate. They can both choose Option A. Or they can both choose Option B. And suppose each person's best response is simply to match the other. Look at the two cards. If both choose A, neither one gains by switching alone. That's stable. If both choose B, neither one gains by switching alone either. That's just as stable. So you have two equilibria. Now here's the twist. The problem isn't necessarily choosing the wrong strategy. A is fine. B is fine. The problem is getting to the same one. If I pick A and you pick B, we both lose. That's called a coordination problem. And it's its own important category of strategic problem. It gets a full module later in the course. Often there isn't one right answer. There are several workable answers. The hard part is landing on the same one together. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to catch. When two people keep missing each other, we tend to assume one of them is choosing wrong. Sometimes nobody is wrong. They're just aiming at different equilibria. So here's a question. Where in your life are you and someone else both reasonable but aiming at different targets?
