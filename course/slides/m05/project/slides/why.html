Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Imagine two people who have been arguing for months. Neither likes the situation. Both would prefer things to be better. But neither one changes. Why? Because each person's behavior is partly a response to the other person's behavior. Look at the four lines on the screen. You stop calling because they never call you. They don't call because they always have to be the one who reaches out. You get defensive because they're always criticizing you. They criticize you because you never listen unless they push. And around and around you go. Here's the thing I want you to notice. Nobody sat down and said "Let's create a miserable relationship." Nobody planned this. Yet the two of you have arrived at a stable pattern. That's the basic intuition behind equilibrium. And it leads to the most important idea in this whole module. Stable doesn't mean good. A bad situation can last a long time without anyone being irrational. Think of one relationship in your life where you keep landing in the same place. Hold it in mind as we go. Now let me name the shift I'm asking you to make.
