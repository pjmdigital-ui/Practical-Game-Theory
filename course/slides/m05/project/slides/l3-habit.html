Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Once a pattern develops, it gets harder to change. Why? Because everyone's expectations adjust to it. Picture two people who keep misunderstanding each other. At first the misunderstandings may be accidental. Nobody means anything by them. But after enough repetitions each person starts anticipating the other. Follow the five boxes with me. You say "We need to talk." Before you've said anything else, they think "Here comes another criticism." So they become defensive. You read the defensiveness as confirmation that they're unwilling to listen. So you push harder. And they become even more defensive. Then it goes around again. Eventually the whole thing becomes almost automatic. Neither person has to consciously choose the pattern anymore. They've learned it. That's what an interpersonal equilibrium can feel like from the inside. Here's the deeper point. People don't merely respond to incentives. They respond to what they expect the other person to do. Look at the second box. They haven't heard a single criticism yet. They're reacting to the criticism they expect. And you're doing the same thing in the fourth box. You're reading their defensiveness through what you already believe about them. So the loop isn't only running on what people do. It's running on what people predict. The common misunderstanding is to think you can fix this with a gentler opening line. Sometimes that helps. But if they've learned to expect criticism, even a gentle "We need to talk" can set it off.
