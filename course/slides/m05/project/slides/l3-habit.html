Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Once a pattern develops, it gets harder to change, because everyone's expectations adjust to it. Picture two people who keep misunderstanding each other. At first the misunderstandings may be accidental. But after enough repetitions, each person starts anticipating the other. You say, "We need to talk." Before you've said anything else, they think, "Here comes another criticism." They become defensive. You read the defensiveness as confirmation that they're unwilling to listen, so you push harder. They get even more defensive. Eventually the whole thing becomes almost automatic. Neither person has to consciously choose the pattern anymore. They've learned it. That's what an interpersonal equilibrium can feel like. People don't merely respond to incentives. They respond to what they expect the other person to do.
