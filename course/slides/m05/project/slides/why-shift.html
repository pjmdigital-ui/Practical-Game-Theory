Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Before we get into the lessons, I want to name the mental shift this module asks of you. Up to now you've learned to see moves. Now I want you to see patterns. Look at the table. On the left are the questions we usually ask. On the right are the questions I want you to start asking. Don't just ask why they said that. Ask why the two of you keep ending up here. Don't just ask how you win this argument. Ask what interaction you're creating together. And don't just ask who's to blame. Ask what would have to change for the pattern to move. Notice what happens to your attention when you switch columns. The left column points at one person and one moment. The right column points at the space between you. It looks at what keeps repeating. That's where the real leverage is. Here's a quick example from the book. You ask for help. They say they're too busy. You say they're always too busy. They say you always ask at the last minute. If you stay in the left column, you'll spend the whole conversation arguing about who's right. If you move to the right column, you'll see a loop. And a loop is something you can actually change. Now look at the line at the bottom. It may be the most important sentence in this module. Don't assume that because a pattern is stable it must be good. It may simply mean nobody sees a better move to make alone. That's a very different thing. So as we go, keep one recurring situation from your own life in mind. Keep asking the right-hand questions about it. Here's what you'll be able to do by the end.
