Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

This may be the single most important thing to understand about equilibrium. Stable doesn't mean good. Look at the three cards. Equilibrium does not mean everyone is happy. It doesn't mean everyone got the best possible outcome. It doesn't even mean anyone thinks the situation is fair. Here's why people get confused. In everyday language we use the word equilibrium to mean balance. We hear harmony. We hear the way things ought to be. When someone says a relationship has found its equilibrium, it sounds like a nice thing. In game theory the word means something much more specific. The existing pattern is stable because changing alone doesn't look attractive. That's it. Nothing about whether the pattern is healthy. Nothing about whether it's kind. A Nash equilibrium can produce an outcome that is terrible for everyone involved. Think about what that means. You can be stuck in a pattern that both of you hate. And the reason you're stuck isn't that one of you secretly enjoys it. The reason is that neither of you sees a better move to make alone. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to watch for. When a pattern lasts a long time, we tend to assume somebody must want it. Somebody must be getting what they want. Sometimes that's true. But often the pattern simply lasts because each person is responding sensibly to the other. That distinction is enormously important. We'll lean on it for the rest of this module.
