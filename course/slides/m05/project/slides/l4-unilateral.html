The heart of equilibrium

If I change while you don't, I'm worse off. If you change while I don't, so are you.

So neither of us moves alone, whether the pattern is beneficial, harmful, unfair, or merely acceptable.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

This point is worth slowing down for, because it's the heart of the whole idea. Equilibrium is about unilateral change. Suppose you and I are in a stable pattern. I could change. You could change. But if I change while you don't, I end up worse off. And if you change while I don't, you end up worse off. So neither of us has a reason to move on our own. Notice that this says nothing about whether the pattern is any good. It might be mutually beneficial. It might be mutually harmful. It might be unfair, or inefficient, or merely acceptable. What makes it an equilibrium is only that each person's current strategy is a best response to the other's.
