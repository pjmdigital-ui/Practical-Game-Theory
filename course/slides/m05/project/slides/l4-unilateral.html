Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

This point is worth slowing down for. It's the heart of the whole idea. Equilibrium is about unilateral change. That just means one person changing while everyone else keeps doing what they were doing. Suppose you and I are in a stable pattern. I could change. You could change. But if I change while you don't, I end up worse off. And if you change while I don't, you end up worse off. So neither of us has a reason to move on our own. That's the heart of Nash equilibrium. Now look at the boxes on the slide. Notice that none of this says whether the pattern is any good. The pattern might be mutually beneficial. It might be mutually harmful. It might be unfair. It might be inefficient. Or it might be merely acceptable. What makes it an equilibrium is only the last box. Each person's current strategy is a best response to the other's. Here's why this matters so much. It changes what you're looking for when you try to understand a stuck situation. You stop asking whether the pattern is good. You start asking what would happen to each person if they changed alone. That question tells you why it's stuck. And here's the misunderstanding to watch for. People often hear "neither of us has a reason to move" and think it means neither of us wants change. Those are different things. You can want change badly and still see no safe way to go first. So here's a question. In your stuck situation, what would happen to you if you changed alone tomorrow? That brings us to some common advice that often backfires.
