Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Let's walk it in three questions. First: which outcome would both players prefer? Both cooperate. Nobody argues with that. Second: what should each player do if the other player's choice is fixed? If they cooperate, defect. If they defect, defect. So: defect. Third: so where do they end up? Both defect. And now you have an equilibrium. But it's a lousy one. Neither player would change on their own, and both would rather be somewhere else. That's one of the deepest lessons game theory has to offer. We usually explain bad outcomes by saying someone must be acting irrationally. Sometimes they are. But here, everyone doing what makes sense locally produces an outcome everyone dislikes globally.
