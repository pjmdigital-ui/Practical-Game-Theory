Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Sometimes the most powerful equilibria are invisible. You're inside them and you don't even realize it. Think about workplace meetings. Look at the top two cards. In private everyone complains that the meetings are too long. Yet in practice every meeting lasts two hours. Why? Look at the bottom two cards. The manager keeps scheduling them. The employees keep attending. And nobody wants to be the person who stands up and says "I'm leaving after 30 minutes." So the pattern continues. Here's what makes this so interesting. Privately almost everyone already wants shorter meetings. But nobody wants to be the first to break the pattern alone. Nobody wants to be the one who says it out loud. So everyone does the thing that makes sense given what everyone else is doing. And the long meeting survives week after week. That's an equilibrium hiding in plain sight. Nobody chose it. Everyone keeps it alive. The misunderstanding to watch for is the idea that if everyone dislikes something, it will naturally go away. It won't. Shared dislike isn't enough. Someone has to change something about the structure. Maybe the expectations. Maybe the sequence. Maybe the available choices. We'll get to those levers in the next lesson. For now here's a question. What's the two-hour meeting in your world? What's the thing everyone grumbles about and everyone keeps doing? Next, let's look at how these patterns turn into habits.
