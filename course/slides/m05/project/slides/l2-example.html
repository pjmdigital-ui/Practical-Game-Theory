Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's a simple example from the book. Read the exchange on the slide with me. You ask a simple question. Can you help me with this? They say they're too busy. You say they're always too busy. They say it's because you always ask at the last minute. You say it's because they never tell you when they're available. They say it's because you don't plan ahead. And around you go. Now I want you to notice something about every line in this exchange. Each one is a reasonable answer to the line before it. Each person is defending themselves. Each person has a point. If you only read one line at a time, you'd probably agree with whoever spoke last. That's what makes these conversations so sticky. Every statement feels justified from the inside. So here's the question most of us ask at this point. Who's right? Take a second and actually pick a side. Is it the person who asks late? Or the person who never shares their schedule? Most people pick one pretty quickly. And most people pick the side that looks like them. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to catch. We think if we can just settle who's right, the problem goes away. But winning this argument doesn't change anything about next week. The next request will still come late. The next answer will still be no. You'll both still have your reasons. So hold your answer for a moment. On the next slide I'll show you a more useful question than who's right. And I'll show you what this exchange looks like when you step back and look at the pattern.
