Example · "Can you help me with this?"

Who's right? Wrong question.

You: "Can you help me with this?"

They: "I'm too busy."

You: "You're always too busy."

They: "Because you always ask at the last minute."

You: "Because you never tell me when you're available."

They: "Because you don't plan ahead."

You ask late → they refuse → you criticize → they defend → you feel unsupported → you ask late again.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's a simple example from the book. You ask, "Can you help me with this?" They say, "I'm too busy." You say, "You're always too busy." They say, "Because you always ask at the last minute." You say, "Because you never tell me when you're available." They say, "Because you don't plan ahead." And around you go. So who's right? That's not the most useful question. Instead ask: what pattern have we created? Maybe it's this. You ask late, they refuse, you criticize, they defend, you feel unsupported, and you ask late again. Now we can see something that wasn't obvious when we looked at the individual statements. The interaction itself is producing the outcome.
