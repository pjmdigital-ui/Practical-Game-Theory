Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

A few mistakes I see all the time. First, treating "stable" as "good" or "chosen." A pattern that repeats may simply mean nobody sees a better move on their own. Ask what keeps it stable, not who secretly prefers it. Second, hunting for the villain. Ask what interaction is producing the pattern. That doesn't erase responsibility; it adds the question of what keeps the pattern going now. Third, fixing the next sentence, or making a one-sided change. If the loop is self-reinforcing, even a better line gets pulled back into it, and a solo move can be read through the old pattern and make things worse at first. Change something structural, especially expectations. Fourth, calling everything a Nash equilibrium, or treating it as a prediction. Persistence can come from habit, coercion, inertia or rules. Test whether changing alone would actually hurt, call it a lens, and keep running the loop: model, act, observe, update.
