Common mistakes

Treating "stable" as "good"

A repeating pattern may just mean nobody sees a better move alone. Ask what keeps it stable.

Hunting for the villain

Ask what interaction is producing the pattern. Ask what keeps it going now.

Fixing the next sentence

If the loop is self-reinforcing, even a better line gets pulled back into it

"Just change"

A one-sided move can be read through the old pattern. It can make things worse at first.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here are the first four mistakes. The first is treating stable as good or as chosen. A repeating pattern may simply mean nobody sees a better move to make alone. The second is hunting for the villain. Instead ask what interaction is producing this pattern. The third is fixing the next sentence instead of the pattern. Think of "We need to talk." The words were fine. The expectation around them wasn't. The fourth is "just change." A one-sided change can backfire when the other person reads it through the old pattern. Remember Marcus. Which of these four is your default? Two more to go.
