Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Let's talk about the mistakes I see all the time. There are six. Here are the first four. The first is treating stable as good. Or treating it as chosen. A pattern that repeats may simply mean nobody sees a better move to make alone. So don't ask who secretly prefers it. Ask what keeps it stable. The second is hunting for the villain. It's so tempting. Someone must be the problem. Instead ask what interaction is producing this pattern. Now this doesn't erase responsibility. People still own their choices. It adds a second question. What keeps the pattern going now? The third is fixing the next sentence instead of the pattern. We spend so much energy on the perfect line. But if the loop is self-reinforcing, even a better line gets pulled back into it. Think of "We need to talk." The words were fine. The expectation around them wasn't. Look at the system and not just the move. The fourth is "just change." It sounds like wisdom. But a one-sided change can make things worse at first. That happens when the other person reads it through the old pattern. Remember Marcus. He tried to be the bigger person and his silence read as disengagement. So change something structural instead. Especially expectations about what the other person will do. Here's a question for you. Which of these four is your default? Most of us have one we reach for first. Two more mistakes to go.
