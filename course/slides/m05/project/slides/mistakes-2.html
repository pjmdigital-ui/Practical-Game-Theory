Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here are the last two mistakes. Both are about using this idea with too much confidence. The fifth mistake is calling everything a Nash equilibrium. Once you have a new idea, it's tempting to see it everywhere. But persistence can come from other places. Habit can keep a pattern going. So can coercion. So can inertia. So can a rule nobody questions. Those are different engines and they need different fixes. So run the test from Lesson 5. Would changing alone actually hurt this person? If the answer is yes, the equilibrium lens fits. If not, look for another explanation. And when you talk about everyday patterns, call it an equilibrium lens. Save "Nash equilibrium" for formal games you've actually modeled. The sixth mistake is treating equilibrium as prediction. It doesn't tell you which stable outcome people will reach. Remember the equilibrium-selection problem. It also doesn't tell you exactly what people will do. Real people make mistakes. They have limited information. They use rules of thumb. They learn as they go. So what's the fix for both mistakes? Look at the bottom line. Hold the model lightly. Use it to generate a guess. Then test the guess. Model. Act. Observe. Update. If someone surprises you, that's not a failure of the idea. It's information. It tells you your read of the game was incomplete. Here's a question to close this section. Think about the last time you were certain about why someone did something. How much of that certainty came from what you actually observed?
