Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Thomas Schelling famously illustrated coordination with a simple thought experiment. Imagine you've agreed to meet someone in New York City tomorrow. You've agreed on the day. But you haven't agreed on the location or the time. Where do you go? A famous result of Schelling's experiments was that people frequently converged on noon at Grand Central Station, even though nothing about those choices was mathematically unique. The place and the time worked as a focal point: something that stands out and helps people line up their expectations. That's fascinating, because it shows people don't always need to communicate explicitly to coordinate. They can use context, conventions, salience and shared expectations.
