Definition · Nash equilibrium

Given what everyone else is doing, no one has a reason to change on their own.

Not that everyone is happy, got the best outcome, or thinks it's fair. Only that changing alone doesn't look attractive.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

In game theory, a Nash equilibrium is a combination of strategies in which each player's strategy is a best response to the strategies of the others. In simpler terms: given what everyone else is doing, no individual player has a reason to change their strategy on their own. Now notice what that does not mean. It doesn't mean everyone is happy. It doesn't mean everyone got the best possible outcome. It doesn't even mean anyone thinks the situation is fair. In everyday language we hear "equilibrium" as balance, harmony, the way things ought to be. In game theory it means something much more specific: the existing pattern is stable because changing unilaterally doesn't look attractive. That distinction is enormously important, and we're going to lean on it for the rest of this module.
