The classic example is the Prisoner's Dilemma. Two people each choose: cooperate or defect. If both cooperate, they get a relatively good outcome. If one defects while the other cooperates, the defector does especially well. If both defect, they both do worse than they would have under mutual cooperation. Now put yourself in one player's shoes. If the other person cooperates, I'd be better off defecting. If the other person defects, I'd better defect too. So regardless of what they do, defection looks like my best response. And the other player reasons exactly the same way. Here's the part I want you to sit with: the problem isn't that they don't understand cooperation would be better. They do. The problem is that, given the structure of the game, each player's individual best response pushes them toward defection.
