Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

So what about learning? This is where things get particularly interesting. Suppose you and I play a game again and again. At first neither of us knows what the other will do. Follow the four boxes with me. We experiment. We observe. We adjust. And eventually we may settle into a stable pattern. Nobody sat down and calculated the equilibrium. We found our way into it one round at a time. The book points to experimental evidence on this. In some games learning can produce convergence toward Nash equilibria under certain conditions. But look at the second line at the bottom. Convergence isn't universal. It depends on the structure of the game. This connects directly to the loop we set up at the very start of the course. Model. Act. Observe. Update. Equilibrium can be something players learn into. It isn't necessarily something they calculate on day one. Here's why that matters for you. Think back to the habit loop from Lesson 3. "We need to talk" leads to defensiveness. That pattern was learned. Each person observed the other and adjusted. And they adjusted their way into something neither of them wanted. That's the hopeful side of this idea too. If a pattern was learned, it can be relearned. New experiences can teach new expectations. The misunderstanding to avoid is thinking every relationship is heading toward some fixed point. It isn't. Some patterns settle. Some keep shifting. Some never settle at all. So here's a question. What's one pattern in your life that you learned your way into? What experience would it take to learn your way out? Now let me give you a rule for using this idea with integrity.
