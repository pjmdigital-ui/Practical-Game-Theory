Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Your tool for this module is the Equilibrium Audit. It's in your workbook. Use it when you're stuck in a recurring interaction. Do it in writing. And do it at a calm moment, not in the middle of the argument. Let's walk the prompts in order. Number one. What keeps happening? Forget the individual incident. Write the pattern as a loop. My move. Their response. My response. Number two. What does my behavior cause them to do? Number three. What does their behavior cause me to do? Those two questions together show you the loop from both sides. Number four. Why might neither of us want to change on our own? Write down what you would risk if you changed alone. Then write what they would risk. Number five. Is this pattern actually good for either of us? What does each of you get from keeping it? What does it cost each of you? Number six. Are there other patterns that could hold steady? Describe at least one. Number seven is the equilibrium check. Is this really held in place by the cost of changing alone? Or is it held by habit or a rule or coercion or simple lack of awareness? Be honest here. And number eight is the most important question. What would have to change for a different pattern to become attractive? Look back at the eight levers from Lesson 4. Pick one. Then design your first small experiment. Here's the mistake people make with this tool. They fill in the first three questions and stop. They've described the problem in more detail. That feels like progress. But the real value is in questions four through eight. So push all the way through. Let's practice on a scenario.
