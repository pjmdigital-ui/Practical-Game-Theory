Research spotlight · Charness, Rigotti & Rustichini

Change the payoffs and behavior changes

What it shows

One-shot Prisoner's Dilemmas with 348 participants. Cooperation ranged from 23% to 60% depending on the payoff for mutual cooperation. Beliefs about others shifted too.

What it doesn't prove

Not that people always cooperate. Not that your relationship is a Prisoner's Dilemma. Not that Nash is either a reliable predictor or useless.

What it suggests: structure matters. But the money-only prediction ("everyone defects") didn't hold. Beliefs and norms matter too.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

This study comes from Charness, Rigotti and Rustichini. They ran one-shot Prisoner's Dilemma experiments with 348 participants. They changed how much players earned from mutual cooperation. Cooperation ranged from 23 percent to 60 percent depending on that payoff. Beliefs about whether others would cooperate shifted too. First, structure matters. Change the payoffs and you change behavior. Second, the money-only prediction that everyone defects didn't hold. This doesn't show that people always cooperate. It doesn't show that any relationship is a Prisoner's Dilemma. If one payoff change can move cooperation that much, what payoff in your stuck situation could you change?
