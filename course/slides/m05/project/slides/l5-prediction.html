Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's another subtle point. A Nash equilibrium tells us: if everyone is behaving this way, no one wants to deviate on their own. It does not tell us this is exactly what real people will do. Human beings make mistakes. They have limited information. They may not calculate perfectly. They use rules of thumb. And they learn over time. Experimental work has found that real behavior in games can differ systematically from Nash predictions. So what about learning? Suppose we play a game again and again. At first neither of us knows what the other will do. We experiment, observe, adjust, and eventually we may settle into a stable pattern. In some games, learning can produce convergence toward equilibrium, though it isn't universal and depends on the structure of the game. That's our loop again: model, act, observe, update. Equilibrium can be something players learn into, not something they calculate on day one.
