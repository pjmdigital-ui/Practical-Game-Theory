Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's another subtle point. I want you to get this one right. Look at the two lines at the bottom. A Nash equilibrium tells us something specific. If everyone is behaving this way, no one wants to deviate on their own. That's what it tells you. Here's what it doesn't tell you. It doesn't tell you that this is exactly what real people will do. Why not? Look at the four cards. Human beings make mistakes. They have limited information. They may not calculate perfectly. They may use rules of thumb instead of working everything out. And they learn over time. They change as they go. The book points to experimental work on exactly this. Actual behavior in games can differ systematically from Nash predictions. Researchers have even developed other behavioral models partly to account for those differences. So this is an important limit for our whole framework. We're using equilibrium as a mental model. We're not using it as a crystal ball. It helps us spot patterns of stability. It doesn't give us certainty about human behavior. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for. Once people learn this idea, they sometimes start using it to forecast others. They say "He'll never change. It's an equilibrium." That's using the lens as a prophecy. People surprise us all the time. When they do, that's information. It tells you your model of the game was incomplete. So here's a question for you. Have you ever been sure how someone would respond and been completely wrong? What did your model miss? Now let's look at that last card more closely. What happens when people learn?
