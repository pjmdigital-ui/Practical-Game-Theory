Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

So who's right? Here's my answer. That's not the most useful question. Instead ask this. What pattern have we created? Look at the first card. The situation is a simple request that turns into a debate about who's busy and who plans badly. Every line answers the one before it. Now look at the middle card. When you step back, the equilibrium might look like this. You ask late. They refuse. You criticize. They defend. You feel unsupported. And because you feel unsupported, you hold off asking until you really need help. Which means you ask late again. The loop closes. Now we can see something that wasn't obvious when we looked at the individual statements. Neither of you is the cause on your own. The interaction itself is producing the outcome. That's the lesson on the third card. Skip who's right. Ask what pattern you've created. Here's why that's so freeing. When you argue about who's right, you're stuck defending your line. When you look at the loop, you can see several places where it could be interrupted. The ask could come earlier. Availability could be shared. The criticism could be dropped. You don't need to win the argument to change the outcome. Now watch for one misunderstanding. Naming the pattern doesn't mean you blame the pattern instead of the person. It means you both get to look at the same thing from the same side of the table. Think of your own version of this loop. Where does it close for you?
