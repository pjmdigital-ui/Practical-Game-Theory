When one side moves alone

Good intentions, worse outcome

"Just stop arguing"

It sounds reasonable. But if the partner reads the silence as abandonment, they may escalate. The change makes things worse at first.

"Just raise your prices"

In a price war one company raises prices. If the other doesn't follow, it may lose customers. So it has little reason to move alone.

One person's good intentions rarely break an equilibrium. Sometimes the players need to change their expectations about what the others will do. That's much harder.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

This explains why "just change" can be bad advice. Look at the first card. Imagine telling someone stuck in a dysfunctional relationship to just stop participating in the argument. That sounds reasonable. It's the kind of advice you'd hear from a friend. But what happens next? If their partner interprets the silence as abandonment, the partner may escalate. The person's change could make the situation worse. At least at first. They made a good move by their own standards. But it landed inside the old pattern. And the old pattern gave it a meaning they never intended. Now look at the second card. Take a company trying to stop a price war. It raises its prices. If the other company doesn't follow, it may lose customers. So the first company has very little reason to move alone. Do you see the common thread? In both cases one person changes while the other person keeps the same expectations. The person who moves first pays the price for moving first. Here's the key idea. An equilibrium isn't necessarily overcome by one person's good intentions. Sometimes the players need to change their expectations about what the others will do. That's much harder. And honestly it's much more interesting. Now I don't want you to hear this as "never change first." Sometimes going first is exactly right. The point is to go first with your eyes open. Expect that your change may be misread. Think about how to make it readable. So here's a question. Have you ever tried to be the bigger person and had it backfire? What did the other person think your change meant? Let's look at what you can change instead.
