When one side moves alone

Good intentions, worse outcome

"Just stop arguing"

It sounds reasonable. But if the partner reads the silence as abandonment, they may escalate. The change makes things worse at first.

"Just raise your prices"

In a price war one company raises prices. If the other doesn't follow, it may lose customers. So it has little reason to move alone.

One person's good intentions rarely break an equilibrium. Sometimes the players need to change their expectations about what the others will do. That's much harder.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Imagine telling someone in a dysfunctional relationship to just stop participating in the argument. But if their partner reads the silence as abandonment, the partner may escalate. Their good move landed inside the old pattern. Or take a company trying to stop a price war. It raises its prices. If the other company doesn't follow, it may lose customers. In both cases one side changes while the other keeps the same expectations. The one who moves first pays the price. An equilibrium isn't necessarily overcome by one person's good intentions.
