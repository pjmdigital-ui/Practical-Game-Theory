When one side moves alone

Good intentions, worse outcome

"Just stop arguing"

If the partner reads the silence as abandonment, they may escalate. The change makes things worse at first.

"Just raise your prices"

In a price war, the company that raises prices alone may simply lose customers.

Sometimes the players need to change their expectations about what the others will do. That's much harder.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

This explains why "just change" can be bad advice. Imagine telling someone stuck in a dysfunctional relationship, "Just stop participating in the argument." Sounds reasonable. But what happens next? If their partner interprets the silence as abandonment, the partner may escalate. The person's unilateral change could make the situation worse, at least at first. Or take a company trying to end a price war. It raises its prices. If the other company doesn't follow, it loses customers. So it has very little reason to move alone. An equilibrium isn't necessarily overcome by one person's good intentions. Sometimes the players need to change their expectations about what the others will do. That's much harder. And, honestly, much more interesting.
