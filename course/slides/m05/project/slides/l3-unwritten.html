Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's an important distinction. An agreement is something people explicitly decide. They sit down. They talk it through. They settle on terms. An equilibrium doesn't require an agreement at all. Two people can arrive at a stable pattern without ever discussing it. In fact many of the strongest social patterns are unwritten. Look at the six boxes on the slide. Think about one of your own relationships as I read them. You know who speaks first. You know who pays. You know who makes the decision. You know who apologizes. You know who gives in. You know who gets interrupted. And you know who gets the last word. Now ask yourself a simple question. When did you negotiate any of that? Most likely you never did. Nobody may have formally agreed to these rules. Yet everyone understands them. They're part of the game. Here's why this matters. If you think a pattern rests on an agreement, you'll try to fix it by renegotiating. You'll say let's make a new deal. But if the pattern is an equilibrium, there was never a deal to begin with. It rests on what each person expects the other to do. So changing it means changing those expectations. A common misunderstanding is that unwritten means weak. The opposite is often true. Unwritten patterns can be the hardest ones to move, because nobody can point to where they came from. So pick one of these six boxes. Who plays that role in your closest relationship? And how did that get decided? Some equilibria go even further. Some are invisible.
