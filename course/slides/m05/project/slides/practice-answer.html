Here's how I'd read it. The loop: Priya flags risks, Marcus goes quiet, Priya escalates with longer emails and the cc, Marcus feels attacked and shares less, Priya sees even less and escalates further. It's "I push, you resist, I push harder" at work. Given Marcus's silence, escalating is Priya's way to make sure risks get handled and she isn't blamed. Given her escalation, guarding information is Marcus's way to protect his reputation. Neither is irrational, and both want a partnership. Stable doesn't mean good. When Marcus changed alone, everyone kept their old expectations, so his silence read as disengagement and he paid for moving first. Honestly, with no formal payoffs, it isn't a proven Nash equilibrium. It resembles an equilibrium trap, and the check supports that. The fix touches structure: a joint risk review changes sequence and information, a no-cc agreement is a commitment, and briefing the director together uses the third party. Now neither carries the whole risk of going first. "Marcus should communicate more" does none of that.
