Example · Waiting for senior management

"Why doesn't anyone take initiative?"

The situation

Everyone waits for senior management before making any decision.

What happens

Management: "Why doesn't anyone take initiative?" Employees: "Because whenever we do, management overrides us."

The lesson

Each side's behavior reinforces the other's. That's an equilibrium.

"Take more initiative" doesn't touch what holds the pattern in place.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Let's put this to work on an example from the book. Look at the first card. Picture a company where everyone waits for senior management before making any decision. Small decisions. Big decisions. Everything goes up the chain. Now the middle card. Management complains. They ask why nobody takes initiative. And the employees have their own answer. Because whenever we do, management overrides us. Now look at the third card. Each side's behavior reinforces the other's. Management overrides, so employees wait. Employees wait, so management steps in. That's an equilibrium. Now imagine management's fix is to tell everyone to take more initiative. Maybe in a meeting. Maybe in an email. What happens? Probably not much. Think about it from the employee's seat. An employee who goes first alone still risks being overridden. The message doesn't change the reason they wait. It doesn't touch what holds the pattern in place. That's the bottom line on the slide. And notice that this is exactly the "just change" problem from earlier in this lesson. It's a request for one side to move alone while everything else stays the same. Here's the misunderstanding to avoid. It's tempting to decide which side is to blame. Management is controlling. Or employees are passive. You could argue either one all day. But blame won't move the pattern. So here's the question for you. Look back at the eight levers. Which one would you pull here? What would have to change for an employee to believe that going first is safe? Take a moment with that. Here's the takeaway for this lesson.
