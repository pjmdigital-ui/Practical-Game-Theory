Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Picture a company where everyone waits for senior management before making any decision. Management complains, "Why doesn't anyone take initiative?" Employees think, "Because whenever we do, management overrides us." Each side's behavior reinforces the other's. That's an equilibrium. Now imagine management's fix is a memo that says, "Take more initiative." What happens? Probably nothing. An employee who goes first alone still risks being overridden, so the memo doesn't change the reason they wait. It doesn't touch what holds the pattern in place. The useful question here is not "Who's to blame?" It's "What would have to change for the pattern to move?" Maybe it's a commitment from management about which decisions they won't override. That changes the structure, not just the slogan.
