Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

I want you to use this idea with integrity. So here's a credibility rule. Look at the left card. Say "Nash equilibrium" when you're talking about a formal game. That means a game where you've actually specified the strategies and the payoffs. Now the right card. For the everyday patterns in your life, say "equilibrium lens." You're asking why a pattern persists. You're not claiming you've proven anything. Here's what that sounds like in practice. Your marriage isn't literally in a Nash equilibrium unless you've modeled it. Nobody has written down the payoffs of your marriage. But a pattern in your marriage can resemble an equilibrium trap. That's a useful and honest thing to notice. Saying it that way keeps you credible. It also keeps you curious. Now look at the bottom of the slide. Not everything that persists is an equilibrium. Habit can keep things going. So can coercion. So can inertia. So can a rule that nobody questions. Those are different engines. They need different fixes. So run a simple test. Would changing alone actually make this person worse off? If the answer is yes, the equilibrium lens fits. If the answer is no, something else is holding the pattern in place. Then you'll want a different explanation. Here's the misunderstanding I see most. Someone learns this idea and suddenly everything is an equilibrium. Every bad habit. Every stuck team. Every family tension. That's not insight. That's a new hammer looking for nails. So here's a question. Take the pattern you've been thinking about. Run the test.
