Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's how this happens to a real person. Imagine a new employee joins a company where everyone stays late. Nobody hands them a rule. There's no policy that says you must stay until seven. Watch what happens in their head over time. Look at the first card. At first they think "Why is everyone still here?" They notice it. It seems odd. Maybe even a little unhealthy. Now the second card. After a few weeks they think "I guess this is normal." The surprise is gone. Now the third card. After a few months they think "If I leave at five, people will think I'm not committed." And now look at what's happened. Their own behavior reinforces the very norm they questioned when they walked in. They've become part of the reason the next new hire will stay late too. Nobody wrote the rule. Nobody told them to stay. They're responding to what they expect everyone else to think. And in doing so they help keep that expectation true. The equilibrium has become cultural. This is one reason social norms can be so powerful. People don't merely respond to incentives. They respond to expectations about what other people will do. Here's the misunderstanding I want you to avoid. It would be easy to look at this office and conclude that everyone there loves working late. That's not what the pattern tells you. It may simply mean nobody sees a better move to make alone. So think about your own workplace or your own family. What's a norm you questioned at first and now follow without thinking?
