Research spotlight · Dal Bó & Fréchette

A better pattern isn't enough

What it shows

In repeated Prisoner's Dilemmas with no known end, cooperation can emerge. But the conditions are more demanding than theory requires. It must survive strategic uncertainty.

What it doesn't prove

These are lab games. They're not marriages or teams. They don't say when your relationship will shift. They don't say cooperation is always right.

The practical question: how do we move to a better pattern without one person carrying all the risk?

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here's a second line of research. It comes from Dal Bó and Fréchette. There's their study in the American Economic Review. And there's their later survey in the Journal of Economic Literature. In these experiments participants played Prisoner's Dilemmas repeated with no known end. Here's what theory says about that. In repeated play mutual cooperation can become sustainable if players value the future enough. So you'd expect cooperation to show up whenever the math allows it. Look at the left card. Cooperation can emerge. But it emerges under conditions more demanding than the bare theoretical requirement. Their survey concludes that being supportable in equilibrium isn't enough. Cooperation also has to be robust to strategic uncertainty. That's a fancy phrase for a simple fear. It's the risk that the other person won't go along. So what does this suggest for real life? Knowing a better equilibrium exists doesn't mean people will find it. Picture two partners who both want more openness. They may still stay guarded. Why? Because whoever opens up first absorbs the risk. Now look at the right card. These are lab games with defined payoffs. They're not marriages or teams. They don't tell you when your own relationship will shift. And they don't say cooperation is always right. Some competitive patterns exist for good reasons. But the practical question at the bottom is a great one. How do we move to a better pattern without one person carrying all the risk? Keep that question close.
