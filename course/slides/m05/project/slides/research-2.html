Research spotlight · Dal Bó & Fréchette

A better pattern isn't enough

What it shows

In indefinitely repeated Prisoner's Dilemmas, cooperation can emerge, but under conditions more demanding than theory requires. It must be robust to strategic uncertainty.

What it doesn't prove

These are lab games, not marriages or teams. They don't say when your relationship will shift, or that cooperation is always right.

The practical question: how do we move to a better pattern without one person carrying all the risk?

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

A second line of research, from Dal Bó and Fréchette, in the American Economic Review and their later survey in the Journal of Economic Literature. Here participants played Prisoner's Dilemmas repeated with no known end. Theory says mutual cooperation can be sustained in repeated play if players value the future enough. And cooperation can emerge, but under conditions more demanding than the bare theoretical requirement. Their survey concludes that being supportable in equilibrium isn't enough; cooperation also has to be robust to strategic uncertainty, the risk that the other person won't go along. In real life: two partners who both want more openness may stay guarded, because whoever opens up first absorbs the risk. These are lab games, not marriages or teams, and some competitive patterns exist for good reasons. But the practical question is a great one: how do we move to a better pattern without one person carrying all the risk?
