Research spotlight · Dal Bó & Fréchette

A better pattern isn't enough

What it shows

In repeated Prisoner's Dilemmas with no known end, cooperation can emerge. But the conditions are more demanding than theory requires. It must survive strategic uncertainty.

What it doesn't prove

These are lab games. They're not marriages or teams. They don't say when your relationship will shift. They don't say cooperation is always right.

The practical question: how do we move to a better pattern without one person carrying all the risk?

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

This research comes from Dal Bó and Fréchette. Participants played Prisoner's Dilemmas repeated with no known end. Theory says mutual cooperation can be sustainable if players value the future enough. Cooperation can emerge. But it needs conditions more demanding than the bare theoretical requirement. It also has to be robust to strategic uncertainty. So knowing a better equilibrium exists doesn't mean people will find it. Two partners who both want more openness may still stay guarded. Whoever opens up first absorbs the risk. How do we move to a better pattern without one person carrying all the risk? Next, your tool.
