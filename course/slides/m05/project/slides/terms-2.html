Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Here are four more terms. These come from the last lesson. A coordination problem is the challenge of getting everyone to the same equilibrium when several are possible. Remember A and A versus B and B. Both are fine. The hard part is landing on the same one. A focal point is something that stands out. It helps people coordinate their expectations without explicit communication. Noon at Grand Central is the classic example. Nothing made it unique. It just stood out. The equilibrium-selection problem is knowing the possible stable outcomes without knowing which one people will actually land on. That's where beliefs and expectations come in. So do conventions and focal points. And finally the equilibrium lens. This is the one you'll use most. It means using equilibrium thinking to ask why an everyday pattern persists. And it means doing that without claiming it's a formally proven Nash equilibrium. That's the honest way to talk about your marriage or your team or your family. A pattern can resemble an equilibrium trap. That doesn't make it a proven one. Here's the misunderstanding to watch for with this whole vocabulary. These words can make you sound certain. Don't let them. They're tools for asking better questions. They aren't verdicts about people. Now let's look at what the research actually shows.
