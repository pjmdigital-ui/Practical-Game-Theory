Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Let's lock in the vocabulary. These words will come back again and again in later modules. A Nash equilibrium is a set of strategies where each player's choice is a best response to the others. So no one has a reason to change on their own. Remember that it says nothing about whether the outcome is good. Unilateral change is one player changing while everyone else keeps doing what they were doing. Equilibrium is defined by the lack of reward for that kind of change. That's why "just change" so often backfires. The Prisoner's Dilemma is the classic game where each player's best response is to defect. So both defect. And they do it even though both would prefer mutual cooperation. An equilibrium trap is a stable pattern that the people inside it dislike. Yet no one sees a good way to leave it alone. Think of the price war. Think of the coworkers working unsustainable hours. And a self-reinforcing pattern is a loop. Each person's response provokes the response that keeps it going. Complaining and withdrawing is the example we used. The complaint invites the withdrawal. The withdrawal invites the complaint. If you want to test yourself, try this. Take one recurring situation in your life. Can you describe it using three of these five terms? If you can, you're already seeing the equilibrium.
