Equilibrium traps are everywhere

Rival companies

"If we don't lower our prices, they'll undercut us." Both cut. Both make less money. Neither wants to raise prices first.

Two coworkers

"If I stop this extra work, they'll take advantage of me." Both keep working unsustainable hours.

A couple

One feels unappreciated and complains. The other feels criticized and withdraws. Each fuels the other.

The pattern persists because each response makes sense given the other's. Self-reinforcing doesn't mean healthy.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Once you see this, you start seeing it everywhere. I call these equilibrium traps. Look at the first card. Two competing companies each think the same thing. If we don't lower our prices, they'll undercut us. So both lower their prices. Now both make less money. Neither company likes it. But neither wants to be the one to raise prices first. Now the second card. Two coworkers each believe the same thing. If I stop doing this extra work, the other person will take advantage of me. So both keep working unsustainable hours. Neither likes it. Neither sees a way out on their own. And the third card brings it home. Imagine a married couple. One partner feels unappreciated. So they complain. The other partner feels criticized. So they withdraw. The withdrawal causes more complaining. The complaining causes more withdrawal. Eventually each of them develops a story. They're the reason we can't communicate. But step back for a second. What if neither behavior makes sense in isolation? The complaint is a response to the withdrawal. The withdrawal is a response to the complaint. Each one reinforces the other. They've created a loop. And the loop can become stable. That doesn't mean it's healthy. It means the pattern is self-reinforcing. That's the thing I want you to start noticing. Here's a question. Which of these three cards looks most like something in your own life right now?
