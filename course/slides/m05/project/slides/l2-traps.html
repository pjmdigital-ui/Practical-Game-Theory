Equilibrium traps are everywhere

Rival companies

Each cuts prices for fear of being undercut. Both make less money.

Two coworkers

Each keeps working unsustainable hours for fear of being taken advantage of.

A couple

Complaint responds to withdrawal. Withdrawal responds to complaint.

Each loop is self-reinforcing. That doesn't make it healthy.

Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Once you see this, you see it everywhere. Two competing companies each think, "If we don't lower our prices, they'll undercut us." So both lower prices, and both make less money. Neither likes it, but neither wants to be the one to raise prices first. Two coworkers each believe, "If I stop doing this extra work, the other person will take advantage of me." So both keep working unsustainable hours. Or a couple. One partner feels unappreciated, so they complain. The other feels criticized, so they withdraw. The withdrawal causes more complaining, the complaining causes more withdrawal, and eventually each has a story: "They're the reason we can't communicate." But neither behavior makes sense in isolation. Each is a response to the other. The loop can become stable. That doesn't mean it's healthy. It means it's self-reinforcing.
