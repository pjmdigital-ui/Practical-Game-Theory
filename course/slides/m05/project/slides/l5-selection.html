Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Multiple equilibria point to a real limitation of the whole concept. I want to be honest with you about it. Suppose a game has two equilibria. Knowing that doesn't tell us which one people will choose. That's called the equilibrium-selection problem. And this is where context becomes important. Look at the first card. Research following Thomas Schelling's work has shown that salient labels and contextual cues can influence which equilibrium people coordinate on. Something that stands out can pull people toward the same answer. Now the second card. The effectiveness of those cues depends on the underlying payoffs. In experiments even small payoff asymmetries could substantially disrupt coordination around choices that were otherwise obvious. In other words, small differences in who gains can break an obvious answer. Now the third card. So what decides which pattern people land on? Beliefs. Expectations. Communication. Conventions. And focal points. That's where all of those enter the picture. Here's why this matters. It keeps us from making an overly simple claim. The claim goes like this. Once you know the equilibria, you know what will happen. Not necessarily. You may know the possible stable outcomes without knowing which one people will select. That's a misunderstanding worth guarding against. Catch yourself when you think "I know exactly where this is going." Then slow down. You may know the options. You may not know the destination. So here's a question. In a situation where you and someone else could settle on several patterns, what's pulling you toward one of them? We'll see a famous example of a focal point in a moment.
