Internomics · Module 5 — See the Equilibrium

Knowing a game has two equilibria doesn't tell us which one people will choose. That's called the equilibrium-selection problem. Research following Thomas Schelling's work has shown that salient labels and contextual cues can influence which equilibrium people coordinate on. But those cues depend on the underlying payoffs. In experiments even small payoff asymmetries could substantially disrupt coordination around otherwise obvious choices. So beliefs and expectations enter the picture. Here's the misunderstanding to guard against. Knowing the equilibria doesn't tell you what will happen. You may know the options without knowing the destination. Next, another limit.
